## 1. Grammar & Vocabulary PYQs

### Error Spotting

**Q1.** Find the error:  
*"Each of the students have completed their assignments on time."*  
**Answer:** "have" → "has" (Each = singular subject)

**Q2.** Find the error:  
*"Neither the manager nor the employees was present at the meeting."*  
**Answer:** "was" → "were" (Verb agrees with the nearest subject: "employees" = plural)

**Q3.** Find the error:  
*"She is one of the girls who has won the prize."*  
**Answer:** "has" → "have" (who refers to "girls" = plural)

---

### Sentence Correction

**Q4.** *"He has been working here since five years."*  
**Correct:** "He has been working here **for** five years."  
> **Rule:** "since" = specific point in time. "for" = duration of time.

**Q5.** *"I am looking forward to hear from you."*  
**Correct:** "I am looking forward **to hearing** from you."  
> **Rule:** "to" here is a preposition, not infinitive → followed by gerund (-ing).

**Q6.** *"The meeting is scheduled on 3 PM."*  
**Correct:** "The meeting is scheduled **at** 3 PM."  
> **Rule:** at = time, on = date/day, in = month/year/season.

---

### Fill in the Blanks (Prepositions/Articles)

**Q7.** "She arrived ___ the airport ___ 6 AM."  
**Answer:** "at" the airport, "at" 6 AM

**Q8.** "He is ___ honest man and ___ good citizen."  
**Answer:** "an" honest man and "a" good citizen  
> **Rule:** "an" before vowel SOUND (h is silent in "honest")

**Q9.** "We discussed ___ the problem for hours."  
**Answer:** Remove "about" — "discuss" is a transitive verb (no preposition needed)

### Commonly Confused Words

| Word 1 | Word 2 | Difference |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| Affect (verb) | Effect (noun) | "The rain **affected** the game." / "The **effect** was significant." |
| Accept (receive) | Except (exclude) | "I **accept** everything **except** the last point." |
| Compliment (praise) | Complement (complete) | "Nice **compliment**!" / "They **complement** each other." |
| Principle (rule) | Principal (main/head) | "Stick to your **principles**." / "The **principal** spoke." |
| Stationary (still) | Stationery (paper) | "The car was **stationary**." / "Buy some **stationery**." |
| Their / There / They're | — | Possession / Place / They are |
| Its / It's | — | Possession / It is |

## 2. Reading Comprehension Tips

### Strategy (2 minutes per passage)
```
Step 1: Read the QUESTIONS first (know what to look for)
Step 2: Skim the passage (topic sentences, first/last paragraphs)
Step 3: Locate specific answers (scan for keywords)
Step 4: Eliminate wrong options → pick the best fit
```

### Common Question Types
1. **Main Idea:** "What is the passage primarily about?"
2. **Detail:** "According to the passage, which is true?"
3. **Inference:** "It can be inferred that..."
4. **Vocabulary in Context:** "The word 'paramount' most nearly means..."
5. **Tone/Attitude:** "The author's tone can best be described as..."

## 3. Listening Comprehension Tips

```
⚡ Use WIRED headphones with a mic (no Bluetooth — delays)
⚡ Sit in a COMPLETELY quiet room
⚡ Audio plays ONCE — no replay!
⚡ Take quick mental notes of: Names, Numbers, Dates, Actions
⚡ Focus on WHO said WHAT and WHY
```

### Practice Topics
- Workplace conversations (meeting scheduling, project updates)
- Customer service scenarios
- Instructions and directions
- News-style reports

## 4. Speaking Tasks

### Task Types

**Type 1: Read Aloud**
- Read a given sentence clearly
- Focus on pronunciation and natural pausing
- Don't rush — steady pace

**Type 2: Hear and Repeat**
- Listen to a sentence and repeat it exactly
- Focus on matching pronunciation and intonation

**Type 3: Speak on a Topic (45-90 seconds)**

### Practice Topics (Most Asked)

1. "Describe your favorite hobby and why you enjoy it"
2. "Talk about a recent challenge you overcame"
3. "Why did you choose your field of study?"
4. "Describe a person who has influenced you"
5. "What are your career goals for the next 5 years?"
6. "Talk about the importance of teamwork"
7. "Describe a project you worked on recently"
8. "What qualities make a good leader?"

### Speaking Framework (PREP Method)

```
P - Point:     State your main point clearly
R - Reason:    Give a reason or explanation
E - Example:   Provide a specific example
P - Point:     Restate or conclude

Example:
"My favorite hobby is reading. (Point)
 It helps me learn new perspectives and improves my vocabulary. (Reason)
 Recently, I read 'Atomic Habits' which taught me about building better routines. (Example)
 So, reading is not just a pastime for me, but a tool for personal growth. (Point)"
```

### Speaking Tips
```
✅ Speak at a NATURAL pace (not too fast, not too slow)
✅ Use simple, clear sentences (avoid complex words you might mispronounce)
✅ Pause briefly between sentences (shows structured thinking)
✅ Avoid filler words (um, uh, like, you know)
✅ AI is scoring — clarity > accent
✅ Practice recording yourself and listening back
```

## 5. Writing — Professional Email

### Email Template

```
Subject: [Clear, specific subject line]

Dear [Mr./Ms. Last Name / Team],

[Opening: State purpose in 1 sentence]
I am writing to [request/inform/follow up on]...

[Body: Key details in 2-3 sentences]
[Provide context, specifics, or action items]

[Closing: Clear next step]
I would appreciate if you could [action] by [deadline].
Please feel free to reach out if you need any further information.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
[Your Designation/Roll No.]
```

### Practice Email Scenarios

**Scenario 1:** Write an email requesting a meeting room extension for your team.

```
Subject: Request for Meeting Room Extension — Project Alpha

Dear Facilities Team,

I am writing to request an extension of Meeting Room B for our team from
2:00 PM to 5:00 PM on September 12, 2026.

Our project review requires additional time for the presentation and
discussion with the client team. We expect 8 attendees.

I would be grateful if you could confirm the availability at your earliest
convenience.

Best regards,
Dilip
B.Tech CSE, LPU
```

**Scenario 2:** Write an email escalating a quality issue to your manager.

```
Subject: Quality Issue — Module 3 Test Failures

Dear Mr. Sharma,

I am writing to bring to your attention a recurring quality issue in Module 3.
During the latest test cycle, 12 out of 30 test cases have failed, primarily
due to input validation errors in the payment gateway integration.

This could potentially delay our sprint deadline by 2 days. I suggest we
schedule a quick triage meeting to identify root causes and assign fixes.

Please let me know a suitable time for the discussion.

Best regards,
Dilip
```

## 📝 Quick Grammar Rules Cheat Sheet

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| Subject-Verb Agreement | "Each student **has**" not "have" |
| Since vs For | Since 2020 / For 5 years |
| Prepositions of time | At (time), On (day/date), In (month/year) |
| Articles | "a" before consonant sound, "an" before vowel sound |
| Tense consistency | Don't switch tenses mid-paragraph |
| Active > Passive | "We completed the project" > "The project was completed by us" |
| Parallel structure | "She likes reading, writing, and swimming" (not "to swim") |
