## 📖 Complete Theory

### 1. Ratio — Basics

A **ratio** is a way of comparing two quantities of the same kind by division.

- Ratio of a to b is written as **a : b** (read as "a is to b")
- a = **antecedent**, b = **consequent**
- A ratio has **no units** — it's a pure number.
- Ratios are always expressed in **simplest form** (divide by HCF).

**Example:** If there are 15 boys and 10 girls, the ratio = 15:10 = **3:2**

### 2. Properties of Ratios

| Property | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Equality** | a:b = c:d if ad = bc |
| **Multiplication** | a:b = ka:kb (multiply both by same number) |
| **Division** | a:b = (a/k):(b/k) (divide both by same number) |
| **Invertendo** | If a:b = c:d, then b:a = d:c |
| **Alternendo** | If a:b = c:d, then a:c = b:d |
| **Componendo** | If a:b = c:d, then (a+b):b = (c+d):d |
| **Dividendo** | If a:b = c:d, then (a-b):b = (c-d):d |
| **Componendo-Dividendo** | If a:b = c:d, then (a+b):(a-b) = (c+d):(c-d) |

### 3. Comparison of Ratios

To compare a:b and c:d:
- **Cross-multiplication:** Compare ad and bc
  - If ad > bc → a:b > c:d
  - If ad < bc → a:b < c:d
  - If ad = bc → a:b = c:d

**Example:** Compare 3:5 and 4:7
- 3 × 7 = 21, 5 × 4 = 20
- 21 > 20, so 3:5 > 4:7

### 4. Combining Ratios

**If A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5, find A:B:C.**

Make B common:
- A:B = 2:3 → multiply by 4 → 8:12
- B:C = 4:5 → multiply by 3 → 12:15
- **A:B:C = 8:12:15**

### 5. Proportion

A **proportion** is a statement that two ratios are equal: a:b = c:d or a/b = c/d.

- **a, b, c, d** are the four terms
- **a and d** are called **extremes**
- **b and c** are called **means**
- **Product of extremes = Product of means:** a × d = b × c

### 6. Types of Proportion

| Type | Definition | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| **Continued Proportion** | a:b = b:c | b² = ac |
| **Mean Proportional** | Mean proportional of a and c | b = √(ac) |
| **Third Proportional** | Third proportional of a and b | c = b²/a |
| **Fourth Proportional** | If a:b = c:x | x = bc/a |

**Examples:**
- Mean proportional of 4 and 16 = √(4 × 16) = √64 = **8**
- Third proportional to 3 and 6: x = 6²/3 = 36/3 = **12**
- Fourth proportional to 2, 3, 4: x = (3 × 4)/2 = **6**

### 7. Direct and Inverse Proportion

| Type | Relationship | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct Proportion** | When one increases, the other also increases at the same rate | More items → More cost |
| **Inverse Proportion** | When one increases, the other decreases | More workers → Less time |

**Direct:** a/b = c/d → a × d = b × c
**Inverse:** a × b = c × d (product remains constant)

### 8. Partnership

When two or more people invest money in a business:
- **Profit sharing ratio** = Investment × Time

**Simple Partnership (same time):**
- Profit ratio = Investment ratio = A's investment : B's investment

**Compound Partnership (different times):**
- A invests ₹I₁ for t₁ months, B invests ₹I₂ for t₂ months
- Profit ratio = I₁ × t₁ : I₂ × t₂

**Example:** A invests ₹5000 for 12 months, B invests ₹6000 for 10 months.
- Ratio = 5000 × 12 : 6000 × 10 = 60000 : 60000 = **1:1**

### 9. Mixture and Alligation

**Rule of Alligation:**
When two ingredients of different prices are mixed:

```
    Cheaper (C)          Dearer (D)
          \              /
           \            /
            Mean (M)
           /            \
          /              \
    (D - M)          (M - C)
```

Ratio of Cheaper : Dearer = (D - M) : (M - C)

**Example:** Mix rice at ₹20/kg with rice at ₹30/kg to get mixture at ₹24/kg.
- Ratio = (30 - 24) : (24 - 20) = 6 : 4 = **3:2**

### 10. Income, Expenditure, and Savings

If income ratio = a:b and expenditure ratio = c:d:
- Individual savings = Income - Expenditure
- Set up equations: Income of A = ak, Expenditure of A = cm, Saving of A = ak - cm

### 11. Dividing a Quantity in a Given Ratio

To divide quantity Q in ratio a:b:c:
- First part = Q × a/(a+b+c)
- Second part = Q × b/(a+b+c)
- Third part = Q × c/(a+b+c)

**Example:** Divide ₹3600 in ratio 2:3:4.
- Sum = 2+3+4 = 9
- Parts: 3600 × 2/9 = ₹800, 3600 × 3/9 = ₹1200, 3600 × 4/9 = ₹1600

## 💡 Tips, Tricks & Shortcuts

### ⚡ Speed Techniques

1. **Ratio to Fraction Conversion:** If ratio = 3:5, the parts are 3/8 and 5/8 of the total.

2. **Percentage to Ratio:** 20% more means 120/100 = 6:5.

3. **Quick Combining:** When combining A:B and B:C, make B the LCM of its two values.

4. **Alligation on Number Line:** Visualise the mean between cheaper and dearer — distances give the ratio.

5. **For partnership problems:** Always convert to same time unit (usually months).

6. **Shortcut for "x is what % of y":** = (x/y) × 100. If ratio x:y = 3:5, then x is 60% of y.

7. **If a:b = 2:3, then:**
   - a = 2k, b = 3k for some constant k
   - a + b = 5k, a - b = -k
   - This substitution technique solves most problems.

### ⚠️ Common Traps to Avoid

- **Ratio has no units** — 2:3 could mean 200:300 or 20:30.
- **Don't confuse ratio with fraction** — 2:3 means 2 parts out of 5, not 2/3.
- **Order matters** — 2:3 ≠ 3:2.
- **In partnership:** Time of investment matters as much as amount.
- **When combining ratios:** Make sure the common term has the same value in both ratios.

**If A:B = 2:3 and B:C = 4:5, then A:B:C is:**

(A) 8:12:15  (B) 2:3:5  (C) 8:12:10  (D) 6:8:10

**Answer: (A) 8:12:15**

**Solution:**
- A:B = 2:3 → ×4 → 8:12
- B:C = 4:5 → ×3 → 12:15
- **A:B:C = 8:12:15**

### Question 2 (RRB NTPC 2021 Phase 2)
**Divide ₹1870 among A, B, and C in the ratio 2:3:6.**

(A) 340, 510, 1020  (B) 310, 560, 1000  (C) 370, 500, 1000  (D) 350, 510, 1010

**Answer: (A) 340, 510, 1020**

**Solution:**
- Sum of ratio = 2+3+6 = 11
- A = 1870 × 2/11 = **340**
- B = 1870 × 3/11 = **510**
- C = 1870 × 6/11 = **1020**

### Question 6 (RRB NTPC 2021 Phase 4)
**The ratio of incomes of A and B is 5:4 and the ratio of their expenditures is 3:2. If each saves ₹1600, find A's income.**

(A) ₹4000  (B) ₹5000  (C) ₹3000  (D) ₹6000

**Answer: (A) ₹4000**

**Solution:**
- Income: A = 5x, B = 4x
- Expenditure: A = 3y, B = 2y
- Savings: A = 5x - 3y = 1600 … (i)
- Savings: B = 4x - 2y = 1600 … (ii)
- From (ii): 2x - y = 800 → y = 2x - 800
- Substitute in (i): 5x - 3(2x - 800) = 1600
- 5x - 6x + 2400 = 1600 → -x = -800 → x = 800
- A's income = 5 × 800 = **₹4000**

### Question 7 (RRB NTPC 2020)
**In what ratio should rice at ₹30/kg be mixed with rice at ₹42/kg to get a mixture worth ₹36/kg?**

(A) 1:1  (B) 2:1  (C) 1:2  (D) 3:2

**Answer: (A) 1:1**

**Solution:**
Using alligation:
- Cheaper = 30, Dearer = 42, Mean = 36
- Ratio = (42 - 36) : (36 - 30) = 6 : 6 = **1:1**

### Question 9 (RRB NTPC 2021 Phase 1)
**The ages of A and B are in the ratio 5:7. Four years from now, the ratio of their ages will be 3:4. What is the present age of B?**

(A) 28  (B) 20  (C) 24  (D) 35

**Answer: (A) 28**

**Solution:**
- Present: A = 5x, B = 7x
- After 4 years: (5x + 4)/(7x + 4) = 3/4
- 4(5x + 4) = 3(7x + 4)
- 20x + 16 = 21x + 12
- x = 4
- B's age = 7 × 4 = **28**

### Question 10 (RRB NTPC 2019)
**Two numbers are in the ratio 3:5. If 9 is subtracted from each, the new ratio becomes 12:23. Find the smaller number.**

(A) 27  (B) 33  (C) 45  (D) 55

**Answer: (B) 33**

**Solution:**
- Numbers = 3x and 5x
- (3x - 9)/(5x - 9) = 12/23
- 23(3x - 9) = 12(5x - 9)
- 69x - 207 = 60x - 108
- 9x = 99 → x = 11
- Smaller number = 3 × 11 = **33**

- 33x = 128 → This doesn't give a whole number. Let me reconsider...

## 🎯 Exam Strategy & Revision Tips

### During Preparation
1. **Master the k-substitution technique:** If a:b = 3:5, put a = 3k, b = 5k. This solves 80% of problems.
2. **Practice combining ratios** — A:B:C type questions are very common.
3. **Learn alligation** — it's the fastest method for mixture problems.
4. **Partnership problems** — always convert to same time period before comparing.

### During the Exam
1. **Read the question carefully** — Are they asking for a share, a ratio, or a number?
2. **Use elimination:** If ratio = 3:5, the answer must be divisible by 3 or 5. Eliminate wrong options.
3. **For age problems:** Set up equations using the given ratios at different time periods.
4. **Time management:** Ratio questions usually take 30-60 seconds if you know the technique.

### Revision Checklist
- [ ] Basic ratio operations and simplification
- [ ] Combining two ratios into a three-term ratio
- [ ] Mean, third, and fourth proportional formulas
- [ ] Alligation rule for mixtures
- [ ] Partnership with different investments and durations
- [ ] Income-expenditure-savings problems
- [ ] Age-based ratio problems
