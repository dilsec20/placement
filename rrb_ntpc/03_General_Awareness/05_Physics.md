### 1. 🔧 Mechanics

#### Motion
- **Distance** — Total path length (scalar, always positive)
- **Displacement** — Shortest path between start & end points (vector)
- **Speed** = Distance / Time (scalar, SI unit: m/s)
- **Velocity** = Displacement / Time (vector, SI unit: m/s)
- **Acceleration** = Change in velocity / Time (SI unit: m/s²)
- **Uniform motion** — Equal distances in equal intervals
- **Non-uniform motion** — Unequal distances in equal intervals

#### Equations of Motion (SUVAT)
| Equation | Formula |
|----------|---------|
| 1st | v = u + at |
| 2nd | s = ut + ½at² |
| 3rd | v² = u² + 2as |

Where: u = initial velocity, v = final velocity, a = acceleration, t = time, s = displacement

#### Newton's Laws of Motion

| Law | Statement | Example |
|-----|-----------|---------|
| **1st Law (Inertia)** | A body at rest stays at rest, a body in motion stays in motion unless an external force acts on it | Passengers lurch forward when bus stops suddenly |
| **2nd Law** | F = ma (Force = mass × acceleration) | Heavier objects need more force to move |
| **3rd Law** | Every action has an equal and opposite reaction | Rocket propulsion, recoil of gun |

- **Inertia** — Property of a body to resist change in its state of motion
- **Momentum** = mass × velocity (p = mv), SI unit: kg·m/s
- **Impulse** = Force × Time = Change in momentum

#### Friction
- **Friction** — Force opposing relative motion between two surfaces
- **Types:** Static friction > Sliding friction > Rolling friction
- **Static friction** is greatest (keeps objects at rest)
- **Rolling friction** is least (wheels, ball bearings reduce friction)
- Friction depends on: nature of surfaces, normal force
- Friction does NOT depend on: area of contact, speed

#### Gravitation
- **Newton's Law of Gravitation:** F = G(m₁m₂)/r²
- **G** (Gravitational constant) = 6.674 × 10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg²
- **g** (acceleration due to gravity on Earth) ≈ 9.8 m/s²
- g is maximum at poles, minimum at equator
- g decreases with altitude and depth
- **Weight** = mg (varies with location), **Mass** is constant everywhere

#### Work, Energy & Power
| Concept | Formula | SI Unit |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Work | W = F × d × cos θ | Joule (J) |
| Kinetic Energy | KE = ½mv² | Joule |
| Potential Energy | PE = mgh | Joule |
| Power | P = W/t | Watt (W) |

- **1 horsepower (HP)** = 746 W
- **Law of Conservation of Energy:** Energy can neither be created nor destroyed, only converted from one form to another
- At highest point: PE is maximum, KE is minimum
- At lowest point: KE is maximum, PE is minimum

#### Simple Machines
- **Lever** — 3 classes based on position of fulcrum, effort, load
- **Mechanical Advantage** = Load / Effort
- **Pulley, Inclined plane, Wheel & axle, Screw, Wedge**

### 2. 💡 Light (Optics)

#### Nature of Light
- Light is an **electromagnetic wave** (does NOT need medium)
- Speed of light in vacuum: **3 × 10⁸ m/s** (fastest in universe)
- Light travels in straight lines (**rectilinear propagation**)

#### Reflection of Light
- **Angle of incidence = Angle of reflection** (law of reflection)
- **Plane mirror:** Image is virtual, erect, same size, laterally inverted
- Image distance = Object distance (behind the mirror)

#### Spherical Mirrors

| Property | Concave Mirror | Convex Mirror |
|----------|---------------|---------------|
| Shape | Curved inward (cave) | Curved outward |
| Focal point | In front of mirror | Behind mirror |
| Image type | Real/Virtual (depends on position) | Always virtual, erect, diminished |
| Uses | Shaving mirrors, headlights, solar furnace, dentist mirror | Rear-view mirrors in vehicles |

**Mirror Formula:** 1/f = 1/v + 1/u

**Magnification:** m = -v/u = height of image / height of object

#### Refraction of Light
- **Bending of light** when passing from one medium to another
- Light bends TOWARDS normal when entering denser medium
- Light bends AWAY from normal when entering rarer medium
- **Snell's Law:** n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r
- **Refractive Index (n)** = Speed of light in vacuum / Speed in medium
- Examples: Coin in water appears raised, stick appears bent

#### Total Internal Reflection
- Occurs when light goes from denser to rarer medium at angle > critical angle
- Applications: Optical fibres, mirages, diamond sparkle

#### Lenses

| Property | Convex (Converging) | Concave (Diverging) |
|----------|-------------------|-------------------|
| Shape | Thick at centre | Thin at centre |
| Focal point | Real | Virtual |
| Image | Real/Virtual | Always virtual, erect, diminished |
| Uses | Magnifying glass, camera, projector, human eye | Spectacles for myopia (short-sight) |

**Lens Formula:** 1/f = 1/v − 1/u

**Power of Lens:** P = 1/f (in metres), SI unit = **Dioptre (D)**

#### Dispersion of Light
- Splitting of white light into 7 colours by a prism
- **VIBGYOR:** Violet, Indigo, Blue, Green, Yellow, Orange, Red
- Violet has shortest wavelength (maximum deviation)
- Red has longest wavelength (minimum deviation)
- **Rainbow** — Natural dispersion by water droplets

#### Defects of Vision

| Defect | Problem | Corrective Lens |
|--------|---------|----------------|
| Myopia (Short-sightedness) | Cannot see distant objects | Concave lens |
| Hypermetropia (Long-sightedness) | Cannot see nearby objects | Convex lens |
| Presbyopia | Old age, loss of accommodation | Bifocal lens |
| Astigmatism | Irregular curvature of cornea | Cylindrical lens |

#### Scattering of Light
- **Sky appears blue** — Shorter wavelength (blue) scattered more (Rayleigh scattering)
- **Sunset/Sunrise appears red** — Blue scattered away, red reaches observer
- **Danger signals are red** — Red is least scattered, visible from far

### 3. 🔊 Sound

#### Nature of Sound
- Sound is a **mechanical (longitudinal) wave** — needs medium to travel
- Sound CANNOT travel through vacuum
- Speed of sound: Solid > Liquid > Gas
- Speed in air ≈ **343 m/s** (at 20°C)
- Speed in water ≈ 1500 m/s
- Speed in steel ≈ 5000 m/s

#### Characteristics of Sound

| Property | Depends on | Description |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| **Pitch** | Frequency | High frequency = shrill; Low frequency = heavy |
| **Loudness** | Amplitude | Large amplitude = louder sound |
| **Quality/Timbre** | Waveform | Distinguishes two sounds of same pitch & loudness |

- **Frequency** — Number of vibrations per second (SI unit: Hertz, Hz)
- **Audible range for humans:** 20 Hz – 20,000 Hz
- **Infrasound:** < 20 Hz (elephants, earthquakes)
- **Ultrasound:** > 20,000 Hz (bats, dolphins, SONAR, medical imaging)

#### Echo
- Reflected sound heard after original sound
- Minimum distance for echo = **17.2 m** (at 20°C) — sound must travel to wall and back in ≥ 0.1 s
- **Reverberation** — Multiple reflections causing prolonged sound (in auditoriums)

#### Doppler Effect
- **Apparent change in frequency** when source or observer is moving
- Source moving TOWARDS observer → frequency increases (higher pitch)
- Source moving AWAY from observer → frequency decreases (lower pitch)
- Applications: Speed guns (police radar), **Red shift** in astronomy (universe expanding)

#### SONAR (Sound Navigation and Ranging)
- Uses ultrasound to measure depth of sea / detect underwater objects
- Formula: Depth = (v × t) / 2

#### Applications of Ultrasound
- **Echocardiography** — Heart imaging
- **Ultrasonography** — Foetal imaging
- **SONAR** — Depth measurement
- **Cleaning jewellery/surgical instruments**
- **Lithotripsy** — Breaking kidney stones

### 4. ⚡ Electricity

#### Electric Charge
- Two types: **Positive** and **Negative**
- Like charges repel, unlike charges attract
- SI unit: **Coulomb (C)**
- Charge of electron = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C

#### Electric Current
- **Flow of electric charge** through a conductor
- I = Q/t (Current = Charge / Time)
- SI unit: **Ampere (A)**
- Direction: Conventional current flows from + to − (opposite to electron flow)

#### Potential Difference (Voltage)
- Work done per unit charge: V = W/Q
- SI unit: **Volt (V)**
- Measured by: **Voltmeter** (connected in parallel)
- Current measured by: **Ammeter** (connected in series)

#### Ohm's Law
- **V = IR** (Voltage = Current × Resistance)
- At constant temperature, current is directly proportional to voltage
- **Resistance (R)** — Opposition to current flow, SI unit: **Ohm (Ω)**
- R = ρL/A (ρ = resistivity, L = length, A = area)

| Factor | Effect on Resistance |
|--------|---------------------|
| Length increases | R increases |
| Area increases | R decreases |
| Temperature increases (metals) | R increases |
| Temperature increases (semiconductors) | R decreases |

#### Resistors in Circuits

| Type | Formula | Current | Voltage |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| **Series** | R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ | Same through all | Divided |
| **Parallel** | 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ | Divided | Same across all |

#### Heating Effect of Current
- **Joule's Law:** H = I²Rt
- Applications: Electric heater, iron, fuse, electric bulb

#### Electric Power
- **P = VI = I²R = V²/R**
- SI unit: **Watt (W)**
- **1 kWh (unit)** = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3.6 × 10⁶ J
- Electricity bill is calculated in **kWh (units)**

#### Fuse & Safety
- Fuse wire: **Low melting point** (tin-lead alloy), connected in **series** with live wire
- **Earthing** — Connects appliance body to earth for safety
- Colour codes: **Live = Red/Brown**, **Neutral = Black/Blue**, **Earth = Green/Yellow-Green**

### 5. 🌡️ Heat & Thermodynamics

#### Heat vs Temperature
| Property | Heat | Temperature |
|----------|------|-------------|
| Definition | Total energy of molecules | Degree of hotness/coldness |
| SI Unit | Joule | Kelvin (K) |
| Measured by | Calorimeter | Thermometer |
| Transfer | Hot → Cold body | — |

#### Temperature Scales & Conversion
| Scale | Freezing Point of Water | Boiling Point of Water |
|-------|------------------------|----------------------|
| Celsius (°C) | 0°C | 100°C |
| Fahrenheit (°F) | 32°F | 212°F |
| Kelvin (K) | 273 K | 373 K |

- °F = (9/5)°C + 32
- K = °C + 273
- −40° is same in both Celsius and Fahrenheit

#### Types of Thermometers
| Type | Use | Principle |
|------|-----|-----------|
| Mercury thermometer | Clinical, lab | Expansion of mercury |
| Clinical thermometer | Human body temperature (35°C–42°C) | Mercury expansion |
| Digital thermometer | Medical | Electrical resistance change |
| Pyrometer | Very high temperatures (furnaces) | Radiation |

#### Modes of Heat Transfer

| Mode | Medium | Mechanism | Example |
|------|--------|-----------|---------|
| **Conduction** | Solid (requires contact) | Molecule to molecule | Metal spoon gets hot in soup |
| **Convection** | Fluids (liquids, gases) | Bulk movement of fluid | Sea breeze, boiling water |
| **Radiation** | No medium needed | Electromagnetic waves | Sun's heat reaching Earth |

- **Good conductors:** Metals (Silver is best conductor)
- **Bad conductors (Insulators):** Wood, rubber, air, wool
- **Black body** absorbs and emits maximum radiation
- **White/shiny surfaces** reflect maximum radiation (thermos flask)

#### Thermal Expansion
- Solids: Linear, Area, Volume expansion
- Liquids: Volume expansion only
- Gases: Maximum expansion
- Application: Bimetallic strip in thermostat, gaps in railway tracks

#### Specific Heat Capacity
- **Q = mcΔT** (Heat = mass × specific heat × change in temperature)
- Water has **highest specific heat capacity** (4200 J/kg·K) among common substances
- This is why water is used as coolant, and coastal areas have moderate climate

#### Latent Heat
- **Latent Heat of Fusion** (ice → water) = 334 kJ/kg (at 0°C)
- **Latent Heat of Vaporization** (water → steam) = 2260 kJ/kg (at 100°C)
- Temperature remains constant during phase change

### 6. 🧲 Magnetism

#### Basic Properties
- Every magnet has two poles: **North (N)** and **South (S)**
- Like poles repel, unlike poles attract
- **Magnetic poles always exist in pairs** (no monopole)
- Freely suspended magnet aligns in N-S direction

#### Magnetic Field
- Region around magnet where its effect is felt
- Direction: N → S (outside the magnet), S → N (inside)
- **Magnetic field lines** never intersect
- Field is strongest at the poles

#### Earth as a Magnet
- Earth behaves as a giant magnet
- Geographic North ≈ Magnetic South (and vice versa)
- **Compass** needle points towards geographic north (magnetic south)

#### Electromagnets
- **Temporary magnet** made by passing current through a solenoid (coil of wire)
- Strength depends on: number of turns, current, iron core
- Applications: Electric bell, MRI machines, cranes for lifting iron, maglev trains

#### Electromagnetic Induction (Faraday's Law)
- Changing magnetic field induces EMF (voltage) in a conductor
- Basis of: **Electric generator, transformer, induction cooker**
- **Fleming's Left Hand Rule** — Direction of force on current-carrying conductor in magnetic field (Motor rule)
- **Fleming's Right Hand Rule** — Direction of induced current (Generator rule)

#### Electric Motor vs Generator

| Property | Electric Motor | Electric Generator |
|----------|---------------|-------------------|
| Converts | Electrical → Mechanical energy | Mechanical → Electrical energy |
| Principle | Current in magnetic field → Force | Electromagnetic induction |
| Rule | Fleming's Left Hand | Fleming's Right Hand |
| Uses | Fan, mixer, washing machine | Power stations |

#### Transformer
- Changes voltage level of AC (not DC)
- **Step-up transformer:** Increases voltage (fewer turns in primary)
- **Step-down transformer:** Decreases voltage (more turns in primary)
- Works on: **Mutual induction**

### 7. ⚛️ Modern Physics (Basics)

#### Atomic Structure
- **Atom** = Nucleus (protons + neutrons) + Electrons
- **Proton** = +1 charge, **Electron** = −1 charge, **Neutron** = 0 charge
- **Atomic Number (Z)** = Number of protons
- **Mass Number (A)** = Protons + Neutrons
- **Isotopes** — Same Z, different A (e.g., C-12, C-14)
- **Isobars** — Same A, different Z (e.g., Ar-40, Ca-40)

#### Radioactivity
- Discovered by **Henri Becquerel** (1896)
- **Marie Curie** — Discovered Radium and Polonium

| Radiation | Symbol | Nature | Charge | Penetrating Power |
|-----------|--------|--------|--------|-------------------|
| Alpha (α) | He²⁺ | Helium nucleus | +2 | Lowest (stopped by paper) |
| Beta (β) | e⁻ | Electron | −1 | Medium (stopped by aluminium) |
| Gamma (γ) | — | EM wave | 0 | Highest (stopped by thick lead) |

- **Half-life** — Time for half the radioactive atoms to decay
- **Carbon-14 dating** — Used to determine age of fossils

#### Nuclear Reactions
- **Nuclear Fission** — Splitting of heavy nucleus (Uranium-235) → huge energy (atom bomb, nuclear reactor)
- **Nuclear Fusion** — Combining of light nuclei (Hydrogen → Helium) → Sun's energy, H-bomb
- **E = mc²** (Einstein's mass-energy equivalence)

#### X-rays
- Discovered by **Wilhelm Röntgen** (1895)
- Applications: Medical imaging, security scanners, crystallography

#### Electromagnetic Spectrum (increasing frequency)

| Type | Use |
|------|-----|
| Radio waves | Communication, radio, TV |
| Microwaves | Microwave oven, radar, satellite |
| Infrared | Remote control, night vision, thermal imaging |
| Visible light | Vision |
| Ultraviolet | Sterilization, vitamin D synthesis |
| X-rays | Medical imaging |
| Gamma rays | Cancer treatment, sterilization |

## 💡 Memory Tricks & Mnemonics

### Newton's Laws
- **1st Law = LAW of INERTIA** → "I am Lazy (Inertia), I won't move unless pushed"
- **2nd Law = F = ma** → "Fat Man Accelerates slowly" (more mass → more force needed)
- **3rd Law** → "Gun fires bullet (action), gun recoils (reaction)"

### Colours of Rainbow (VIBGYOR)
- **V**iolent **I**ndian **B**atsman **G**ets **Y**ellow **O**range **R**uns
- Violet = shortest wavelength, Red = longest wavelength

### Electromagnetic Spectrum (increasing wavelength)
- **G**amma → **X**-rays → **U**V → **V**isible → **I**nfrared → **M**icrowave → **R**adio
- Mnemonic: "**G**ranny's **X**-tra **U**gly **V**est **I**s **M**ouldy **R**ed"

### Ohm's Law Triangle
```
    V
   / \
  I × R
```
- Cover what you want: V = IR, I = V/R, R = V/I

### Heat Transfer: "CCC"
- **C**onduction = **C**ontact (solids)
- **C**onvection = **C**urrent flow (fluids)
- **R**adiation = **R**ays (no medium needed)

### Eye Defects
- **MY**opia = can't see far → con**CAVE** lens (M before H, Concave before Convex)
- **H**ypermetropia = can't see near → con**VEX** lens

### Series vs Parallel
- **S**eries = **S**ame current
- **P**arallel = **P**otential (voltage) is same

### Fleming's Rules (Left/Right)
- **L**eft hand = **M**otor (L before M in alphabet)
- **R**ight hand = **G**enerator (R after G? → Just remember: "Right for Generator")
- Thumb = Motion/Force, Forefinger = Field, Middle finger = Current

### Radiation Types (α, β, γ) — Penetrating Power
- "**A**ll **B**oys **G**row" — Alpha < Beta < Gamma (increasing penetration)

## 📝 RRB NTPC Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

**Q1.** Which of the following is NOT a vector quantity?
- A) Velocity
- B) Acceleration
- C) Speed ✅
- D) Force

**Explanation:** Speed is a scalar quantity (only magnitude, no direction). Velocity, acceleration, and force are vectors.

---

**Q2.** The SI unit of electric current is:
- A) Volt
- B) Ohm
- C) Ampere ✅
- D) Coulomb

**Explanation:** Electric current is measured in Ampere (A). Volt = potential difference, Ohm = resistance, Coulomb = charge.

---

**Q3.** A concave mirror is used in:
- A) Rear-view mirror of vehicles
- B) Headlights of a car ✅
- C) Periscope
- D) Kaleidoscope

**Explanation:** Concave mirrors are used in headlights to produce a powerful parallel beam of light. Convex mirrors are used as rear-view mirrors.

---

**Q4.** The speed of sound is maximum in:
- A) Vacuum
- B) Air
- C) Water
- D) Steel ✅

**Explanation:** Sound travels fastest in solids. Speed: Solid > Liquid > Gas. Sound cannot travel through vacuum.

---

**Q5.** Which law explains why a passenger in a moving bus tends to fall forward when the bus stops suddenly?
- A) Newton's First Law of Motion ✅
- B) Newton's Second Law of Motion
- C) Newton's Third Law of Motion
- D) Law of Conservation of Energy

**Explanation:** Newton's First Law (Law of Inertia) — the upper body continues in motion while the lower body stops with the bus.

---

**Q6.** Myopia (short-sightedness) is corrected using:
- A) Convex lens
- B) Concave lens ✅
- C) Bifocal lens
- D) Cylindrical lens

**Explanation:** In myopia, image forms before the retina. A concave (diverging) lens corrects this by shifting the image onto the retina.

---

**Q7.** The colour of the sky appears blue because of:
- A) Refraction of light
- B) Reflection of light
- C) Scattering of light ✅
- D) Dispersion of light

**Explanation:** Blue light has shorter wavelength and is scattered more by atmospheric molecules (Rayleigh scattering).

---

**Q8.** 1 horsepower is equal to:
- A) 646 Watts
- B) 746 Watts ✅
- C) 846 Watts
- D) 546 Watts

**Explanation:** 1 HP = 746 W (standard conversion factor).

---

**Q9.** The minimum distance for an echo to be heard is approximately:
- A) 10 m
- B) 15 m
- C) 17 m ✅
- D) 20 m

**Explanation:** Sound must travel at least 17.2 m to the wall and back (total ~34.4 m) in ≥ 0.1 seconds for echo.

---

**Q10.** A fuse wire is made of an alloy with:
- A) High melting point
- B) Low melting point ✅
- C) High resistance only
- D) Low resistance

**Explanation:** Fuse wire has low melting point so it melts and breaks the circuit when excess current flows, protecting appliances.

---

**Q11.** The instrument used to measure electric current is:
- A) Voltmeter
- B) Galvanometer
- C) Ammeter ✅
- D) Barometer

**Explanation:** Ammeter measures current (connected in series). Voltmeter measures voltage (connected in parallel).

---

**Q12.** Which type of heat transfer does NOT require a medium?
- A) Conduction
- B) Convection
- C) Radiation ✅
- D) Both A and B

**Explanation:** Radiation travels as electromagnetic waves and does not require any medium. That's how Sun's heat reaches Earth.

---

**Q13.** Which of the following is used in an optical fibre?
- A) Refraction
- B) Total internal reflection ✅
- C) Diffraction
- D) Interference

**Explanation:** Optical fibres work on the principle of total internal reflection, where light bounces within the fibre repeatedly.

---

**Q14.** Radioactivity was discovered by:
- A) Marie Curie
- B) Henri Becquerel ✅
- C) Rutherford
- D) J.J. Thomson

**Explanation:** Henri Becquerel discovered radioactivity in 1896. Marie Curie discovered radium and polonium.

---

**Q15.** In nuclear reactors, which element is commonly used as fuel?
- A) Thorium
- B) Uranium ✅
- C) Plutonium
- D) Radium

**Explanation:** Uranium-235 is the most common fuel in nuclear fission reactors. India also has thorium-based reactor programs.

## 🎯 Exam Strategy & Revision Tips

### Time Allocation
- Spend **20–30 seconds** per Physics question in the exam
- Don't waste time on calculation-heavy questions — move on and return later

### High-Frequency Topics (Focus Here!)
1. **Units & Measurements** — SI units of all quantities
2. **Newton's Laws** — Examples and identification
3. **Mirror & Lens** — Uses, image properties, defects of vision
4. **Sound** — Speed in different media, echo, ultrasound applications
5. **Electricity** — Ohm's law, series/parallel, power, fuse
6. **Heat transfer** — Conduction, convection, radiation with examples
7. **Scientists & discoveries** — Who discovered what

### Last-Minute Revision Strategy
1. Revise the **Quick Revision Table** — most questions are direct fact-based
2. Remember **SI units** of all quantities (very frequent in exams!)
3. Focus on **applications** — "Which mirror is used in...?", "Which device uses...?"
4. Practice **PYQs** — same type of questions repeat across phases
5. Don't attempt if unsure — **-1/3 negative marking** can hurt your score

### Common Traps to Avoid
- Confusing **concave vs convex** mirror/lens properties
- Mixing up **speed vs velocity**, **mass vs weight**
- Confusing **series vs parallel** connections
- Forgetting that sound **cannot travel in vacuum**
- Mixing up **Becquerel (radioactivity) vs Marie Curie (radium)**
