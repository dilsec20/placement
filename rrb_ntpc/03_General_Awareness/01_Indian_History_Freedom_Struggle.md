## 1. Ancient India

### 1.1 Indus Valley Civilisation (Harappan Civilisation) — c. 3300–1300 BCE

| Feature | Details |
|---------|---------|
| **Period** | 3300 BCE – 1300 BCE (Mature phase: 2600–1900 BCE) |
| **Discovered by** | Sir John Marshall (Director-General of ASI) |
| **First site discovered** | Harappa (1921) by Daya Ram Sahni |
| **Mohenjo-daro** | Discovered by R.D. Banerji (1922); means "Mound of the Dead" |
| **Important sites** | Harappa, Mohenjo-daro, Lothal, Kalibangan, Dholavira, Rakhigarhi, Banawali, Chanhudaro |
| **Key features** | Town planning, grid pattern streets, Great Bath (Mohenjo-daro), dockyard (Lothal), bead-making, bronze tools |
| **Script** | Undeciphered; written right to left |
| **Religion** | Worship of Mother Goddess, Pashupati (Proto-Shiva), trees, animals |
| **Economy** | Agriculture (wheat, barley), trade with Mesopotamia |
| **Decline** | Theories: Aryan invasion, floods, climate change, tectonic activity |

| Lothal | Gujarat, India | Dockyard, rice husk, fire altars, chess-like game |
| Kalibangan | Rajasthan, India | Fire altars, ploughed field, camel bones |
| Dholavira | Gujarat, India | Water reservoir, sign board (inscription), stadium |
| Rakhigarhi | Haryana, India | Largest IVC site in India |
| Banawali | Haryana, India | Toy plough, barley |
| Chanhudaro | Sindh, Pakistan | Bead-making factory, lipstick, ink pot |
| Surkotada | Gujarat, India | Horse bones |
| Alamgirpur | UP, India | Easternmost site |

### 1.2 Vedic Period (1500–600 BCE)

#### Early Vedic / Rig Vedic Period (1500–1000 BCE)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **River** | Sapta Sindhu (land of seven rivers); Saraswati most sacred |
| **Political unit** | Tribe (Jana); chief = Rajan |
| **Economy** | Pastoral; cattle = wealth (Gavishthi = war for cattle) |
| **Society** | Patriarchal, no rigid caste; women had education rights |
| **Religion** | Indra (most important god), Agni, Varuna; no idol/temple worship |
| **Text** | Rig Veda (oldest; 10 Mandalas, 1028 Suktas) |
| **Battle** | Battle of Ten Kings (Dasharagya) on River Parushni (Ravi) — Sudas (Bharata tribe) won |

#### Later Vedic Period (1000–600 BCE)

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Region** | Expanded to Ganga-Yamuna Doab |
| **Political** | Kingdoms (Janapadas) emerged; Rajan became more powerful |
| **Economy** | Agriculture became primary; iron use (Krishna Ayas) |
| **Society** | Caste system became rigid; Ashrama system (Brahmacharya, Grihastha, Vanaprastha, Sannyasa) |
| **Religion** | Prajapati (supreme god), Vishnu & Rudra gained importance |
| **Texts** | Sama Veda, Yajur Veda, Atharva Veda, Brahmanas, Aranyakas, Upanishads |

#### The Four Vedas

| Veda | Content | Special Note |
|------|---------|-------------|
| **Rig Veda** | Hymns of praise | Oldest text; Gayatri Mantra in 3rd Mandala |
| **Sama Veda** | Melodies/music | "Book of Songs"; basis of Indian music |
| **Yajur Veda** | Rituals/sacrifices | Prose + verse; two versions (Krishna & Shukla) |
| **Atharva Veda** | Spells, charms, medicine | Non-Aryan influence; daily life aspects |

### 1.4 Maurya Empire (322–185 BCE)

| Ruler | Period | Key Facts |
|-------|--------|-----------|

| **Ashoka** | 273–232 BCE | Greatest Mauryan; Kalinga War (261 BCE) → embraced Buddhism; Rock & Pillar edicts; Dhamma policy |

#### Ashoka's Edicts & Pillars

| Item | Detail |
|------|--------|
| **Edicts language** | Prakrit (mostly Brahmi script); Greek & Aramaic at NW frontier |
| **Lion Capital (Sarnath)** | National Emblem of India; 4 lions, Dharma Chakra (24 spokes → national flag) |
| **Kalinga War** | 261 BCE; massive casualties → Ashoka's transformation |
| **Dhamma** | Tolerance, non-violence, respect for elders, welfare of people |

### 3.2 British Consolidation

| Event / Policy | Year | Details |
|---------------|------|---------|
| **Battle of Plassey** | 1757 | Clive defeated Siraj-ud-Daulah (Nawab of Bengal); beginning of British political rule |
| **Battle of Buxar** | 1764 | Most decisive battle; British defeated combined forces of Mir Qasim, Shuja-ud-Daulah, Shah Alam II |

| **Doctrine of Lapse** | 1848 | Lord Dalhousie; annexed states without natural heir (Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur) |
| **Charter Acts** | 1813, 1833, 1853 | Progressive opening of trade, centralising power |

### 3.4 Revolt of 1857

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **When** | 10 May 1857 |
| **Where started** | Meerut |
| **Immediate cause** | Greased cartridges (cow & pig fat) — Enfield rifles |
| **First soldier to revolt** | Mangal Pandey (29 March 1857, Barrackpore) |
| **Leaders** | Bahadur Shah Zafar (Delhi), Rani Lakshmibai (Jhansi), Tantia Tope (Kanpur), Nana Sahib (Kanpur), Kunwar Singh (Bihar), Begum Hazrat Mahal (Lucknow) |
| **Why failed** | Lack of coordination, limited to North India, no unified leadership, modern British weapons |
| **Result** | End of EIC rule; Crown took over (Government of India Act 1858); Queen Victoria's Proclamation |

### 4.4 Gandhian Era (1919–1947)

#### Mahatma Gandhi — Key Facts

| Aspect | Details |
|--------|---------|
| **Full name** | Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi |
| **Born** | 2 October 1869, Porbandar, Gujarat |
| **Died** | 30 January 1948 (assassinated by Nathuram Godse) |
| **South Africa** | 1893–1915; fought against racial discrimination; developed Satyagraha |
| **Return to India** | 1915; Sabarmati Ashram, Ahmedabad |
| **Title** | Mahatma (by Rabindranath Tagore); Father of the Nation |
| **Autobiography** | "The Story of My Experiments with Truth" |

#### Major Movements Led by Gandhi

| Movement | Year | Cause | Key Facts |
|----------|------|-------|-----------|
| **Champaran Satyagraha** | 1917 | Indigo planters' exploitation (Bihar) | First civil disobedience in India |
| **Kheda Satyagraha** | 1918 | Farmers couldn't pay tax due to crop failure (Gujarat) | Tax relief granted |
| **Ahmedabad Mill Strike** | 1918 | Workers' wages | First hunger strike by Gandhi |
| **Rowlatt Satyagraha** | 1919 | Rowlatt Act ("Black Act" — arrest without trial) | Led to Jallianwala Bagh massacre (13 April 1919) |
| **Non-Cooperation Movement** | 1920–1922 | Jallianwala Bagh + Khilafat issue | Withdrawn after Chauri Chaura incident (5 Feb 1922) |
| **Civil Disobedience Movement** | 1930–1934 | Demand for Purna Swaraj | Dandi March (12 Mar–6 Apr 1930); salt law violation; Gandhi-Irwin Pact (1931) |
| **Quit India Movement** | 1942 | Failure of Cripps Mission; demand for immediate independence | "Do or Die" slogan; 8 August 1942; massive arrests |

# 🎯 Exam Strategy & Revision Tips

## ✅ How to Prepare Indian History for RRB NTPC

1. **Focus on Facts, Not Stories** — RRB NTPC asks factual one-liner questions. Know the WHO, WHEN, WHERE for each event.

2. **Priority Topics** (based on PYQ analysis):
   - Freedom Movement (Gandhi's movements, dates) — **Highest weight**
   - Revolt of 1857 — leaders & centres
   - Mughal Empire — rulers, architecture, administration
   - Ancient India — Indus Valley sites, Maurya & Gupta empires
   - Governor-Generals & Viceroys
   - Famous slogans & books

3. **Use the Quick Revision Tables** above daily. Read them 5 minutes before sleeping — your brain consolidates them during sleep.

4. **Avoid These Mistakes:**
   - Don't confuse "First woman President of INC" (Annie Besant) with "First Indian woman President" (Sarojini Naidu)
   - Don't confuse Battle of Plassey (1757) with Battle of Buxar (1764)
   - Don't confuse Lord Cornwallis (Permanent Settlement) with Lord Canning (first Viceroy)

5. **Negative Marking Strategy:**
   - If you can eliminate 2 options → ATTEMPT the question
   - If you can only eliminate 1 or none → SKIP
   - History questions are usually straightforward — if you know it, mark it confidently

6. **Revise These Weekly:**
   - Timeline of events (1498–1947)
   - Slogans & their leaders
   - IVC sites & discoveries
   - Buddhist & Jain councils

7. **Last 30 Days Before Exam:**
   - Focus ONLY on revision tables and PYQs
   - Take mock tests to test recall speed
   - Don't start new topics — consolidate what you know
