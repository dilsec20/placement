Chemistry is a **crucial sub-topic** under General Science in the RRB NTPC General Awareness section. Expect **3–5 questions** directly from Chemistry in every CBT-1 paper. All topics are at the **10th CBSE level** — focus on factual recall, definitions, and everyday applications.

### 1. 🧪 Matter & Its Classification

#### States of Matter
| Property | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
|----------|-------|--------|-----|
| Shape | Fixed | Takes shape of container | No fixed shape |
| Volume | Fixed | Fixed | Not fixed |
| Compressibility | Negligible | Very slight | Highly compressible |
| Particle arrangement | Tightly packed | Loosely packed | Widely spaced |
| Kinetic energy | Lowest | Medium | Highest |

- **Plasma** — 4th state (ionized gas), found in Sun, lightning, neon signs
- **Bose-Einstein Condensate (BEC)** — 5th state (near absolute zero)

#### Classification of Matter
```
Matter
├── Pure Substances
│   ├── Elements (H, O, Fe, Au)
│   └── Compounds (H₂O, NaCl, CO₂)
└── Mixtures
    ├── Homogeneous (solutions — salt water, air)
    └── Heterogeneous (suspensions — mud water, sand + salt)
```

#### Element vs Compound vs Mixture

| Property | Element | Compound | Mixture |
|----------|---------|----------|---------|
| Composition | Single type of atom | Two or more elements chemically bonded | Two or more substances physically mixed |
| Separation | Cannot be broken down | By chemical methods | By physical methods |
| Fixed ratio | — | Yes (law of constant proportions) | No |
| Example | Iron, Gold, Oxygen | Water (H₂O), Salt (NaCl) | Air, Alloys |

### 2. ⚛️ Atoms & Molecules

#### Atomic Models (Historical)

| Scientist | Model | Key Feature |
|-----------|-------|-------------|
| **John Dalton** (1808) | Solid sphere model | Atoms are indivisible solid spheres |
| **J.J. Thomson** (1897) | Plum pudding model | Discovered electron; positive sphere with embedded electrons |
| **Ernest Rutherford** (1911) | Nuclear model | Discovered nucleus; most of atom is empty space |
| **Niels Bohr** (1913) | Planetary model | Electrons revolve in fixed orbits (shells) |

#### Subatomic Particles

| Particle | Symbol | Charge | Mass (amu) | Location |
|----------|--------|--------|------------|----------|
| Proton | p⁺ | +1 | 1 | Nucleus |
| Neutron | n⁰ | 0 | 1 | Nucleus |
| Electron | e⁻ | −1 | 1/1836 | Orbits (shells) |

- **Atomic Number (Z)** = Number of protons = Number of electrons (in neutral atom)
- **Mass Number (A)** = Protons + Neutrons
- **Valency** — Combining capacity of an atom

#### Important Definitions

| Term | Definition | Example |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Isotopes** | Same atomic number, different mass numbers | ¹H, ²H (Deuterium), ³H (Tritium) |
| **Isobars** | Same mass number, different atomic numbers | ⁴⁰Ar and ⁴⁰Ca |
| **Isotones** | Same number of neutrons | ¹⁴C and ¹⁵N (both 8 neutrons) |
| **Ion** | Atom with charge (+cation / −anion) | Na⁺, Cl⁻ |
| **Molecule** | Two or more atoms bonded together | O₂, H₂O |

### 3. 📊 Periodic Table

#### History
- **Döbereiner's Triads** (1829) — Groups of 3 elements with middle element's atomic mass ≈ average of other two
- **Newlands' Law of Octaves** (1866) — Every 8th element has similar properties (like musical notes)
- **Mendeleev's Periodic Table** (1869) — Arranged by **increasing atomic mass**; left gaps for undiscovered elements (Eka-boron = Scandium, Eka-aluminium = Gallium, Eka-silicon = Germanium)
- **Modern Periodic Table** (Henry Moseley, 1913) — Arranged by **increasing atomic number**

#### Structure of Modern Periodic Table
- **7 Periods** (horizontal rows) = 7 shells (K, L, M, N, O, P, Q)
- **18 Groups** (vertical columns) = similar chemical properties

#### Important Groups

| Group | Name | Elements | Properties |
|-------|------|----------|-----------|
| **1** | Alkali Metals | Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr | Most reactive metals, soft, stored in kerosene |
| **2** | Alkaline Earth Metals | Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra | Less reactive than Group 1 |
| **17** | Halogens | F, Cl, Br, I, At | Most reactive non-metals, form salts |
| **18** | Noble/Inert Gases | He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn | Completely filled valence shell, least reactive |

#### Periodic Trends

| Property | Across a Period (→) | Down a Group (↓) |
|----------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Atomic radius | Decreases | Increases |
| Ionization energy | Increases | Decreases |
| Electronegativity | Increases | Decreases |
| Metallic character | Decreases | Increases |
| Non-metallic character | Increases | Decreases |
| Valency | 1→4 then 4→0 | Same |

#### Special Elements to Remember

| Element | Symbol | Key Fact |
|---------|--------|----------|
| Hydrogen | H | Lightest element, most abundant in universe |
| Helium | He | Second lightest, used in weather balloons |
| Carbon | C | Basis of organic chemistry, allotropes: diamond, graphite, fullerene |
| Nitrogen | N | 78% of atmosphere |
| Oxygen | O | 21% of atmosphere, supports combustion |
| Gold | Au | Most malleable and ductile metal |
| Mercury | Hg | Only liquid metal at room temperature |
| Bromine | Br | Only liquid non-metal at room temperature |
| Tungsten | W | Highest melting point metal (used in bulb filaments) |
| Francium | Fr | Most reactive metal |
| Fluorine | F | Most reactive non-metal, most electronegative |
| Osmium | Os | Densest naturally occurring element |

### 4. ⚗️ Chemical Reactions & Equations

#### Types of Chemical Reactions

| Type | General Formula | Example |
|------|----------------|---------|
| **Combination** | A + B → AB | 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O |
| **Decomposition** | AB → A + B | 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂ (electrolysis) |
| **Displacement** | A + BC → AC + B | Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu |
| **Double Displacement** | AB + CD → AD + CB | NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O |
| **Combustion** | Fuel + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + Heat | CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O |
| **Redox** | Oxidation + Reduction simultaneously | Rusting of iron |

#### Key Concepts
- **Oxidation** — Gain of oxygen / Loss of hydrogen / Loss of electrons
- **Reduction** — Loss of oxygen / Gain of hydrogen / Gain of electrons
- **Oxidizing Agent** — Gets reduced itself (e.g., O₂, KMnO₄)
- **Reducing Agent** — Gets oxidized itself (e.g., H₂, Carbon)

#### Balancing Equations
- **Law of Conservation of Mass** — Atoms of each element must be equal on both sides
- Count atoms → balance one element at a time → check

#### Important Reactions to Remember

| Reaction | Description |
|----------|-------------|
| **Rusting** | Fe + O₂ + moisture → Fe₂O₃ (iron oxide) — can be prevented by painting, galvanizing, oiling |
| **Photosynthesis** | 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → (sunlight) → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ |
| **Respiration** | C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + Energy |
| **Neutralization** | Acid + Base → Salt + Water |
| **Rancidity** | Oxidation of fats/oils → bad smell (prevented by antioxidants, vacuum packing, nitrogen flushing) |

#### Exothermic vs Endothermic

| Property | Exothermic | Endothermic |
|----------|-----------|-------------|
| Heat | Released | Absorbed |
| Temperature | Increases | Decreases |
| Example | Combustion, neutralization, respiration | Photosynthesis, melting ice, evaporation |

#### Properties

| Property | Acids | Bases |

| Feel | — | Soapy/slippery |

### 7. 🔬 Carbon & Its Compounds

#### Unique Properties of Carbon
- **Catenation** — Ability to form bonds with other carbon atoms (long chains, rings)
- **Tetravalency** — Carbon has valency 4, can form 4 covalent bonds

#### Allotropes of Carbon

| Allotrope | Structure | Properties |
|-----------|-----------|-----------|
| **Diamond** | Tetrahedral, 3D network | Hardest natural substance, non-conductor |
| **Graphite** | Layered, hexagonal sheets | Soft, slippery, conductor of electricity (used in pencils) |
| **Fullerene (C₆₀)** | Spherical (Buckminsterfullerene) | Hollow cage-like structure |

#### Hydrocarbons

| Type | Bonds | General Formula | Example |
|------|-------|----------------|---------|
| **Alkane** (Saturated) | Single bonds only | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | Methane (CH₄), Ethane (C₂H₆) |
| **Alkene** (Unsaturated) | At least one double bond | CₙH₂ₙ | Ethylene (C₂H₄) |
| **Alkyne** (Unsaturated) | At least one triple bond | CₙH₂ₙ₋₂ | Acetylene (C₂H₂) |

#### Important Carbon Compounds

| Compound | Formula | Use / Notes |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| Methane | CH₄ | Main component of natural gas, CNG |
| Ethanol | C₂H₅OH | Alcoholic beverages, antiseptic, fuel |
| Ethanoic acid | CH₃COOH | Vinegar (5-8% solution) |
| Carbon dioxide | CO₂ | Fire extinguisher, photosynthesis, dry ice (solid CO₂) |
| Carbon monoxide | CO | Toxic gas, binds with haemoglobin 200× more than O₂ |
| Carbonic acid | H₂CO₃ | In aerated drinks |

#### Coal & Petroleum
- **Coal:** Ancient plant matter → Peat → Lignite → Bituminous → **Anthracite** (highest carbon content)
- **Petroleum** (Black Gold) → Fractional distillation products:
  - LPG → Petrol → Kerosene → Diesel → Lubricating oil → Paraffin wax → Bitumen/Tar

## 📝 RRB NTPC Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

**Q1.** The pH value of pure water is:
- A) 0
- B) 7 ✅
- C) 14
- D) 1

**Explanation:** Pure water is neutral with pH = 7. Acidic < 7, Basic > 7.

---

**Q2.** Which gas is known as "Laughing Gas"?
- A) Carbon dioxide
- B) Nitrous oxide ✅
- C) Nitrogen dioxide
- D) Carbon monoxide

**Explanation:** Nitrous oxide (N₂O) is known as laughing gas. It is used as an anaesthetic in surgery.

---

**Q3.** Which of the following is the chemical formula of washing soda?
- A) NaHCO₃
- B) Na₂CO₃·10H₂O ✅
- C) CaCO₃
- D) NaCl

**Explanation:** Na₂CO₃·10H₂O is washing soda (hydrated sodium carbonate). NaHCO₃ is baking soda.

---

**Q4.** Galvanization is a process of coating iron with:
- A) Copper
- B) Tin
- C) Zinc ✅
- D) Aluminium

**Explanation:** Galvanization coats iron/steel with zinc to prevent rusting.

---

**Q5.** Who is known as the Father of Modern Chemistry?
- A) John Dalton
- B) Antoine Lavoisier ✅
- C) Dmitri Mendeleev
- D) Robert Boyle

**Explanation:** Antoine Lavoisier is called the Father of Modern Chemistry for his work on combustion, oxygen naming, and law of conservation of mass.

---

**Q6.** Which acid is present in vinegar?
- A) Citric acid
- B) Formic acid
- C) Acetic acid ✅
- D) Tartaric acid

**Explanation:** Vinegar contains 5-8% acetic acid (CH₃COOH).

---

**Q7.** The most abundant gas in Earth's atmosphere is:
- A) Oxygen
- B) Nitrogen ✅
- C) Carbon dioxide
- D) Argon

**Explanation:** Nitrogen constitutes approximately 78% of the Earth's atmosphere, followed by oxygen at 21%.

---

**Q8.** Which non-metal is a good conductor of electricity?
- A) Sulphur
- B) Phosphorus
- C) Graphite ✅
- D) Diamond

**Explanation:** Graphite (allotrope of carbon) is the only non-metal that conducts electricity due to free electrons in its layered structure.

---

**Q9.** Plaster of Paris is obtained by heating:
- A) Calcium Carbonate
- B) Calcium Sulphate Dihydrate (Gypsum) ✅
- C) Calcium Oxide
- D) Calcium Hydroxide

**Explanation:** Gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O) heated at 373 K gives Plaster of Paris (CaSO₄·½H₂O).

---

**Q10.** Which of the following is the hardest natural substance?
- A) Iron
- B) Graphite
- C) Diamond ✅
- D) Quartz

**Explanation:** Diamond (allotrope of carbon) is the hardest known natural substance (10 on Mohs scale).

---

**Q11.** Which metal is stored in kerosene?
- A) Iron
- B) Copper
- C) Sodium ✅
- D) Aluminium

**Explanation:** Sodium and potassium are highly reactive alkali metals that react vigorously with water and air, so they are stored in kerosene.

---

**Q12.** Which of the following is used in fire extinguishers?
- A) Nitrogen
- B) Carbon dioxide ✅
- C) Oxygen
- D) Hydrogen

**Explanation:** CO₂ is heavier than air, non-combustible, and cuts off oxygen supply — making it ideal for fire extinguishers.

---

**Q13.** The chemical name of bleaching powder is:
- A) Sodium hypochlorite
- B) Calcium hypochlorite ✅
- C) Calcium carbonate
- D) Sodium carbonate

**Explanation:** Bleaching powder is calcium hypochlorite — Ca(OCl)₂ or CaOCl₂, made by action of chlorine on slaked lime.

## 🎯 Exam Strategy & Revision Tips

### High-Frequency Topics (Most Asked!)
1. **Chemical names & formulas** — Common name ↔ Chemical name ↔ Formula
2. **Acids in daily life** — Which acid in which fruit/food
3. **pH scale** — pH of common substances
4. **Metals & Non-metals** — Properties, reactivity series, alloys
5. **Gases** — Which gas = which property/use
6. **Periodic Table** — Groups, trends, special elements
7. **Everyday processes** — Galvanization, pasteurization, vulcanization

### Smart Approach
- **Learn the Quick Revision Tables** — 70% of chemistry questions come from direct recall
- **Focus on "common name ↔ chemical name"** — appears in every single exam
- **Remember acids in foods** — citric (lemon), acetic (vinegar), tartaric (tamarind), lactic (curd)
- **Know alloys** — composition and uses (steel, brass, bronze, stainless steel)

### Common Traps
- Confusing **baking soda (NaHCO₃) vs washing soda (Na₂CO₃)**
- Mixing up **galvanization (zinc) vs electroplating (any metal)**
- Confusing **diamond (hardest, non-conductor) vs graphite (soft, conductor)**
- Forgetting that **Mercury is liquid metal** and **Bromine is liquid non-metal**

### Negative Marking Strategy
- Chemistry questions are usually **factual** — you either know it or you don't
- If you're **50% sure**, skip the question (−1/3 penalty not worth it)
- If you're **75%+ sure**, attempt it
- Focus on **common salts, acids, gases** — these repeat every phase
