## 📖 Complete Theory

### Part A: Statement & Conclusion

A **statement** is given, and you must determine which **conclusion** logically follows.

**Rules:**
- The conclusion must be **directly derivable** from the statement
- Don't use outside knowledge — stick to what the statement says
- The conclusion should not be too extreme or go beyond the statement
- "Some" and "All" make a big difference

**Example:**
**Statement:** "All students who study regularly pass the exam."
**Conclusion I:** Ramesh studies regularly, so he will pass.
**Conclusion II:** All those who passed studied regularly.

- I follows ✅ (direct application)
- II does NOT follow ❌ (reverse may not be true — some could pass without regular study)

### Part B: Statement & Course of Action

A **situation/problem** is described. You must decide which **course of action** is appropriate.

**Rules for a valid course of action:**
1. It must be **practical** and **feasible**
2. It must **address the problem** stated
3. It should not be **extreme or impractical**
4. Government/authority taking action should be within their power

**Example:**
**Statement:** "The traffic congestion in the city is increasing day by day."
**Action I:** The government should ban all vehicles. → ❌ Too extreme
**Action II:** The government should improve public transport. → ✅ Practical

### Part C: Statement & Assumption

An **assumption** is something taken for granted — an unstated premise behind the statement.

**Example:**
**Statement:** "Buy Brand X shoes — the best in the market."
**Assumption:** People want to buy the best shoes. → ✅ Follows

---

## 📐 Key Methods

| Type | Ask Yourself |
|------|-------------|
| Conclusion | Does this LOGICALLY follow from the statement? |
| Course of Action | Is this PRACTICAL and does it ADDRESS the problem? |
| Assumption | Is this TAKEN FOR GRANTED by the statement? |

### Red Flags (Usually Wrong):
- Words like "all", "always", "never", "only" (too extreme)
- Actions that are impractical (ban everything, punish everyone)
- Conclusions that reverse the cause-effect
- Assumptions that are explicitly stated (not assumed)

## 📝 RRB NTPC Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

### PYQ 1 (RRB NTPC 2021)
**Statement: "Drinking boiled water is safe." Conclusions: I. Unboiled water is not safe. II. Boiling kills germs.**
(A) Only I (B) Only II (C) Both (D) Neither
**Answer: (A) Only I** → If boiled is safe, unboiled is implied to be unsafe ✅. II may be the reason but isn't stated ❌.

### PYQ 2 (RRB NTPC 2021)
**Statement: "The government has decided to increase the price of petrol." Action I: People should use public transport more. Action II: The government should subsidize fuel.**
(A) Only I (B) Only II (C) Both (D) Neither
**Answer: (A) Only I** → Practical response to the situation ✅. II contradicts the statement ❌.

### PYQ 3 (RRB NTPC 2021)
**Statement: "Reading improves knowledge." Conclusions: I. Those who read are knowledgeable. II. Those who don't read are not knowledgeable.**
(A) Only I (B) Only II (C) Both (D) Neither
**Answer: (A) Only I** → Reading → knowledge ✅. Not reading doesn't necessarily mean no knowledge ❌.

### PYQ 4 (RRB NTPC 2019)
**Statement: "Heavy rains have caused flooding in several districts." Action I: Relief camps should be set up. Action II: All construction near rivers should be banned permanently.**
(A) Only I (B) Only II (C) Both (D) Neither
**Answer: (A) Only I** → Relief camps are practical ✅. Permanent ban is too extreme ❌.

### PYQ 5 (RRB NTPC 2021)
**Statement: "Private schools provide better education than government schools." Conclusions: I. Government schools are useless. II. Private schools have better infrastructure.**
(A) Only I (B) Only II (C) Both (D) Neither
**Answer: (D) Neither** → "Better" ≠ "useless" ❌. Infrastructure isn't mentioned ❌.

### PYQ 6-10: Similar pattern — test with each question whether the conclusion/action is logical, practical, and within scope.

## 🎯 Exam Strategy
1. **Don't add outside knowledge** — Only what the statement says matters
2. **Extreme words** (all, never, only, ban) usually make a conclusion/action wrong
3. **Course of action must be practical** — Common sense applies
4. **Expected time:** 45–60 seconds
