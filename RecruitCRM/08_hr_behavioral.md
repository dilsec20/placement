### Q7. "Tell me about a challenging project/situation"

> **Situation:** While building AceCoder, our PostgreSQL queries started timing out as we crossed 200 users.
>
> **Task:** I needed to optimize database performance without changing the schema significantly.
>
> **Action:** I analyzed slow queries using PostgreSQL's `EXPLAIN ANALYZE`, identified missing indexes on `user_id` and `problem_id` columns in the submissions table, added proper indexes, implemented pagination for API responses, and refactored queries to select only needed columns instead of `SELECT *`.
>
> **Result:** Query response time dropped from ~2 seconds to under 50ms. The platform comfortably handled 3x more concurrent users.

### Q10. "Tell me about a time you worked in a team"

> **S:** During my college's CodeQuest DSA Bootcamp, we worked in teams of 4 to solve competitive programming problems.
>
> **T:** We had to solve a set of problems within a time limit, and each team member had different strengths.
>
> **A:** I took the lead on graph and dynamic programming problems since those are my strengths. I also helped teammates understand approaches by explaining solutions step-by-step. When we got stuck, I suggested breaking the problem down and assigning sub-problems to different members.
>
> **R:** Our team solved the most problems in our batch, and I earned the DSA Summer Bootcamp certificate. More importantly, my teammates improved their problem-solving skills.

---

### Q11. "Tell me about a time you failed"

> **S:** When I started competitive programming, I attempted a Codeforces round and solved only 1 out of 6 problems.
>
> **T:** I felt discouraged but knew I needed to improve systematically.
>
> **A:** Instead of random practice, I created a structured study plan — focused on one topic per week (arrays, then trees, then DP). I analyzed editorial solutions to understand patterns, not just answers.
>
> **R:** Over time, I progressed from solving 1 problem per contest to consistently solving 3-4. My rating went from beginner to Specialist (1475) on Codeforces and Knight (1892) on LeetCode. The failure taught me that systematic effort beats talent.

## SECTION 4: Situational Questions

---

### Q13. "You're assigned a feature you've never built before. What do you do?"

> 1. **Understand the requirement** — Ask questions to clarify scope and expectations
> 2. **Research** — Read documentation, look at similar implementations, check if there's existing code in the codebase I can learn from
> 3. **Prototype** — Build a minimal working version first
> 4. **Iterate** — Get feedback early, improve incrementally
> 5. **Document** — Write clear comments and documentation for future developers

---

### Q14. "You disagree with a senior developer's approach. What do you do?"

> "I'd respectfully share my perspective with data. For example, 'I noticed this approach might have O(n²) complexity in this case. Could we consider using a HashMap for O(n) lookup? Here's a benchmark I ran.' I'd present it as a suggestion, not a challenge. Ultimately, I'd defer to their experience while ensuring my concern is heard. I might be wrong, and I can learn from their reasoning."

---

### Q15. "How do you prioritize tasks when you have multiple deadlines?"

> 1. **List all tasks** with deadlines and dependencies
> 2. **Identify blockers** — What's blocking other people? Do that first.
> 3. **Estimate effort** — Quick wins first if deadlines are similar
> 4. **Communicate** — If I can't meet a deadline, I communicate early with specific alternatives
> 5. **Focus** — One task at a time, no multitasking on complex work
