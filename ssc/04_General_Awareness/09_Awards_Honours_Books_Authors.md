## 1. Highest Civilian Awards of India (Bharat Ratna & Padma Awards)

### 1.1 Bharat Ratna (Est. January 2, 1954)

* **Date of Institution:** **January 2, 1954** by President Dr. Rajendra Prasad.
* **Nature:** Highest civilian award of the Republic of India; awarded for exceptional service/performance of the highest order in **any field of human endeavour** (originally limited to Arts, Literature, Science, and Public Services; expanded to *"any field of human endeavour"* in **December 2011**, paving the way for sports).
* **Design & Specifications:**
  * Shaped like a **Peepal leaf** (*Ficus religiosa*), made of toned bronze (~59 mm long, 48 mm wide, 3.2 mm thick).
  * **Obverse:** An embossed **Sun** in platinum in the centre with **"Bharat Ratna"** inscribed in Devanagari script below it.
  * **Reverse:** The **State Emblem of India** (Ashoka Lion Capital) in platinum in the centre with **"Satyameva Jayate"** inscribed in Devanagari below it.
  * Worn around the neck on a **2-inch-wide white ribbon**.
  * Manufactured at the **Alipore Mint, Kolkata**.
* **Rules & Constitutional Status:**
  * **Annual Limit:** Normally restricted to a maximum of **3 recipients per year** (Exceptions: **4** in 1999; **5** in 2024).
  * **Monetary Grant:** Carries **no cash prize**; recipients receive a *Sanad* (certificate signed by the President) and the medallion, and rank **7A** in the Indian Order of Precedence.
  * **Recommending Authority:** Prime Minister recommends names directly to the President of India (no formal nomination committee required).
  * **Article 18(1) Rule:** Cannot be used as a prefix or suffix to the recipient's name (*Balaji Raghavan vs. Union of India, 1996*).
  * **Posthumous Provision:** Original 1954 statutes **did not permit posthumous awards**; amended in **January 1966** (Lal Bahadur Shastri became the 1st posthumous recipient).
  * **Suspensions:** Twice suspended — **July 1977 to January 1980** (Janata Party govt.) and **1992 to December 1995** (pending Supreme Court PILs on Art. 18).
  * **Total Recipients (1954–2024):** **53** (including **18 posthumous** recipients).

### 1.2 Padma Awards (Instituted January 2, 1954)

* **Hierarchy of Civilian Awards:**
  1. **Bharat Ratna** (1st highest)
  2. **Padma Vibhushan** (2nd highest — *exceptional and distinguished service*)
  3. **Padma Bhushan** (3rd highest — *distinguished service of a high order*)
  4. **Padma Shri** (4th highest — *distinguished service in any field*)
* **Renaming History:** Originally instituted in 1954 as *Padma Vibhushan* in three classes (*Pahela Varg, Dusra Varg, Tisra Varg*); renamed via Presidential Notification on **January 8, 1955** to **Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan, and Padma Shri**.
* **Key Rules:**
  * Announced annually on the **eve of Republic Day (January 25)**.
  * **Annual Cap:** Maximum **120 awards** per year (excluding posthumous awards and NRI/foreigner/PIO/OCI recipients).
  * **Cooling-Off Period:** A higher Padma award can normally be conferred **only after at least 5 years** from the previous Padma award.
  * **Padma Awards Committee:** Constituted every year by the Prime Minister and **headed by the Cabinet Secretary** (includes Home Secretary, Secretary to President, and 4–6 eminent persons).
  * Anyone can nominate (including self-nomination via Rashtriya Puraskar Portal). No cash reward; not a title under Article 18.

## 2. Gallantry, Defence & Service Awards

### 2.1 Classification & Official Order of Precedence

Gallantry awards are announced twice a year: on **Republic Day (Jan 26)** and **Independence Day (Aug 15)**.

| Precedence Rank | Award Name | Category | Equivalent To | Instituted |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Param Vir Chakra (PVC)** | Wartime | Highest Military Award | Jan 26, 1950 *(w.e.f. Aug 15, 1947)* |
| **2** | **Ashoka Chakra (AC)** | Peacetime | Equivalent to PVC | Jan 4, 1952 *(w.e.f. Aug 15, 1947)* |
| **3** | **Maha Vir Chakra (MVC)** | Wartime | 2nd Wartime | Jan 26, 1950 *(w.e.f. Aug 15, 1947)* |
| **4** | **Kirti Chakra (KC)** | Peacetime | Equivalent to MVC | Jan 4, 1952 *(renamed Jan 27, 1967)* |
| **5** | **Vir Chakra (VrC)** | Wartime | 3rd Wartime | Jan 26, 1950 *(w.e.f. Aug 15, 1947)* |
| **6** | **Shaurya Chakra (SC)** | Peacetime | Equivalent to VrC | Jan 4, 1952 *(renamed Jan 27, 1967)* |

* **Wartime Series (in the presence of the enemy):** PVC → MVC → VrC.
* **Peacetime Series (other than in the face of the enemy — includes civilians):** AC → KC → SC. *(Note: On Jan 27, 1967, Ashoka Chakra Class I, II, III were renamed **Ashoka Chakra, Kirti Chakra, and Shaurya Chakra**).*

### 4.2 The Nobel Prize (Est. 1895; First Awarded 1901)

* **Origin:** Instituted under the will of Swedish chemist **Alfred Nobel** (inventor of dynamite, signed Nov 27, 1895); first awarded on **December 10, 1901** (death anniversary of Alfred Nobel).
* **6 Categories & Award Cities:**
  * **Stockholm, Sweden (5 prizes):** Physics, Chemistry, Physiology or Medicine, Literature, and **Economic Sciences** (Sveriges Riksbank Prize in Economic Sciences in Memory of Alfred Nobel — instituted **1968**, first awarded **1969**).
  * **Oslo, Norway (1 prize):** **Nobel Peace Prize** (awarded by the Norwegian Nobel Committee).
* **Rules:** Maximum **3 laureates** can share a prize in a category; **never awarded posthumously** (unless the recipient dies *after* the official October announcement).
* **Age Records:** Youngest laureate — **Malala Yousafzai** (Peace, 2014, age **17**); Oldest laureate — **John B. Goodenough** (Chemistry, 2019, age **97**).
