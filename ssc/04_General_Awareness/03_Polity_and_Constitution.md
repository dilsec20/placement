## 1. Historical Background (Company Rule & Crown Rule: 1773–1947)

The constitutional evolution of India under British rule falls into two distinct phases:
1. **Company Rule (1773–1858):** Rule of the British East India Company (EIC) regulated by British Parliamentary Acts.
2. **Crown Rule (1858–1947):** Direct rule of the British Crown following the Revolt of 1857.

### 1.1 Company Rule (1773–1858)

| Act / Year | Key Officials & Institutional Changes | Major Exam-Oriented Provisions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Regulating Act, 1773** | • First Governor-General of Bengal: **Lord Warren Hastings**<br>• First Chief Justice of SC: **Sir Elijah Impey** | • First step by British Govt to control & regulate EIC affairs; recognised EIC's political & administrative functions; laid foundation of **central administration**.<br>• Designated Governor of Bengal as **Governor-General of Bengal** with a **4-member Executive Council**.<br>• Made Governors of **Bombay & Madras** subordinate to Governor-General of Bengal.<br>• Established **Supreme Court at Calcutta (1774)** (1 Chief Justice + 3 Judges).<br>• Prohibited EIC servants from private trade or accepting bribes/presents.<br>• Required Court of Directors to report revenue, civil & military affairs to British Govt. |
| **Amending Act, 1781** *(Act of Settlement)* | — | • Passed to rectify defects of Regulating Act, 1773.<br>• Exempted Governor-General & Council and EIC servants from Supreme Court jurisdiction for official acts.<br>• Excluded **revenue matters** from SC jurisdiction.<br>• Gave SC jurisdiction over all inhabitants of Calcutta; mandated applying **personal laws** (Hindu law for Hindus, Mohammedan law for Muslims).<br>• Appeals from Provincial Courts routed to **Governor-General-in-Council** (not SC). |
| **Pitt's India Act, 1784** | • Created **Board of Control** (6 members) | • Distinguished between **commercial** and **political** functions of EIC.<br>• **Court of Directors** → Commercial affairs; **Board of Control** → Political, civil, military & revenue affairs (**Double Government**).<br>• EIC territories in India called **"British possessions in India"** for the first time.<br>• Gave British Govt supreme control over EIC administration in India. |
| **Act of 1786** | • **Lord Cornwallis** | • Enacted to meet Cornwallis's conditions for becoming GG of Bengal: (1) power to **override Council** in special cases; (2) also act as **Commander-in-Chief**. |
| **Charter Act, 1793** | — | • Extended Council-override power to all future Governors-General and Governors.<br>• Extended EIC's trade monopoly in India for **20 more years**.<br>• Commander-in-Chief excluded from GG's Council unless specifically appointed.<br>• Salaries of Board of Control & staff to be paid from **Indian revenues** (continued till 1919). |
| **Charter Act, 1813** | — | • Abolished EIC's trade monopoly in India **except** monopoly over **tea trade** and **trade with China**.<br>• Asserted **sovereignty of British Crown** over EIC territories.<br>• Allowed **Christian missionaries** to enter India.<br>• Provided **₹1 lakh annually** for spread of western education.<br>• Authorised local governments to impose taxes and punish defaulters. |
| **Charter Act, 1833** | • First Governor-General of India: **Lord William Bentinck**<br>• 4th (Law) Member: **Lord Macaulay** | • Final step towards **centralisation**.<br>• Made Governor-General of Bengal the **Governor-General of India** with all civil & military powers.<br>• Deprived Governors of Bombay & Madras of legislative powers; GG of India got exclusive legislative power for all British India.<br>• Previous laws termed **Regulations**; laws under this Act termed **Acts**.<br>• Ended EIC's **commercial activities completely** (became purely administrative body).<br>• Attempted open competition for civil services (negated by Court of Directors).<br>• First **Law Commission (1834)** set up under **Lord Macaulay**. |
| **Charter Act, 1853** | • **Macaulay Committee (1854)** on Indian Civil Service | • Separated **legislative and executive functions** of GG's Council for the first time.<br>• Added **6 legislative councillors** → created **Indian (Central) Legislative Council** (acted as a mini-Parliament).<br>• Introduced **open competition** for recruitment of civil servants (thrown open to Indians).<br>• Extended EIC rule indefinitely (no fixed 20-year term) on trust for the Crown.<br>• Introduced **local representation** in Central Legislative Council (4 of 6 new members appointed by local govts of Madras, Bombay, Bengal, Agra). |

### 1.2 Crown Rule (1858–1947)

| Act / Event / Year | Key Personalities | High-Yield Constitutional Provisions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Government of India Act, 1858** *(Act for the Good Government of India)* | • First Viceroy: **Lord Canning**<br>• First Secretary of State: **Lord Stanley** | • Abolished EIC; transferred powers, territories & revenues to **British Crown**.<br>• Changed Governor-General of India to **Viceroy of India** (direct Crown representative).<br>• Ended **Double Government** (abolished both Court of Directors & Board of Control).<br>• Created **Secretary of State for India** (British Cabinet member, accountable to British Parliament) assisted by a **15-member Council of India** (advisory body). |
| **Indian Councils Act, 1861** | • Viceroy: **Lord Canning** | • Initiated **representative institutions**: Viceroy nominated 3 Indians to expanded council in 1862 (**Raja of Benaras, Maharaja of Patiala, Sir Dinkar Rao**).<br>• Initiated **decentralisation** by restoring legislative powers to **Bombay & Madras**.<br>• Provided for new legislative councils for **Bengal (1862)**, **North-Western Provinces (1886)**, and **Punjab (1897)**.<br>• Recognised **Portfolio System** (introduced by Lord Canning in 1859).<br>• Empowered Viceroy to issue **ordinances** in emergencies (valid for **6 months**). |
| **Indian Councils Act, 1892** | — | • Increased non-official members in Central & Provincial Councils (retained official majority).<br>• Gave councils power to **discuss the budget** and ask questions to the executive.<br>• Introduced limited **indirect election** (though the word "election" was not explicitly used in the Act). |
| **Indian Councils Act, 1909** *(Morley-Minto Reforms)* | • SoS: **Lord Morley**<br>• Viceroy: **Lord Minto** *(Father of Communal Electorate)*<br>• First Indian in Viceroy's Exec. Council: **Satyendra Prasad Sinha** (Law Member) | • Enlarged Central Legislative Council from **16 to 60** members; retained official majority at Centre but allowed **non-official majority in provinces**.<br>• Allowed **supplementary questions** and **resolutions on budget**.<br>• Associated Indians with Executive Councils of Viceroy & Governors for the first time.<br>• Introduced **communal representation (Separate Electorate)** for **Muslims** — legalised communalism. |
| **Government of India Act, 1919** *(Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms)* | • SoS: **Edwin Montagu**<br>• Viceroy: **Lord Chelmsford**<br>• Came into force: **1921** | • Separated **Central and Provincial subjects**.<br>• Introduced **Dyarchy in Provinces**: Provincial subjects split into **Transferred** (Governor + responsible ministers) and **Reserved** (Governor + Executive Council).<br>• Introduced **bicameralism** & **direct elections** at Centre: **Council of State** (Upper House) & **Legislative Assembly** (Lower House).<br>• **3 of 6 members** of Viceroy's Executive Council (excluding C-in-C) to be **Indians**.<br>• Extended separate electorates to **Sikhs, Indian Christians, Anglo-Indians, Europeans**.<br>• Limited franchise based on **property, tax, or education**.<br>• Created **High Commissioner for India in London**.<br>• Provided for **Public Service Commission** → **Central PSC set up in 1926** (Lee Commission, 1923–24).<br>• Separated **provincial budgets from Central budget**.<br>• Mandated a statutory review commission after 10 years. |
| **Simon Commission (1927) & Nehru Report (1928)** | • **Sir John Simon** (7 British members)<br>• **Motilal Nehru** (Chairman, Nehru Report) | • **Simon Commission (Nov 1927):** All-white statutory body boycotted by Indians; Report (1930) recommended abolishing dyarchy, provincial responsible govt, federation & continuing communal electorates. Led to **3 Round Table Conferences (1930–32)**.<br>• **Nehru Report (Aug 1928):** First major Indian constitutional draft; demanded **Dominion Status**, 19 Fundamental Rights, joint electorates (no separate electorates), linguistic provinces, secular state.<br>• **Communal Award (Aug 1932)** by PM Ramsay MacDonald extended separate electorates to Depressed Classes → modified by **Poona Pact (Sept 1932)** between Mahatma Gandhi & Dr. B.R. Ambedkar (joint electorates with reserved seats). |
| **Government of India Act, 1935** | • Longest British Act (**321 Sections, 10 Schedules**) | • Proposed **All-India Federation** (Provinces + Princely States) — *never came into effect* (princely states did not join).<br>• Divided powers into **3 Lists**: **Federal List (59)**, **Provincial List (54)**, **Concurrent List (36)**; **residuary powers** vested in **Viceroy**.<br>• **Abolished Dyarchy in Provinces** → introduced **Provincial Autonomy** (operated 1937–39).<br>• Provided **Dyarchy at Centre** (*never implemented*).<br>• Introduced **bicameralism in 6 of 11 provinces** (Bengal, Bombay, Madras, Bihar, Assam, United Provinces).<br>• Extended separate electorates to **Depressed Classes (SCs), Women, and Labour**.<br>• Abolished **Council of India (1858)**.<br>• Extended franchise to ~**10% of population**.<br>• Provided for **Reserve Bank of India (RBI)** (established 1935), **Federal PSC, Provincial PSCs, Joint PSCs**, and **Federal Court** (set up in **1937**, first CJ: **Sir Maurice Gwyer**). |
| **August Offer (Aug 8, 1940)** | • Viceroy: **Lord Linlithgow** | • First explicit recognition of Indians' right to frame their own constitution (after WWII); promised Dominion Status, expanded Viceroy's Executive Council, and minority veto on future constitution. Rejected by Congress. |
| **Cripps Mission (March 1942)** | • **Sir Stafford Cripps** | • Offered **Dominion Status** after WWII and an elected/nominated Constituent Assembly; allowed dissenting provinces to opt out/secede. Rejected by Congress (Gandhi called it a **"post-dated cheque"**) and Muslim League. |
| **Cabinet Mission (March 1946)** | • **Lord Pethick-Lawrence** (Chairman), **Sir Stafford Cripps**, **A.V. Alexander** | • Plan published **May 16, 1946**. Rejected separate Pakistan; proposed a 3-tier **Union of India** (Defence, Foreign Affairs, Communications) and province groupings (A, B, C).<br>• Laid down composition of the **Constituent Assembly (389 seats)** and formation of an **Interim Government**. |
| **Mountbatten Plan (June 3, 1947) & Indian Independence Act, 1947** | • **Lord Mountbatten** (Last Viceroy & 1st GG of independent India)<br>• **C. Rajagopalachari** (1st & last Indian GG) | • **Mountbatten Plan (3rd June Plan):** Accepted partition of India into two Dominions.<br>• **Indian Independence Act, 1947** (Royal Assent: **July 18, 1947**; effective **Aug 15, 1947**):<br>  – Ended British rule; created independent Dominions of **India & Pakistan** with right to leave Commonwealth.<br>  – Abolished **Viceroy** & **Secretary of State for India**; dropped **"Emperor of India"** from British King's title.<br>  – Empowered Constituent Assemblies of both dominions to frame constitutions and repeal any British Act.<br>  – **Lapse of British paramountcy** over Princely States.<br>  – Dominions governed under **GOI Act, 1935** (with modifications) until new constitutions took effect. |

## 2. Making of the Constitution, Committees & Borrowed Features

### 2.1 Timeline of Demand & Making
- **1934:** **M.N. Roy** (pioneer of the communist movement in India) first proposed the idea of a Constituent Assembly.
- **1935:** **Indian National Congress (INC)** officially demanded a Constituent Assembly for the first time.
- **1938:** **Jawaharlal Nehru** declared on behalf of INC that the Constitution must be framed without outside interference by a Constituent Assembly elected on **adult franchise**.
- **1940:** Accepted in principle by British Govt in the **August Offer**.
- **1942:** **Cripps Mission** proposed an independent Constituent Assembly post-WWII (rejected).
- **Nov 1946:** Constituent Assembly constituted under the **Cabinet Mission Plan (1946)**.
- **Total Time Taken:** **2 years, 11 months, and 18 days** (11 sessions; Draft Constitution considered for 114 days; Constitution makers studied ~60 countries' constitutions; expenditure ~₹64 lakh).

## 3. Preamble, All 25 Parts & All 12 Schedules

### 3.1 The Preamble

#### Exact Text of the Preamble
> **WE, THE PEOPLE OF INDIA**, having solemnly resolved to constitute India into a **SOVEREIGN, SOCIALIST, SECULAR, DEMOCRATIC, REPUBLIC** and to secure to all its citizens:
> **JUSTICE**, social, economic and political;
> **LIBERTY** of thought, expression, belief, faith and worship;
> **EQUALITY** of status and of opportunity; and to promote among them all
> **FRATERNITY** assuring the dignity of the individual and the **unity and integrity** of the Nation;
> IN OUR CONSTITUENT ASSEMBLY this **twenty-sixth day of November, 1949**, do HEREBY ADOPT, ENACT AND GIVE TO OURSELVES THIS CONSTITUTION.

#### Key Facts & Keywords
- **Source of Authority:** People of India (*"We, the People of India"*).
- **5-Word Nature of State:** **Sovereign, Socialist, Secular, Democratic, Republic**.
  - *Democratic* includes political, social, and economic democracy; India follows **representative parliamentary democracy** with direct democracy devices like Universal Adult Franchise.
  - *Republic* means elected Head of State (President) for a fixed term (unlike hereditary monarchy in UK), absence of privileged class, and public offices open to all citizens.
- **4 Objectives:**
  - **Justice (3 types):** Social, Economic, Political *(from Russian Revolution, 1917)*.
  - **Liberty (5 types):** Thought, Expression, Belief, Faith, Worship *(from French Revolution, 1789)*.
  - **Equality (2 types):** Status, Opportunity *(civic, political & economic equality)*.
  - **Fraternity:** Dignity of the individual + Unity & Integrity of the Nation *(from French Revolution, 1789)*.
- **Amendment to Preamble:** Amended **only once** by the **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976**, which added **3 new words**: **Socialist**, **Secular**, and **Integrity** (*"unity of the Nation"* → *"unity and integrity of the Nation"*).
- **Enforceability:** **Non-justiciable**; neither a source of legislative power nor a prohibition on legislative power.

#### Famous Descriptions of the Preamble
- **N.A. Palkhivala:** *"Identity card of the Constitution"*
- **K.M. Munshi:** *"Horoscope of our sovereign democratic republic"*
- **Thakur Das Bhargava:** *"Soul of the Constitution" / "Key to the Constitution" / "Jewel set in the Constitution"*
- **Sir Ernest Barker:** *"Key-note to the Constitution"*
- **Alladi Krishnaswami Iyer:** *"Expresses what we had thought or dreamt so long"*

#### Landmark Supreme Court Cases on the Preamble
| Case & Year | Supreme Court Ruling |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Berubari Union Case (1960)** | Held that the Preamble shows general purposes and is a key to the minds of the makers, **but is NOT a part of the Constitution**. |
| **Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)** | Overruled Berubari; held **Preamble IS an integral part of the Constitution**; can be amended under Art 368 **subject to the 'Basic Structure' doctrine**. |
| **S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)** | Reiterated Preamble is an integral part of the Constitution; declared **Secularism** and **Federalism** as part of the **Basic Structure**. |
| **LIC of India v. Consumer Education & Research Centre (1995)** | Again affirmed that the **Preamble is an integral part of the Constitution**. |

## 4. Union & Its Territory (Part I: Articles 1–4) & Citizenship (Part II: Articles 5–11)

### 4.1 Part I: The Union and Its Territory (Articles 1–4)

| Article | Provision & Constitutional Significance |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Article 1** | **Name & Territory of the Union:** *"India, that is Bharat, shall be a Union of States."*<br>• Dr. Ambedkar explained **"Union of States"** implies: (1) Indian Federation is **not** the result of an agreement among states; (2) States have **no right to secede**.<br>• **"Territory of India"** (wider term) comprises: (a) Territories of States, (b) Union Territories, (c) Territories that may be acquired in future. **"Union of India"** includes only the States. |
| **Article 2** | **Admission or establishment of new States:** Empowers Parliament to admit into the Union, or establish, **new states** that were **not previously part of the Union of India** (e.g., Sikkim, Puducherry). |
| **Article 3** | **Formation of new States & alteration of areas, boundaries, or names of existing States:** Relates to **internal re-adjustment** of existing states/UTs. Parliament can by law: (a) form a new state by separating/uniting territories; (b) increase area; (c) diminish area; (d) alter boundaries; (e) alter name.<br>• **Two Mandatory Conditions:** (1) Bill can be introduced in either House **only with prior recommendation of the President**; (2) President must refer the bill to the concerned **State Legislature** for expressing its views within a specified period (though **President/Parliament is NOT bound** by those views; no reference needed for UTs).<br>• India is an **"indestructible Union of destructible states"** *(USA is an "indestructible Union of indestructible states")*. |
| **Article 4** | **Laws under Art 2 & Art 3:** Consequential amendments to **1st and 4th Schedules** made under Art 2 or Art 3 are passed by **Simple Majority** (ordinary legislative process) and are **NOT deemed Constitutional Amendments under Article 368**.<br>• **Berubari Union Case (1960):** SC ruled Parliament **cannot** cede Indian territory to a foreign state under Art 3; cession requires a Constitutional Amendment under **Art 368** (hence **9th Amendment Act, 1960**; **100th Amendment Act, 2015** for India-Bangladesh Land Boundary Agreement). However, settling a boundary dispute without ceding territory can be done by executive action (1969 SC ruling). |

### 4.2 Integration & Reorganization of States

- **Integration of Princely States (1947–49):** Out of 552 princely states within India's geographical boundaries, 549 joined immediately; remaining 3 integrated via:
  - **Hyderabad:** Police action (*Operation Polo*, Sept 1948)
  - **Junagadh:** Referendum (Feb 1948)
  - **Jammu & Kashmir:** Instrument of Accession (Oct 26, 1947)
- **Commissions & Committees:**
  1. **Dhar Commission (June 1948):** *Linguistic Provinces Commission* headed by **S.K. Dhar** (Report: Dec 1948). Recommended reorganisation based on **administrative convenience**, not language.
  2. **JVP Committee (Dec 1948):** **J**awaharlal Nehru, **V**allabhbhai Patel, **P**attabhi Sitaramayya (Report: April 1949). Formally **rejected language** as basis for reorganisation.
  3. **Andhra State (Oct 1, 1953):** First linguistic state created (Telugu-speaking areas separated from Madras State) following **Potti Sriramulu's 56-day hunger strike** and death (Dec 1952).
  4. **Fazl Ali Commission (Dec 1953):** *States Reorganisation Commission* — **Fazl Ali (Chairman), K.M. Panikkar, H.N. Kunzru** (Report: Sept 1955). Accepted **language** as basis for reorganisation, **rejected "one language–one state"** theory; proposed abolishing 4-fold classification (Part A, B, C, D states).
- **States Reorganisation Act, 1956 & 7th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1956:**
  - Effective **November 1, 1956**; abolished Part A/B/C/D classification and repealed Part VII (Art 238).
  - Created **14 States and 6 Union Territories**:
    - *14 States:* Andhra Pradesh, Assam, Bihar, Bombay, Jammu & Kashmir, Kerala, Madhya Pradesh, Madras, Mysore, Orissa, Punjab, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal.
    - *6 UTs:* Andaman & Nicobar Islands, Delhi, Himachal Pradesh, Laccadive-Minicoy-Amindivi Islands, Manipur, Tripura.

| :--- | :--- |
| **Article 5** | **Citizenship at commencement (Jan 26, 1950):** Domicile in India + any one of: (a) born in India; (b) either parent born in India; (c) ordinarily resident in India for at least **5 years** immediately before commencement. |
| **Article 6** | **Migrants from Pakistan to India:** Cut-off date **July 19, 1948** (permit system introduced); those migrating before/on/after with specified conditions became Indian citizens. |

| **Article 9** | **Voluntary acquisition of foreign citizenship:** Any person voluntarily acquiring citizenship of a foreign State **ceases** to be an Indian citizen *(establishes Single Citizenship)*. |

| **Article 11** | **Parliament to regulate citizenship:** Empowers Parliament to make laws regarding acquisition, termination, and all other matters relating to citizenship. |

#### Citizenship Act, 1955 *(Amended in 1986, 1992, 2003, 2005, 2015, 2019)*

##### 5 Modes of Acquisition of Citizenship
1. **By Birth (Section 3):**
   - *Jan 26, 1950 – July 1, 1987:* Anyone born in India (irrespective of parents' nationality).
   - *July 1, 1987 – Dec 3, 2004:* Born in India + **either parent** is an Indian citizen at birth.
   - *From Dec 3, 2004 onwards:* Born in India + **both parents** are citizens, OR **one parent is a citizen and the other is not an illegal migrant**. *(Excludes children of foreign diplomats & enemy aliens).*
2. **By Descent (Section 4):**
   - Born outside India: Jan 26, 1950–Dec 10, 1992 (father must be citizen); Dec 10, 1992 onwards (**either parent** must be citizen); Dec 3, 2004 onwards (birth must be registered at Indian consulate within **1 year**).
3. **By Registration (Section 5):**
   - Central Govt may register eligible applicants (not illegal migrants), e.g., Persons of Indian Origin ordinarily resident in India for **7 years**, persons married to Indian citizens (7 years residence), minor children of Indian citizens. Must take **Oath of Allegiance**.
4. **By Naturalisation (Section 6):**
   - Foreigner (not illegal migrant) ordinarily resident in India for **12 years** (11 years in preceding 14 years + 12 months immediately prior to application), good character, knowledge of a language in the **8th Schedule**, renounces previous citizenship.
   - *Citizenship (Amendment) Act, 2019 (CAA):* Reduced residence requirement from **11 years to 5 years** for Hindu, Sikh, Buddhist, Jain, Parsi, and Christian migrants from **Afghanistan, Bangladesh, and Pakistan** who entered India on or before **December 31, 2014**.
5. **By Incorporation of Territory (Section 7):**
   - When a foreign territory becomes part of India, Central Govt specifies persons who become citizens (e.g., Puducherry via Citizenship (Pondicherry) Order, 1962).

##### 3 Modes of Loss of Citizenship
1. **By Renunciation (Section 8):** Voluntary declaration by an adult citizen; minor children also lose citizenship (may resume within 1 year of turning 18).
2. **By Termination (Section 9):** Automatic loss upon **voluntarily acquiring citizenship of another country** *(suspended during war)*.
3. **By Deprivation (Section 10):** Compulsory termination by Central Govt if citizenship was obtained by fraud/false representation, disloyalty to Constitution, unlawful trading/communication with enemy during war, imprisonment in any country for **2 years within 5 years** of registration/naturalisation, or ordinary residence outside India continuously for **7 years**.

#### Single Citizenship
- Borrowed from the **United Kingdom**. Unlike the USA or Switzerland (dual citizenship: national + state), India has **only one citizenship** with uniform political and civil rights nationwide.
- *Exceptions:* Parliament can prescribe residence for certain public employments (**Art 16(3)**); States can offer concessions based on residence (not place of birth, **Art 15**); reasonable restrictions in Scheduled/Tribal areas (**Art 19**).

## 5. Fundamental Rights (Part III: Articles 12–35)

### 5.1 Overview, Origin & Key Features
- **Magna Carta of India:** Part III of the Constitution (Articles 12–35) is described as the **Magna Carta of India** (after the 1215 Charter of Rights in England).
- **Source:** Borrowed from the **Bill of Rights** of the **United States Constitution**.
- **Objective:** Establish a government of laws, not of men; promote **Political Democracy** and protect individual liberty against arbitrary State action.
- **Core Features:**
  - **Justiciable:** Directly enforceable by courts (Supreme Court under **Art 32**; High Courts under **Art 226**).
  - **Not Absolute:** Qualified and subject to **reasonable restrictions** imposed by the State (except absolute rights like **Art 17** and **Art 24**).
  - **Negative & Positive Obligations:** Some place limitations on State power (e.g., Art 14, 15(1), 21), while others confer positive privileges (e.g., Art 19, 25, 29, 30).
  - **Amendable, Not Sacrosanct:** Parliament can amend Fundamental Rights under **Art 368**, provided the **Basic Structure** is not destroyed (*Kesavananda Bharati*, 1973).
  - **Enforceability Against Private Individuals:** Most FRs protect against State action, **except Arts 15(2), 17, 23, and 24**, which are enforceable against private individuals as well.
  - **Suspension During National Emergency (Art 352):**
    - **Art 358:** Automatically suspends **Art 19** freedoms when Emergency is declared on grounds of **War or External Aggression** (External Emergency), **NOT** on the ground of **Armed Rebellion** (**44th Amendment Act, 1978**).
    - **Art 359:** Empowers the President to suspend the right to move any court for enforcement of specified FRs during Emergency, **EXCEPT Articles 20 and 21**, which can **never** be suspended (**44th Amendment Act, 1978**).

### 5.2 Article 12 & Article 13 (Foundational Provisions)

#### Article 12: Definition of "State"
Applies to Part III (and Part IV via Art 36). **"The State"** includes:
1. **Government and Parliament of India** (Executive and Legislature of the Union).
2. **Government and Legislature of each State** (Executive and Legislature of States).
3. **All Local Authorities** — Municipalities, Panchayats, District Boards, Improvement Trusts, etc.
4. **Other Authorities** — Statutory and non-statutory authorities acting as agencies or instrumentalities of the State (e.g., LIC, ONGC, SAIL, BHEL, Airport Authority).
   - *Key Cases:* *R.D. Shetty v. International Airport Authority of India* (1979) and *Ajay Hasia v. Khalid Mujib* (1981) laid down tests (financial, functional, administrative control) to determine "instrumentality of State".
   - **Judiciary as State:** When exercising rule-making or administrative functions (e.g., Art 145, staff appointments), the Judiciary is "State"; when exercising strictly judicial functions, it is generally not treated as "State" (*Naresh Shridhar Mirajkar*, 1966).

#### Article 13: Laws Inconsistent with Fundamental Rights & Judicial Review
| Clause | Provision & Constitutional Doctrine |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Art 13(1)** | **Pre-Constitutional Laws** (in force before 26 Jan 1950) become **void to the extent of inconsistency** with Part III.<br>• **Doctrine of Eclipse** (*Bhikaji Narain Dhakras v. State of MP*, 1955): Pre-constitutional laws inconsistent with FRs are not dead, only overshadowed ("eclipsed") and revive if the FR restriction is removed.<br>• **Doctrine of Severability** (*R.M.D.C. v. UOI*, 1957): Only the offending portion is void if separable from the valid portion. |
| **Art 13(2)** | **Post-Constitutional Laws:** State shall not make any law that takes away or abridges FRs; any such law is **void ab initio** (void from inception) to the extent of contravention. |
| **Art 13(3)** | Defines **"Law"** broadly to include any Ordinance, order, bye-law, rule, regulation, notification, custom or usage having the force of law in India. (Also defines "laws in force"). |
| **Art 13(4)** | Inserted by **24th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1971**: Nothing in Art 13 applies to any amendment of the Constitution made under **Art 368**. |

> **High-Yield Exam Fact — Judicial Review:**
> - **Art 13** explicitly provides the constitutional basis for **Judicial Review** (along with Arts 32, 226, 131–136, 143, 245, 246).
> - Note: The exact phrase **"Judicial Review"** is **NOT mentioned** anywhere in the Indian Constitution.
> - Power of Judicial Review is borrowed from the **USA** and is part of the **Basic Structure** (*Minerva Mills*, 1980).

### 5.4 Right to Equality (Articles 14–18)

#### Article 14: Equality Before Law & Equal Protection of Laws
*"The State shall not deny to any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws within the territory of India."*

| Feature | Equality Before Law | Equal Protection of Laws |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Origin** | **British** origin | **American** origin (14th Amendment, US Constitution) |
| **Nature** | **Negative** concept (absence of special privileges) | **Positive** concept (equality of treatment in equal circumstances) |
| **Meaning** | No person is above the law; equal subjection of all classes to ordinary law | Likes must be treated alike; unlikes cannot be treated equally |
| **Classification** | Focuses on non-discrimination in application | Permits **reasonable classification** (forbids *class legislation*) |

- **Rule of Law (A.V. Dicey):** Art 14 embodies Dicey's concept of *Rule of Law* (Basic Structure):
  1. **Absence of arbitrary power** (supremacy of law) — *Applicable in India*.
  2. **Equality before the law** (no one above ordinary courts) — *Applicable in India*.
  3. **Primacy of individual rights** (Constitution is the result of rights defined by courts) — *NOT applicable in India* (in India, the Constitution is the source of individual rights).
- **Reasonable Classification Test** (*State of West Bengal v. Anwar Ali Sarkar*, 1952): Classification is valid only if it satisfies two conditions: (i) **Intelligible Differentia** (distinct basis separating grouped persons/things), and (ii) **Rational Nexus** with the object sought to be achieved by the statute.
- **Anti-Arbitrariness Doctrine** (*E.P. Royappa v. State of Tamil Nadu*, 1974): Equality is a dynamic concept; **arbitrariness is the sworn enemy of equality** under Art 14.
- **Exceptions to Art 14:**
  - **Art 361:** President and Governors are not answerable to any court for exercise of official powers; no criminal proceedings or arrest/imprisonment during term of office; civil proceedings require 2 months' prior notice.
  - **Art 361A:** Immunity for substantially true publication of Parliamentary/State Legislature proceedings.
  - **Arts 105 & 194:** Parliamentary/State Legislative privileges for anything said or vote given in the House/committees.
  - **Art 31C:** Laws implementing **Art 39(b) & 39(c)** cannot be challenged under Art 14.
  - **International Immunity:** Foreign sovereigns, ambassadors, diplomats, and UN agencies enjoy immunity from local courts.

#### Article 15: Prohibition of Discrimination on Certain Grounds
- **Art 15(1):** State shall not discriminate against any citizen on grounds **ONLY** of **5 grounds**: **Religion, Race, Caste, Sex, Place of Birth** (or any of them). *(Note: "ONLY" means discrimination based on other factors like merit, age, or domicile is not barred by Art 15(1)).*
- **Art 15(2):** No citizen shall be subjected to any disability or restriction regarding access to shops, public restaurants, hotels, places of public entertainment, or use of wells, tanks, bathing ghats, roads, and places of public resort maintained wholly/partly out of State funds or dedicated to general public use. *(Applies to both **State and private individuals**).*
- **Art 15(3):** State can make **special provisions for women and children** (e.g., free education, reserved seats in local bodies, maternity benefits).
- **Art 15(4):** Added by **1st Constitutional Amendment Act, 1951** (following *State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan*, 1951) — enables special provisions for advancement of **socially and educationally backward classes (SEBCs)** or **SCs and STs**.
- **Art 15(5):** Added by **93rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2005** — enables reservation for SEBCs/SCs/STs in admission to educational institutions, including **private educational institutions** (whether aided or unaided), **EXCEPT minority educational institutions** covered under Art 30(1).
  - Upheld in *Ashoka Kumar Thakur v. Union of India* (2008) (27% OBC quota in Central Educational Institutions).
- **Art 15(6):** Added by **103rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 2019** — enables special provisions, including up to **10% reservation for Economically Weaker Sections (EWS)** of citizens (other than SCs/STs/OBCs already covered) in admission to educational institutions (including private aided/unaided, excluding minority institutions).
  - Upheld by a 3:2 majority in *Janhit Abhiyan v. Union of India* (2022).

#### Article 16: Equality of Opportunity in Public Employment
- **Art 16(1):** Equality of opportunity for all citizens in employment or appointment to any office under the State.
- **Art 16(2):** No citizen shall be ineligible or discriminated against in public employment on grounds **ONLY** of **7 grounds**: **Religion, Race, Caste, Sex, Descent, Place of Birth, Residence**. *(Adds **Descent** and **Residence** to Art 15's 5 grounds).*
- **Art 16(3):** **Parliament** (NOT State Legislatures) may prescribe **residence** within a State/UT as a condition for certain classes of public employment (e.g., *Public Employment (Requirement as to Residence) Act, 1957* — now mostly expired except **Art 371D** for Andhra Pradesh & Telangana).
- **Art 16(4):** Enables State to make reservation in appointments/posts for any **backward class of citizens** not adequately represented in State services.
- **Art 16(4A):** Added by **77th Amendment Act, 1995** (reservation in **promotions** for SCs and STs); amended by **85th Amendment Act, 2001** (granting **consequential seniority** retrospectively from 17 June 1995).
  - *M. Nagaraj v. UOI* (2006) & *Jarnail Singh v. Lachhmi Narain Gupta* (2018): Upheld Art 16(4A), subject to quantifiable data on inadequacy of representation and administrative efficiency (Art 335).
- **Art 16(4B):** Added by **81st Amendment Act, 2000** — permits unfilled reserved vacancies (**backlog vacancies**) to be carried forward as a separate class and **excluded from the 50% ceiling** in subsequent years.
- **Art 16(5):** Incumbent of an office connected with a religious/denominational institution may be required to profess that particular religion/denomination.
- **Art 16(6):** Added by **103rd Amendment Act, 2019** — enables up to **10% reservation for EWS** in appointments/posts under the State.

> **Mandal Commission & *Indra Sawhney v. Union of India* (1992):**
> - **Second Backward Classes Commission (1979):** Headed by **B.P. Mandal** (report submitted **1980**); identified 3,743 socially & educationally backward castes (~52% population excluding SC/ST) and recommended **27% reservation** for OBCs.
> - **Implementation:** **1990** (V.P. Singh government) announced 27% OBC quota; **1991** (P.V. Narasimha Rao government) added 10% EWS quota (then without constitutional amendment).
> - **Supreme Court Judgment (9-Judge Bench, 1992):**
>   1. Upheld **27% OBC reservation** under Art 16(4), subject to **exclusion of "Creamy Layer"** (advanced sections among OBCs).
>   2. Struck down the 1991 10% EWS executive order (as Art 16(4) did not permit purely economic criteria — later addressed via **103rd Amend 2019**).
>   3. **50% Ceiling Rule:** Total reservation shall **not exceed 50%** (except extraordinary situations).
>   4. **No reservation in promotions** (overturned by **77th Amend, 1995**).

#### Article 17: Abolition of Untouchability
- Abolishes **"Untouchability"** and forbids its practice in any form; enforcement of any disability arising from it is a punishable offence.
- **Absolute Right:** No exceptions or reasonable restrictions; enforceable against both State and private individuals.
- **Note:** The term "Untouchability" is **NOT defined** in the Constitution or the Act. In *Devarajiah v. Padmanna* (1958), the Mysore High Court held it refers to historical social disabilities imposed on certain classes by reason of birth in a particular caste, not the literal/grammatical sense.
- **Statutory Enforcement:**
  - **Untouchability (Offences) Act, 1955** → comprehensively amended in **1976** and renamed the **Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955** (conviction disqualifies a person from contesting Parliament/State Legislature elections under RPA, 1951).
  - **Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes (Prevention of Atrocities) Act, 1989** (amended 2015, 2018).

#### Article 18: Abolition of Titles
- **Art 18(1):** State shall not confer any title on any citizen or foreigner, **except military or academic distinctions** (e.g., Param Vir Chakra, Doctorates).
- **Art 18(2):** Prohibits a **citizen of India** from accepting any title from any foreign State.
- **Art 18(3):** A **foreigner** holding any office of profit or trust under the State cannot accept any title from any foreign State without the **consent of the President**.
- **Art 18(4):** No citizen or foreigner holding any office of profit or trust under the State shall accept any **present, emolument, or office** of any kind from or under any foreign State without the **consent of the President**.
- **National Awards (*Balaji Raghavan v. Union of India*, 1996):**
  - Instituted in **1954** (**Bharat Ratna, Padma Vibhushan, Padma Bhushan, Padma Shri**).
  - Supreme Court upheld their constitutional validity, ruling they are **awards recognizing merit**, NOT "titles" prohibited by Art 18(1).
  - **Strict Condition:** Recipients must **NOT use them as prefixes or suffixes** to their names; violation can lead to forfeiture of the award.

#### Article 20: Protection in Respect of Conviction for Offences
*(Cannot be suspended even during National Emergency — 44th Amend, 1978)*
1. **Art 20(1) — Protection against Ex-Post Facto Law:**
   - No person shall be convicted of any offence except for violation of a law in force at the time of commission, nor subjected to a penalty greater than that prescribed at the time.
   - **Scope:** Applies **ONLY to criminal laws** (NOT civil liabilities or retrospective tax laws). Does **not** bar retrospective reduction of punishment (*T. Barai*, 1983).
2. **Art 20(2) — Protection against Double Jeopardy:**
   - No person shall be **prosecuted and punished** for the same offence more than once.
   - **Scope:** Requires **both** prosecution and punishment before a **court of law or judicial tribunal** (*Maqbool Hussain*, 1953). Departmental or administrative inquiries alongside criminal prosecution are **not** barred.
3. **Art 20(3) — Protection against Self-Incrimination:**
   - No person **accused of any offence** shall be compelled to be a witness against himself.
   - **Scope:** Applies only to criminal proceedings after formal accusation. Covers both oral and documentary evidence.
   - *State of Bombay v. Kathi Kalu Oghad* (1961): Taking **fingerprints, thumb impressions, handwriting specimens, or blood/DNA samples** is **NOT** testimonial compulsion and does not violate Art 20(3).
   - *Selvi v. State of Karnataka* (2010): Involuntary **Narco-analysis, Polygraph (lie-detector), and Brain-mapping (BEAP)** tests violate **Art 20(3)** and **Art 21**.

#### Article 21: Protection of Life and Personal Liberty
*"No person shall be deprived of his life or personal liberty except according to **procedure established by law**."*

| Landmark Shift | *A.K. Gopalan v. State of Madras* (1950) | *Maneka Gandhi v. Union of India* (1978) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Interpretation of Art 21** | **Narrow:** "Procedure established by law" (Japanese model) protects only against arbitrary **executive** action, not arbitrary legislative action. | **Expansive:** Overruled *Gopalan*; law must establish a **"fair, just and reasonable"** procedure — effectively reading in American **"Due Process of Law"**. |
| **Relationship of Arts 14, 19, 21** | Treated as mutually exclusive watertight compartments. | Established the **Golden Triangle (Arts 14, 19, 21)**: any law depriving liberty under Art 21 must also satisfy Art 14 (non-arbitrariness) and Art 19 (reasonableness). |

**High-Yield List of Expanded Rights under Article 21:**
- **Right to Privacy:** *Justice K.S. Puttaswamy (Retd.) v. Union of India* (2017, **9-judge bench**) — overruled *M.P. Sharma* (1954) & *Kharak Singh* (1962).
- **Right to Marry a Person of One's Choice:** *Shafin Jahan v. Asokan K.M.* (**Hadiya Case**, 2018); *Lata Singh v. State of UP* (2006).
- **Right to Clean / Pollution-Free Environment:** *Subhash Kumar v. State of Bihar* (1991); *M.C. Mehta* cases.
- **Right to Sleep:** *In Re Ramlila Maidan Incident* (2012).
- **Right to Livelihood:** *Olga Tellis v. Bombay Municipal Corporation* (1985).
- **Right to Shelter:** *Chameli Singh v. State of UP* (1996).
- **Right to Health & Emergency Medical Aid:** *Parmanand Katara v. UOI* (1989); *Paschim Banga Khet Mazdoor Samity* (1996).
- **Right to Free Legal Aid & Speedy Trial:** *M.H. Hoskot* (1978) & *Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar* (1979).
- **Right to Die with Dignity (Passive Euthanasia & Living Will):** *Common Cause v. Union of India* (2018) *(Note: Right to life does NOT include Right to Suicide — Gian Kaur v. State of Punjab, 1996)*.
- **Right to Travel Abroad:** *Satwant Singh Sawhney* (1967) & *Maneka Gandhi* (1978).
- **Right to Food** (*PUCL v. UOI*, 2001), **Right against Handcuffing/Solitary Confinement** (*Sunil Batra*, 1978; *Prem Shankar Shukla*, 1980), **Right to Reputation**, **Right to Electricity**.

#### Article 21A: Right to Education
- **Text:** The State shall provide **free and compulsory education** to all children of the age of **6 to 14 years** in such manner as the State may, by law, determine.
- **Inserted by:** **86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002** (which also amended **Art 45** for ages 0–6 and added the 11th Fundamental Duty under **Art 51A(k)**).
- **Judicial Precursor:** *Mohini Jain* (1992) and *Unni Krishnan v. State of Andhra Pradesh* (1993) declared education up to 14 years a Fundamental Right under Art 21.
- **Enabling Legislation:** **Right of Children to Free and Compulsory Education (RTE) Act, 2009** — came into force on **1 April 2010** (mandates 25% reservation for disadvantaged/EWS children in private unaided non-minority schools).

#### Article 22: Protection Against Arrest and Detention
| Dimension | Punitive Detention (Ordinary Law) — Arts 22(1)–22(2) | Preventive Detention — Arts 22(4)–22(7) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Purpose** | To punish a person for an offence already committed after trial | To detain a person **without trial** to prevent future offence |
| **Safeguards** | 1. Right to be informed of grounds of arrest<br>2. Right to consult & be defended by a legal practitioner of choice<br>3. ### 5.6 Right against Exploitation (Articles 23–24)

#### Article 23: Prohibition of Traffic in Human Beings and Forced Labour
- **Art 23(1):** Prohibits **traffic in human beings** (selling/buying humans, prostitution, slavery, *devadasis*), ***begar*** (compulsory work without remuneration), and other similar forms of **forced labour**.
- **Scope:** Available to both citizens and non-citizens; enforceable against **both State and private individuals**.
- *PUDR v. Union of India* (**Asiad Workers Case**, 1982): Paying **less than the statutory minimum wage** amounts to "forced labour" under Art 23.
- **Art 23(2) Exception:** State may impose **compulsory service for public purposes** (e.g., military or social service), provided it does not discriminate on grounds only of **religion, race, caste, or class** *(note: "sex" is omitted from this exception's prohibited grounds)*.
- **Laws:** *Immoral Traffic (Prevention) Act, 1956*; *Bonded Labour System (Abolition) Act, 1976*; *Minimum Wages Act, 1948*.

#### Article 24: Prohibition of Employment of Children in Factories, etc.
- **Text:** No child **below the age of 14 years** shall be employed to work in any **factory or mine** or engaged in any other **hazardous employment**.
- **Absolute Prohibition** in hazardous work (enforceable against both State and private employers).
- **Child Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Amendment Act, 2016** (amending the 1986 Act, renamed *Child and Adolescent Labour (Prohibition and Regulation) Act, 1986*):
  - **Complete ban** on employing **children (<14 years)** in **all occupations and processes** (exceptions: helping family/family enterprise after school hours or during vacations, or working as an artist in audio-visual entertainment/sports *excluding circus*, provided schooling is not affected).
  - Prohibits employment of **adolescents (14–18 years)** in **hazardous occupations and processes**.
- **Institutional Mechanism:** National & State Commissions for Protection of Child Rights (**NCPCR / SCPCR**) under the *CPCR Act, 2005*.

### 5.8 Cultural and Educational Rights (Articles 29–30)

- **Article 29: Protection of Interests of Minorities** *(Available ONLY to Citizens)*
  - **Art 29(1):** **Any section of the citizens** residing in India having a **distinct language, script, or culture** of its own has the right to conserve the same. *(SC in *T.M.A. Pai Foundation*, 2002 & *Ahmedabad St. Xavier's*, 1974 clarified that Art 29(1) protects **all sections of citizens** — both minorities and majorities — even though the marginal heading says "minorities")*.
  - **Art 29(2):** No citizen shall be denied admission into any educational institution maintained by the State or receiving State aid on grounds **ONLY** of **religion, race, caste, language** (or any of them). *(Notice: "sex" and "place of birth" are NOT in Art 29(2)).*

- **Article 30: Right of Minorities to Establish & Administer Educational Institutions** *(Available ONLY to Citizens)*
  - **Art 30(1):** All **minorities, whether based on religion or language** (only 2 types recognized; "minority" is **not defined** in the Constitution), have the right to establish and administer educational institutions of their choice.
  - **Art 30(1A):** Inserted by **44th Amendment Act, 1978** — if the State compulsorily acquires property of a minority educational institution, the compensation fixed must not restrict or abrogate the right under Art 30(1).
  - **Art 30(2):** State shall not, in granting aid to educational institutions, discriminate against any institution on the ground that it is under the management of a religious or linguistic minority.
  - *T.M.A. Pai Foundation v. State of Karnataka* (2002, 11-judge bench): Minority status is determined **State-wise** (with respect to State legislation); the right to administer does not include the right to maladminister.

### 5.9 Right to Property: Deletion & Present Status (Articles 31, 31A–31C, 300A)

- **Original Position (1950):** Protected as a Fundamental Right under **Art 19(1)(f)** and **Art 31**.
- **44th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1978:**
  - **Deleted** Art 19(1)(f) and Art 31 from Part III.
  - Inserted **Article 300A** in **Part XII** (Chapter IV): *"No person shall be deprived of his property save by authority of law."*
- **Current Status:** Property is a **Constitutional / Legal Right**, **NOT a Fundamental Right**.
  - Remedy: A person cannot move the Supreme Court directly under **Art 32** for violation of Art 300A; they can approach the **High Court under Art 226** or ordinary civil courts.
  - Protects against **executive action**, not against a valid legislative enactment.
- **Saving Provisions Still in Part III:**
  - **Art 31A (1st Amend, 1951):** Saves 5 categories of laws (acquisition of estates, taking over management of property, amalgamation of corporations, etc.) from challenge under Arts 14 and 19.
  - **Art 31B (1st Amend, 1951):** Validates Acts and Regulations placed in the **Ninth Schedule** against Part III challenges. *(In **I.R. Coelho v. State of Tamil Nadu**, 2007, SC held that laws added to the 9th Schedule after **24 April 1973** are open to Judicial Review if they violate the Basic Structure).*
  - **Art 31C (25th Amend, 1971):** Saves laws implementing **Art 39(b)** and **Art 39(c)** from challenge under **Arts 14 and 19**. *(SC in Kesavananda Bharati, 1973 struck down its second clause barring judicial review; SC in Minerva Mills, 1980 struck down the 42nd Amend expansion covering all DPSPs).*

### 5.10 Right to Constitutional Remedies (Article 32) & The 5 Writs

- **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:** Called Article 32 **"the very soul of the Constitution and the very heart of it."**
- **Art 32(1)–(4):**
  - Guarantees the right to move the **Supreme Court** by appropriate proceedings for enforcement of **Fundamental Rights only**.
  - Supreme Court is the **Defender and Guarantor** of Fundamental Rights.
  - **Art 32(3):** **Parliament** may by law empower **any other court** to exercise writ powers within its local jurisdiction (never exercised so far).
  - **Art 32(4):** Right under Art 32 cannot be suspended except as provided by the Constitution (Art 359 during Emergency).

#### Comparison of the 5 Prerogative Writs (Borrowed from English Law)

| Writ | Literal Meaning | Purpose & Scope | Against Whom Issued? | Key Exceptions / Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Habeas Corpus** | *"To have the body of"* | Protects personal liberty against **unlawful detention**; orders detaining authority to produce the body before court to examine legality. | **Both Public Authorities & Private Individuals** | Not issued if detention is lawful, for contempt of legislature/court, by a competent court, or outside court's jurisdiction. **Can be sought by any person** on detainee's behalf. |
| **2. Mandamus** | *"We command"* | Commands a public official/body to perform an **official/statutory public duty** that they have failed or refused to perform. | Public officials, public bodies, corporations, inferior courts, tribunals, **government** | **NOT** against private individuals, **President or Governors** (Art 361), Chief Justice acting judicially, discretionary duties, or contractual obligations. |
| **3. Prohibition** | *"To forbid"* | Issued by a higher court to a lower court/tribunal to **prevent** it from exceeding or usurping jurisdiction (**"Prevention is better than cure"**). | **ONLY Judicial and Quasi-Judicial authorities** | **NOT** available against administrative authorities, legislative bodies, or private individuals. Issued while proceedings are **pending**. |
| **4. Certiorari** | *"To be certified"* / *"To be informed"* | Issued by a higher court to **transfer a case** or **quash an order** already passed due to excess/lack of jurisdiction or **error of law apparent on the face of record** (**Both Preventive & Curative**). | Judicial, quasi-judicial, and *(since **A.K. Kraipak**, 1969)* **administrative authorities** affecting rights | **NOT** available against legislative bodies or private individuals. Issued **after** an order is passed (or during proceedings to transfer). |
| **5. Quo-Warranto** | *"By what authority / warrant?"* | Enquires into the **legality of a person's claim to a public office**; prevents illegal usurpation of public office. | Holder of a **substantive, permanent public office** created by statute/Constitution | **NOT** against ministerial offices or private offices. **No locus standi restriction** — can be sought by **any interested citizen**, not just an aggrieved person. |

#### Article 32 (Supreme Court) vs Article 226 (High Court)

| Feature | Article 32 (Supreme Court) | Article 226 (High Court) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scope of Writs** | **Narrower** — ONLY for enforcement of **Fundamental Rights** | **Wider** — For Fundamental Rights **AND** *"for any other purpose"* (ordinary legal rights) |
| **Nature of Right** | Itself a **Fundamental Right** (cannot be refused by SC) | **Discretionary** constitutional remedy (HC may refuse if alternative remedy exists) |
| **Territorial Jurisdiction** | **All over India** | **Within State/UT jurisdiction** (or outside if cause of action arises wholly/partly within its territory) |
| **Emergency Suspension** | Can be suspended under **Art 359** (except for Arts 20 & 21) | Art 226 for ordinary legal rights is not automatically suspended under Art 359 (though FR enforcement is barred) |

---

### 5.11 Articles 33, 34, 35 & Citizen vs Foreigner Matrix

- **Article 33 (Armed Forces & Discipline):** Empowers **Parliament** (exclusively, not State Legislatures) to restrict or abrogate FRs of members of Armed Forces, paramilitary forces, police forces, intelligence agencies (IB, R&AW), and civilian employees/telecommunication staff connected with them, to ensure proper discharge of duties and maintenance of discipline. Laws made under Art 33 cannot be challenged for violating FRs. *(Examples: Army Act 1950, Navy Act 1957, Air Force Act 1950, BSF Act, Police Forces (Restriction of Rights) Act 1966).*
- **Article 34 (Martial Law):** Empowers Parliament to indemnify any government servant or other person for acts done during the operation of **Martial Law** in any area within India. *(Note: **"Martial Law" is NOT defined** in the Constitution; it differs from National Emergency under Art 352 as it suspends ordinary law/courts in a local area under military administration).*
- **Article 35 (Exclusive Parliamentary Legislation):** Confers **exclusive power on Parliament** (and denies State Legislatures) to make laws for:
  - Prescribing residence requirements for public employment (**Art 16(3)**), empowering other courts to issue writs (**Art 32(3)**), restricting FRs of armed forces (**Art 33**), and indemnifying acts under martial law (**Art 34**).
  - Prescribing punishments for acts declared as offences under Part III: **Art 17** (Untouchability) and **Art 23** (Human trafficking / forced labour).

| Available **ONLY to Indian Citizens** | Available to **BOTH Citizens & Foreigners** *(except enemy aliens for Art 22)* |

## 6. Directive Principles of State Policy (Part IV: Articles 36–51)

### 6.1 Origin, Nature & Famous Descriptions
- **Source:** Borrowed from the **Irish Constitution (1937)**, which had copied it from the **Spanish Constitution**.
- **Historical Link:** Resembles the **"Instrument of Instructions"** enumerated in the **Government of India Act, 1935** (addressed to the Governor-General and Governors).
- **Goal:** Establish a **Welfare State** and achieve **Social and Economic Democracy** (complementing Part III's Political Democracy).
- **Non-Justiciable (Art 37):** Not enforceable by any court for their violation; **however**, they are **"fundamental in the governance of the country"** and it is the **duty of the State to apply these principles in making laws**.
- **Art 36:** Defines "State" with the same meaning as **Art 12**.
- **Famous Quotes (Frequently Asked in SSC):**
  - **Dr. B.R. Ambedkar:** *"Novel features of the Indian Constitution"*; *"Instrument of Instructions"*.
  - **Granville Austin:** DPSPs and FRs together are the **"Conscience of the Constitution"**.
  - **K.T. Shah:** *"A cheque on a bank, payable only when the resources of the bank permit."*
  - **T.T. Krishnamachari:** *"A veritable dustbin of sentiments."*
  - **Sir Ivor Jennings:** *"Pious aspirations"*.

### 6.4 Conflict & Harmony Between Fundamental Rights (Part III) and DPSPs (Part IV)

1. ***State of Madras v. Champakam Dorairajan* (1951):**
   - **Held:** In case of conflict, **Fundamental Rights prevail over DPSPs**; DPSPs are subsidiary to FRs. Parliament can amend FRs to implement DPSPs.
   - **Result:** Parliament passed the **1st Amendment Act, 1951** (Art 15(4)), **4th Amendment (1955)**, and **17th Amendment (1964)**.
2. ***I.C. Golaknath v. State of Punjab* (1967):**
   - **Held (11-judge bench, 6:5):** Parliament **cannot abridge or take away any Fundamental Right** even to implement DPSPs. FRs occupy a "transcendental and immutable" position.
3. **24th & 25th Constitutional Amendment Acts (1971):**
   - **24th Amend:** Declared Parliament has power to amend any part of the Constitution including FRs (**Art 13(4) & Art 368(3)**).
   - **25th Amend:** Inserted **Art 31C**, providing that (i) no law implementing **Art 39(b) & 39(c)** shall be void for violating **Arts 14, 19, or 31**, and (ii) no such law can be questioned in any court.
4. ***Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala* (1973):**
   - **Held (13-judge bench, 7:6):** Upheld the 24th Amendment and the **first part of Art 31C**, but **struck down the second part** (barring judicial review) as unconstitutional because **Judicial Review is part of the Basic Structure**.
5. **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act (1976):**
   - Expanded **Art 31C** to give primacy to **ALL DPSPs** over Fundamental Rights under Arts 14, 19, and 31.
6. ***Minerva Mills v. Union of India* (1980):**
   - **Held:** Struck down the 42nd Amendment's expansion of Art 31C as unconstitutional.
   - **Doctrine of Harmony / Balance:** The Indian Constitution is founded on the **"bedrock of the balance"** between Part III (FRs) and Part IV (DPSPs). They are like two wheels of a chariot; giving absolute primacy to one over the other disturbs the **Basic Structure**.
   - **Present Position:** **FRs enjoy supremacy over DPSPs**, **EXCEPT** that laws specifically implementing **Art 39(b) & 39(c)** can override **Arts 14 & 19** (under original Art 31C), subject to the **Basic Structure** test.

## 7. Fundamental Duties (Part IV-A: Article 51A)

### 7.1 Origin, Committee & Constitutional Status
- **Original Constitution (1950):** Contained **NO** Fundamental Duties (only Rights).
- **Source:** Borrowed from the **Constitution of the USSR (Soviet Union)** *(among major democracies, Japan's Constitution also contains citizen duties)*.
- **Swaran Singh Committee (1976):**
  - 12-member committee chaired by **Sardar Swaran Singh** set up during the Internal Emergency (1976).
  - Recommended **8 Fundamental Duties** in a separate chapter, along with **penalties/punishments** for non-compliance and a duty to **pay taxes**.
  - **Parliament's Action:** Rejected the tax duty and penalty recommendations, and enacted **10 Fundamental Duties** instead of 8.
- **42nd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1976:** Inserted **Part IV-A** containing **Article 51A** with **10 Fundamental Duties** (51A(a) to 51A(j)).
- **86th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2002:** Added the **11th Fundamental Duty** — **Article 51A(k)**.
- **Key Features:**
  - **Applicable ONLY to Indian Citizens** (NOT to foreigners).
  - **Non-Justiciable:** No direct legal sanction in the Constitution for their violation, though Parliament can enforce specific duties through ordinary laws.
  - Some are **moral duties** (e.g., cherishing noble ideals of freedom struggle) and some are **civic duties** (e.g., respecting the Constitution, National Flag, National Anthem).

### 7.3 Verma Committee (1999) on Legal Enforcement of Fundamental Duties
The **Justice J.S. Verma Committee** (*Committee on Operationalization of Fundamental Duties of Citizens*, **1999**) identified existing statutory laws that enforce various Fundamental Duties:
1. **Prevention of Insults to National Honour Act, 1971:** Punishes disrespect to the Constitution, **National Flag**, and **National Anthem** (links to **51A(a)**).
2. **Protection of Civil Rights Act, 1955:** Punishes offences related to caste and untouchability (links to **51A(e)**).
3. **Representation of the People Act, 1951:** Provides disqualification of MPs/MLAs for corrupt practices, including soliciting votes on grounds of religion or promoting enmity between different classes (links to **51A(e)**).
4. **Unlawful Activities (Prevention) Act, 1967:** Empowers declaring communal or secessionist associations as unlawful (links to **51A(c)**).
5. **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972** & **Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980:** Enforce protection of forests, wildlife, and environment (links to **51A(g)**).
6. **Criminal Law Provisions (IPC Sec 153A / 153B; now BNS):** Punish imputations and assertions prejudicial to national integration and communal harmony (links to **51A(c), (e)**).

## 8. Basic Structure Doctrine & Landmark Supreme Court Judgments

### 8.1 Evolution of the Basic Structure Doctrine
The phrase **"Basic Structure"** is **NOT mentioned** in the Constitution; it was propounded by the Supreme Court in ***Kesavananda Bharati* (1973)**.

1. ***Shankari Prasad v. Union of India* (1951):** Held that Parliament's amending power under **Art 368** includes the power to amend Fundamental Rights; a Constitutional Amendment is **not** a "law" under Art 13(2).
2. ***Sajjan Singh v. State of Rajasthan* (1965):** Reaffirmed *Shankari Prasad* (though Justice J.R. Mudholkar first hinted at "basic features" in his dissent).
3. ***I.C. Golaknath v. State of Punjab* (1967):** 11-judge bench (6:5) **overruled** *Shankari Prasad* & *Sajjan Singh*; held a Constitutional Amendment **is** a "law" under Art 13(2), so **Parliament cannot amend FRs**.
4. **24th Amendment Act (1971):** Inserted **Art 13(4)** and **Art 368(3)** to restore Parliament's power to amend any part of the Constitution, including FRs.
5. ***Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala* (24 April 1973):** Largest-ever **13-judge bench (7:6)** overruled *Golaknath*, upheld the 24th Amendment, and ruled that Parliament can amend any part of the Constitution **EXCEPT its "Basic Structure"**.
6. ***Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain* (1975):** Applied Basic Structure to strike down **Art 329A(4)** (inserted by **39th Amend, 1975**), holding **free & fair elections, democracy, and judicial review** as Basic Structure.
7. **42nd Amendment Act (1976):** Added **Art 368(4) & (5)** claiming unlimited amending power for Parliament with no judicial review.
8. ***Minerva Mills v. Union of India* (1980):** Struck down **Art 368(4) & (5)**, holding that **limited amending power** of Parliament is itself part of the Basic Structure.
9. ***Waman Rao v. Union of India* (1981):** Fixed **24 April 1973** (*Kesavananda* judgment date) as the cut-off date for reviewing laws placed in the **Ninth Schedule**.

**Key Elements Recognized as Part of the "Basic Structure":**
- Supremacy of the Constitution
- Sovereign, Democratic, and Republican nature of Indian polity
- **Secular** character (*S.R. Bommai*, 1994)
- **Federal** character (*S.R. Bommai*, 1994)
- **Separation of Powers** between Legislature, Executive & Judiciary
- Unity and Integrity of the Nation
- **Judicial Review** (*Minerva Mills*, 1980; *L. Chandra Kumar*, 1997)
- **Rule of Law** & **Golden Triangle (Arts 14, 19, 21)** (*I.R. Coelho*, 2007)
- **Harmony and Balance** between Fundamental Rights and DPSPs (*Minerva Mills*, 1980)
- **Free and Fair Elections** (*Indira Gandhi v. Raj Narain*, 1975; *Kihoto Hollohan*, 1992)
- **Independence of Judiciary** (*Supreme Court Advocates-on-Record*, 2015 — NJAC case)
- **Parliamentary System** & **Limited Amending Power of Parliament**
- **Effective Access to Justice** (*Anita Kushwaha*, 2016)

## 9. Union Executive (Part V: Articles 52–78; 88, 111, 123)

The Union Executive consists of the **President**, the **Vice-President**, the **Prime Minister**, the **Council of Ministers**, and the **Attorney General of India**.

### A. The President of India (Articles 52–62, 72, 111, 123)

* **Article 52:** There shall be a President of India (Head of the State, First Citizen of India, *de jure* / nominal executive).
* **Article 53:** Executive power of the Union is vested in the President; Supreme Command of the Defence Forces is vested in the President (**Art 53(2)**).

#### 1. Qualifications, Conditions & Oath
| Feature | Constitutional Provision & Key Details |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Qualifications (Art 58)** | 1. Citizen of India (by birth or naturalisation).<br>2. Completed **35 years** of age.<br>3. Qualified for election as a member of the **Lok Sabha**.<br>4. Must **not hold any office of profit** under Union/State Govt or any local/public authority *(sitting President, VP, Governor, and Union/State Ministers are exempted)*. |
| **Nomination & Deposit** | Proposed by **50 electors** and seconded by **50 electors**; Security Deposit: **₹15,000** (forfeited if candidate fails to secure **1/6th** of total valid votes polled). |
| **Conditions (Art 59)** | Must not be a member of Parliament or any State Legislature (seat deemed vacated upon entering office). Emoluments and allowances cannot be diminished during term. |
| **Oath / Affirmation (Art 60)** | Administered by the **Chief Justice of India (CJI)**, or in his absence, the **senior-most Judge of the Supreme Court** available. Oath: *"to preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law"*. |
| **Term (Art 56 & 57)** | **5 years** from date of entering office. Eligible for re-election **any number of times** (Art 57). Resignation addressed to the **Vice-President** (who immediately informs the **Speaker of Lok Sabha** under Art 56(2)). |

#### 2. Election of the President (Articles 54, 55, 71)
* **System (Art 55):** Indirect election via **Proportional Representation by means of Single Transferable Vote (PR-STV)** and **secret ballot** *(Anti-Defection Law / 10th Schedule does NOT apply to Presidential elections)*.
* **Electoral College (Art 54):**
  * **Included:**
    1. **Elected** members of both Houses of Parliament (LS + RS).
    2. **Elected** members of the Legislative Assemblies of all States.
    3. **Elected** members of the Legislative Assemblies of the Union Territories of **Delhi and Puducherry** *(added by **70th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992**, effective 1995)*.
  * **Excluded:**
    1. **Nominated** members of both Houses of Parliament and State Legislative Assemblies.
    2. **All** members (elected and nominated) of State Legislative Councils (*Vidhan Parishads*) and UT Legislative Councils.
* **Vote Value Formulas (Art 55):**
  * **Population Base:** **1971 Census** (frozen until the first census after **2026** by the **84th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2001**).
  * **Value of Vote of 1 MLA** = $(\text{Total Population of the State} \div \text{Total Elected MLAs in State Assembly}) \times \frac{1}{1000}$
  * **Value of Vote of 1 MP** = $\text{Total Value of Votes of all MLAs of all States} \div \text{Total Number of Elected MPs (LS + RS)}$
  * **Electoral Quota** = $\left(\frac{\text{Total Valid Votes Polled}}{2}\right) + 1$
* **Election Disputes (Art 71):**
  * All doubts and disputes are inquired into and decided exclusively by the **Supreme Court**, whose decision is **final**.
  * Cannot be challenged on the ground of any vacancy in the Electoral College (**11th Amendment Act, 1961**).
  * Acts done by the President prior to his election being declared void are **not invalidated**.

#### 3. Impeachment of the President (Article 61)
* **Ground:** Only **"violation of the Constitution"** *(phrase not defined in the Constitution)*.
* **Nature:** Quasi-judicial procedure in Parliament.
* **Step-by-Step Procedure:**
  1. Charges can be initiated in **either House of Parliament** (Lok Sabha or Rajya Sabha).
  2. Notice must be signed by at least **1/4th of the total membership** of that House, with **14 days' prior notice** in writing.
  3. Resolution must be passed by a majority of **not less than 2/3rd of the total membership** of that House.
  4. Sent to the **other House**, which investigates (or causes investigation of) the charges; the President has the right to appear and be represented.
  5. If the second House also sustains the charge and passes the resolution by a majority of **not less than 2/3rd of the total membership** of that House, the President stands removed from that date.
* **Crucial SSC Distinction (Electoral College vs Impeachment):**
  * **Nominated members of Parliament** do **NOT** vote in the election, **but DO participate** in impeachment.
  * **Elected MLAs of States & Delhi/Puducherry** **DO** vote in the election, **but do NOT participate** in impeachment.
* **Fact:** No President of India has ever been impeached.

#### 4. Vacancy & Acting President (Articles 62 & 65)
* **Expiry of Term:** Election must be completed **before** the expiration of the 5-year term.
* **Casual Vacancy (Death / Resignation / Removal):** Election must be held within **6 months**; the newly elected President serves a **full 5-year term**.
* **Order of Acting President (Art 65 & President (Discharge of Functions) Act, 1969):**
  1. **Vice-President** acts as President.
  2. If VP's office is also vacant, the **Chief Justice of India (CJI)** acts as President *(occurred once in 1969: Justice M. Hidayatullah)*.
  3. If CJI is unavailable, the **senior-most Judge of the Supreme Court** available acts as President.

#### 5. Powers of the President
* **Executive Powers:** All executive actions of GoI taken in his name (Art 77); appoints PM & CoM (Art 75), Attorney General (Art 76), CAG (Art 148), CEC & ECs (Art 324), UPSC Chairman & Members (Art 316), Governors (Art 155), Finance Commission (Art 280), SC/HC Judges (Arts 124, 217), SC/ST/OBC Commissions (Arts 338, 338A, 338B), and UT Administrators; establishes Inter-State Council (Art 263).
* **Legislative Powers:** Summons/prorogues Parliament & dissolves Lok Sabha (Art 85); addresses Parliament at start of first session after every general election and first session of each year (Art 87); calls Joint Sitting (Art 108); nominates **12 members to Rajya Sabha** (Art 80(3) — Literature, Science, Art, Social Service); decides MP disqualifications in consultation with EC (Art 103); gives prior recommendation for Money Bills, Art 3 Bills (state reorganization), and Financial Bill I; lays CAG, UPSC, and FC reports before Parliament.
* **Financial Powers:** Money Bills require prior recommendation; causes **Annual Financial Statement (Art 112)** to be laid; no Demand for Grant without his recommendation (Art 113(3)); holds **Contingency Fund of India (Art 267)**; constitutes Finance Commission every 5 years (Art 280).
* **Diplomatic Powers:** International treaties/agreements negotiated and concluded in his name (subject to Parliamentary approval); sends and receives Ambassadors/High Commissioners.
* **Military Powers:** Supreme Commander of Defence Forces (Art 53(2)); appoints Army, Navy, and Air Chiefs; can declare war or conclude peace subject to Parliament's approval.
* **Emergency Powers (Part XVIII):** National Emergency (**Art 352**), President's Rule (**Art 356 & 365**), Financial Emergency (**Art 360**).

#### 6. Veto Powers of the President (Article 111 & Article 201)
Under **Article 111**, when a bill is presented to the President, he may: **(i) give assent**, **(ii) withhold assent**, or **(iii) return the bill** (if not a Money Bill) for reconsideration.

| Veto Type | Meaning & Mechanism | Example / Fact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Absolute Veto** | Withholding assent to a bill; the bill ends and does not become an Act. | Used by **Dr. Rajendra Prasad** (PEPSU Appropriation Bill, 1954) and **R. Venkataraman** (MPs' Salary & Pension Amendment Bill, 1991). |
| **Suspensive Veto** | Returning a bill for reconsideration of Parliament. If Parliament passes it again (with or without amendments) by an ordinary majority, the President **must** give assent. | Returned by **A.P.J. Abdul Kalam** (Office of Profit Bill, 2006) — later passed again and assented. |
| **Pocket Veto** | Taking **no action** (neither ratifying, rejecting, nor returning) indefinitely because Art 111 prescribes **no time limit** *(unlike the US President's 10-day limit)*. | **Giani Zail Singh** exercised Pocket Veto on the **Indian Post Office (Amendment) Bill, 1986**. |

*(Note: **Qualified Veto**, which is overridden by a higher special majority, exists in the USA, **not** in India.)*

**Veto Power Across Types of Bills:**
| Bill Type | Absolute Veto | Suspensive Veto | Pocket Veto | Special Constitutional Rule |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Ordinary Bill** | Yes | Yes | Yes | Becomes obligatory to assent if passed again after return. |
| **Money Bill (Art 110)** | Yes *(rare)* | **No** | Yes *(rare)* | Cannot be returned for reconsideration. |
| **Constitutional Amendment Bill (Art 368)** | **No** | **No** | **No** | President **MUST** give assent (**24th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1971**). |
| **State Bill Reserved by Governor (Art 200/201)** | Yes | Yes | Yes | Even if State Legislature passes the returned bill again within 6 months, President is **NOT bound** to give assent. |

#### 7. Pardoning Powers of the President (Article 72)
Applies to sentences by a **Court Martial**, offences against a **Union Law**, and **all sentences of death** (even under State laws). Subject to limited judicial review if arbitrary or *mala fide* (*Maru Ram case, 1980*; *Epuru Sudhakar case, 2006*).

| Term | Exact Constitutional Definition |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Pardon** | Completely absolves the convict of both sentence and conviction, removing all punishments and disqualifications. |
| **Commutation** | Substitution of one form of punishment for a **lighter form** *(e.g., death sentence to rigorous imprisonment, or rigorous to simple imprisonment)*. |
| **Remission** | Reducing the **period/quantum** of sentence **without changing its character** *(e.g., 2 years rigorous imprisonment reduced to 1 year rigorous imprisonment)*. |
| **Respite** | Awarding a **lesser sentence** in place of the original due to a **special fact** *(e.g., physical disability of convict, pregnancy of a woman offender)*. |
| **Reprieve** | A **temporary stay** on the execution of a sentence (especially a death sentence) to give time to seek pardon or commutation. |

#### 8. Ordinance-Making Power (Article 123)
* **Condition:** Promulgated only when **either or both Houses of Parliament are not in session** and President is satisfied circumstances require immediate action (*44th Amendment Act, 1978* made satisfaction justiciable on grounds of *mala fide*; *R.C. Cooper case, 1970*).
* **Scope:** **Co-extensive** with the legislative power of Parliament (cannot be issued on State List matters unless Parliament has competence; subject to Fundamental Rights; **cannot amend the Constitution**).
* **Duration / Max Life:** Must be laid before both Houses upon reassembly; ceases to operate **6 weeks** after reassembly of Parliament (counted from the **later date** if Houses reassemble on different dates), unless approved earlier or disapproved by resolutions of both Houses. Since the max gap between two sessions is **6 months** (Art 85(1)), the **maximum life of an Ordinance** without parliamentary approval is **6 months and 6 weeks**.
* **Re-promulgation:** Repeated re-promulgation without placing before legislature is unconstitutional (*D.C. Wadhwa v. State of Bihar, 1987*; *Krishna Kumar Singh v. State of Bihar, 2017*).

### B. The Vice-President of India (Articles 63–71)

* **Article 63:** There shall be a Vice-President of India (2nd highest constitutional office; modelled on the US Vice-President).
* **Article 64:** Ex-officio **Chairman of the Rajya Sabha** (draws salary as RS Chairman, not as VP).
* **Qualifications (Art 66(3)):** Citizen of India, **35 years** of age, qualified for election as a member of the **Rajya Sabha**, no office of profit.
* **Oath (Art 69):** Administered by the **President** (or person appointed by him).
* **Term & Resignation (Art 67):** **5 years**; eligible for re-election (*Dr. S. Radhakrishnan* and *Mohammad Hamid Ansari* served two full terms; *Krishan Kant* was the only VP to die in office). Resignation addressed to the **President**.
* **Acting President (Art 65):** Acts as President during vacancy for a **maximum of 6 months** (until new President is elected). During this period, does **not** perform duties of Chairman of Rajya Sabha.

#### President vs Vice-President — Key Differences
| Parameter | President | Vice-President |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Electoral College** | **Elected** MPs + **Elected** MLAs of States & Delhi/Puducherry (Art 54). | **ALL MPs (elected + nominated)** of both LS & RS; **no State MLAs** (Art 66). |
| **Proposers & Seconders** | 50 proposers + 50 seconders. | 20 proposers + 20 seconders. |
| **House Eligibility** | Qualified for **Lok Sabha**. | Qualified for **Rajya Sabha**. |
| **Removal Procedure** | **Impeachment (Art 61):** Initiated in **either House**; passed by **2/3rd of total membership** of both Houses. | **Removal (Art 67(b)):** Initiated **only in Rajya Sabha** (14 days' notice); passed by **effective majority in RS** (majority of all the then members) and **agreed to by LS** (simple majority). No ground specified in Constitution. |

### C. Prime Minister & Union Council of Ministers (Articles 74, 75, 78, 88)

* **Article 74(1):** Council of Ministers (CoM) with PM at the head to **aid and advise** the President, who shall act in accordance with such advice.
  * **42nd Amendment Act, 1976:** Made CoM's advice **binding** on the President.
  * **44th Amendment Act, 1978:** Empowered President to return advice **once** for reconsideration; reconsidered advice is **binding**.
  * **Art 74(2):** Advice tendered by Ministers cannot be inquired into by any court.
* **Article 75 — Key Clauses:**
  * **Art 75(1):** PM is appointed by the **President**; other Ministers are appointed by the President on the **advice of the PM**.
  * **Art 75(1A) [91st Amendment Act, 2003]:** Total number of Ministers (including PM) shall **not exceed 15% of the total strength of the Lok Sabha**.
  * **Art 75(1B) [91st Amendment Act, 2003]:** An MP disqualified under the **10th Schedule (Defection)** is disqualified from being appointed a Minister.
  * **Art 75(2) — Individual Responsibility:** Ministers hold office during the **pleasure of the President** (practically means PM can remove a Minister).
  * **Art 75(3) — Collective Responsibility:** The CoM is **collectively responsible to the Lok Sabha** (*"swim and sink together"*). Passage of a No-Confidence Motion in LS forces the entire CoM (including RS Ministers) to resign.
  * **Art 75(4):** Oath of office and secrecy administered by the **President**.
  * **Art 75(5):** A person who is not an MP can be appointed Minister/PM, but ceases to hold office if not elected/nominated to **either House** within **6 consecutive months**.
* **Article 78 (Duties of PM):** Communicate all decisions of CoM on administration and legislative proposals to President; furnish info called for by President; submit any individual Minister's decision for consideration of CoM if required by President.
* **Article 88:** Every Minister has the right to speak and take part in proceedings of **both Houses**, joint sittings, and any Parliamentary committee of which he is a member, **but can vote ONLY in the House of which he is a member**.
* **Special Concepts:**
  * **Cabinet:** Word *"Cabinet"* appears in the Constitution **only once** — in **Article 352** (inserted by **44th Amendment Act, 1978**).
  * **Kitchen Cabinet (Inner Cabinet):** Informal, extra-constitutional body consisting of the PM and 2–4 trusted colleagues/advisors who influence major decisions.
  * **Caretaker Government:** Continues in office after dissolution of LS or resignation of CoM until a new government is sworn in; conventionally refrains from major policy decisions.

#### Prime Minister Milestones (High-Yield SSC)
* **1st & Longest-serving PM:** Jawaharlal Nehru (1947–1964; ~16 years 9 months).
* **1st Acting PM:** Gulzarilal Nanda (1964 and 1966).
* **1st Woman PM:** Indira Gandhi (1966).
* **PMs who were members of Rajya Sabha (4):** **Indira Gandhi** (1966), **H.D. Deve Gowda** (1996), **I.K. Gujral** (1997), **Dr. Manmohan Singh** (2004, 2009).
* **Youngest PM:** Rajiv Gandhi (40 years).
* **Oldest PM:** Morarji Desai (81 years; also 1st non-Congress PM & 1st PM to resign).
* **1st PM to die abroad:** Lal Bahadur Shastri (Tashkent, 1966).
* **PM who never faced Parliament:** Chaudhary Charan Singh (1979–80).
* **Shortest single tenure:** Atal Bihari Vajpayee (13 days in May 1996).

### D. Attorney General of India (Articles 76 & 88)

* **Status (Art 76):** **Highest Law Officer** of the country; part of the Union Executive.
* **Appointment & Term:** Appointed by the **President**; holds office during the **pleasure of the President** (no fixed tenure; remuneration fixed by President; conventionally resigns with CoM).
* **Qualification:** Must be qualified to be appointed a **Judge of the Supreme Court** (Citizen of India; HC Judge for 5 yrs OR HC Advocate for 10 yrs OR distinguished jurist).
* **Rights (Art 76(3) & Art 88):**
  * Right of **audience in all courts** in India.
  * Right to **speak and take part in proceedings of both Houses of Parliament**, joint sittings, and any Parliamentary committee of which he is a member, **without a right to vote**.
  * Enjoys all privileges and immunities of an MP (**Art 105(4)**).
* **Limitations:** Not a full-time government servant; **permitted private legal practice**, provided he does **not** advise/hold a brief against GoI, defend criminal accused without GoI permission, or accept company directorship without GoI permission.
* *(Note: Solicitor General and Additional Solicitors General are statutory/non-constitutional offices.)*

## 10. Parliament of India (Part V: Articles 79–122)

### A. Structure & Comparison: Rajya Sabha vs Lok Sabha (Articles 79–83)
Under **Article 79**, Parliament comprises the **President**, the **Rajya Sabha** (Council of States), and the **Lok Sabha** (House of the People).

| Feature | Rajya Sabha (Upper House / Art 80) | Lok Sabha (Lower House / Art 81) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Maximum Strength** | **250** (238 States & UTs + 12 nominated by President). | **550** (530 States + 20 UTs). *2 Anglo-Indian nominations under **Art 331** discontinued by **104th Amendment Act, 2019** (effective Jan 2020).* |
| **Current Strength** | **245** (225 States + 8 UTs + 12 nominated). | **543** (524 States + 19 UTs — *post J&K Reorganisation Act, 2019*). |
| **UT Representation** | **8 seats**: Delhi (3), Puducherry (1), J&K (4). | **19 seats**: Delhi (7), J&K (5), Ladakh (1), Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu (2), Chandigarh (1), Puducherry (1), A&N Islands (1), Lakshadweep (1). |
| **Nominations** | **12 members** nominated by President (Art 80(3)) from **Literature, Science, Art, Social Service**. | **Nil** (Art 331 lapsed via 104th CAA, 2019; SC/ST reservation under Art 330/334 extended till 2030). *(**106th CAA, 2023** added **Art 330A** for 33% Women's Reservation, effective after post-census delimitation).* |
| **Top 5 States by Seats** | **UP (31), Maharashtra (19), Tamil Nadu (18), West Bengal (16), Bihar (16)**. | **UP (80), Maharashtra (48), West Bengal (42), Bihar (40), Tamil Nadu (39)**. |

| **Election Mode** | Indirect election by elected MLAs via **PR-STV** (open ballot since 2003). | Direct election via **Universal Adult Suffrage (Art 326)** & **FPTP**. Voting age reduced 21 → **18 years** by **61st CAA, 1988**. |
| **Tenure** | **Permanent House** (not subject to dissolution); **1/3rd retire every 2nd year**; member's term = **6 years** (RPA 1951). | **5 years** (Art 83(2)); can be dissolved earlier by President; extendable during National Emergency for **1 year at a time** (max 6 months post-Emergency). |
| **Minimum Age (Art 84)** | **30 years** | **25 years** |
| **Special Exclusive Powers** | **Art 249** (Parliament to legislate on State List); **Art 312** (create All-India Services); **Art 67(b)** (initiate VP removal); approve Emergency if LS is dissolved. | Money Bills (**Art 110**); No-Confidence & Censure Motions (**Art 75(3)**); Voting on Demands for Grants (**Art 113**); Discontinuance of National Emergency (**Art 352**). |

### B. Qualifications, Disqualifications, Oath & Vacating Seats

* **Qualifications (Art 84 & RPA 1951):** Citizen of India; oath before EC-authorised person (3rd Schedule); Age ≥ 30 (RS) / ≥ 25 (LS); registered elector in *any* parliamentary constituency in India.
* **Disqualifications under Art 102(1):**
  1. Office of profit under Union/State Govt;
  2. Unsound mind (declared by competent court);
  3. Undischarged insolvent;
  4. Not a citizen / voluntarily acquired foreign citizenship;
  5. Disqualified under Parliamentary law (**RPA 1951** — e.g., conviction ≥ 2 years causes immediate disqualification per *Lily Thomas v. UOI, 2013*).
  * **Deciding Authority (Art 103):** **President**, acting strictly according to the opinion of the **Election Commission**; decision is final.
* **Disqualification on Defection — Art 102(2) & 10th Schedule:**
  * Inserted by **52nd Amendment Act, 1985**; amended by **91st Amendment Act, 2003** *(removed 1/3rd "split" exemption; retained only **2/3rd "merger"** exemption)*.
  * **Grounds:** (a) Elected party member voluntarily gives up party membership, OR votes/abstains contrary to party whip without prior permission (not condoned within 15 days); (b) Independent member joins any political party; (c) Nominated member joins a political party **after 6 months** of taking seat.
  * **Deciding Authority:** **Presiding Officer of the House** (Speaker in LS, Chairman in RS).
  * **Landmark Case:** ***Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu (1992)*** — SC held the Presiding Officer acts as a **Tribunal**, making his decision subject to **Judicial Review** (for *mala fides*, perversity, or violation of natural justice).
* **Oath (Art 99) & Penalty (Art 104):** Oath taken before President (or **Pro-tem Speaker** appointed by him). Sitting/voting without taking oath or when disqualified attracts a penalty of **₹500 per day** (Art 104).
* **Vacating of Seats (Art 101):**
  * **Double Membership:**
    * Elected to both LS & RS → must intimate choice within **10 days**, else **RS seat** becomes vacant.
    * Sitting MP elected to the other House → seat in the **first House** becomes vacant.
    * Elected to two seats in same House → must choose one, else **both** fall vacant.
    * Elected as both MP & MLA → must resign State seat within **14 days**, else **MP seat** falls vacant.
  * **Absence (Art 101(4)):** Absent without permission for **60 consecutive days** (excluding periods when House is prorogued/adjourned for >4 consecutive days) → House may declare seat vacant.
  * **Resignation:** Submitted to Speaker/Chairman (who may reject it if not voluntary/genuine — **33rd CAA, 1974**).

### D. Sessions & Parliamentary Proceedings

* **Summoning (Art 85(1)):** Done by **President**. Max gap between two sessions: **6 months** (3 regular sessions by convention: Budget, Monsoon, Winter).
* **Adjournment vs Adjournment Sine Die vs Prorogation vs Dissolution:**
  * **Adjournment:** Suspends sitting for specified time (hours/days/weeks) — by **Presiding Officer**.
  * **Adjournment *Sine Die*:** Terminates sitting for an **indefinite period** — by **Presiding Officer**.
  * **Prorogation (Art 85(2)(a)):** Terminates a **session** — by **President**. Pending bills do **not** lapse (Art 107(3)).
  * **Dissolution (Art 85(2)(b)):** Ends the life of **Lok Sabha** — by **President**.

#### Effect of Lok Sabha Dissolution on Pending Bills (Art 107(4) & (5)) — High-Yield Trap!
| Status of the Bill | Effect on Dissolution of LS |
| :--- | :---: |
| Pending in **Lok Sabha** (originated in LS or transmitted from RS) | **LAPSES** |
| Passed by **Lok Sabha**, pending in **Rajya Sabha** | **LAPSES** |
| Pending in **Rajya Sabha**, **not passed** by Lok Sabha | **DOES NOT LAPSE** |
| Not passed due to disagreement, and President has **notified Joint Sitting** before dissolution | **DOES NOT LAPSE** |
| Passed by both Houses, awaiting **President's assent** | **DOES NOT LAPSE** |
| Passed by both Houses, **returned by President** for reconsideration | **DOES NOT LAPSE** |

* **Quorum (Art 100(3)):** **1/10th** of total strength (including Presiding Officer) → **55 in LS**, **25 in RS**.
* **Lame-duck Session:** Last session of outgoing Lok Sabha after a new Lok Sabha has been elected.
* **Question Hour (11 AM – 12 PM):**
  * **Starred (`*`):** Oral answer; **supplementary questions allowed** (Green).
  * **Unstarred:** Written answer; **no supplementary questions** (White).
  * **Short Notice:** Urgent matter; notice **< 10 days**; answered **orally** (Light Pink).
* **Zero Hour (12 PM onwards):** **Indian innovation** (since **1962**); **not mentioned in Rules of Procedure**; informal time to raise urgent matters without prior notice.
* **Key Motions & Resolutions:**
  * **Closure Motion:** Ends debate and puts matter to vote (Simple, Compartments, Kangaroo, Guillotine).
  * **Privilege Motion:** Moved against a Minister/member for breach of parliamentary privilege (e.g., withholding/distorting facts).
  * **Calling Attention Motion:** **Indian innovation (1954)**; **mentioned in Rules**; draws Minister's attention to urgent public matter.
  * **Adjournment Motion:** **Lok Sabha only** (needs **50 members'** support); interrupts normal business for urgent public importance (≥ 2.5 hrs debate); carries an element of censure.
  * **Resolutions:** Substantive proposals submitted for House decision (always voted upon, unlike some motions).
  * **Youth Parliament:** Started on 4th All India Whips Conference (1962) recommendation to train youth in parliamentary democracy.

#### No-Confidence Motion vs Censure Motion (Lok Sabha Only)
| Feature | No-Confidence Motion (Rule 198) | Censure Motion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reasons Stated?** | **Need NOT state reasons** for adoption. | **MUST state specific reasons/charges**. |
| **Against Whom?** | Against the **entire Council of Ministers** only. | Against an **individual Minister, group, or entire CoM**. |
| **Support Needed** | **50 members** of Lok Sabha. | No fixed numerical floor to move (subject to Speaker's admissibility). |
| **Consequence if Passed** | **Entire CoM MUST resign** (Art 75(3)). | **CoM need NOT resign**, though morally damaged. |

### F. Budget & Funds of India (Articles 112–116, 266–267)

* **Annual Financial Statement (Art 112):** Constitution does not use the word *"Budget"*. *(Railway Budget separated in 1924 via Acworth Committee; merged back into General Budget in **2017–18** via Bibek Debroy Committee).*
* **Charged vs Voted Expenditure (Art 112(3) & 113):** **Charged** expenditure (emoluments of President, SC Judges, CAG, debt charges) is **non-votable** (only discussed); **Voted** expenditure is presented as Demands for Grants (**LS only**).
* **6 Stages of Budget:** (1) Presentation → (2) General Discussion → (3) Scrutiny by DRSCs → (4) Voting on Demands for Grants (LS) → (5) **Appropriation Bill (Art 114)** (authorises withdrawal from CFI) → (6) **Finance Bill** (legalises tax proposals within 75 days).
* **Cut Motions (Moved in LS during Demands for Grants; passage implies no-confidence):**
  * **Policy Cut:** Demand *"be reduced to **Re. 1**"* (disapproves underlying policy).
  * **Economy Cut:** Demand *"be reduced by a **specified amount**"* (seeks economy).
  * **Token Cut:** Demand *"be reduced by **Rs. 100**"* (ventilates a specific grievance).
* **Vote on Account (Art 116):** Advance grant made by LS pending passing of Appropriation Bill (traditionally 2 months = 1/6th of total estimates).

#### Three Funds of India
| Fund | Article | Source & Operation | Parliamentary Approval for Withdrawal? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Consolidated Fund of India (CFI)** | **Art 266(1)** | All revenues, loans raised, and loan repayments received by GoI. | **Yes** — Appropriation Act (Art 114) mandatory. |
| **Public Account of India** | **Art 266(2)** | Provident funds, small savings, judicial/departmental deposits (Govt acts as banker). | **No** — operated by executive action. |
| **Contingency Fund of India** | **Art 267(1)** | Created by Contingency Fund Act, 1950 (corpus raised to **₹30,000 crore** in 2021). At disposal of **President** for unforeseen expenditure. | **Post-facto** approval by Parliament required to recoup the fund. |

## 11. State Executive & Legislature (Part VI: Articles 152–213)

### A. The Governor (Articles 153–162, 163, 174, 200, 201, 213)

* **Dual Role:** Constitutional Head of State + Representative/Agent of the Centre.
* **Article 153 & 7th Amendment Act, 1956:** One Governor for each State; **7th CAA, 1956** enabled appointment of the **same person as Governor for two or more States** (salary shared per President's order — Art 158(3A)).
* **Appointment & Tenure (Arts 155–156):** Appointed by **President** by warrant (Canadian model); holds office during **pleasure of the President** (normal term 5 years; *B.P. Singhal v. UOI, 2010*: pleasure cannot be exercised arbitrarily). Resigns to **President**.
* **Qualifications (Art 157):** Citizen of India; completed **35 years**. *(Sarkaria Commission 1988 recommended: eminent outsider to the State, non-active in recent politics, consultation with CM, security of 5-year tenure).*
* **Oath (Art 159):** Administered by **Chief Justice of the State High Court** (or senior-most HC Judge).
* **Discretionary Powers (Art 163):** Explicitly provided in **Art 163(1)**; Governor's decision on discretion is final (**Art 163(2)**), subject to constitutional limits (*Shamsher Singh, 1974*; *Nabam Rebia, 2016*).
* **Reservation of Bills for President (Art 200 & Art 201):**
  * Under **Art 200**, Governor can: Assent, Withhold assent, Return once (if not Money Bill), or **Reserve for President** *(mandatory if bill endangers position of State High Court)*.
  * Under **Art 201**, President may assent, withhold assent, or direct return; even if repassed by State Legislature within 6 months, President is **not bound** to assent.
* **Ordinance Power (Art 213):** Max life **6 months + 6 weeks**; requires **President's prior instructions** if bill would have required President's prior sanction, reservation, or assent.
* **Pardoning Power (Art 161 vs Art 72):** Covers offences against State laws; **Governor CANNOT pardon a death sentence** (only President under Art 72 can, though Governor can suspend/remit/commute) and has **no power over Court Martial** sentences.

---

### B. Chief Minister, State CoM & Advocate General (Articles 163–167, 177)

* **Chief Minister & State CoM (Arts 163–164, 167):**
  * **Art 164(1):** CM appointed by Governor; other Ministers on CM's advice. Mandatory **Tribal Welfare Minister** in **Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Madhya Pradesh, and Odisha** *(Bihar excluded by **94th CAA, 2006**)*.
  * **Art 164(1A) [91st Amendment Act, 2003]:** Total Ministers (including CM) shall **not exceed 15%** of total strength of Legislative Assembly, **subject to a minimum of 12 Ministers**.
  * **Art 164(2):** Collective responsibility to **State Legislative Assembly (Vidhan Sabha)**.
  * **Art 164(4):** Non-member Minister must enter either House within **6 consecutive months**.
  * **Art 167:** CM's duty to communicate CoM decisions and furnish info to Governor.
* **Advocate General of State (Arts 165 & 177):**
  * Highest Law Officer of the State; appointed by **Governor**; holds office during **pleasure of Governor**.
  * **Qualification:** Qualified to be a **Judge of a High Court**.
  * **Rights (Art 177):** Right to speak and participate in proceedings of State Legislature (both Houses if bicameral) **without a right to vote**.

### C. State Legislature (Articles 168–212)

* **Art 168:** Governor + Legislative Assembly (*Vidhan Sabha*) [+ Legislative Council (*Vidhan Parishad*) in bicameral States].

| House | Strength & Exceptions | Election & Composition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legislative Assembly (*Vidhan Sabha*, Art 170)** | **Max: 500**, **Min: 60**.<br>**Exceptions:** **Sikkim (32)**, **Goa (40)**, **Mizoram (40)**; UT of **Puducherry (30)**, Delhi (70), J&K (90 elected).<br>*(1 Anglo-Indian under Art 333 discontinued by 104th CAA, 2019; 106th CAA, 2023 added **Art 332A** for 33% Women's Reservation).* | Directly elected by people (FPTP); term **5 years**. |
| **Legislative Council (*Vidhan Parishad*, Art 171)** | **Max: 1/3rd of Assembly strength**; **Min: 40**.<br>**6 Bicameral States:** **UP (100), Maharashtra (78), Bihar (75), Karnataka (75), Andhra Pradesh (58), Telangana (40)**. | Permanent House; **1/3rd retire every 2nd year**; member term **6 years**.<br>**Composition (5/6th elected + 1/6th nominated):**<br>• **1/3rd:** Local bodies<br>• **1/3rd:** MLAs<br>• **1/12th:** Graduates (3 yrs standing)<br>• **1/12th:** Teachers (3 yrs standing, secondary+)<br>• **1/6th:** Nominated by **Governor** (*Literature, Science, Art, **Cooperative Movement**, Social Service*). |

* **Creation / Abolition of Legislative Council (Article 169):**
  * **Parliament** may by law create or abolish a State Legislative Council if the **State Legislative Assembly** passes a resolution by a **Special Majority** (majority of total membership + 2/3rd present & voting).
  * Parliament passes this law by **Simple Majority**, and it is **NOT deemed a Constitutional Amendment under Art 368** (Art 169(3)).
* **Deadlock on Ordinary Bills (Art 197):** **No Joint Sitting** in State Legislature. Legislative Council can delay an Ordinary Bill for a maximum of **4 months** (**3 months** in 1st instance + **1 month** in 2nd instance). Money Bills (Art 198): **14 days** max delay.

## 12. The Indian Judiciary (Union, States, Subordinate Courts & Tribunals)

### A. Supreme Court of India (Part V: Articles 124–147)

* **Establishment:** Inaugurated on **28 January 1950** (replacing Federal Court of India, 1935). *1st CJI: Justice H.J. Kania; 1st Woman Judge: Justice M. Fathima Beevi (1989).*
* **Strength (Art 124(1)):** Originally 8 (1 + 7); currently **34 Judges (1 CJI + 33 Judges)** via **SC (Number of Judges) Amendment Act, 2019**.
* **Appointment, Collegium & NJAC:**
  * Appointed by **President** (Art 124(2)).
  * **First Judges Case (1981):** *Consultation ≠ concurrence* (executive primacy).
  * **Second Judges Case (1993):** *Consultation = concurrence*; CJI + 2 senior-most judges (**Collegium System** born).
  * **Third Judges Case (1998):** CJI + **4 senior-most SC judges**.
  * **Fourth Judges Case (2015):** SC struck down **99th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2014** & **NJAC Act, 2014** as unconstitutional (violated Basic Structure — Judicial Independence) and restored the Collegium.
* **Qualifications (Art 124(3)):** Citizen of India + (a) HC Judge for **5 years**, OR (b) HC Advocate for **10 years**, OR (c) Distinguished jurist in President's opinion *(no minimum age)*.
* **Tenure & Removal (Art 124(2), (4), (5); Judges Enquiry Act, 1968):**
  * Retirement age: **65 years**; resigns to **President**.
  * **Grounds:** **Proved misbehaviour** or **incapacity**.
  * **Procedure:** Motion signed by **100 LS members** or **50 RS members** → admitted by Speaker/Chairman → **3-member Inquiry Committee** (CJI/SC Judge + HC CJ + distinguished jurist) → if guilty, passed by **each House in the same session** by **Special Majority** (total majority + 2/3rd present & voting) → President removes. *(No SC Judge has ever been removed).*
  * **Post-retirement ban (Art 124(7)):** Barred from pleading/acting before any court or authority in India.
* **Acting, Ad-hoc & Retired Judges:**
  * **Acting CJI (Art 126):** Appointed by **President**.
  * **Ad-hoc Judges (Art 127):** Appointed by **CJI** (with President's prior consent & HC CJ consultation) from qualified **HC Judges** when quorum lacks.
  * **Retired Judges (Art 128):** Requested by **CJI** (with President's prior consent) to sit temporarily.
* **Seat (Art 130):** **Delhi** (CJI may sit elsewhere with President's approval).

#### Jurisdictions & Powers of the Supreme Court
| Jurisdiction / Power | Article | Key Scope & Details |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Original** | **Art 131** | Exclusive federal disputes: Centre vs State(s), or State vs State(s). *Excludes* pre-1950 treaties & inter-state water disputes (Art 262). |
| **Writ** | **Art 32** | Original (not exclusive) for **Fundamental Rights only** (5 writs: *Habeas Corpus, Mandamus, Prohibition, Certiorari, Quo-Warranto*). |
| **Appellate** | **Arts 132–136** | Constitutional (132), Civil (133), Criminal (134), Certificate (134A), **Special Leave Petition — SLP (Art 136)** from any court/tribunal *except military courts*. |
| **Advisory** | **Art 143** | President seeks opinion: **143(1)** (public importance — SC *may* refuse); **143(2)** (pre-Constitution treaty — SC *must* opine). Not binding. |
| **Court of Record** | **Art 129** | Judgments have evidentiary value; power to **punish for contempt** of itself (and HCs/subordinate courts). |
| **Review** | **Art 137** | SC may review its own judgments/orders. |
| **Curative Petition** | *Case Law* | Evolved in ***Rupa Ashok Hurra v. Ashok Hurra (2002)*** as final remedy after dismissal of Review Petition to prevent miscarriage of justice. |
| **Complete Justice / Law Binding** | **Arts 142 / 141** | Law declared by SC binding on all courts (**Art 141**); orders for doing complete justice (**Art 142**). |

* **Judicial Review:** Implicit in **Arts 13, 32, 131–136, 226**; part of Basic Structure (*Minerva Mills, 1980*; *L. Chandra Kumar, 1997*).
* **Public Interest Litigation (PIL):** Relaxes *locus standi*; pioneered in India by **Justice V.R. Krishna Iyer** and **Justice P.N. Bhagwati** (*Hussainara Khatoon, 1979*; *S.P. Gupta, 1981*).

### B. High Courts (Part VI: Articles 214–231)

* **Total High Courts:** **25** *(Delhi is the only UT with its own separate High Court, est. 1966)*.
* **Oldest HCs (1862):** **Calcutta** (oldest — July 1862), **Bombay**, and **Madras** *(Allahabad 4th, 1866)*.
* **Latest (25th) HC:** **Andhra Pradesh High Court** at Amaravati (**1 January 2019**).
* **7 Common High Courts (Art 231, 7th CAA 1956):**
  1. **Bombay HC:** Maharashtra, Goa, DNH & DD.
  2. **Calcutta HC:** West Bengal, A&N Islands.
  3. **Guwahati HC:** Assam, Nagaland, Mizoram, Arunachal Pradesh *(largest state coverage: 4 states)*.
  4. **Punjab & Haryana HC:** Punjab, Haryana, Chandigarh.
  5. **Madras HC:** Tamil Nadu, Puducherry.
  6. **Kerala HC:** Kerala, Lakshadweep.
  7. **J&K and Ladakh HC:** UT of J&K, UT of Ladakh.
* **Appointment, Qualifications & Tenure (Art 217):**
  * Appointed by **President** (consulting CJI, State Governor, and HC CJ).
  * **Qualification:** Citizen + **10 years** judicial office OR **10 years** HC Advocate *(no "distinguished jurist" category)*.
  * **Retirement Age:** **62 years** *(raised from 60 by **15th Amendment Act, 1963**)*.
  * **Oath (Art 219):** Administered by **Governor**.
  * **Removal:** Same as SC Judge. **Transfer (Art 222):** By **President** after consulting **CJI** (Collegium).
  * **Practice Ban (Art 220):** Retired permanent HC Judge can practice only in **SC and other HCs**.
  * *1st Woman HC Judge:* **Anna Chandy** (Kerala, 1959); *1st Woman HC CJ:* **Leila Seth** (Himachal Pradesh, 1991).
* **Writ Jurisdiction (Art 226 vs Art 32):** **Art 226 is wider** than Art 32 because HCs can issue writs for **both Fundamental Rights AND ordinary legal rights** (*"for any other purpose"*), whereas Art 32 covers **Fundamental Rights only**. However, Art 32 is itself a Fundamental Right (SC cannot refuse), while Art 226 is discretionary.
* **Art 215:** Court of Record; **Art 227:** Superintendence over all subordinate courts and tribunals in the State (except armed forces tribunals).

### C. Subordinate Courts, NALSA, Lok Adalats & Tribunals

* **Subordinate Courts (Arts 233–237):**
  * **District Judges (Art 233):** Appointed/posted/promoted by **Governor** in consultation with the **High Court** (7 yrs advocate experience if direct).
  * **Other Judicial Officers (Art 234):** Appointed by **Governor** in consultation with **SPSC** and **High Court**.
  * **Control (Art 235):** Vested in the **High Court**.
* **Gram Nyayalayas (Gram Nyayalayas Act, 2008; enforced 2 Oct 2009):** Mobile village courts at intermediate Panchayat level presided by *Nyayadhikari*; both **civil and criminal** jurisdiction; guided by **natural justice** (not strictly bound by Evidence Act).
* **NALSA (Art 39A & Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987):**
  * **Art 39A** (added by **42nd CAA, 1976**): Equal justice and free legal aid.
  * **NALSA** constituted under **1987 Act** (enforced **9 Nov 1995** — *National Legal Services Day*); Patron-in-Chief: **CJI**.
* **Lok Adalat ("People's Court"):**
  * Statutory status under **Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987** *(first held in Gujarat, 1982)*.
  * **No court fee**; award is equivalent to a **civil court decree**, **final and binding** with **NO appeal**; handles civil & **compoundable** criminal cases *(not non-compoundable offences)*.
  * **Permanent Lok Adalat (2002 Amendment):** Handles **Public Utility Services** up to **₹1 crore**; can decide on merits if compromise fails.
* **Tribunals (Part XIV-A: Articles 323A & 323B — added by 42nd CAA, 1976):**
  * **Art 323A (Administrative Tribunals):** Established **ONLY by Parliament**; exclusively for **public service matters**; e.g., **CAT (1985)** & SATs; no hierarchy.
  * **Art 323B (Other Tribunals):** Established by **both Parliament & State Legislatures** for taxation, labour, land reforms, elections, etc.; hierarchy allowed.
  * **Landmark Case:** ***L. Chandra Kumar v. Union of India (1997)*** — SC held that Tribunal decisions are subject to judicial review before a **Division Bench of the High Court**, as Judicial Review is part of the **Basic Structure**.

## 13. Centre-State Relations & Special Provisions (Parts XI, XII, XXI)

The Constitution of India establishes a **quasi-federal** system with a strong Centre (inspired by the **Canadian Constitution** and the **Government of India Act, 1935**). Centre-State relations are divided into three dimensions across **Part XI** and **Part XII**:

| Dimension | Constitutional Part | Articles | Core Scope |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Legislative Relations** | Part XI (Chapter I) | **Arts 245–255** | Territorial jurisdiction, 7th Schedule Lists, Residuary powers, Parliamentary legislation on State List |
| **Administrative Relations** | Part XI (Chapter II) | **Arts 256–263** | Executive directions, All India Services (Art 312), Inter-State Water Disputes, Inter-State Council |
| **Financial Relations** | Part XII | **Arts 264–293** | Distribution of tax revenues, Grants-in-aid, GST Council, Finance Commission, Borrowing powers |

---

### A. Legislative Relations (Part XI: Articles 245–255)

#### 1. Territorial Extent of Union and State Legislation (Art 245)
* **Parliament:** Can make laws for the whole or any part of the **territory of India** (States, UTs, and acquired territories). **Only Parliament** can make **extra-territorial legislation** (**Art 245(2)**) applicable to Indian citizens and their property anywhere in the world.
* **State Legislature:** Can make laws for the whole or any part of the State. State laws cannot operate outside the State unless there is a **sufficient territorial nexus** between the State and the object (*State of Bombay v. R.M.D.C.*, 1957).
* **Exceptions to Parliamentary Laws:**
  * **Art 240:** President can make regulations for Peace, Progress, and Good Governance in **Andaman & Nicobar, Lakshadweep, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, and Ladakh** (can repeal/amend any Parliamentary Act in these UTs).
  * **5th Schedule:** Governor can direct that any Parliamentary Act shall not apply or apply with modifications to a **Scheduled Area**.
  * **6th Schedule:** Governor of **Assam** (and President for **Meghalaya, Tripura, Mizoram**) can restrict/modify Parliamentary Acts in **Tribal Areas**.

#### 2. Distribution of Legislative Subjects (Art 246 & 7th Schedule)
Borrowed from the **Government of India Act, 1935**, the **7th Schedule** divides subjects into three lists:

| List | Constitutional Basis | Original Subjects | Current Subjects | Law-Making Authority | Key Exam Subjects |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Union List (List I)** | Art 246(1) | **97** | **100** | Parliament exclusively | Defence, Foreign Affairs, Atomic Energy, Railways, Banking, Insurance, Currency, Post & Telegraph, Census, Citizenship, War & Peace |
| **State List (List II)** | Art 246(3) | **66** | **61** | State Legislature (normal times) | Public Order, Police, Prisons, Public Health & Sanitation, Agriculture, Land Revenue, Fisheries, Markets & Fairs, Betting & Gambling, Local Govt |
| **Concurrent List (List III)** | Art 246(2) | **47** | **52** | Both Parliament & State Legislature | Criminal Law & Procedure, Marriage & Divorce, Forests, Education, Weights & Measures, Electricity, Economic & Social Planning, Population Control, Bankruptcy |

> **High-Yield Exam Fact — 42nd Amendment Act, 1976:** Transferred **5 subjects** from the **State List to the Concurrent List**:
> 1. **Education**
> 2. **Forests**
> 3. **Weights and Measures**
> 4. **Protection of wild animals and birds**
> 5. **Administration of justice**; constitution and organisation of all courts except the Supreme Court and High Courts.

* **Art 246A (101st Amendment Act, 2016):** Grants **simultaneous legislative power** to Parliament and State Legislatures for intra-state **GST**; **exclusive power** to Parliament for inter-state GST (**IGST**).
* **Repugnancy (Art 254):** In case of conflict on a Concurrent List subject, **Central law prevails** over State law. *Exception:* If the State law was reserved for the **President's consideration** and received **Presidential assent** (**Art 254(2)**), it prevails in that State — though Parliament can still override it by a subsequent law.

#### 3. Residuary Powers of Legislation (Art 248)
* Vested exclusively in **Parliament** to make laws (including imposing taxes) on any matter **not enumerated in the Concurrent List or State List** (e.g., Cyber laws, Space law, Gift tax, Wealth tax).
* *Contrast:* Under GOI Act 1935, residuary powers lay with the **Governor-General**; in the Indian Constitution, vesting them in the **Centre** follows the **Canadian model**.

#### 4. Five Situations Where Parliament Can Legislate on the State List

| Article | Trigger / Condition | Approval / Majority | Duration & Effect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Art 249** | **In National Interest** — Rajya Sabha passes a resolution declaring it necessary | Rajya Sabha by **not less than 2/3rd of members present and voting** | Remains in force for **1 year** (renewable indefinitely, 1 year at a time); ceases **6 months** after resolution expires. |
| **Art 250** | **During National Emergency (Art 352)** | Proclamation of Emergency | Operates during Emergency; ceases **6 months** after Emergency ends. |
| **Art 252** | **With Consent of Two or More States** passing resolutions requesting Parliament | Resolutions by **2+ State Legislatures** | Applies to consenting States (+ adopting States); can be **amended/repealed ONLY by Parliament** (e.g., *Wildlife Protection Act 1972*, *Water Act 1974*). |
| **Art 253** | **To Implement International Treaties / Agreements / Conventions** | Simple Parliamentary legislation | Parliament can override State List to honour global obligations (e.g., *Geneva Conventions Act 1960*, *TRIPS* compliance). |
| **Art 356** | **During President's Rule** in a State | Proclamation under Art 356 | Laws made by Parliament continue in force even after President's Rule ends until repealed/altered by State Legislature (**Art 357(2)**). |

---

### B. Administrative Relations (Part XI: Articles 256–263)

* **Art 256 (General Obligation):** State executive power must be exercised to ensure compliance with Parliamentary laws; Centre can issue binding directions.
* **Art 257 (Control of Union):** State executive power must not impede Union executive power; Centre can issue directions for construction/maintenance of **means of communication** of national/military importance and **protection of railways**.
* **Art 365 (Coercive Sanction):** If any State fails to comply with Union directions, the President can lawfully conclude that constitutional machinery has failed, triggering **President's Rule (Art 356)**.
* **Art 258 & 258A:** President (with Governor's consent) may entrust Union executive functions to a State (**Art 258**); Governor (with Centre's consent) may entrust State functions to the Union (**Art 258A**, added by **7th Amendment Act, 1956**).
* **Art 261 (Full Faith and Credit):** Full faith and credit throughout India to public acts, records, and judicial proceedings of the Union and every State.
* **All India Services (Art 312):**
  * **3 Services:** **IAS** (Indian Administrative Service), **IPS** (Indian Police Service), and **IFoS** (Indian Forest Service — created in **1966** under *All India Services Act, 1951*).
  * Parliament can create a **new All India Service** (including **All India Judicial Service**, added by **42nd Amend 1976**) **only if Rajya Sabha passes a resolution by 2/3rd of members present and voting**.
  * **Dual Control:** **Immediate control** vests with the State Government; **ultimate control** (dismissal/disciplinary major penalties) vests with the Central Government.

#### Inter-State Water Disputes (Art 262) & Inter-State Council (Art 263)

| Mechanism | Article | Enacted Law / Establishment | Key Constitutional & Operational Facts |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Inter-State Water Disputes** | **Art 262** | **River Boards Act, 1956** & **Inter-State Water Disputes Act, 1956** | Parliament may provide for adjudication of inter-state river disputes and **bar the jurisdiction of Supreme Court and all other courts** (**Art 262(2)**). *2002 Amendment:* Tribunal must be formed within **1 year**; award within **3 years** (+2 yrs max); award is **final and binding** (force of SC decree). |
| **Inter-State Council** | **Art 263** | Established in **1990** (Presidential Order, May 28, 1990) on **Sarkaria Commission** recommendation | **Chairman:** Prime Minister. **Members:** CMs of all States & UTs with Assemblies, Administrators of UTs without Assemblies, **6 Union Cabinet Ministers** (including Home Minister) nominated by PM. **Standing Committee** is chaired by the **Union Home Minister**. |

#### Zonal Councils (Statutory Bodies — NOT Constitutional)
* **5 Zonal Councils** were created under **Part III of the States Reorganisation Act, 1956** (on PM Jawaharlal Nehru's initiative to promote emotional integration and interstate cooperation).
* **Chairman of ALL 5 Zonal Councils:** **Union Home Minister** (ex-officio). **Vice-Chairman:** Chief Ministers of member States by rotation (**1 year** each).

| Zonal Council | Headquarters | Member States & Union Territories |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Northern Zonal Council** | **New Delhi** | Haryana, Himachal Pradesh, Punjab, Rajasthan, Delhi, Chandigarh, J&K, Ladakh |
| **Central Zonal Council** | **Prayagraj** (Allahabad) | Chhattisgarh, Madhya Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Uttar Pradesh |
| **Eastern Zonal Council** | **Kolkata** | Bihar, Jharkhand, Odisha, West Bengal |
| **Western Zonal Council** | **Mumbai** | Goa, Gujarat, Maharashtra, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu |
| **Southern Zonal Council** | **Chennai** | Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Kerala, Tamil Nadu, Telangana, Puducherry |

* **North Eastern Council (NEC):** Created separately by the **North Eastern Council Act, 1971** (effective 1972; **HQ: Shillong**). Covers **8 States** — Arunachal Pradesh, Assam, Manipur, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Tripura + **Sikkim** (added as 8th member in **2002**). **Chairman:** Union Home Minister; **Vice-Chairman:** Minister of DoNER.

### C. Financial Relations (Part XII: Articles 264–293)

* **Art 265:** *"No tax shall be levied or collected except by authority of law."*
* **Three Constitutional Funds:**
  * **Consolidated Fund of India / State (Art 266(1)):** All revenues, loans, loan repayments; withdrawal requires **Appropriation Act** by Parliament/Legislature.
  * **Public Account of India / State (Art 266(2)):** Provident funds, judicial deposits, small savings; operated by executive action without Parliamentary appropriation.
  * **Contingency Fund of India / State (Art 267):** Placed at disposal of **President** (Art 267(1)) / **Governor** (Art 267(2)) for unforeseen expenditure pending Parliamentary authorization. Corpus of Contingency Fund of India was raised from ₹500 crore to **₹30,000 crore** (Finance Act, 2021).
* **Distribution of Tax Revenues (Post-101st Amendment Act, 2016):**
  * **Art 268:** Duties levied by Union, **collected and appropriated by States** (e.g., Stamp duties on bills of exchange, cheques, promissory notes).
  * **Art 269:** Taxes levied and collected by Union, **assigned to States** (taxes on inter-state sale/purchase of goods other than newspapers; consignment of goods).
  * **Art 269A (101st Amend):** **IGST** — levied and collected by Union on inter-state supply, apportioned between Union and States on **GST Council** recommendations.
  * **Art 270:** Taxes levied and collected by Union and **distributed between Union and States** (Income Tax, Corporate Tax, CGST, Union Excise on non-GST items, etc.) based on **Finance Commission** recommendations.
  * **Art 271:** **Surcharge** on taxes under Arts 269 & 270 for Union purposes exclusively (proceeds NOT shared with States; **cannot be levied on GST**).
* **Grants-in-Aid to States:**
  * **Statutory Grants (Art 275):** Charged on Consolidated Fund of India; given to States **in need of assistance** on recommendation of **Finance Commission** (includes specific grants for Scheduled Tribes welfare & Scheduled Areas administration, especially Assam).
  * **Discretionary Grants (Art 282):** Both Centre and States can make grants for **any public purpose**, even outside their legislative competence (basis for Centrally Sponsored Schemes).
* **Finance Commission (Art 280):** Quasi-judicial body constituted by the **President every 5 years** (or earlier) consisting of a **Chairman + 4 Members**. Recommends vertical/horizontal tax devolution, principles for Art 275 grants, and measures to augment State Consolidated Funds for **Panchayats** and **Municipalities** based on SFC recommendations.
* **GST Council (Art 279A — 101st Amendment Act, 2016):**
  * **Chairman:** **Union Finance Minister**. **Members:** Union MoS (Revenue/Finance) + Finance/Taxation Ministers of all States & UTs with Legislatures.
  * **Voting Weightage:** **Centre = 1/3rd** of total votes cast; **All States combined = 2/3rd** of total votes cast.
  * **Decision Majority:** **3/4th (75%)** of weighted votes of members present and voting (**Quorum = 50%** of total members).
* **Borrowing Powers (Arts 292–293):** Union can borrow within India or abroad; States can borrow **only within India** and need Central consent if any Central loan/guarantee is outstanding (**Art 293(3)**).

### E. Special Provisions for Certain States (Part XXI: Articles 370–371J)

#### 1. Article 370 (Jammu & Kashmir) — History & Abrogation
* **Origin:** Adopted on **October 17, 1949** as a *"Temporary Provision with respect to the State of Jammu and Kashmir"* following the **Instrument of Accession** signed by Maharaja Hari Singh on **October 26, 1947**.
* **Special Status:** J&K had its own **Constitution** (adopted Nov 17, 1956; enforced Jan 26, 1957). **Article 35A** was added via Presidential Order (**C.O. 48 of 1954**) under Art 370(1)(d), empowering the J&K legislature to define "permanent residents" and confer exclusive rights in property, jobs, and scholarships.
* **Abrogation (August 5–6, 2019):**
  * **August 5, 2019:** President Ram Nath Kovind issued **Constitution (Application to Jammu and Kashmir) Order, 2019 (C.O. 272)** superseding the 1954 Order, extending all provisions of the Indian Constitution to J&K and amending Art 367 to interpret "Constituent Assembly" in Art 370(3) as "Legislative Assembly".
  * **August 6, 2019:** **C.O. 273** (under Art 370(3)) declared all clauses of Art 370 inoperative except a modified Clause 1 applying the entire Indian Constitution to J&K.
  * **Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019** (effective **October 31, 2019** — *Rashtriya Ekta Diwas*): Bifurcated the state into two Union Territories — **UT of Jammu & Kashmir** (with Legislative Assembly) and **UT of Ladakh** (without Legislative Assembly).
* **Supreme Court Verdict (*In Re: Article 370 of the Constitution*, Dec 11, 2023):** A 5-judge Constitution Bench (headed by CJI D.Y. Chandrachud) **unanimously upheld** the abrogation of Art 370 (ruling it was a temporary provision and J&K retained no internal sovereignty after accession) and the carving out of UT of Ladakh, while directing restoration of J&K statehood and Assembly elections.

## 14. Emergency Provisions (Part XVIII: Articles 352–360)

Emergency provisions are designed to safeguard sovereignty, unity, integrity, and constitutional stability. Structurally derived from the **GOI Act, 1935**, while suspension of Fundamental Rights during emergency is borrowed from the **Weimar Constitution of Germany**.

### A. Complete Comparative Table of the Three Emergencies

| Feature | National Emergency (**Art 352**) | President's Rule / State Emergency (**Arts 356 & 365**) | Financial Emergency (**Art 360**) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Grounds** | **War**, **External Aggression**, or **Armed Rebellion** *(44th Amend 1978 replaced "Internal Disturbance" with "Armed Rebellion")* | Failure of constitutional machinery in a State (**Art 356**) OR failure to comply with Union directions (**Art 365**) | Threat to **financial stability or credit of India** or any part of its territory |
| **Pre-Condition** | **Written recommendation of the Union Cabinet** (**Art 352(3)**, added by 44th Amend 1978) | Report from **Governor** or President's own satisfaction (*otherwise*) | President's satisfaction |
| **Parliamentary Approval Time** | Within **1 month** *(reduced from 2 months by 44th Amend)* | Within **2 months** | Within **2 months** |
| **Approval Majority** | **Special Majority** in both Houses (majority of total membership + 2/3rd present & voting — *44th Amend*) | **Simple Majority** in both Houses | **Simple Majority** in both Houses |
| **If Lok Sabha is Dissolved** | Continues till **30 days** from 1st sitting of reconstituted LS (if approved by Rajya Sabha meanwhile) | Continues till **30 days** from 1st sitting of reconstituted LS (if approved by RS) | Continues till **30 days** from 1st sitting of reconstituted LS (if approved by RS) |
| **Duration & Maximum Period** | **6 months** at a time; can be extended **indefinitely** with Parliamentary approval every 6 months | **6 months** at a time; **Maximum 3 years** *(beyond 1 year ONLY if National Emergency is in force AND EC certifies elections cannot be held — 44th Amend)* | Continues **indefinitely** once approved *(no repeated Parliamentary approval required; no max limit)* |
| **Revocation** | By President anytime; OR **Lok Sabha** passes resolution by **Simple Majority** disapproving it *(44th Amend; 1/10th LS members can call special sitting within 14 days)* | By President anytime **(no Parliamentary approval required)** | By President anytime **(no Parliamentary approval required)** |
| **Effect on State Executive & Legislature** | State govt & legislature **NOT suspended/dissolved**, but brought under complete Centre control; Centre can give directions on **any** matter | State CoM dismissed; **State Assembly suspended or dissolved**; President exercises State executive powers; Parliament/President makes State laws | Executive authority of Union extends to directing States to observe **canons of financial propriety**; State Assembly **NOT dissolved** |
| **Effect on Life of LS / Assemblies** | Parliament can extend term of Lok Sabha & State Assemblies by **1 year at a time** (max **6 months** after emergency ends) | No extension of Lok Sabha; State Assembly may be dissolved/suspended | No effect on tenure |
| **Effect on Fundamental Rights** | **Art 358** (Art 19) & **Art 359** (other FRs except Arts 20 & 21) | **NO effect** on Fundamental Rights | **NO effect** on Fundamental Rights (salaries of SC/HC Judges & govt staff can be reduced) |
| **Times Declared** | **3 times** (1962, 1971, 1975) | **130+ times** across States | **NEVER declared (0 times)** |

### B. History of Emergency Declarations & Records

#### 1. Three National Emergencies Declared in India
1. **October 26, 1962 – January 10, 1968:** Declared during the **India-China War** on the ground of **External Aggression** (PM: Jawaharlal Nehru; President: Dr. S. Radhakrishnan).
2. **December 3, 1971 – March 21, 1977:** Declared during the **India-Pakistan War** on the ground of **External Aggression** (PM: Indira Gandhi; President: V.V. Giri).
3. **June 25, 1975 – March 21, 1977:** Declared on the ground of **"Internal Disturbance"** while the 1971 emergency was still in force (PM: Indira Gandhi; President: Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed). Both 1971 and 1975 emergencies were revoked in March 1977.

#### 2. President's Rule (Art 356) Records & Landmark Judgment
* **First State:** **Punjab** (**June 20, 1951** — during ministerial crisis).
* **Most Number of Times:** **Manipur (10 times)** and **Uttar Pradesh (10 times)** jointly hold the record *(followed by Bihar & Punjab)*.
* **Longest Continuous Spell under Art 356:** **Punjab** (~5 years: May 1987 – Feb 1992, extended beyond 3 years via specific Constitutional Amendments — 59th, 64th, 67th, 68th).
* **Longest Cumulative Duration:** **Jammu & Kashmir** (including spells of Governor's Rule under Sec 92 of J&K Constitution + President's Rule).
* **States Never Under President's Rule (post-creation up to 2023):** **Chhattisgarh** and **Telangana**.
* **Landmark Supreme Court Case — *S.R. Bommai v. Union of India* (1994):**
  * Presidential proclamation under Art 356 is **subject to Judicial Review** (can be struck down if *mala fide* or based on extraneous grounds).
  * State Legislative Assembly **cannot be dissolved** before Parliament approves the Art 356 proclamation (only suspended).
  * **Floor Test** on the floor of the House is the only valid test of majority, not Governor's subjective assessment.
  * **Secularism** is part of the **Basic Structure**; a State govt acting against secularism can invite Art 356.

### C. Impact of 38th & 44th Amendments and Articles 358 vs 359

* **38th Amendment Act, 1975:** Made President's satisfaction in declaring Emergencies (Arts 352, 356, 360) **final, conclusive, and non-justiciable** (beyond judicial review); allowed multiple proclamations on different grounds simultaneously.
* **44th Amendment Act, 1978:**
  * Deleted the 38th Amendment's non-justiciability bar, restoring **Judicial Review** (*Minerva Mills v. Union of India*, 1980).
  * Replaced **"Internal Disturbance"** with **"Armed Rebellion"**.
  * Made **written Cabinet recommendation** mandatory (**Art 352(3)**).
  * Cut Parliamentary approval window for Art 352 from **2 months to 1 month** and raised majority from **Simple to Special Majority**.
  * Added **Lok Sabha simple majority revocation** power.
  * Restricted **Art 358** only to **War/External Aggression** and shielded **Arts 20 & 21** under **Art 359**.

#### Article 358 vs Article 359 (Crucial SSC Comparison)

| Basis | Article 358 | Article 359 |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scope of FRs** | Confined **ONLY to Article 19** (6 Freedoms) | Extends to **all Fundamental Rights specified in Presidential Order** *(EXCEPT Arts 20 & 21)* |
| **Mode of Suspension** | **Automatically suspends** Art 19 upon proclamation of National Emergency | Does **NOT automatically suspend** FRs; suspends **right to move court** for enforcement via specific **Presidential Order** |
| **Grounds Required** | Applies **ONLY during War or External Aggression** (**NEVER during Armed Rebellion** — *44th Amend 1978*) | Applies during **War, External Aggression, AND Armed Rebellion** |
| **Territorial Extent** | Operates across the **entire country** | May apply to the **entire country or any specific part** |
| **Duration** | Operates for the **entire duration of Emergency** | Operates for the **period specified in Presidential Order** (or duration of Emergency) |
| **Immunity for Laws** | Protects only laws containing a recital that they relate to Emergency (*44th Amend*) | Protects only laws containing a recital that they relate to Emergency (*44th Amend*) |
| **Non-Suspendable FRs** | Art 19 not suspended in Armed Rebellion | **Articles 20 & 21 can NEVER be suspended** even during Emergency (*44th Amend 1978*) |

## 15. Local Self-Government: Panchayati Raj & Municipalities (Parts IX, IX-A, IX-B)

* **Historical Roots:** **Lord Ripon's Resolution of 1882** (*Magna Carta of Local Self-Government*; Ripon is called the **"Father of Local Self-Government in India"**); Royal Commission on Decentralisation (1907); **Art 40 (DPSP)** directs the State to organise Village Panchayats; Community Development Programme (1952) & National Extension Service (1953).

| Committee | Year | Key Recommendations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |

* **Second State to Adopt:** **Andhra Pradesh** (**1959**).

### B. 73rd Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 (Panchayati Raj)

* **Enactment & Enforcement:** Passed in Dec 1992; received Presidential assent April 20, 1993; **came into force on April 24, 1993** (celebrated annually as **National Panchayati Raj Day**).
* **Constitutional Additions:** Inserted **Part IX ("The Panchayats")**, **Articles 243 to 243O**, and the **11th Schedule** containing **29 functional items**.

#### Compulsory vs Voluntary Provisions (73rd Amendment)

| Compulsory (Mandatory) Provisions | Voluntary (Discretionary) Provisions |
| :--- | :--- |
| 1. Organisation of **Gram Sabha** (**Art 243A** — all registered voters in village) | 1. Representation of **MPs, MLAs, and MLCs** in Panchayats at different levels |
| 2. **3-tier system** (Village, Intermediate, District — **Art 243B**; States with population **< 20 lakh** may skip intermediate tier) | 2. Reservation of seats/chairpersons for **Backward Classes (OBCs)** (**Art 243D(6)**) |
| 3. **Direct elections** to all seats at all 3 levels (**Art 243C(2)**) | 3. Method of election of Chairperson of **Village Panchayat** (direct or indirect) |
| 4. **Indirect elections** for Chairperson at Intermediate & District levels (**Art 243C(5)**) | 4. Devolving powers & responsibilities from the **29 items of 11th Schedule** (**Art 243G**) |
| 5. Minimum age to contest = **21 years** (**Art 243F**) | 5. Granting financial powers to Panchayats to levy, collect & appropriate taxes, tolls, fees (**Art 243H**) |
| 6. Reservation for **SC/ST** in proportion to population + **minimum 1/3rd (33%) reservation for Women** in seats & Chairperson posts (**Art 243D**) | 6. Manner of audit of Panchayat accounts (**Art 243J**) |
| 7. Fixed **5-year tenure**; fresh elections within **6 months** of dissolution (**Art 243E** — new Panchayat serves only **remainder period**) | |
| 8. **State Election Commission (Art 243K)** — State Election Commissioner appointed by Governor, removed ONLY like a **High Court Judge** | |
| 9. **State Finance Commission (Art 243I)** — constituted by **Governor every 5 years** | |

* **Exempted Areas (Art 243M):** Part IX does **NOT** apply to **Nagaland, Meghalaya, Mizoram**, Scheduled Areas (5th Schedule), Tribal Areas (6th Schedule), Hill areas of Manipur with District Councils, and Darjeeling Gorkha Hill Council (WB).
* **PESA Act, 1996 (*Provisions of the Panchayats (Extension to the Scheduled Areas) Act, 1996*):** Enacted on **Dileep Singh Bhuria Committee** recommendation; extended Part IX (with modifications) to **5th Schedule Areas in 10 States** (AP, Telangana, Chhattisgarh, Gujarat, HP, Jharkhand, MP, Maharashtra, Odisha, Rajasthan), empowering **Gram Sabhas** over minor forest produce, minor minerals, land alienation, and local institutions.

### C. 74th Constitutional Amendment Act, 1992 (Municipalities)

* **Historical Note:** First Municipal Corporation in India was set up at **Madras (1687/1688)**, followed by **Bombay & Calcutta (1726)**.
* **Enforcement & Additions:** Came into force on **June 1, 1993**. Inserted **Part IX-A ("The Municipalities")**, **Articles 243P to 243ZG**, and the **12th Schedule** containing **18 functional items**.
* **Three Types of Municipalities (Art 243Q):**
  1. **Nagar Panchayat:** For a *transitional area* (rural to urban).
  2. **Municipal Council:** For a *smaller urban area*.
  3. **Municipal Corporation:** For a *larger urban area*.
* **Ward Committees (Art 243S):** Mandatory for municipalities having a population of **3 lakh or more**.
* **Common Rules with Part IX:** **21 years** minimum age (**Art 243V**); **5-year tenure** + 6-month re-election (**Art 243U**); **1/3rd Women reservation** + SC/ST proportional reservation (**Art 243T**); **State Finance Commission (Art 243Y)**; **State Election Commission (Art 243ZA)**.
* **Planning Committees:**
  * **District Planning Committee — DPC (Art 243ZD):** Consolidates plans of Panchayats and Municipalities in the district; **4/5th** of members elected by and from elected members of District Panchayat and Municipalities.
  * **Metropolitan Planning Committee — MPC (Art 243ZE):** For every Metropolitan area (population **10 lakh or more**); **2/3rd** of members elected by and from elected members of Municipalities and Chairpersons of Panchayats in the metro area.

### D. Co-operative Societies (Part IX-B: Articles 243ZH–243ZT)

* **97th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2011** (effective Feb 15, 2012):
  1. Made the right to form co-operative societies a **Fundamental Right** under **Art 19(1)(c)**.
  2. Added **Art 43B (DPSP)** directing the State to promote voluntary formation, autonomous functioning, and democratic control of co-operative societies.
  3. Inserted **Part IX-B ("The Co-operative Societies", Arts 243ZH–243ZT)** — max **21 directors** on board, **5-year** term, reservation of **1 SC/ST seat** and **2 women seats** on every board, audit within **6 months** of FY end.
* **Supreme Court Ruling (*Union of India v. Rajendra N. Shah*, July 2021):** Struck down provisions of **Part IX-B** applicable to **state-level co-operative societies** for lacking ratification by half of the State Legislatures under Art 368(2); upheld Part IX-B **only for Multi-State Co-operative Societies (MSCS)**.

### B. Elections (Part XV: Articles 324–329) & Electoral Reforms

* **Art 324:** Superintendence, direction, and control of elections to **Parliament, State Legislatures, President, and Vice-President** vested in ECI *(Panchayat & Municipal elections are conducted by **State Election Commission** under Arts 243K & 243ZA)*.
* **Art 325:** **Single general electoral roll** for every territorial constituency; no person excluded on grounds of religion, race, caste, or sex.
* **Art 326:** **Universal Adult Suffrage** — voting age reduced from **21 to 18 years** by **61st Amendment Act, 1988** (effective March 28, 1989).
* **Arts 327–328:** Power of Parliament and State Legislatures to legislate on election matters (*Representation of the People Acts, 1950 & 1951*).
* **Art 329:** Bars courts from interfering in delimitation/allotment of seats; election disputes can be challenged **only via an Election Petition** (tried by High Court).
* **Delimitation Commission:** Statutory body under **Art 82** (after every Census) & **Art 170**; constituted **4 times**: **1952, 1962, 1972, 2002**. Its orders have the force of law and **cannot be questioned in any court**. Lok Sabha/Assembly seat totals are frozen at the **1971 Census** until **2026** (**84th Amend, 2001**), while internal boundaries were redrawn using the **2001 Census** (**87th Amend, 2003**).
* **EVMs & VVPAT:**
  * **EVM** first used on an experimental basis in **Parur Assembly constituency, Kerala (1982)**; first full state election in **Goa (1999)**; universal use in Lok Sabha elections from **2004**.
  * **VVPAT** (*Voter Verifiable Paper Audit Trail*) first used in **Noksen Assembly constituency, Nagaland (2013)**; 100% VVPAT used in **2019 Lok Sabha elections** (following *Subramanian Swamy v. ECI*, 2013).
* **NOTA (*None of the Above*):** Introduced in **2013** following SC judgment in ***PUCL v. Union of India* (Sept 27, 2013)**; first used in November–December 2013 State Assembly elections and 2014 General Elections.

### C. Anti-Defection Law (10th Schedule)

* **Origin:** Added by the **52nd Amendment Act, 1985** (under PM Rajiv Gandhi), inserting the **10th Schedule** and amending Arts 101, 102, 190, 191.
* **Grounds for Disqualification:**
  * **Elected Member:** (a) Voluntarily gives up membership of their political party; OR (b) Votes or abstains from voting in the House contrary to any **party whip** without prior permission, and such act is not condoned by the party within **15 days**.
  * **Independent Member:** Joins any political party after the election.
  * **Nominated Member:** Joins any political party **after the expiry of 6 months** from the date of taking their seat in the House *(allowed to join within 6 months)*.
* **Merger Exception:** No disqualification if a member's original political party **merges** with another party AND **not less than 2/3rd** of the members of the legislature party agree to such merger.
* **91st Amendment Act, 2003:**
  * **Deleted** the **1/3rd "split" exception** (Paragraph 3 of 10th Schedule).
  * Capped total size of Council of Ministers (including PM/CM) at **15%** of total strength of Lok Sabha / State Assembly (**minimum 12** ministers in States).
  * Barred disqualified defectors from being appointed as Ministers or holding remunerative political posts.
* **Deciding Authority & Judicial Review:** Decided by the **Presiding Officer** of the House (Speaker/Chairman).
  * ***Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu* (1992):** Presiding Officer acts as a **Tribunal**, so their decision is **subject to Judicial Review** (though courts cannot intervene before a decision is made).
  * ***Keisham Meghachandra Singh v. Speaker, Manipur* (2020):** SC directed Speakers to decide disqualification petitions within **3 months** unless exceptional circumstances exist.

**Q1. Which Constitutional Amendment Act reduced the voting age from 21 years to 18 years?** *(SSC CGL 2017 / CHSL 2020)*

* (B) 44th Amendment Act
* (C) 61st Amendment Act
* (D) 73rd Amendment Act
* **Answer:** **(C) 61st Amendment Act**

**Q2. Under which Article of the Constitution can the President declare a Financial Emergency?** *(SSC CHSL 2019 / CGL 2022)*
* (A) Article 352
* (B) Article 356
* (C) Article 360
* (D) Article 365
* **Answer:** **(C) Article 360**
* **Explanation:** **Article 360** empowers the President to proclaim a Financial Emergency if financial stability or credit of India is threatened. It has **never been declared** in India.

* (A) Andhra Pradesh
* (B) Rajasthan
* (C) Maharashtra
* (D) Gujarat
* **Answer:** **(B) Rajasthan**
* **Explanation:** On **October 2, 1959**, PM Jawaharlal Nehru inaugurated India's first Panchayati Raj system in **Nagaur district, Rajasthan** (based on the Balwant Rai Mehta Committee, 1957). Andhra Pradesh was the second state (1959).

**Q4. Which two Fundamental Rights can NEVER be suspended even during a National Emergency?** *(SSC CGL 2018 / CHSL 2021)*
* (A) Articles 14 and 19
* (B) Articles 19 and 21
* (C) Articles 20 and 21
* (D) Articles 21 and 32
* **Answer:** **(C) Articles 20 and 21**
* **Explanation:** The **44th Amendment Act, 1978** amended **Article 359** so that the President cannot suspend the right to move any court for enforcement of **Article 20** (protection in respect of conviction) and **Article 21** (life and personal liberty).

**Q5. How many items/subjects are included in the 11th Schedule of the Constitution (Panchayati Raj)?** *(SSC CGL 2020 / CHSL 2022)*
* (A) 18 items
* (B) 22 items
* (C) 29 items
* (D) 32 items
* **Answer:** **(C) 29 items**
* **Explanation:** The **73rd Amendment Act, 1992** added the **11th Schedule** with **29 functional items** for Panchayats (**Art 243G**), whereas the **74th Amendment** added the **12th Schedule** with **18 items** for Municipalities.

**Q6. Which Constitutional Amendment gave constitutional status to the National Commission for Backward Classes (NCBC)?** *(SSC CGL 2021 / CHSL 2022)*
* (A) 100th Amendment Act
* (B) 101st Amendment Act
* (C) 102nd Amendment Act
* (D) 103rd Amendment Act
* **Answer:** **(C) 102nd Amendment Act**
* **Explanation:** The **102nd Amendment Act, 2018** inserted **Article 338B** granting constitutional status to NCBC (previously a statutory body created in 1993) and inserted **Article 342A**.

**Q7. Who is the ex-officio Chairman of all five Zonal Councils in India?** *(SSC CGL 2019 / CPO 2020)*

* **Answer:** **(C) Union Home Minister**
* **Explanation:** Under the **States Reorganisation Act, 1956**, the **Union Home Minister** is the common Chairman of all five statutory Zonal Councils (Northern, Central, Eastern, Western, Southern).

**Q8. Which Article empowers Parliament to legislate on a State List subject in the "national interest" if Rajya Sabha passes a resolution?** *(SSC CGL 2021 / CHSL 2023)*
* (A) Article 248
* (B) Article 249
* (C) Article 252
* (D) Article 253
* **Answer:** **(B) Article 249**
* **Explanation:** Under **Article 249**, if **Rajya Sabha** passes a resolution by **not less than 2/3rd of members present and voting** declaring it necessary in the national interest, Parliament can legislate on a State List matter for **1 year** at a time.

**Q9. Which Constitutional Amendment added the 10th Schedule (Anti-Defection Law) to the Indian Constitution?** *(SSC GD 2021 / CHSL 2020)*

* (D) 91st Amendment Act, 2003
* **Answer:** **(B) 52nd Amendment Act, 1985**
* **Explanation:** The **52nd Amendment Act, 1985** introduced the **10th Schedule** to curb political defections. Later, the **91st Amendment Act, 2003** removed the 1/3rd split exemption.

**Q15. Which of the following states has special provisions under Article 371A of the Constitution?** *(SSC CGL 2022 / CHSL 2023)*

**Q21. Who removes the Chairman and members of a State Public Service Commission (SPSC)?** *(SSC CGL 2017 / CHSL 2022)*

**Q23. Who among the following is known as the "Guardian of the Public Purse" in India?** *(SSC CGL 2019 / CHSL 2021)*

**Q24. Which of the following statements about the Attorney General of India (Art 76) is INCORRECT?** *(SSC CGL 2022)*

**Q30. Under Article 352, a National Emergency can be proclaimed on the ground of "Armed Rebellion". Which Amendment replaced "Internal Disturbance" with "Armed Rebellion"?** *(SSC CGL 2020 / GD 2022)*
* (A) 38th Amendment Act, 1975
* (B) 42nd Amendment Act, 1976
* (C) 44th Amendment Act, 1978
* (D) 52nd Amendment Act, 1985
* **Answer:** **(C) 44th Amendment Act, 1978**
* **Explanation:** To prevent misuse like the 1975 emergency, the **44th Amendment Act, 1978** replaced the vague term "Internal Disturbance" with **"Armed Rebellion"** and required written Cabinet recommendation.

**Q31. Within how many days must a National Emergency proclamation be approved by both Houses of Parliament?** *(SSC CHSL 2021 / CGL 2023)*
* (A) 14 days
* (B) 1 month
* (C) 2 months
* (D) 6 months
* **Answer:** **(B) 1 month**
* **Explanation:** The **44th Amendment Act, 1978** reduced the Parliamentary approval time for National Emergency (Art 352) from 2 months to **1 month** (by Special Majority). For President's Rule (Art 356) and Financial Emergency (Art 360), the limit remains **2 months**.

**Q32. In which landmark case did the Supreme Court rule that President's Rule under Article 356 is subject to Judicial Review?** *(SSC CGL 2021 / CPO 2022)*
* (A) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973)
* (B) Minerva Mills v. Union of India (1980)
* (C) S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)
* (D) Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu (1992)
* **Answer:** **(C) S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994)**
* **Explanation:** In ***S.R. Bommai (1994)***, a 9-judge SC bench held that Art 356 proclamations are justiciable, the Assembly cannot be dissolved before Parliamentary ratification, and a **floor test** is mandatory to prove majority.

**Q34. Which Constitutional Amendment introduced 10% reservation for Economically Weaker Sections (EWS)?** *(SSC CGL 2020 / CHSL 2022)*

**Q35. The 106th Constitutional Amendment Act, 2023 (Nari Shakti Vandan Adhiniyam) provides what percentage of reservation for women in Lok Sabha and State Assemblies?** *(SSC CGL / CHSL 2023–24 Pattern)*

**Q36. Which Constitutional Amendment was struck down as unconstitutional by the Supreme Court in 2015?** *(SSC CGL 2019 / CHSL 2022)*
