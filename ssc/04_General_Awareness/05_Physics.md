## Mechanics: Motion, Force & Newton's Laws

### Scalar vs Vector Quantities
- **Scalar (Magnitude only):** Mass, Distance, Speed, Time, Temperature, Work, Energy, Power, Pressure, Electric Current.
- **Vector (Magnitude + Direction):** Displacement, Velocity, Acceleration, Force, Momentum, Weight, Electric Field, Magnetic Field.

### Equations of Linear Motion (Constant Acceleration $a$)
1. $v = u + at$
2. $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$
3. $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$
*(Where $u$ = initial velocity, $v$ = final velocity, $t$ = time, $s$ = displacement)*

### Newton's Three Laws of Motion
```
1. First Law (Law of Inertia):
   "An object remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted upon by an external net force."
   Examples: 
   - Passenger falls backward when a bus starts suddenly (Inertia of rest).
   - Passenger falls forward when a moving bus brakes suddenly (Inertia of motion).
   - Dust particles fall off when a carpet is beaten with a stick.

2. Second Law (Law of Force & Momentum):
   "Force is directly proportional to the rate of change of linear momentum."
   Formula: F = ma  (Momentum p = mv)
   Examples:
   - A cricket fielder pulls his hands backwards while catching a ball (increases contact time, decreases impulsive force).

3. Third Law (Action-Reaction):
   "To every action, there is an equal and opposite reaction."
   Examples:
   - Recoil of a gun when a bullet is fired.
   - Propulsion of rockets and jet planes.
   - Swimming (pushing water backward to move forward).
   - Walking on ground (pushing ground backward).
```

## Gravitation & Planetary Motion

### Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation
$$F = G \frac{m_1 m_2}{r^2}$$
- $G$ = Universal Gravitational Constant = $6.674 \times 10^{-11} \text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{kg}^2$ (Value discovered by Henry Cavendish).
- Acceleration due to gravity on Earth's surface: $g = \frac{GM}{R^2} \approx 9.8 \text{ m/s}^2$.

### Variations in $g$:
- $g$ is **maximum at the poles** (due to smaller radius and zero centrifugal force).
- $g$ is **minimum at the equator** (larger radius and maximum centrifugal force).
- $g$ **decreases** as we go above the surface of Earth or deep below into Earth.
- $g$ is **zero at the center of the Earth**.
- On the Moon, gravity is $\frac{1}{6}\text{th}$ of Earth's gravity ($g_{\text{moon}} = \frac{g}{6}$).
- **Mass remains constant** everywhere in the universe; **Weight ($W = mg$) changes** with $g$.

### Escape Velocity ($v_e$)
- Minimum velocity required for an object to escape the gravitational pull of a celestial body.
- Formula: $v_e = \sqrt{2gR}$
- Earth's escape velocity: **$11.2\text{ km/s}$**.
- Moon's escape velocity: **$2.38\text{ km/s}$** (too low to hold an atmosphere $\rightarrow$ reason Moon has no atmosphere!).

### Kepler's Laws of Planetary Motion
1. **Law of Orbits:** All planets revolve around the Sun in elliptical orbits with the Sun at one focus.
2. **Law of Areas:** A line joining a planet and the Sun sweeps out equal areas in equal intervals of time (areal velocity is constant; implies conservation of angular momentum).
3. **Law of Periods:** $T^2 \propto R^3$ (The square of the orbital period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis).

## Work, Energy, Power & Friction

- **Work:** $W = F \cdot s \cdot \cos\theta$.
  - Work done is **zero** when force is perpendicular to displacement ($\theta = 90^\circ$), e.g., a coolie carrying luggage on his head walking on a flat platform, or a satellite revolving in a circular orbit.
- **Kinetic Energy:** $KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{p^2}{2m}$. (If momentum doubles, KE quadruples!).
- **Potential Energy:** $PE = mgh$.
- **Friction:** Rolling friction < Sliding friction < Static friction. Ball bearings convert sliding friction into rolling friction to reduce energy loss.

## Properties of Matter: Pressure, Surface Tension & Viscosity

### Pressure & Pascal's Law
- **Atmospheric Pressure:** Measured by **Barometer** (invented by Torricelli).
  - Sudden fall in barometer reading $\rightarrow$ **Storm/cyclone**.
  - Slow fall in barometer reading $\rightarrow$ **Rain**.
  - Slow rise in barometer reading $\rightarrow$ **Clear, dry weather**.
- **Pascal's Principle:** Hydraulic lifts, hydraulic brakes, and hydraulic presses work on Pascal's law.

### Archimedes' Principle & Buoyancy
- When a body is immersed fully or partially in a fluid, it experiences an upward buoyant force equal to the weight of fluid displaced.
- **Lactometer** (purity of milk) and **Hydrometer** (density of liquids) work on Archimedes' Principle.
- Floating of an ice cube in water: **$\frac{9}{10}\text{th}$** of ice remains submerged, only **$\frac{1}{10}\text{th}$** is visible above the water surface.

### Surface Tension & Capillarity
- **Surface Tension:** Tendency of liquid surfaces to shrink into minimum surface area.
  - Raindrops are **spherical** due to surface tension.
  - Detergent/soap lowers surface tension of water, helping clean clothes better.
  - Mosquito larvae drown when oil is sprayed on stagnant water because surface tension decreases.
- **Capillarity:** Rise or fall of liquid in a narrow tube.
  - Kerosene/oil rising in the wick of a lamp.
  - Blotting paper absorbing ink.
  - Roots of plants drawing water from soil.

## Heat & Thermodynamics

### Temperature Scales & Conversions
$$\frac{C}{5} = \frac{F - 32}{9} = \frac{K - 273.15}{5}$$
- Temperature where Celsius and Fahrenheit scales read the same: **$-40^\circ$** (i.e., $-40^\circ\text{C} = -40^\circ\text{F}$).
- **Absolute Zero:** $0\text{ K} = -273.15^\circ\text{C}$ (molecular motion completely ceases).
- Normal human body temperature: $37^\circ\text{C} = 98.6^\circ\text{F} = 310.15\text{ K}$.

### Anomalous Expansion of Water
- Water contracts when heated from $0^\circ\text{C}$ to $4^\circ\text{C}$, and expands beyond $4^\circ\text{C}$.
- **Density of water is maximum at $4^\circ\text{C}$** (volume is minimum).
- *Biological Significance:* Lakes freeze only on the surface; water beneath remains liquid at $4^\circ\text{C}$, preserving aquatic life in winter.

### Heat Transfer Modes
1. **Conduction:** Transfer through solids without actual movement of particles (e.g., cooking utensil heating).
2. **Convection:** Transfer in fluids (liquids & gases) through actual mass movement of particles (e.g., land and sea breezes, boiling water).
3. **Radiation:** Transfer through electromagnetic waves; requires **no medium** (e.g., heat from the Sun reaching Earth).

## Waves & Sound

### Characteristics of Sound
- Sound is a **mechanical, longitudinal wave** (cannot travel through a **vacuum**; travels fastest in **solids**, then liquids, slowest in gases).
- Speed of sound in dry air at $20^\circ\text{C} \approx 343\text{ m/s}$. Speed increases with temperature and humidity.
- **Frequency Ranges:**
  - Infrasonic: $< 20\text{ Hz}$ (Elephants, whales, earthquakes).
  - Audible: $20\text{ Hz}$ to $20,000\text{ Hz}$ ($20\text{ kHz}$) (Human ear).
  - Ultrasonic: $> 20,000\text{ Hz}$ (Bats, dolphins, SONAR, ultrasonography).
- **Pitch:** Determined by **frequency** (high frequency = shrill sound; women's voice has higher pitch).
- **Loudness:** Determined by **amplitude** (measured in Decibels, dB).
- **Quality / Timbre:** Distinguishes sounds of same pitch and loudness.
- **Echo:** Minimum distance required between source and reflector for distinct echo = **$\approx 17.2\text{ metres}$** (persistence of hearing is $0.1\text{ s}$).
- **SONAR (Sound Navigation and Ranging):** Uses **ultrasonic waves** to measure ocean depth and locate underwater objects.

## Optics: Light, Reflection, Refraction & Human Eye

### Nature of Light
- Light is an **electromagnetic, transverse wave**. Speed in vacuum: $c = 3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$.

### Mirrors & Lenses
| Optical Element | Type of Image | Common Applications |
|-----------------|---------------|---------------------|
| **Plane Mirror** | Virtual, erect, same size, laterally inverted | Looking glass; Periscope |
| **Concave Mirror** | Real & inverted (except when object is between Pole & Focus: virtual, erect & magnified) | Shaving/makeup mirror, dentist mirror, car headlights, solar furnaces |
| **Convex Mirror** | Always virtual, erect, and diminished; wide field of view | **Rear-view mirror in vehicles**, street lights |
| **Convex Lens** | Converging lens; real or virtual | Magnifying glass, camera, microscope, telescope |
| **Concave Lens** | Diverging lens; always virtual, erect, diminished | Correction of Myopia |

### Refraction Phenomena
- **Total Internal Reflection (TIR):** Occurs when light travels from denser to rarer medium and angle of incidence $>$ critical angle.
  - Examples: **Mirage in deserts**, **sparkling of diamonds**, **optical fibre cables**, endoscopy.
- **Atmospheric Refraction:**
  - **Twinkling of stars**.
  - Advanced sunrise (2 min early) and delayed sunset (2 min late).
  - Oval shape of the Sun during sunrise and sunset.
- **Scattering of Light (Rayleigh Scattering $\propto 1/\lambda^4$):**
  - **Blue colour of the sky** (blue scatters more due to shorter wavelength).
  - **Red colour of sky at sunrise/sunset** (red has longest wavelength, scatters least).
  - Red lights used as danger signals.

### Defects of Human Vision
| Defect | Other Name | Problem | Cause | Correction |
|--------|------------|---------|-------|------------|
| **Myopia** | Short-sightedness | Can see near clearly; cannot see distant objects | Elongation of eyeball; focal length too short; image forms in front of retina | **Concave lens** (diverging) |
| **Hypermetropia** | Far-sightedness | Can see distant clearly; cannot see near objects | Eyeball too short; image forms behind retina | **Convex lens** (converging) |
| **Presbyopia** | Old age sight | Stiffening of ciliary muscles; loss of accommodation | Aging | **Bifocal lens** (upper concave, lower convex) |
| **Astigmatism** | - | Cannot focus horizontal and vertical lines simultaneously | Irregular cornea curvature | **Cylindrical lens** |

## Electricity & Magnetism

### Ohm's Law & Circuits
$$V = IR$$
- Resistance: $R = \rho \frac{l}{A}$ ($\rho$ = resistivity; unit: $\Omega\cdot\text{m}$).
- Resistivity depends only on material and temperature (not on length or area).
- **Superconductors:** Materials whose electrical resistance becomes zero at very low temperatures (discovered by Heike Kamerlingh Onnes).
- **Resistors in Series:** $R_{\text{eq}} = R_1 + R_2 + R_3$ (same current).
- **Resistors in Parallel:** $\frac{1}{R_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3}$ (same voltage; domestic wiring is in **parallel**).
- **Electric Fuse:** Made of an alloy of **Tin (63%) and Lead (37%)**; has **high resistance and low melting point**.
- **Bulb Filament:** Made of **Tungsten** (highest melting point $\approx 3422^\circ\text{C}$). Filled with inert gas like **Argon/Nitrogen**.
- **Heating Element in Heaters/Irons:** Made of **Nichrome** (Nickel + Chromium; high resistivity and high melting point).

### Magnetic Effects of Current
- Discovered by **Hans Christian Oersted**.
- **Fleming's Left-Hand Rule:** Used for **Electric Motor** (Converts electrical energy to mechanical energy).
- **Fleming's Right-Hand Rule:** Used for **Electric Generator / Dynamo** (Converts mechanical energy to electrical energy; works on Michael Faraday's Electromagnetic Induction).
- **Transformer:** Works on mutual induction and **only on Alternating Current (AC)** (cannot work on DC).

## Modern Physics & Radioactivity

- **Photoelectric Effect:** Emission of electrons when light hits a metal surface (explained by **Albert Einstein**, for which he received the 1921 Nobel Prize).
- **Radioactivity:** Discovered by **Henri Becquerel** (unit: Becquerel). Term coined by Marie Curie.
  - $\alpha$-rays: Helium nuclei ($^4_2\text{He}$), positive charge, highest ionizing power, lowest penetrating power.
  - $\beta$-rays: Fast-moving electrons, negative charge, moderate penetrating power.
  - $\gamma$-rays: High energy electromagnetic waves, neutral, lowest ionizing power, **highest penetrating power**.
- **Nuclear Fission:** Splitting of heavy nucleus into lighter nuclei (Atomic Bomb; Nuclear Power Reactors use Uranium-235 or Plutonium-239). Moderator: Heavy water ($D_2O$) or Graphite; Control rods: Cadmium or Boron.
- **Nuclear Fusion:** Combining light nuclei to form heavier nucleus (Source of **Sun's energy**; Hydrogen Bomb). Requires extremely high temperature.

## Last 10 Years PYQs with Detailed Explanations

### PYQ 1 — SSC CHSL 2024
**Q.** Which type of mirror is used by dentists to inspect teeth?
- A) Convex mirror
- B) Plane mirror
- C) Concave mirror
- D) Cylindrical mirror
- **Answer:** **C) Concave mirror** ✅
- *Explanation:* Concave mirror produces an erect, magnified, virtual image of the tooth when placed close to the tooth (between pole and focus).

### PYQ 2 — SSC CHSL 2023
**Q.** What is the SI unit of electric resistance?
- A) Farad
- B) Henry
- C) Ohm
- D) Siemens
- **Answer:** **C) Ohm ($\Omega$)** ✅
- *Explanation:* Ohm is the SI unit of electrical resistance ($R = V/I$). Siemens is electrical conductance.

### PYQ 3 — SSC CHSL 2023
**Q.** A rear-view mirror used in motor vehicles is a:
- A) Concave mirror
- B) Convex mirror
- C) Plane mirror
- D) Inverted mirror
- **Answer:** **B) Convex mirror** ✅
- *Explanation:* Convex mirrors always form an erect, diminished image and provide a wide field of view to the driver.

### PYQ 4 — SSC CHSL 2022
**Q.** Sound waves cannot travel through which of the following?
- A) Water
- B) Steel
- C) Vacuum
- D) Hydrogen gas
- **Answer:** **C) Vacuum** ✅
- *Explanation:* Sound waves are mechanical longitudinal waves requiring a material medium for propagation. Light can travel in vacuum, but sound cannot.

### PYQ 5 — SSC CHSL 2022
**Q.** At what temperature do the Celsius and Fahrenheit scales indicate the same reading?
- A) $0^\circ$
- B) $-40^\circ$
- C) $100^\circ$
- D) $-32^\circ$
- **Answer:** **B) $-40^\circ$** ✅
- *Explanation:* Using $\frac{C}{5} = \frac{F - 32}{9}$, substituting $C = F = x$: $\frac{x}{5} = \frac{x - 32}{9} \Rightarrow 9x = 5x - 160 \Rightarrow 4x = -160 \Rightarrow x = -40$.

### PYQ 6 — SSC CHSL 2021
**Q.** Optical fibre cables operate on which optical principle?
- A) Refraction
- B) Total Internal Reflection
- C) Interference
- D) Polarization
- **Answer:** **B) Total Internal Reflection (TIR)** ✅
- *Explanation:* Light signals bounce within the glass core of optical fibres through successive total internal reflections with zero energy loss.

### PYQ 7 — SSC CHSL 2020
**Q.** What happens to the weight of a person at the center of the Earth?
- A) Increases
- B) Decreases by half
- C) Becomes zero
- D) Remains unchanged
- **Answer:** **C) Becomes zero** ✅
- *Explanation:* Acceleration due to gravity $g = 0$ at Earth's center, so weight $W = mg = m(0) = 0$. Mass, however, remains unchanged.

### PYQ 8 — SSC CHSL 2019
**Q.** An electric fuse wire must have:
- A) High melting point and low resistance
- B) Low melting point and high resistance
- C) High melting point and high resistance
- D) Low melting point and low resistance
- **Answer:** **B) Low melting point and high resistance** ✅
- *Explanation:* High resistance produces rapid Joule heating ($H = I^2Rt$), and low melting point ensures the wire melts and breaks the circuit to prevent fire.

### PYQ 9 — SSC CHSL 2018
**Q.** The blue colour of the clear sky is caused by:
- A) Reflection of light
- B) Refraction of light
- C) Dispersion of light
- D) Scattering of light
- **Answer:** **D) Scattering of light** ✅
- *Explanation:* Shorter wavelengths (blue/violet) are scattered significantly more by air molecules than longer wavelengths (Rayleigh scattering $\propto 1/\lambda^4$).

### PYQ 10 — SSC CHSL 2017
**Q.** A person suffering from myopia (near-sightedness) is advised to wear spectacles containing:
- A) Convex lens
- B) Concave lens
- C) Bifocal lens
- D) Cylindrical lens
- **Answer:** **B) Concave lens** ✅
- *Explanation:* In myopia, image forms in front of the retina. A diverging (concave) lens spreads the light rays outwards before they enter the eye, focusing the image onto the retina.
