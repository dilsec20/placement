The International System of Units (**SI**, *Système International*, adopted in 1960) consists of **7 fundamental (base) units** and **2 supplementary units**.

| 4 | Electric Current | Ampere | A | `[A]` or `[I]` |
| 5 | Thermodynamic Temperature | Kelvin | K | `[Θ]` or `[K]` |

| S.No. | Physical Quantity | SI Unit | Symbol | Formula | Dimensional Formula |

### 1.4 Scalar vs Vector Quantities

- **Scalar Quantity:** Possesses **only magnitude** and obeys ordinary algebraic addition (e.g., $2\text{ kg} + 3\text{ kg} = 5\text{ kg}$).
- **Vector Quantity:** Possesses **both magnitude and direction** AND obeys **triangle/parallelogram law of vector addition**.
- **Tensor Quantity:** Magnitude varies with direction/plane (e.g., *Moment of Inertia*, *Stress*).

| Scalar Quantities (Magnitude Only) | Vector Quantities (Magnitude + Direction + Vector Law) |
| :--- | :--- |
| Distance, Speed, Mass, Volume, Density | Displacement, Velocity, Acceleration, Retardation |
| Time, Temperature, Heat, Entropy | Force, Weight, Thrust, Tension, Friction |
| Work, Energy (KE, PE), Power | Linear Momentum ($p$), Impulse ($J$) |
| Pressure, Surface Tension, Viscosity | Torque ($\tau$), Angular Velocity ($\omega$), Angular Momentum ($L$) |
| **Electric Current ($I$)**, Electric Charge ($Q$), Potential ($V$) | **Electric Field ($\vec{E}$)**, **Magnetic Field ($\vec{B}$)**, Current Density ($\vec{J}$) |
| Resistance ($R$), Capacitance ($C$), Inductance ($L$) | Area Vector ($\vec{A}$), Gravitational Field ($\vec{g}$) |
| Frequency, Wavelength, Refractive Index | Dipole Moment ($\vec{p}$), Magnetic Moment ($\vec{M}$) |

> **Exam Exceptions (Frequently Tested):**
> 1. **Electric Current ($I$)** has both magnitude and direction, yet it is a **SCALAR** because it obeys ordinary algebra and does NOT obey vector addition.
> 2. **Pressure** and **Surface Tension** are **SCALARS** (act equally in all directions at a point / along surface).
> 3. **Weight** is a **VECTOR** (directed towards Earth's centre), whereas **Mass** is a **SCALAR**.

## 2. Kinematics, Laws of Motion, Circular & Rotational Motion

### 2.1 Basic Kinematic Quantities

| Parameter | Distance | Displacement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | Actual length of path travelled | Shortest straight-line vector from initial to final position |
| **Type** | Scalar | Vector |
| **Value** | Always positive (for moving body) | Positive, negative, or **zero** (e.g., complete circular lap) |
| **Relation** | $\text{Distance} \ge |\text{Displacement}|$ | $|\text{Displacement}| \le \text{Distance}$ |

- **Speed vs Velocity:** $\text{Speed} = \frac{\text{Distance}}{\text{Time}}$ (Scalar, always $\ge 0$); $\text{Velocity} = \frac{\text{Displacement}}{\text{Time}}$ (Vector, can be $+, -, 0$). Note: $\text{Average Speed} \ge |\text{Average Velocity}|$.
- **Uniform Circular Motion:** Speed is **constant**, but velocity **continuously changes** (due to changing direction) $\rightarrow$ it is an **accelerated motion**!
- **Acceleration ($a$):** Rate of change of velocity ($a = \frac{v - u}{t}$). Negative acceleration opposing velocity is **Retardation (Deceleration)**.

### 2.2 Equations of Motion (For Uniform Acceleration)

1. **First Equation:** $v = u + at$
2. **Second Equation:** $s = ut + \frac{1}{2}at^2$
3. **Third Equation:** $v^2 = u^2 + 2as$
4. **Distance in $n$-th second:** $s_n = u + \frac{a}{2}(2n - 1)$

*(For vertical motion under gravity: replace $a$ with $-g$ when thrown upward, and $+g$ when falling downward. If dropped from rest, $u = 0 \Rightarrow v = \sqrt{2gh}$ and $t = \sqrt{2h/g}$.)*

### 2.3 Motion Graphs (Slope & Area Rules)

| Graph Type | Slope ($\frac{\Delta y}{\Delta x}$) Gives | Area Under Curve Gives | Key Graph Shapes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Displacement–Time ($s\text{–}t$)** | **Velocity** | No physical significance | Horizontal line = Rest ($v=0$); Straight inclined line = Uniform velocity ($a=0$); Parabola = Uniform acceleration. |
| **Velocity–Time ($v\text{–}t$)** | **Acceleration** | **Displacement** (and sum of $\| \text{area} \|$ = Distance) | Horizontal line = Uniform velocity ($a=0$); Straight inclined line = Uniform acceleration; Curve = Non-uniform acceleration. |
| **Acceleration–Time ($a\text{–}t$)** | Jerk | **Change in Velocity ($\Delta v$)** | Horizontal line = Uniform acceleration. |

### 2.4 Projectile Motion

When an object is projected with initial velocity $u$ at an angle $\theta$ with the horizontal (neglecting air resistance):
- **Path / Trajectory:** **Parabola**.
- **Horizontal Component ($u_x = u\cos\theta$):** Remains **constant** throughout flight ($a_x = 0$).
- **Vertical Component ($u_y = u\sin\theta$):** Accelerated downward by $g$ ($a_y = -g$).
- **At the Highest Point:** Vertical velocity $v_y = 0$, total velocity $= u\cos\theta$ (minimum, **not zero** unless $\theta = 90^\circ$), and acceleration is still $g$ downward (angle between $\vec{v}$ and $\vec{g}$ is **$90^\circ$**).

| Quantity | Formula | Key Exam Condition |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Time of Ascent / Descent** | $t = \frac{u\sin\theta}{g}$ | Time up = Time down (without air drag). |
| **Total Time of Flight ($T$)** | $T = \frac{2u\sin\theta}{g}$ | Maximum at $\theta = 90^\circ$. |
| **Maximum Height ($H$)** | $H = \frac{u^2 \sin^2\theta}{2g}$ | Maximum at $\theta = 90^\circ$: $H_{\max} = \frac{u^2}{2g}$. |
| **Horizontal Range ($R$)** | $R = \frac{u^2 \sin 2\theta}{g}$ | **Maximum at $\theta = 45^\circ$**: $R_{\max} = \frac{u^2}{g}$. |

> **Projectile Exam Highlights:**
> - For max range ($\theta = 45^\circ$), the relation between max range and max height achieved is **$R_{\max} = 4H$**.
> - Horizontal range is **equal** for any pair of complementary angles **$\theta$ and $(90^\circ - \theta)$** (e.g., $30^\circ$ and $60^\circ$, or $15^\circ$ and $75^\circ$) for the same speed $u$.

### 2.5 Newton's Laws of Motion & Linear Momentum

#### 1. Newton's First Law (Law of Inertia — originally stated by Galileo)
- *Statement:* A body continues in its state of rest or of uniform motion along a straight line unless compelled by an external unbalanced force to change that state.
- **Gives qualitative definition of Force & Inertia.** **Mass** is the quantitative measure of inertia (heavier body = greater inertia).

**Three Types of Inertia (15+ Everyday SSC Examples):**
- **Inertia of Rest:**
  1. Dust falls out of a carpet when beaten with a stick.
  2. Passengers lean/fall **backward** when a stationary bus/train starts suddenly.
  3. Fruits/leaves fall when the branch of a tree is shaken vigorously.
  4. A coin placed on a card over a glass drops straight into the glass when the card is flicked sharply.
  5. Bullet fired at a window pane makes a clean hole, whereas a thrown stone shatters the whole pane (bullet gives too little time for surrounding glass to share motion).
- **Inertia of Motion:**
  6. Passengers lurch **forward** when a moving bus brakes suddenly.
  7. An athlete runs some distance before taking a **long jump** (to gain momentum/inertia of motion).
  8. A person jumping out of a moving train/bus falls **forward** (should run a few steps in the direction of motion).
  9. A ball thrown vertically upward by a passenger in a uniformly moving train falls back into their own hand.
  10. A fan continues to rotate for a while after the switch is turned off.
- **Inertia of Direction:**
  11. Mud/water flies off **tangentially** from a rotating vehicle tyre.
  12. Sparks fly off **tangentially** from a knife-sharpening grinding stone.
  13. Passengers inside a car are pushed **outward** when the car takes a sharp turn.
  14. Rotating an umbrella shakes off raindrops tangentially.

#### 2. Newton's Second Law & Impulse
- *Statement:* The rate of change of linear momentum of a body is directly proportional to the applied external unbalanced force and takes place in the direction of the force.
- **Formula:** $\vec{F} = \frac{d\vec{p}}{dt} = m\vec{a}$ *(gives quantitative measurement of Force)*. Note: 1st Law is a special case of 2nd Law ($F = 0 \Rightarrow a = 0$).
- **Impulse ($J$):** Product of large force acting for a very short time: $J = F \cdot \Delta t = \Delta p = m(v - u)$.
- **SSC Examples:**
  15. A **cricket fielder pulls their hands backward** while catching a fast ball (increases $\Delta t \rightarrow$ decreases force $F$ on hands).
  16. High-jumpers land on a **cushioned mat or sand pit** instead of hard concrete.
  17. **Shock absorbers (springs)** in cars/bikes increase the time of jerk impact.
  18. Chinaware/glassware is packed in **straw or bubble wrap**.

#### 3. Newton's Third Law (Action–Reaction)
- *Statement:* For every action, there is always an equal and opposite reaction ($\vec{F}_{AB} = -\vec{F}_{BA}$).
- **Crucial Rule:** Action and reaction **always act on TWO DIFFERENT BODIES**, so they never cancel each other out!
- **SSC Examples:**
  19. **Rocket propulsion** and **jet engines** (exhaust gases pushed down $\rightarrow$ rocket pushed up).
  20. **Recoil of a gun** when a bullet is fired.
  21. **Jumping from a boat** onto a riverbank (boat moves backward).
  22. **Walking on the ground** (feet push ground backward $\rightarrow$ ground friction pushes feet forward).
  23. **Swimming** (hands push water backward) and **rowing a boat** with oars.
  24. **Lawn sprinkler** rotating as water jets exit sideways.

#### Conservation of Linear Momentum
- If net external force on a system is zero ($F_{\text{ext}} = 0$), total linear momentum remains constant:
  $$m_1 u_1 + m_2 u_2 = m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2$$
- **Recoil Velocity of Gun:** $V = -\frac{mv}{M}$ (where $m, v$ = bullet mass & speed; $M$ = gun mass; negative sign shows opposite direction).
- **Rocket Propulsion** works on **Conservation of Linear Momentum** (and Newton's 3rd Law), even in vacuum!

### 2.6 Circular & Rotational Motion

#### Centripetal vs Centrifugal Force

| Feature | Centripetal Force | Centrifugal Force |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature** | **Real force** (gravity, tension, friction, electrostatic) | **Pseudo / Fictitious force** (arises in rotating frame) |
| **Direction** | Radially **inward** (towards centre) | Radially **outward** (away from centre) |
| **Formula** | $F_c = \frac{mv^2}{r} = m\omega^2 r$ | $F_{cf} = \frac{mv^2}{r} = m\omega^2 r$ (magnitude) |
| **Work Done** | **Always ZERO** (perpendicular to displacement) | — |
| **Examples** | • Planets orbiting Sun (gravity)<br>• Electrons orbiting nucleus (electrostatic)<br>• Cyclist leaning inward on a curved turn<br>• **Banking of roads:** $\tan\theta = \frac{v^2}{rg}$ (safe max speed $v = \sqrt{\mu rg}$ on flat road) | • **Cream separator** (milk vs lighter cream near centre)<br>• **Washing machine spin dryer** (water flung out)<br>• **Centrifuge** (blood plasma separation)<br>• Outward push felt on a merry-go-round |

#### Torque, Couple, Centre of Mass & Moment of Inertia
- **Torque ($\vec{\tau}$):** Turning/rotational effect of a force about an axis: $\tau = r F \sin\theta$.
  - *Applications:* Door handles are fitted at the outer edge farthest from hinges (max $r \rightarrow$ min force needed); long-handled spanners/wrenches easily loosen tight nuts; hand flour-mill handle near the rim.
- **Couple:** A pair of two equal, parallel, opposite forces acting along different lines of action ($\tau = F \times \text{perpendicular distance}$). *Examples:* Turning a car steering wheel, opening/closing a water tap, turning a key in a lock, unscrewing a bottle cap.
- **Centre of Mass (CoM) & Centre of Gravity (CoG):** Point where entire mass/weight appears concentrated.
  - **Conditions for Stability:** (1) Lower Centre of Gravity, (2) Wider base area, (3) Vertical line from CoG must pass within the base.
  - *Examples:* Racing cars are built low and wide; **Leaning Tower of Pisa** does not topple because the vertical through its CoG falls inside its base; a person carrying a heavy load on their back leans forward; double-decker buses prohibit standing on the upper deck.
- **Moment of Inertia ($I$):** Rotational analogue of mass ($I = \sum m_i r_i^2$). Depends on mass AND its distribution from the axis (plus axis choice). A **flywheel** has most of its mass concentrated in its outer rim to maximize $I$ and maintain uniform rotational speed.

#### Conservation of Angular Momentum
- When net external torque is zero ($\tau_{\text{ext}} = 0$), angular momentum remains constant:
  $$L = I\omega = \text{constant} \implies I_1 \omega_1 = I_2 \omega_2$$
- **Classic SSC Examples:**
  1. **Ice skater / ballet dancer spinning:** Pulls arms close to body $\rightarrow$ $I$ decreases $\rightarrow$ angular speed $\omega$ **increases** (spins faster). Stretches arms out $\rightarrow$ $I$ increases $\rightarrow$ spins slower.
  2. **Diver jumping off a springboard:** Curls body into a tight ball ($I$ decreases) to perform rapid somersaults, then straightens out before hitting water.
  3. **Kepler's 2nd Law:** A planet moves faster near the Sun (perihelion, smaller $r$) and slower far from the Sun (aphelion, larger $r$).

### 2.7 Levers (Classes I, II & III)

A lever is a rigid bar turning about a fixed point called the **Fulcrum (F)**.
- **Mechanical Advantage ($\text{MA}$):** $\text{MA} = \frac{\text{Load (L)}}{\text{Effort (E)}} = \frac{\text{Effort Arm}}{\text{Load Arm}}$
- **Memory Trick — `1-2-3 = F-L-E`:** Look at what lies **in the middle**:
  - Class **1** $\rightarrow$ **F**ulcrum in middle
  - Class **2** $\rightarrow$ **L**oad in middle
  - Class **3** $\rightarrow$ **E**ffort in middle

| Class | Middle Component | Order | Mechanical Advantage (MA) | Primary Function | Everyday Tools & Examples (SSC Must-Knows) |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Class I** | **Fulcrum (F)** | `E – F – L` | $\text{MA} > 1$, $= 1$, or $< 1$ | Changes direction / multiplies force or speed | **Scissors**, **Pliers**, **See-saw**, **Crowbar**, **Beam balance**, **Claw hammer (nail puller)**, **Hand-pump handle**, **Bicycle brake lever** |
| **Class II** | **Load (L)** | `F – L – E` | **Always $> 1$** (Effort arm $>$ Load arm) | **Force multiplier** (lifts heavy load with small effort) | **Nutcracker**, **Wheelbarrow**, **Lemon squeezer**, **Bottle opener**, **Paper cutter (guillotine)**, **Hinged door** |
| **Class III** | **Effort (E)** | `F – E – L` | **Always $< 1$** (Effort arm $<$ Load arm) | **Speed / distance multiplier** (precision & range) | **Human forearm** (biceps lifting hand weight), **Ice/Sugar tongs**, **Tweezers**, **Fishing rod**, **Broom**, **Spade/Shovel**, **Stapler**, **Human jaw** |

## 3. Gravitation, Planetary Motion & Satellites

### 3.1 Newton's Universal Law of Gravitation

$$F = \frac{G m_1 m_2}{r^2}$$

- **Universal Gravitational Constant ($G$):** $G = 6.674 \times 10^{-11}\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2\text{/kg}^2$.
- **Discovered/Measured experimentally by:** **Henry Cavendish** (1798) using a torsion balance.
- **Properties of Gravitational Force:**
  - Always **attractive** (never repulsive); acts along the line joining centres (**central** & **conservative** force).
  - **Weakest** of the 4 fundamental forces in nature (Strong Nuclear $>$ Electromagnetic $>$ Weak Nuclear $>$ Gravitational).
  - $G$ is **independent** of the intervening medium, temperature, pressure, or chemical nature of bodies.

---

### 3.2 Acceleration Due to Gravity ($g$) & All Its Variations

$$g = \frac{GM}{R^2} \approx 9.8\text{ m/s}^2 \quad \text{(on Earth's surface)}$$

- $g$ is **independent of the mass, size, and shape** of the falling body. In vacuum, a feather and an iron ball dropped together reach the ground at the **same time**.

#### All 5 Variations of $g$ (Extremely High-Yield)

| Factor | Formula / Rule | Effect on $g$ | Key SSC Takeaway |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Altitude ($h$)** | $g' = \frac{g}{(1 + h/R)^2} \approx g\left(1 - \frac{2h}{R}\right)$ for $h \ll R$ | **Decreases** with height | $g$ drops by 75% at height $h = R$ ($g' = g/4$). |
| **2. Depth ($d$)** | $g' = g\left(1 - \frac{d}{R}\right)$ | **Decreases linearly** with depth | Rate of decrease is faster with altitude ($2h/R$) than depth ($d/R$) near surface. |
| **3. Centre of Earth** | $d = R \implies g' = 0$ | **Zero ($0$)** | Weight ($mg$) is zero at Earth's centre, though mass stays unchanged. |
| **4. Shape (Equator vs Poles)** | $g \propto \frac{1}{R^2}$; Earth is an oblate spheroid ($R_{\text{eq}} > R_{\text{pole}}$ by $\sim 21\text{ km}$) | **Minimum at Equator**, **Maximum at Poles** | $g_{\text{pole}} > g_{\text{equator}}$ by $\approx 0.052\text{ m/s}^2$ (shape + rotation combined). |
| **5. Rotation of Earth** | $g' = g - R\omega^2\cos^2\lambda$ ($\lambda$ = latitude) | Decreases $g$ everywhere **except Poles** | • At **Poles** ($\lambda = 90^\circ$): **No effect**.<br>• At **Equator** ($\lambda = 0^\circ$): Max reduction.<br>• If Earth **stops rotating**, $g$ **increases at Equator** (unchanged at Poles).<br>• If $\omega$ increases **~17 times**, objects at Equator become weightless! |

---

### 3.3 Mass vs Weight & Apparent Weight in a Lift

| Property | Mass ($m$) | Weight ($W = mg$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | Quantity of matter in a body | Gravitational pull acting on a body |
| **Type & SI Unit** | **Scalar** \| Kilogram ($\text{kg}$) | **Vector** \| Newton ($\text{N}$) |
| **Variation** | **Constant everywhere** in universe | **Varies** place to place with $g$ |
| **Measuring Instrument** | Beam balance (physical balance) | Spring balance |
| **At Centre of Earth** | Non-zero (same) | **Zero** |
| **On Moon** | Same as Earth | **$1/6\text{th}$ of Earth weight** ($g_{\text{moon}} = g_{\text{earth}}/6$) |

#### Apparent Weight in a Lift (Elevator)

| State of Lift | Apparent Weight ($W_{\text{app}}$) | Feeling of Passenger |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **At rest** or **uniform velocity** ($a = 0$) | $W_{\text{app}} = mg$ | **Normal** (No change) |
| Accelerating **Upward** ($a$) | $W_{\text{app}} = m(g + a)$ | Feels **Heavier** |
| Accelerating **Downward** ($a < g$) | $W_{\text{app}} = m(g - a)$ | Feels **Lighter** |
| **Free Fall** (cable breaks, $a = g$ down) | $W_{\text{app}} = m(g - g) = 0$ | **Weightlessness** |
| Accelerating **Downward** ($a > g$) | $W_{\text{app}} < 0$ | Passenger lifts off floor and **hits the ceiling** |

---

### 3.4 Escape Velocity, Orbital Velocity & Satellites

| Parameter | Escape Velocity ($v_e$) | Orbital Velocity ($v_o$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | Minimum speed to escape planet's gravity permanently | Speed required to stay in circular orbit near surface |
| **Formula** | $v_e = \sqrt{\frac{2GM}{R}} = \sqrt{2gR}$ | $v_o = \sqrt{\frac{GM}{R+h}} \approx \sqrt{gR}$ (for $h \ll R$) |
| **Value for Earth** | **$11.2\text{ km/s}$** | **$7.92\text{ km/s}$** ($\approx 8\text{ km/s}$) |
| **Value for Moon** | **$2.38\text{ km/s}$** (Reason Moon has **no atmosphere**!) | **$1.68\text{ km/s}$** |
| **Mass / Angle Dependence** | **Independent** of mass of object & projection angle | **Independent** of mass of satellite |

- **Exact Relationship:**
  $$v_e = \sqrt{2}\,v_o \approx 1.414\,v_o$$
  - If an orbiting satellite's velocity increases by $\sqrt{2}$ times (**$\approx 41.4\%$ increase**), or its kinetic energy doubles, it **escapes** into space!

#### Geostationary vs Polar Satellites

| Feature | Geostationary (Geosynchronous) Satellite | Polar Satellite |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Altitude** | **35,786 km** ($\approx 36,000\text{ km}$) above surface | **500 – 800 km** (Low Earth Orbit) |
| **Time Period** | **24 hours** (matches Earth's rotation) | **84 – 100 minutes** (near-surface min $\approx 84.6\text{ min}$) |
| **Orbit Plane** | **Equatorial plane** (directly above Equator) | **North–South plane** (passes over poles) |
| **Direction** | **West to East** (same as Earth's spin) | North–South / South–North |
| **Orbital Speed** | $\approx 3.07\text{ km/s}$ | $\approx 7.5\text{–}7.9\text{ km/s}$ |
| **Primary Uses** | Telecommunications, DTH TV broadcasting, GPS/Weather (INSAT, GSAT) | Remote sensing, mapping, spying, environmental monitoring (IRS, Cartosat) |

> **Why do astronauts feel weightless inside an orbiting satellite?**
> **Not** because $g = 0$ (at 400 km altitude, $g$ is still ~89% of surface $g$!). They feel weightless because both the satellite and the astronaut are in **continuous free fall** towards Earth with the same centripetal acceleration ($N = 0$).

### 3.5 Kepler's Laws & Simple Pendulum

#### Kepler's 3 Laws of Planetary Motion
1. **First Law (Law of Orbits):** Every planet revolves around the Sun in an **elliptical orbit** with the Sun at one of the two foci.
2. **Second Law (Law of Areas):** The line joining a planet and the Sun sweeps out **equal areas in equal intervals of time** ($\frac{dA}{dt} = \text{constant}$). *Consequence of Conservation of Angular Momentum* — planet moves fastest at **Perihelion** (closest to Sun) and slowest at **Aphelion** (farthest from Sun).
3. **Third Law (Law of Periods):** The square of the time period of revolution is directly proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis of its orbit: **$T^2 \propto r^3$**.

#### Simple Pendulum
$$T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{l}{g}}$$

*(Where $l$ = effective length from point of suspension to Centre of Gravity of bob; Seconds Pendulum has $T = 2\text{ s}$ and $l \approx 1\text{ m}$.)*

| Factor Changed | Effect on Time Period ($T$) | Clock / Exam Result |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mass or material of bob** | **No change (Independent!)** | Changing iron bob to wooden bob of same size/CoG does not alter $T$. |
| **Girl on swing stands up** | CoG rises $\rightarrow l$ **decreases** $\rightarrow T$ **decreases** | Swing oscillates **faster**. |
| **Summer (heat)** | Rod expands $\rightarrow l$ **increases** $\rightarrow T$ **increases** | Pendulum clock **loses time (runs slow)**. |
| **Winter (cold)** | Rod contracts $\rightarrow l$ **decreases** $\rightarrow T$ **decreases** | Pendulum clock **gains time (runs fast)**. |
| **Lift accelerating UP** | $g_{\text{eff}} = g + a \rightarrow T$ **decreases** | Clock runs **fast**. |
| **Lift accelerating DOWN** | $g_{\text{eff}} = g - a \rightarrow T$ **increases** | Clock runs **slow**. |
| **Free fall / Satellite** | $g_{\text{eff}} = 0 \implies T = \infty$ | Pendulum **does NOT oscillate**. |
| **Taken to the Moon** | $g_{\text{moon}} = g/6 \implies T_{\text{moon}} = \sqrt{6}\,T_{\text{earth}}$ | Oscillates **slower** ($T$ increases). |
| **Mountain / Deep Mine** | $g$ **decreases** $\rightarrow T$ **increases** | Clock runs **slow**. |
| **Equator vs Poles** | $g_{\text{pole}} > g_{\text{eq}}$ | $T$ is **minimum at Poles**, **maximum at Equator**. |

## 4. Work, Energy, Power, Friction & Properties of Matter

### 4.1 Work, Energy, Collisions & Power

#### Work
$$W = F \cdot s \cos\theta$$

- **Scalar quantity** | SI Unit: **Joule (J)** | CGS Unit: **Erg** ($1\text{ J} = 10^7\text{ erg}$).
- **Positive Work ($\theta < 90^\circ$):** Force has component along displacement (e.g., gravity on a falling stone).
- **Negative Work ($\theta > 90^\circ$):** Force opposes displacement (e.g., friction, gravity on a ball thrown upward).
- **Zero Work ($\theta = 90^\circ$ or $s = 0$):**
  - A coolie walking horizontally with a load on their head ($\theta = 90^\circ$ between gravity/lift and displacement).
  - Centripetal force during circular motion ($\theta = 90^\circ$).
  - Pushing hard against a rigid wall ($s = 0$).

#### Energy & Work–Energy Theorem
- **Kinetic Energy ($KE$):** Energy due to motion: $KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{p^2}{2m}$.
  - *Exam Relations:* If speed doubles $\rightarrow KE$ becomes **4 times**. If momentum doubles $\rightarrow KE$ becomes **4 times**.
- **Potential Energy ($PE$):** Energy due to position/configuration:
  - Gravitational $PE = mgh$ | Elastic Spring $PE = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$.
- **Work–Energy Theorem:** Work done by net force equals change in kinetic energy: $W_{\text{net}} = \Delta KE = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 - \frac{1}{2}mu^2$.
- **Law of Conservation of Energy:** Energy can neither be created nor destroyed, only transformed from one form to another. Total Mechanical Energy ($KE + PE$) remains constant under conservative forces.

#### Collisions (Elastic vs Inelastic)

| Feature | Elastic Collision | Inelastic Collision | Perfectly Inelastic Collision |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Linear Momentum** | **Conserved** | **Conserved** | **Conserved** |
| **Kinetic Energy** | **Conserved** | **NOT conserved** (lost as heat/sound) | **Maximum KE loss** |
| **Total Energy** | Conserved | Conserved | Conserved |
| **Coefficient of Restitution ($e$)** | $e = 1$ | $0 < e < 1$ | $e = 0$ (bodies stick together) |
| **Examples** | Collisions between subatomic particles / ideal gas molecules; billiard balls (approx.) | Bouncing ball losing height; car crash | Bullet embedded in wooden block; mud thrown on wall |

*(Note: When two **equal masses** undergo a head-on elastic collision, they simply **exchange velocities**.)*

#### Power & Commercial Energy Units
- **Power:** Rate of doing work: $P = \frac{W}{t} = \vec{F} \cdot \vec{v}$ (Scalar, SI Unit: **Watt** = $\text{J/s}$).
- **1 Horsepower (HP)** = **746 Watts**.
- **1 Kilowatt-hour (kWh):** Commercial unit of **electrical energy** (1 "unit" on electricity bill) — **NOT a unit of power!**
  $$1\text{ kWh} = 1000\text{ W} \times 3600\text{ s} = 3.6 \times 10^6\text{ J}$$

---

### 4.2 Friction

Friction is a contact force that **opposes relative motion (or impending relative motion)** between two surfaces.
- Depends on: **Nature/roughness of surfaces** and **Normal reaction ($N$)**.
- **Independent of the apparent area of contact** (for rigid solids).

#### Types of Friction (Order of Magnitude)
$$\text{Limiting (Static) Friction} > \text{Sliding (Kinetic) Friction} > \text{Rolling Friction}$$

1. **Static Friction ($f_s \le \mu_s N$):** Acts when the object is at rest; **self-adjusting** force equal to applied force up to a maximum value (**Limiting Friction**).
2. **Sliding / Kinetic Friction ($f_k = \mu_k N$):** Acts when one body slides over another ($\mu_k < \mu_s$).
3. **Rolling Friction ($f_r$):** Acts when a body rolls over a surface (smallest!).

- **Reducing Friction:** Ball bearings (convert sliding into rolling friction), lubricants (oil, grease, graphite powder), streamlining (aeroplanes/fishes), polishing (*Note: over-polishing increases friction due to cold-welding/intermolecular adhesion!*).
- **Increasing Friction:** Grooves/treads on tyres, spikes on athletes' shoes, sand spread on icy/slippery roads, brake pads.

---

### 4.3 Elasticity & Hooke's Law

- **Elasticity:** Property of a body to regain its original shape and size after the deforming force is removed.
- **Plasticity:** Failure to regain original shape (e.g., putty, wet clay, wax).
- **Stress:** Internal restoring force per unit area ($\text{Stress} = \frac{F}{A}$, SI Unit: $\text{N/m}^2$ or **Pa**).
- **Strain:** Fractional change in dimension ($\text{Strain} = \frac{\Delta L}{L}$, **dimensionless & unitless**).
- **Hooke's Law:** Within the elastic limit, **Stress is directly proportional to Strain**:
  $$\frac{\text{Stress}}{\text{Strain}} = E \quad (\text{Modulus of Elasticity})$$
- **Three Moduli:**
  1. **Young's Modulus ($Y$):** Longitudinal stress / longitudinal strain (**Solids only**).
  2. **Bulk Modulus ($B$):** Hydraulic stress / volumetric strain (**Solids, liquids & gases**; $\text{Compressibility} = \frac{1}{B}$).
  3. **Shear Modulus / Rigidity ($\eta$):** Tangential stress / shear strain (**Solids only**).

> **Classic SSC Trap:** **Steel is MORE elastic than Rubber!**
> *Reason:* Elasticity is measured by Young's Modulus ($Y = \text{Stress}/\text{Strain}$). For the same applied stress, steel undergoes much smaller strain than rubber, so $Y_{\text{steel}} \gg Y_{\text{rubber}}$. (**Quartz** and **Phosphor bronze** are nearly perfectly elastic.)

---

### 4.4 Fluid Mechanics

#### Pressure & Pascal's Law
- **Fluid Pressure ($P = h\rho g$):** Depends on depth ($h$), fluid density ($\rho$), and $g$; **independent of shape or cross-sectional area** of container (*Hydrostatic Paradox*).
- **Pascal's Law:** Pressure applied at any point to an enclosed, incompressible fluid is transmitted **equally and undiminished** in all directions throughout the fluid.
  - **Applications:** **Hydraulic lift**, **Hydraulic brakes**, **Hydraulic press**.

#### Atmospheric Pressure & Barometer
- $1\text{ atm} = 1.013 \times 10^5\text{ Pa} = 76\text{ cm}$ ($760\text{ mm}$) of $\text{Hg} = 760\text{ Torr} = 1.013\text{ bar}$ ($1\text{ bar} = 10^5\text{ Pa}$).
- **Barometer** (invented by **Torricelli**): Uses **Mercury ($\text{Hg}$)** because of its high density ($13.6\text{ g/cm}^3$, needs only a ~76 cm tube vs ~10.3 m for water!), non-wetting nature, and very low vapour pressure.

| Barometer Mercury Level | Weather Indication |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Sudden fall** | **Storm / Cyclone** approaching |
| **Slow / gradual fall** | **Rain** expected |
| **Slow / gradual rise** | **Clear / fair weather** ahead |

- **Altitude Effects:** Atmospheric pressure **decreases** with altitude $\rightarrow$ (1) **Nose bleeding** (internal blood pressure > external air pressure), (2) **Fountain pens leak** on airplanes, (3) **Boiling point of water decreases** ($<100^\circ\text{C}$) on mountains so cooking takes longer (a **pressure cooker** traps steam, raising pressure $\rightarrow$ boiling point rises to $\sim 120^\circ\text{C}$ $\rightarrow$ cooks faster).

#### Archimedes' Principle, Buoyancy & Floatation
- **Archimedes' Principle:** When a body is partially or fully immersed in a fluid, it experiences an upward buoyant force (**Upthrust**) equal to the **weight of the fluid displaced** by it ($F_B = V_{\text{sub}} \rho_{\text{fluid}} g$).
- **Law of Floatation:** A body floats if $\rho_{\text{body}} \le \rho_{\text{fluid}}$ (Weight $\le$ max upthrust).
  - **Ship entering sea from river:** **Rises slightly**, because seawater has higher density (salinity) than river water, so less submerged volume is needed for the same upthrust.
  - **Applications:** **Lactometer** (milk purity), **Hydrometer** (specific gravity/density of liquids), **Submarine** (ballast tanks control weight vs buoyancy), **Life jackets**, **Iron ship** (hollow shape displaces large volume of water).
- **Ice Floating & Melting (High-Frequency SSC Question):**
  - Pure ice floating in pure water stays **~9/10th ($90\%$) submerged** and **1/10th ($10\%$) above the surface** ($\rho_{\text{ice}} \approx 0.917\text{ g/cm}^3$).
  - When a floating piece of pure ice **melts completely** in a glass of water, the **water level REMAINS THE SAME** (volume of melted water equals the submerged displaced volume).
  - *Exception:* If the melting ice holds an embedded **lead/iron ball** (denser than water, sinks), the water level **FALLS**; if it holds a **cork/wood** (floats), the water level **REMAINS THE SAME**.

#### Bernoulli's Principle
- For steady, incompressible, non-viscous, irrotational fluid flow:
  $$P + \frac{1}{2}\rho v^2 + \rho g h = \text{constant} \quad (\text{Conservation of Energy for fluids})$$
- **Core Rule:** Where fluid speed ($v$) **increases**, static pressure ($P$) **decreases**!
- **Applications:**
  1. **Aeroplane Wing Lift (Aerofoil):** Air moves faster over the curved upper surface $\rightarrow$ lower pressure above, higher pressure below $\rightarrow$ upward lift.
  2. **Magnus Effect:** Swing/curve of a spinning cricket ball, football, or tennis ball.
  3. **Tin roofs blown off during storms:** Fast wind above lowers top pressure while calm indoor air pushes the roof upward.
  4. **Person pulled towards a fast-moving train** on a railway platform (fast air between person and train lowers pressure).
  5. **Atomizer / Perfume sprayer**, **Bunsen burner**, **Venturimeter** (measures fluid flow speed).

#### Cohesive vs Adhesive Forces, Surface Tension & Capillarity
- **Cohesive Force:** Force of attraction between molecules of the **same substance** (strongest in solids). *Example:* Water drops cling together; mercury does not wet glass (cohesive $\text{Hg–Hg} >$ adhesive $\text{Hg–glass}$).
- **Adhesive Force:** Force of attraction between molecules of **different substances**. *Example:* Water wets glass (adhesive $\text{water–glass} >$ cohesive $\text{water–water}$), ink writing on paper, glue.
- **Surface Tension ($T = F/l$):** Property by which a free liquid surface acts like a stretched elastic membrane and tends to acquire **minimum surface area** (caused by unbalanced inward cohesive forces).
  - **Examples:** Spherical **raindrops** and soap bubbles (sphere has minimum surface area for a given volume); sewing needle floating gently on water; insects walking on water; wet paintbrush bristles sticking together; **hot soup tastes better** than cold soup (lower surface tension spreads easily over taste buds).
  - **Factors:** Rising **temperature decreases** surface tension (becomes zero at critical temperature). Adding **soap/detergent decreases** surface tension (improves cleaning). Adding soluble **salt ($\text{NaCl}$) increases** surface tension.
- **Capillarity:** Rise or depression of a liquid in a fine capillary tube:
  $$h = \frac{2T\cos\theta}{r\rho g}$$
  - Liquid **rises** if it wets the wall ($\theta < 90^\circ$, concave meniscus, e.g., **water** in glass) and **falls** if it does not wet ($\theta > 90^\circ$, convex meniscus, e.g., **mercury** in glass).
  - **Examples:** **Oil rising in a lamp wick**, **blotting paper absorbing ink**, **towel soaking water**, **sap/water rising in plant xylem**, **farmers ploughing fields after rain** (breaks soil capillaries to prevent underground moisture loss).

#### Viscosity, Stokes' Law & Terminal Velocity
- **Viscosity ($\eta$):** Internal friction between adjacent fluid layers opposing relative motion.
  - SI Unit: **Pa·s** (Poiseuille) | CGS Unit: **Poise** ($1\text{ Pa}\cdot\text{s} = 10\text{ Poise}$). Ideal fluid has **zero viscosity**.
- **Temperature Effect (Classic Exam Trap!):**
  - **Liquids:** Viscosity **DECREASES** with rise in temperature (intermolecular cohesion weakens; e.g., hot oil flows faster).
  - **Gases:** Viscosity **INCREASES** with rise in temperature (random molecular momentum exchange increases).
- **Stokes' Law:** Viscous drag force on a small sphere of radius $r$ moving at velocity $v$ in a fluid of viscosity $\eta$:
  $$F = 6\pi\eta r v$$
- **Terminal Velocity ($v_t$):** Constant maximum speed attained when downward gravity is balanced by upward buoyancy + viscous drag:
  $$v_t = \frac{2r^2(\rho - \sigma)g}{9\eta} \implies v_t \propto r^2$$
  - **Consequences:** Larger raindrops fall much faster than tiny mist droplets; microscopic cloud droplets have negligible $v_t$ and appear to **float** in air; a skydiver with an open parachute achieves a safe low terminal velocity.

## 5. Heat & Thermodynamics

### 5.1 Temperature & Temperature Scales
* **Heat ($Q$):** A form of energy transferred due to temperature difference. **SI Unit:** Joule ($\text{J}$); **CGS/Practical Unit:** Calorie ($\text{cal}$), where $1\text{ cal} = 4.186\text{ J}$ ($\approx 4.2\text{ J}$).
* **Temperature ($T$):** Measure of the degree of hotness or coldness of a body; determines the **direction of heat flow** (always from higher temperature to lower temperature). **SI Unit:** Kelvin ($\text{K}$).

#### Temperature Scale Comparison & Conversion
$$\frac{C - 0}{100} = \frac{F - 32}{180} = \frac{K - 273.15}{100} = \frac{R - 0}{80}$$

Simplified SSC exam form (using $273$):
$$\frac{C}{5} = \frac{F - 32}{9} = \frac{K - 273}{5} = \frac{R}{4}$$

| Scale | Symbol | Lower Fixed Point (Ice Point) | Upper Fixed Point (Steam Point) | Divisions | Key Exam Notes |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Celsius** | $^\circ\text{C}$ | $0^\circ\text{C}$ | $100^\circ\text{C}$ | $100$ | Also called Centigrade scale |
| **Fahrenheit** | $^\circ\text{F}$ | $32^\circ\text{F}$ | $212^\circ\text{F}$ | $180$ | Clinical thermometers commonly use $^\circ\text{F}$ |
| **Kelvin** | $\text{K}$ | $273.15\text{ K}$ ($273\text{ K}$) | $373.15\text{ K}$ ($373\text{ K}$) | $100$ | **SI Unit**; no degree ($^\circ$) sign used; never negative |
| **Réaumur** | $^\circ\text{R}$ | $0^\circ\text{R}$ | $80^\circ\text{R}$ | $80$ | $1^\circ\text{R} = 1.25^\circ\text{C}$ |
| **Rankine** | $^\circ\text{Ra}$ | $491.67^\circ\text{Ra}$ | $671.67^\circ\text{Ra}$ | $180$ | Absolute Fahrenheit scale ($\text{Ra} = F + 459.67$) |

#### Exam-Tested Temperature Facts
* **Coincidence of Celsius & Fahrenheit:** **$-40^\circ\text{C} = -40^\circ\text{F}$** (the only temperature where both scales read the same).
* **Coincidence of Fahrenheit & Kelvin:** **$574.25^\circ\text{F} = 574.25\text{ K}$** (using $K = C + 273$; exact with $273.15$ is $574.59$).
* **Normal Human Body Temperature:** **$37^\circ\text{C} = 98.6^\circ\text{F} = 310.15\text{ K}$** ($\approx 310\text{ K}$).
* **Absolute Zero:** **$0\text{ K} = -273.15^\circ\text{C} = -459.67^\circ\text{F}$**. It is the lowest theoretically attainable temperature at which all thermal molecular motion ceases and the pressure/volume of an ideal gas theoretically becomes zero.

#### Types of Thermometers

| Thermometer | Range | Thermometric Property / Liquid | Key Features & Exam Exceptions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Clinical Thermometer** | $35^\circ\text{C}$ to $42^\circ\text{C}$ ($94^\circ\text{F}$ to $108^\circ\text{F}$) | Mercury | Has a **kink (constriction)** near the bulb to prevent mercury from falling back before reading |
| **Laboratory Thermometer** | $-10^\circ\text{C}$ to $110^\circ\text{C}$ | Mercury | **No kink**; must be read while bulb is still in contact with the substance |
| **Alcohol Thermometer** | $-115^\circ\text{C}$ to $78^\circ\text{C}$ | Coloured Alcohol | Used in **very cold regions** (below $-39^\circ\text{C}$) because alcohol freezes at $-115^\circ\text{C}$ whereas mercury freezes at $-39^\circ\text{C}$ |
| **Platinum Resistance** | $-200^\circ\text{C}$ to $1200^\circ\text{C}$ | Electrical resistance of Pt wire | High precision industrial/lab measurement |
| **Constant Volume Gas** | $-200^\circ\text{C}$ to $500^\circ\text{C}$ ($\text{H}_2$); up to $1600^\circ\text{C}$ ($\text{N}_2$) | Pressure of gas at constant volume | Used as primary standard thermometer |
| **Optical / Total Radiation Pyrometer** | $> 800^\circ\text{C}$ (up to $6000^\circ\text{C}+$) | Thermal radiation emitted by hot body | Measures **very high temperatures without physical contact** (Sun, stars, blast furnaces); works on **Stefan–Boltzmann Law** |

> **Why Mercury is Preferred in Thermometers:** Good conductor of heat, uniform thermal expansion, does not wet/stick to glass, opaque & shiny (easy to read), low specific heat capacity, high boiling point ($357^\circ\text{C}$), freezing point ($-39^\circ\text{C}$).

### 5.2 Thermal Expansion, Specific Heat, Latent Heat & Calorimetry

#### Thermal Expansion of Solids
1. **Linear Expansion ($\alpha$):** $\Delta L = L_0 \alpha \Delta T$
2. **Superficial / Area Expansion ($\beta$):** $\Delta A = A_0 \beta \Delta T$
3. **Cubical / Volume Expansion ($\gamma$):** $\Delta V = V_0 \gamma \Delta T$
* **Relation between coefficients:** $\alpha : \beta : \gamma = 1 : 2 : 3$, i.e., $\beta = 2\alpha$ and $\gamma = 3\alpha$.
* **Applications:** Small gaps left between **railway tracks**, sagging of electric/telephone wires in summer, fitting an iron rim onto a wooden cart wheel by heating, loosening a tight metal cap on a glass bottle using hot water.
* **Pendulum Clock Effect:** In summer, length $L$ increases $\implies$ Time period $T = 2\pi\sqrt{L/g}$ increases $\implies$ clock runs **slow (loses time)**. In winter, $L$ decreases $\implies$ clock runs **fast (gains time)**.

#### Anomalous Expansion of Water ($0^\circ\text{C}$ to $4^\circ\text{C}$)
* Most liquids expand on heating and contract on cooling. **Water is an exception** between **$0^\circ\text{C}$ and $4^\circ\text{C}$**:
  * On heating from $0^\circ\text{C}$ to $4^\circ\text{C}$, water **contracts** (volume decreases, density increases).
  * Above $4^\circ\text{C}$, water behaves normally and expands on heating.
* **At $4^\circ\text{C}$:** Water has **MAXIMUM Density** ($1000\text{ kg/m}^3$ or $1\text{ g/cm}^3$) and **MINIMUM Volume**.
* **Ecological & Practical Consequences:**
  * **Survival of Aquatic Life in Winter:** When atmospheric temperature drops below $0^\circ\text{C}$, surface water freezes into ice (density $\approx 0.917\text{ g/cm}^3$, which floats). Ice acts as a thermal insulator, keeping water at the bottom of lakes/ponds at **$4^\circ\text{C}$**, allowing fish and aquatic organisms to survive.
  * **Bursting of Water Pipes:** Water trapped in pipes expands on freezing below $4^\circ\text{C}$ (volume increases by ~9%), exerting huge pressure and bursting pipes. (Same mechanism causes **rock weathering**).

#### Bimetallic Strip
* Consists of two strips of metals with **different coefficients of linear expansion** (typically **Brass** and **Iron/Invar**) riveted together.
* On heating, it bends toward the metal with **lower $\alpha$** (Brass expands more than Iron, so it stays on the convex/outer side).
* **Uses:** **Thermostat** (automatic temperature control in electric irons, refrigerators, geysers, air conditioners) and fire alarms.

#### Specific Heat Capacity ($c$)
$$Q = m c \Delta T$$
* **Definition:** Heat required to raise the temperature of $1\text{ kg}$ of a substance by $1\text{ K}$ (or $1^\circ\text{C}$). **SI Unit:** $\text{J kg}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$.
* **Water has the highest specific heat capacity** among common liquids and solids: **$4186\text{ J kg}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$** ($\approx 4200\text{ J kg}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$ or $1\text{ cal g}^{-1}{}^\circ\text{C}^{-1}$).
* **Applications of Water's High Specific Heat:**
  * Used as a **coolant** in automobile radiators and industrial engines.
  * Used in **hot water bottles** for fomentation (retains heat longer).
  * **Moderating coastal climate** (land heats and cools much faster than sea water, producing sea breeze and land breeze).
* **Thermal Capacity (Heat Capacity):** $C = m c = Q / \Delta T$ (**SI Unit:** $\text{J/K}$).
* **Mayer's Relation (for ideal gases):** $C_p - C_v = R$, and adiabatic index $\gamma = \frac{C_p}{C_v}$ (Monatomic $= 1.67$, Diatomic $= 1.40$, Triatomic/Polyatomic $= 1.33$).

#### Latent Heat ($L$)
$$Q = m L$$
* **Definition:** Heat absorbed or released by unit mass of a substance during a **change of state (phase) at constant temperature**. **SI Unit:** $\text{J/kg}$.
* **Latent Heat of Fusion of Ice:** **$80\text{ cal/g}$** ($3.34 \times 10^5\text{ J/kg}$).
* **Latent Heat of Vaporization of Steam/Water:** **$540\text{ cal/g}$** ($2.26 \times 10^6\text{ J/kg}$).
* **Exam Classics:**
  * **Steam at $100^\circ\text{C}$ causes much severer burns than boiling water at $100^\circ\text{C}$** because steam carries an extra **$540\text{ cal/g}$** of latent heat of vaporization, which is released on the skin upon condensation.
  * **Ice at $0^\circ\text{C}$ is more effective at cooling** than water at $0^\circ\text{C}$ because ice absorbs **$80\text{ cal/g}$** of latent heat of fusion to melt first.

#### Calorimetry & Regelation
* **Principle of Calorimetry (Law of Mixtures):** In an isolated system, **Heat lost by hot body = Heat gained by cold body** (based on **Conservation of Energy**). A calorimeter is made of **Copper** because copper has low specific heat capacity and high thermal conductivity.
* **Regelation:** The phenomenon in which ice melts under increased pressure (lowering its melting point) and refreezes once the pressure is removed.
  * **Examples:** Making a snowball by pressing loose snow, **ice skating** (pressure of narrow skate blade melts ice into a thin lubricating water film), a weighted wire passing completely through an ice block without cutting it into two pieces.

### 5.3 Phase Changes & Effect of Pressure

#### Summary of Phase Transitions
* **Melting (Fusion):** Solid $\rightarrow$ Liquid (absorbs heat).
* **Freezing (Solidification):** Liquid $\rightarrow$ Solid (releases heat).
* **Vaporization:** Liquid $\rightarrow$ Gas (occurs as **Boiling** at fixed BP throughout bulk, or **Evaporation** at any temperature below BP from the **surface only**).
  * **Evaporation causes cooling:** Sweating, water staying cool in an **earthen pot (*matka*)** through micro-pores, desert air coolers, palm feeling cool when acetone/petrol evaporates. Rate of evaporation increases with **surface area, temperature, wind speed**, and decreases with **humidity**.
* **Condensation:** Gas $\rightarrow$ Liquid (releases heat; causes dew, fog, clouds).
* **Sublimation:** Solid $\rightarrow$ Gas directly without becoming liquid (Examples: **Camphor, Naphthalene, Dry Ice [solid $\text{CO}_2$], Iodine, Ammonium chloride**).
* **Deposition (Desublimation):** Gas $\rightarrow$ Solid directly (e.g., frost formation).

#### Effect of Pressure & Impurities on Melting Point and Boiling Point

| Property | Condition / Change | Effect | Exam Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Melting Point (MP)** | Pressure **increases** (for solids that **expand** on melting: Wax, Iron, Gold) | MP **increases** | Wax needs higher temp to melt under pressure |
| **Melting Point (MP)** | Pressure **increases** (for solids that **contract** on melting: **Ice, Cast Iron, Bismuth, Antimony**) | MP **decreases** | Regelation, ice skating |
| **Melting Point (MP)** | Adding **soluble impurities** (e.g., salt in ice) | MP **decreases** | Freezing mixture (ice + salt $\approx -22^\circ\text{C}$) for making *kulfi*; sprinkling salt on icy roads |
| **Boiling Point (BP)** | Pressure **increases** | BP **increases** | **Pressure Cooker:** Trapped steam raises internal pressure ($\sim 2\text{ atm}$), increasing water's BP to $\sim 120^\circ\text{C}$ so food cooks faster |
| **Boiling Point (BP)** | Pressure **decreases** | BP **decreases** | **High Altitudes / Mountains:** Lower atmospheric pressure makes water boil below $100^\circ\text{C}$, so food takes longer to cook in open vessels |
| **Boiling Point (BP)** | Adding **non-volatile impurities** (e.g., salt in water) | BP **increases** | Salted water boils above $100^\circ\text{C}$ |

### 5.4 Heat Transfer & Thermal Radiation Laws

#### Comparison of Modes of Heat Transfer

| Feature | Conduction | Convection | Radiation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Medium Required?** | Yes (mainly **Solids** + Mercury) | Yes (**Fluids**: Liquids & Gases) | **No medium required** (works in vacuum) |
| **Particle Movement** | Vibrate about mean positions; **no bulk flow** | **Actual bulk movement** of heated particles | **No particle movement** (EM waves / Infrared) |
| **Speed** | Slowest | Moderate | **Fastest** (speed of light, $3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$) |
| **Path** | Any path through solid | Upward currents (driven by gravity/buoyancy) | Straight line |
| **Key Examples** | Heating one end of a metal rod; cooking utensil handles (ebonite/bakelite) | **Land & Sea breezes**, trade winds, heating water in a pot, AC/ventilators near ceiling, freezer at top of fridge | **Sun's heat reaching Earth**, heat felt near a bonfire, Thermos silvering |

* **Thermal Conductivity Order:** **Silver > Copper > Gold > Aluminium** > Brass > Iron.
* **Poor Conductors / Insulators:** Wood, glass, plastic, wool, **air**, **water**, **ice**.
* **Exam Applications:**
  * **Two thin blankets are warmer than one thick blanket** of the same material because a layer of **trapped air** (poor conductor) forms between them.
  * **Igloos** are made of snow/ice because snow traps air pockets, preventing internal heat from escaping by conduction.
  * **Zero-gravity exception:** Convection cannot occur in a gravity-free environment (e.g., inside an orbiting space station).

#### Thermos Flask (Dewar Flask)
Designed to minimize heat loss/gain by all three modes:
1. **Vacuum** between double glass walls $\rightarrow$ prevents **Conduction and Convection**.
2. **Silvered (shiny) inner & outer walls** $\rightarrow$ reflect thermal waves, preventing **Radiation**.
3. **Insulating cork/plastic stopper & supports** $\rightarrow$ minimize **Conduction**.

#### Blackbody & Radiation Laws
* **Perfect Blackbody:** A body that absorbs **100%** of incident thermal radiation of all wavelengths ($a = 1$, $e = 1$). Lampblack absorbs ~96–98%; **Ferry's Blackbody** is a laboratory approximation.
* **Kirchhoff's Law of Thermal Radiation:** At a given temperature, the ratio of emissive power to absorptive power is constant for all bodies and equals the emissive power of a perfect blackbody ($\frac{e_\lambda}{a_\lambda} = E_\lambda$). **Rule:** *"Good absorbers are good emitters."*
  * Explains **dark Fraunhofer lines** in the solar spectrum and why a **white/shiny teapot stays hot longer** than a black/rough teapot.
  * **Desert days are very hot and nights are very cold** because sand has low specific heat and radiates heat rapidly at night.
* **Stefan–Boltzmann Law:** Total radiant energy emitted per unit area per unit time by a blackbody is proportional to the **fourth power of its absolute temperature ($T$ in Kelvin)**:
  $$E = \sigma T^4 \quad (\text{and } E = e\sigma T^4 \text{ for a general body})$$
  where $\sigma = 5.67 \times 10^{-8}\text{ W m}^{-2}\text{K}^{-4}$ (Stefan's constant).
* **Wien's Displacement Law:** The wavelength of maximum emission ($\lambda_m$) is inversely proportional to absolute temperature ($T$):
  $$\lambda_m T = b \quad (b \approx 2.898 \times 10^{-3}\text{ m}\cdot\text{K})$$
  * **Application:** Determines the **surface temperature and colour of stars**. **Blue stars are the hottest** (shortest $\lambda_m$), white/yellow stars are intermediate, and **Red stars are the coolest** (longest $\lambda_m$).
* **Newton's Law of Cooling:** The rate of cooling of a body is directly proportional to the temperature difference between the body and its surroundings, provided the temperature difference is small:
  $$-\frac{dT}{dt} \propto (T - T_0)$$
  * **Application:** Hot tea cools much faster from $90^\circ\text{C}$ to $80^\circ\text{C}$ than from $50^\circ\text{C}$ to $40^\circ\text{C}$ in the same room.

### 5.5 Laws of Thermodynamics, Processes & Engines

#### The Four Laws of Thermodynamics

| Law | Core Statement | Physical Quantity Defined | Key Exam Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Zeroth Law** | If system $A$ is in thermal equilibrium with $B$, and $B$ with $C$, then $A$ is in thermal equilibrium with $C$. | **Temperature ($T$)** | Basis of all thermometry (formulated by R.H. Fowler) |
| **First Law** | $\Delta Q = \Delta U + \Delta W$ (Heat supplied = change in internal energy + work done by system). | **Internal Energy ($U$)** | Restates **Law of Conservation of Energy**; perpetual motion machine of 1st kind is impossible |
| **Second Law** | **Kelvin–Planck:** No engine can convert 100% of heat into work in a cycle.<br>**Clausius:** Heat cannot spontaneously flow from cold to hot without external work. | **Entropy ($S$)** & **Direction of heat flow** | Entropy of an isolated system always increases or stays constant ($\Delta S \ge 0$) |
| **Third Law** | Entropy of a pure perfect crystal approaches zero as temperature approaches absolute zero ($0\text{ K}$). | **Absolute Entropy** | Reaching exact $0\text{ K}$ in finite steps is impossible |

#### Thermodynamic Processes

| Process | Constant Quantity | Equation / Law | First Law Form | Classic Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Isothermal** | Temperature ($T = \text{const}$) | $PV = \text{const}$ (Boyle's Law) | $\Delta U = 0 \implies \Delta Q = \Delta W$ | **Slow** compression/expansion in conducting cylinder |
| **Adiabatic** | Heat ($\Delta Q = 0$) | $PV^\gamma = \text{const}$ | $0 = \Delta U + \Delta W \implies \Delta W = -\Delta U$ | **Sudden bursting of a bicycle tyre** (air expands suddenly and cools!) |
| **Isobaric** | Pressure ($P = \text{const}$) | $V/T = \text{const}$ (Charles's Law) | $\Delta W = P\Delta V$ | Boiling water in an open vessel |
| **Isochoric** | Volume ($V = \text{const}$) | $P/T = \text{const}$ (Gay-Lussac's Law) | $\Delta W = 0 \implies \Delta Q = \Delta U$ | Heating gas inside a rigid sealed container |

#### Heat Engine, Carnot Engine & Refrigerator
* **Heat Engine:** Converts heat into mechanical work by taking heat $Q_1$ from a hot source ($T_1$), doing work $W = Q_1 - Q_2$, and rejecting $Q_2$ to a cold sink ($T_2$).
  $$\text{Efficiency } (\eta) = \frac{W}{Q_1} = 1 - \frac{Q_2}{Q_1}$$
* **Carnot Engine (Ideal Reversible Engine):** Maximum theoretical efficiency depends **only on source and sink temperatures (in Kelvin)**:
  $$\eta_{\text{Carnot}} = 1 - \frac{T_2}{T_1}$$
  *(Efficiency $= 100\%$ only if $T_2 = 0\text{ K}$, which is impossible).*
* **Refrigerator:** Operates as a heat engine in reverse; uses external electrical work $W$ to pump heat $Q_2$ from inside ($T_2$) to the outside room ($T_1$).
  $$\text{Coefficient of Performance (COP, } \beta\text{)} = \frac{Q_2}{W} = \frac{Q_2}{Q_1 - Q_2} = \frac{T_2}{T_1 - T_2}$$
  > **Classic SSC Trap:** *If the door of a working refrigerator is left open inside a closed room, the room becomes **WARMER*** (because the compressor rejects more heat $Q_1 = Q_2 + W$ into the room than it removes from it).

## 6. Waves, Oscillations & Sound

### 6.1 Oscillations & Simple Pendulum
* **Simple Harmonic Motion (SHM):** Restoring force is directly proportional to displacement and directed toward the mean position ($F = -kx$).
* **Simple Pendulum Time Period:**
  $$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}$$
  *(Independent of the **mass of the bob** and small **amplitude**).*
* **Exam-Tested Cases:**
  * **Girl stands up on a moving swing:** Centre of mass rises $\implies$ effective length $L$ decreases $\implies$ $T$ decreases $\implies$ swing oscillates **faster**.
  * **Inside an orbiting satellite / free-falling lift ($g_{\text{eff}} = 0$):** $T = \infty$ (pendulum does **not oscillate**).
  * **On the Moon ($g_{\text{moon}} = g/6$):** $T$ increases by $\sqrt{6}$ times (pendulum clock runs slow).
  * **Lift accelerating up ($g_{\text{eff}} = g + a$):** $T$ decreases (faster). **Lift accelerating down ($g_{\text{eff}} = g - a$):** $T$ increases (slower).
* **Universal Wave Relation:** $v = f\lambda$ (where $v =$ wave velocity, $f =$ frequency, $\lambda =$ wavelength).
  * When a wave passes from one medium to another, **speed ($v$) and wavelength ($\lambda$) change, but frequency ($f$) NEVER changes** (depends only on the source).

### 6.2 Wave Classifications

#### Transverse vs Longitudinal Waves

| Feature | Transverse Waves | Longitudinal Waves |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Particle Vibration** | **Perpendicular ($\perp$)** to direction of propagation | **Parallel ($\parallel$)** to direction of propagation |
| **Wave Pattern** | **Crests** and **Troughs** | **Compressions** (high density/pressure) and **Rarefactions** (low density/pressure) |
| **Medium** | Solids and liquid surfaces (and vacuum for EM waves) | **Solids, liquids, and gases** |
| **Polarization** | **Can be polarized** | **Cannot be polarized** |
| **Examples** | Light & all EM waves, ripples on water surface, vibrating string, Seismic **S-waves** | **Sound waves in air**, waves in a slinky/spring, Seismic **P-waves** |

#### Mechanical vs Electromagnetic Waves

| Feature | Mechanical (Elastic) Waves | Electromagnetic (EM) Waves |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Medium Requirement** | **Strictly require** a material medium (cannot travel in vacuum) | **Require NO medium** (travel in vacuum at $c = 3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$) |
| **Nature** | Can be longitudinal or transverse | Always **transverse**; oscillations of mutually $\perp$ Electric ($E$) & Magnetic ($B$) fields |
| **Charge / Deflection** | Material disturbance | **Uncharged** (not deflected by electric or magnetic fields) |
| **Examples** | Sound, water waves, seismic waves | Gamma, X-rays, UV, Visible, IR, Microwaves, Radio waves |

> **Exam Alert — NOT EM Waves:** **Cathode rays** (electrons), **Alpha ($\alpha$) rays** ($\text{He}^{2+}$ nuclei), **Beta ($\beta$) rays** (electrons/positrons), **Canal/Anode rays**, and **Sound/Ultrasonic waves** are **NOT** electromagnetic waves!

### 6.4 Sound Waves

#### Speed of Sound in Different Media
Sound is a **longitudinal mechanical wave**; it **cannot travel in vacuum** (e.g., astronauts on the Moon cannot hear each other without radio).

$$\text{Speed Order: } \mathbf{v_{\text{Solids}} > v_{\text{Liquids}} > v_{\text{Gases}}}$$

*Depends on elasticity ($E$) and density ($\rho$) of the medium: $v = \sqrt{E/\rho}$ (Solids have much higher elasticity/bulk modulus than gases).*

| Medium | Speed of Sound ($\text{m/s}$) | Exam Notes |
| :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Aluminium** | $\sim 6420\text{ m/s}$ | Highest among common metals |
| **Steel / Iron** | $\sim 5960\text{ m/s}$ (Iron $\sim 5950$) | Sound travels ~17× faster in steel than in air |
| **Glass (Flint)** | $\sim 3980\text{ m/s}$ | — |
| **Sea Water** | $\sim 1531\text{ m/s}$ | Faster than fresh water due to dissolved salts |
| **Distilled Water** | $\sim 1498\text{ m/s}$ ($\sim 1480\text{ m/s}$ at $20^\circ\text{C}$) | ~4.3× faster than air |
| **Hydrogen ($\text{H}_2$, $0^\circ\text{C}$)** | $\sim 1284\text{ m/s}$ | Fastest among gases (lowest molar mass) |
| **Air** | **$331\text{ m/s}$** ($0^\circ\text{C}$) / **$343\text{ m/s}$** ($20^\circ\text{C}$) / **$346\text{ m/s}$** ($25^\circ\text{C}$) | Standard exam value: **$332\text{ m/s}$** ($0^\circ\text{C}$) or **$343\text{–}344\text{ m/s}$** (room temp) |

#### Factors Affecting Speed of Sound in a Gas
Formula (Laplace's correction): $v = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma P}{\rho}} = \sqrt{\frac{\gamma R T}{M}}$

1. **Pressure:** **NO EFFECT at constant temperature!** *(Because when $P$ increases, density $\rho$ increases in the same proportion, keeping $P/\rho$ constant — one of the most repeated SSC questions).*
2. **Temperature:** **Increases** with temperature ($v \propto \sqrt{T}$). In air, speed increases by **$\approx 0.61\text{ m/s}$ per $1^\circ\text{C}$** rise.
3. **Humidity:** **Increases** with humidity. Moist air has lower density than dry air (molar mass of $\text{H}_2\text{O} = 18 < \text{dry air} \approx 29$), so sound travels **faster on a rainy/humid day** than on a dry day.
4. **Density / Molecular Mass:** $v \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho}}$ and $v \propto \frac{1}{\sqrt{M}}$ (at same $T, P$).
5. **Wind:** Increases if wind blows in the same direction as sound, decreases if opposite.

#### Frequency Classification of Sound

| Category | Frequency Range | Examples & Applications |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Infrasonic** | **$< 20\text{ Hz}$** | Produced by earthquakes, volcanoes, ocean waves; heard by **elephants, whales, rhinoceroses** |
| **Audible** | **$20\text{ Hz}$ to $20,000\text{ Hz}$ ($20\text{ kHz}$)** | Human hearing range (sensitivity drops for high frequencies with age) |
| **Ultrasonic** | **$> 20,000\text{ Hz}$ ($20\text{ kHz}$)** | Used/heard by **bats** (echolocation), **dolphins, dogs** (Galton whistle); **SONAR**, **ultrasonography**, detecting cracks in metal blocks, cleaning watch/electronic parts, breaking kidney stones (lithotripsy) |

#### Characteristics of Musical Sound

| Characteristic | Depends On | Key Exam Facts |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pitch (Shrillness)** | **Frequency ($f$)** | Higher frequency = higher pitch/shriller sound (mosquito buzz / woman's voice > lion's roar / man's voice) |
| **Loudness** | **Amplitude ($A$)** ($\text{Intensity } I \propto A^2$) | Measured in **Decibel ($\text{dB}$)** (logarithmic scale). Normal talk $\approx 60\text{ dB}$; whisper $\approx 20\text{–}30\text{ dB}$; WHO safe limit / noise pollution $\ge 75\text{–}80\text{ dB}$; pain threshold $\approx 120\text{–}130\text{ dB}$ |
| **Quality (Timbre)** | **Waveform / Overtones (Harmonics)** | Distinguishes two sounds of the **same pitch and loudness** (e.g., sitar vs flute, or recognizing a friend's voice) |

#### Echo, Reverberation, Doppler Effect, Mach Number, Resonance & SONAR
* **Echo:** Repetition of sound due to reflection from a rigid obstacle.
  * **Persistence of hearing** in human ear = **$0.1\text{ s}$** ($1/10\text{ s}$).
  * **Minimum distance** to hear a distinct echo in air (at $22^\circ\text{C}$, $v = 344\text{ m/s}$):
    $$d = \frac{v \times t}{2} = \frac{344 \times 0.1}{2} = \mathbf{17.2\text{ m}} \quad (\text{or } 17\text{ m at } 340\text{ m/s})$$
  * **Whispering Gallery** (e.g., Gol Gumbaz, Bijapur) works on **multiple reflections** of sound. **Stethoscope** also works on multiple reflections of sound.
* **Reverberation:** Persistence of sound in a big hall due to repeated reflections even after the source stops. Reduced by covering walls/ceilings with **sound-absorbing materials** (compressed fibreboard, rough plaster, heavy curtains, carpeted floors, cushioned seats).
* **Doppler Effect:** Apparent change in frequency (pitch) of a wave due to **relative motion** between source and observer.
  * Source & observer approaching $\rightarrow$ apparent frequency **increases** (higher pitch).
  * Source & observer moving apart $\rightarrow$ apparent frequency **decreases** (lower pitch).
  * **Applications:** Police speed radar guns, **Redshift** of galaxies proving the **expanding universe** (Edwin Hubble), Doppler ultrasound (blood flow).
  * *No Doppler shift* if source moves perpendicular to line of sight or both move with identical velocity.
* **Mach Number:**
  $$\text{Mach Number} = \frac{\text{Speed of body}}{\text{Speed of sound in the same medium}}$$
  * **Subsonic:** $\text{Mach} < 1$
  * **Transonic:** $\text{Mach} \approx 1$
  * **Supersonic:** $\text{Mach} > 1$ (produces shock waves $\rightarrow$ **Sonic Boom**, e.g., fighter jets, BrahMos missile)
  * **Hypersonic:** $\text{Mach} > 5$
* **Resonance:** A special case of forced vibrations where the **frequency of the external force equals the natural frequency** of the vibrating body, causing **maximum amplitude**.
  * **Examples:** **Soldiers are ordered to break step while crossing a suspension bridge** (to prevent resonant collapse); tuning a radio/TV to a station; shattering a wine glass with a matching musical note.
* **SONAR (Sound Navigation And Ranging):** Uses **ultrasonic waves** ($d = \frac{vt}{2}$) to measure ocean depth and locate underwater submarines, reefs, sunken ships, and icebergs.

## 7. Optics: Light, Mirrors, Lenses & Optical Phenomena

### 7.1 Nature of Light & Reflection

* **Nature of Light:** Dual nature — behaves as a **transverse electromagnetic wave** (interference, diffraction, polarization) and as **particles/photons** (photoelectric effect, Compton effect).
* **Speed of Light in Vacuum ($c$):** **$3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$** (first estimated by **Ole Roemer**).
  * **Sunlight** takes **~8 minutes 20 seconds ($\approx 500\text{ s}$)** to reach Earth.
  * **Moonlight** takes **~1.28 seconds ($\approx 1.3\text{ s}$)** to reach Earth.
* **Laws of Reflection** (valid for ALL mirrors — plane, concave, convex, and rough surfaces):
  1. Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection: $\angle i = \angle r$.
  2. Incident ray, reflected ray, and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.

#### Plane Mirror — Key Formulas & Properties
* **Image Nature:** Always **Virtual, Erect, Same size ($m = +1$), Laterally inverted** (left appears right), and formed at the same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front ($u = v$).
* **Focal Length & Power:** $f = \infty$, $P = 0\text{ D}$.
* **Minimum height of plane mirror** required for a person of height $H$ to see their full-length image = **$H/2$**.
* **Number of images ($N$)** formed by two plane mirrors inclined at angle $\theta$:
  * Compute $n = \frac{360^\circ}{\theta}$.
  * If $n$ is **even**: $N = n - 1$ (for any position).
  * If $n$ is **odd**: $N = n - 1$ (if object is on the angle bisector/symmetric); $N = n$ (if asymmetric). *(Standard SSC formula: $N = \frac{360}{\theta} - 1$; if parallel, $\theta = 0^\circ \implies N = \infty$)*.
* **Mirror rotation:** If a plane mirror is rotated by angle $\theta$, the reflected ray rotates by **$2\theta$**.
* **Relative speed:** If a person walks toward a plane mirror at speed $v$, the image approaches the person at **$2v$**.

---

### 7.2 Spherical Mirrors (Concave & Convex)

#### New Cartesian Sign Convention & Mirror Formula
*(Light travels from left to right; Pole $P$ is the origin)*
* **Object distance ($u$):** Always **negative ($-$)**.
* **Concave Mirror (Converging):** Focal length $f$ is **negative ($-$)**; Radius of curvature $R = 2f$ is **negative ($-$)**.
* **Convex Mirror (Diverging):** Focal length $f$ is **positive ($+$)**; $R$ is **positive ($+$)**.
* **Image distance ($v$) & Magnification ($m$):**
  * **Real & Inverted image:** $v$ is **negative ($-$)**, $m$ is **negative ($-$)**.
  * **Virtual & Erect image:** $v$ is **positive ($+$)**, $m$ is **positive ($+$)**.
* **Mirror Formula:** $\displaystyle \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} + \frac{1}{u}$
* **Linear Magnification:** $\displaystyle m = \frac{h_i}{h_o} = -\frac{v}{u}$

#### Complete Concave Mirror Image Formation Table (All 6 Positions)

| Object Position | Image Position | Nature of Image | Size of Image ($m$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. At Infinity ($\infty$)** | At Focus ($F$) | Real & Inverted | Highly diminished (point-sized) |
| **2. Beyond Centre of Curvature ($C$)** | Between $F$ and $C$ | Real & Inverted | Diminished ($\|m\| < 1$) |
| **3. At $C$** | At $C$ | Real & Inverted | **Same size** ($m = -1$) |
| **4. Between $C$ and $F$** | Beyond $C$ | Real & Inverted | Magnified / Enlarged ($\|m\| > 1$) |
| **5. At Focus ($F$)** | At Infinity ($\infty$) | Real & Inverted | Highly magnified |
| **6. Between $F$ and Pole ($P$)** | **Behind the mirror** | **Virtual & Erect** | **Magnified ($m > +1$)** *(Only virtual case!)* |

#### Complete Convex Mirror Image Formation Table (2 Positions)

| Object Position | Image Position | Nature of Image | Size of Image ($m$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. At Infinity ($\infty$)** | At $F$ (behind mirror) | Virtual & Erect | Highly diminished (point-sized) |
| **2. Anywhere between $\infty$ and $P$** | Between $P$ and $F$ (behind mirror) | **Virtual & Erect** | **Diminished ($0 < m < +1$)** |

#### Uses of Spherical Mirrors
* **Concave Mirror:** **Shaving & makeup mirrors** (object within $F$ gives enlarged erect image), **dentist & ENT doctor mirrors**, **vehicle headlights, torches & searchlights** (bulb at $F$ produces parallel beam), **solar cookers/furnaces**, astronomical reflecting telescopes.
* **Convex Mirror:** **Rear-view / side-view mirrors in vehicles** (always forms an erect, diminished image and provides a **much wider field of view**), blind-turn road mirrors, ATM/shop security mirrors, street-light reflectors.

### 7.3 Refraction of Light & Refractive Index

* **Refraction:** Bending of light when it passes obliquely from one transparent medium to another due to a **change in speed of light**.
  * **Rarer $\rightarrow$ Denser:** Speed decreases, ray bends **towards the normal** ($\angle i > \angle r$).
  * **Denser $\rightarrow$ Rarer:** Speed increases, ray bends **away from the normal** ($\angle r > \angle i$).
  * *Note:* **Frequency remains unchanged**; speed and wavelength change.
* **Snell's Law:** $\displaystyle \frac{\sin i}{\sin r} = \text{constant} = n_{21} = \frac{n_2}{n_1} = \frac{v_1}{v_2}$
* **Absolute Refractive Index ($n$):** $\displaystyle n = \frac{c}{v}$ (always $\ge 1$; dimensionless).
  * **Standard Values (NCERT):** Vacuum $= 1$, Air $\approx 1.0003$, Ice $= 1.31$, **Water $= 1.33$**, **Crown Glass $= 1.50\text{–}1.52$**, Flint Glass $= 1.65$, **Diamond $= 2.42$** *(highest among common natural transparent materials; speed of light is slowest in diamond)*.
* **Real Depth vs Apparent Depth:**
  $$n = \frac{\text{Real Depth}}{\text{Apparent Depth}}$$
  * **Examples:** A **coin at the bottom of a water tank appears raised**; a pencil/stick partially immersed in water appears bent; **advanced sunrise & delayed sunset** (Sun visible ~2 minutes before actual sunrise and ~2 minutes after actual sunset = **~4 minutes extra daylight** due to atmospheric refraction); **twinkling of stars** (due to fluctuating atmospheric refraction; *planets do not twinkle* because they are closer and act as extended sources).

### 7.4 Lenses (Convex & Concave)

* **Lens Formula:** $\displaystyle \frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{v} - \frac{1}{u}$
* **Magnification:** $\displaystyle m = \frac{h_i}{h_o} = +\frac{v}{u}$
* **Sign Convention:**
  * **Convex (Converging) Lens:** Focal length $f$ is **Positive ($+$)**.
  * **Concave (Diverging) Lens:** Focal length $f$ is **Negative ($-$)**.
* **Power of a Lens ($P$):** Measure of degree of convergence/divergence:
  $$P = \frac{1}{f\text{ (in metres)}} = \frac{100}{f\text{ (in cm)}}$$
  * **SI Unit:** **Dioptre ($\text{D}$)** ($1\text{ D} = 1\text{ m}^{-1}$). Convex lens has **positive power**, Concave lens has **negative power**.
  * **Lenses in contact:** $P = P_1 + P_2$ and $\frac{1}{f} = \frac{1}{f_1} + \frac{1}{f_2}$.

#### Complete Convex Lens Image Formation Table (All 6 Positions)

| Object Position | Image Position | Nature of Image | Size of Image ($m$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. At Infinity ($\infty$)** | At $F_2$ | Real & Inverted | Highly diminished (point-sized) |
| **2. Beyond $2F_1$** | Between $F_2$ and $2F_2$ | Real & Inverted | Diminished ($\|m\| < 1$) |
| **3. At $2F_1$** | At $2F_2$ | Real & Inverted | **Same size** ($m = -1$) |
| **4. Between $F_1$ and $2F_1$** | Beyond $2F_2$ | Real & Inverted | Magnified ($\|m\| > 1$) |
| **5. At $F_1$** | At Infinity ($\infty$) | Real & Inverted | Highly magnified |
| **6. Between $F_1$ and Optical Centre ($O$)** | **On the same side as object** | **Virtual & Erect** | **Magnified ($m > +1$)** *(Magnifying glass)* |

#### Complete Concave Lens Image Formation Table (2 Positions)

| Object Position | Image Position | Nature of Image | Size of Image ($m$) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. At Infinity ($\infty$)** | At $F_1$ (same side) | Virtual & Erect | Highly diminished (point-sized) |
| **2. Anywhere between $\infty$ and $O$** | Between $F_1$ and $O$ (same side) | **Virtual & Erect** | **Diminished ($0 < m < +1$)** |

> **Exam Exceptions on Lenses:**
> * **An air bubble inside water behaves as a CONCAVE (diverging) lens** (since $n_{\text{air}} < n_{\text{water}}$), even though it looks convex in shape!
> * If a glass lens ($n = 1.5$) is immersed in a liquid of the **same refractive index ($n = 1.5$)**, its focal length becomes **$\infty$**, power becomes **$0$**, and the lens becomes **invisible**. If immersed in a liquid with $n > 1.5$ (e.g., $\text{CS}_2$, $n=1.63$), a convex lens behaves like a diverging lens.

### 7.5 Optical Phenomena

#### 1. Total Internal Reflection (TIR)
* **Two Necessary Conditions:**
  1. Light must travel from an **optically denser medium to a rarer medium**.
  2. Angle of incidence must be **greater than the Critical Angle ($i > i_c$)**, where $\sin i_c = \frac{1}{n}$ (Critical angles: Diamond $\approx 24.4^\circ$, Crown Glass $\approx 41.1^\circ\text{–}42^\circ$, Water $\approx 48.75^\circ$).
* **Applications & Examples:**
  * **Sparkling / brilliance of diamond** (very high $n = 2.42 \implies$ very small $i_c \approx 24.4^\circ$).
  * **Mirage** in hot deserts (inverted virtual image due to refraction + TIR) & **Looming** in cold polar regions.
  * **Optical Fibres** (telecommunications, high-speed internet, medical **endoscopy**; **Narinder Singh Kapany** — *"Father of Fibre Optics"*).
  * Air bubble in water shining like silver; cracks in glass glittering; $45^\circ\text{–}90^\circ\text{–}45^\circ$ **totally reflecting prisms** in periscopes and binoculars.

#### 2. Dispersion of Light
* Splitting of white light into its **7 constituent colours (VIBGYOR)** when passing through a prism (discovered by **Isaac Newton**).
* **Cause:** Different colours have different wavelengths and travel at **different speeds inside glass** ($n_{\text{violet}} > n_{\text{red}}$).
* **Order of Deviation:** **Violet** has the shortest wavelength, highest refractive index, lowest speed in glass, and **bends/deviates MOST**. **Red** has the longest wavelength, lowest refractive index, highest speed in glass, and **bends/deviates LEAST**.
* **Rainbow Formation:** Caused by **Dispersion + Refraction + Internal Reflection** of sunlight inside suspended raindrops; always seen **opposite to the Sun**.
  * **Primary Rainbow:** **2 refractions + 1 internal reflection**; brighter; **Red on outer/top, Violet on inner/bottom**.
  * **Secondary Rainbow:** **2 refractions + 2 internal reflections**; fainter; **Violet on outer/top, Red on inner/bottom** (reversed order).

#### 3. Scattering of Light
* **Rayleigh Scattering:** When scattering particle size $\ll \lambda$, scattered intensity $\displaystyle I \propto \frac{1}{\lambda^4}$. Shorter wavelengths (Blue/Violet) scatter much more than longer wavelengths (Red).
  * **Blue colour of sky** (blue scatters much more than red; our eyes are more sensitive to blue than violet).
  * **Reddish colour of Sun at sunrise & sunset** (light travels longest atmospheric path; blue scatters away, leaving red).
  * **Red danger signals / tail lights** (longest $\lambda$, least scattered by fog/smoke, visible from afar).
  * **Sky appears BLACK to astronauts / from Moon** (no atmosphere $\implies$ no scattering).
* **Tyndall Effect:** Scattering of light by **colloidal particles** making the light beam visible (e.g., sunlight entering a dusty/smoky room or dense forest canopy; blue appearance of sea water / iris).
* **Raman Effect:** Inelastic scattering of light by molecules accompanied by a change in wavelength/frequency. Discovered by **Sir C.V. Raman** on **28 February 1928** $\rightarrow$ celebrated every year in India as **National Science Day (Feb 28)**; won the **1930 Nobel Prize in Physics**.

#### 4. Interference, Diffraction & Polarization
* **Interference:** Superposition of two coherent waves producing bright (constructive) and dark (destructive) fringes (Young's Double Slit Experiment — **proves wave nature of light**).
  * **Examples:** Brilliant colours of **soap bubbles** and **thin oil films on water**.
* **Diffraction:** Bending of light around sharp edges of obstacles or narrow slits (comparable to $\lambda$). Sound diffracts easily ($\lambda \sim 1\text{ m}$), whereas light diffracts minimally ($\lambda \sim 10^{-7}\text{ m}$).
* **Polarization:** Restricting vibrations of the electric field vector of light to a single plane.
  * **Crucial Exam Fact:** **Polarization proves that light is a TRANSVERSE wave** (longitudinal waves like sound **cannot** be polarized).
  * **Uses:** **Polaroid sunglasses** (cut glare from water/roads), **3D movie glasses**, LCD screens.

### 7.6 Human Eye, Defects & Optical Instruments

#### Human Eye Quick Facts
* **Image formed on Retina:** **Real and Inverted**.
* **Photoreceptor cells:** **Rods** (sensitive to dim light / intensity; Vitamin A deficiency affects rods $\rightarrow$ Night Blindness) and **Cones** (sensitive to bright light & **colour vision**).
* **Iris & Pupil:** Iris regulates the size of the pupil, controlling the amount of light entering the eye.
* **Near Point (Least distance of distinct vision):** **$25\text{ cm}$**; **Far Point:** **Infinity ($\infty$)**.
* **Persistence of Vision:** **$1/16\text{ s}$** ($\approx 0.0625\text{ s}$).

#### Human Eye Defects Table

| Defect | Vision Symptom | Image Position & Cause | Corrective Lens / Treatment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Myopia** *(Near/Short-sightedness)* | Near clear; **Distant blurry** | Forms **in front of retina** (eyeball elongated or lens too curved) | **Concave (Diverging) lens** ($-$ power) |
| **Hypermetropia** *(Far/Long-sightedness)* | Distant clear; **Near blurry** | Forms **behind retina** (eyeball too short or focal length too long) | **Convex (Converging) lens** ($+$ power) |
| **Presbyopia** | Both near & far blurry in **old age** | Weakening of **ciliary muscles** & loss of lens flexibility (reduced accommodation) | **Bifocal lens** (upper = concave for distance, lower = convex for reading) |
| **Astigmatism** | Horizontal & vertical lines cannot be focused simultaneously | Uneven / non-spherical curvature of **cornea** or eye lens | **Cylindrical lens** |
| **Cataract** | Cloudy / milky / hazy vision | Eye lens becomes **opaque** (usually with ageing) | **Surgery** (replacing cloudy lens with artificial Intraocular Lens) |
| **Colour Blindness** | Cannot distinguish certain colours (usually Red–Green) | Genetic (X-linked) defect/absence of **cone cells** | **No lens correction / no permanent cure** |

#### Optical Instruments
* **Simple Microscope:** Single convex lens of short focal length ($u < f$); forms **virtual, erect, magnified** image.
* **Compound Microscope:** Two convex lenses — **Objective** (small $f$, small aperture) and **Eyepiece** (moderate $f$, larger aperture); final image is **virtual, inverted, highly magnified**.
* **Astronomical Telescope (Refracting):** Two convex lenses — **Objective** (**large $f$, large aperture** to gather more light) and **Eyepiece** (small $f$, small aperture); final image is **virtual and inverted**. *(Terrestrial telescope adds an erecting lens; Galilean telescope uses a concave eyepiece for an erect image).*
* **Periscope:** Used in submarines and bunkers; uses two plane mirrors (or $45^\circ$ TIR prisms) placed parallel to each other at **$45^\circ$** to the horizontal/vertical.
* **Kaleidoscope:** Uses three plane mirrors inclined at **$60^\circ$** to produce symmetric multiple images.

## 8. Electricity, Magnetism & Modern Physics

### 8.1 Electrostatics
* **Electric Charge ($Q$):** Scalar; **SI Unit:** **Coulomb ($\text{C}$)**.
  * **Quantization of Charge:** $Q = \pm n e$, where $e = 1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$.
  * **Conservation of Charge:** Net charge of an isolated system remains constant.
* **Coulomb's Law:** Force between two point charges $q_1, q_2$ separated by distance $r$:
  $$F = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2} \quad \left(\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} = 9 \times 10^9\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}^2\right)$$
* **Electric Field ($E = F/q$):** Unit $\text{N/C}$ or $\text{V/m}$.
  * **Faraday Cage / Electrostatic Shielding:** **Electric field inside a hollow charged conductor is ALWAYS ZERO ($E = 0$)**. Hence, during a thunderstorm/lightning, it is **safer to sit inside a closed car** than to stand under a tree or in an open field.
* **Electric Potential ($V = W/q$):** Scalar; **SI Unit:** **Volt ($\text{V}$)** ($1\text{ V} = 1\text{ J/C}$).
* **Lightning Conductor:** Invented by **Benjamin Franklin**. Consists of a pointed **Copper** spike at the top of a tall building connected via a thick copper strip to a metal plate buried deep in the moist earth (provides an easy, low-resistance path for lightning discharge).
* **Gold-Leaf Electroscope:** Detects the **presence** and **nature (polarity)** of electric charge on a body.

---

### 8.2 Current Electricity & Domestic Circuits

#### Fundamental Relations
* **Electric Current ($I = Q/t$):** Scalar (does not obey vector addition); **SI Unit:** **Ampere ($\text{A}$)**. Conventional current flows from $+$ to $-$ (opposite to electron flow).
* **Ohm's Law:** At constant temperature and physical conditions, $V \propto I \implies \mathbf{V = I R}$.
  * **Ohmic conductors:** Metals (linear $V\text{–}I$ graph). **Non-ohmic:** Semiconductors, diodes, transistors, electrolytes.
* **Resistance ($R$) & Resistivity ($\rho$):**
  $$R = \rho \frac{l}{A}$$
  * **Unit of $R$:** **Ohm ($\Omega$)**; **Unit of Resistivity ($\rho$):** **Ohm-metre ($\Omega\cdot\text{m}$)**.
  * **Resistivity ($\rho$)** depends **ONLY on the material and temperature** — it does **NOT** depend on length or thickness! (Silver has the lowest resistivity / highest conductivity among metals, followed by Copper).
  * **Effect of Stretching a Wire** (volume $V = A \cdot l$ remains constant):
    * If a wire is stretched to **$n$ times its original length**, area becomes $A/n$, so **new resistance $R_{\text{new}} = n^2 R$** ($R \propto l^2$).
    * In terms of radius: **$R \propto \frac{1}{r^4}$** (if radius is halved, resistance becomes $16$ times!).
  * **Effect of Temperature on Resistance:**
    * **Metals (Conductors):** $R$ **increases** with temperature ($\alpha > 0$).
    * **Semiconductors ($\text{Si, Ge}$) & Electrolytes:** $R$ **decreases** with temperature ($\alpha < 0$).
    * **Alloys (Manganin, Constantan):** Very high $\rho$ and negligible temperature dependence ($\alpha \approx 0$) $\rightarrow$ used in **standard resistance coils**.
    * **Superconductors:** Resistance drops abruptly to **zero** below a critical temperature (discovered by **Heike Kamerlingh Onnes**, 1911, in Mercury at $4.2\text{ K}$).

#### Series vs Parallel Combinations

| Feature | Series Combination | Parallel Combination |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Formula** | $R_{\text{eq}} = R_1 + R_2 + R_3 + \dots$ | $\displaystyle \frac{1}{R_{\text{eq}}} = \frac{1}{R_1} + \frac{1}{R_2} + \frac{1}{R_3} + \dots$ |
| **Same Quantity** | **Current ($I$)** is same in all | **Voltage ($V$)** is same across all |
| **Equivalent $R$** | Greater than the largest individual $R$ | **Smaller than the smallest individual $R$** |
| **Appliance Control** | One switch controls all; if one fails, all stop (e.g., decorative fairy lights) | Independent switches; failure of one does not affect others |
| **Use** | Fuse, decorative series lights, voltage division | **Household / domestic wiring** |

#### Kirchhoff's Laws
1. **Kirchhoff's Current Law (KCL / Junction Rule):** $\sum I = 0$ at any junction $\rightarrow$ based on **Conservation of Charge**.
2. **Kirchhoff's Voltage Law (KVL / Loop Rule):** $\sum V = 0$ around any closed loop $\rightarrow$ based on **Conservation of Energy**.

#### Electric Power, Energy & Joule's Law of Heating
* **Electric Power ($P$):** $\displaystyle P = V I = I^2 R = \frac{V^2}{R}$ (**SI Unit:** **Watt ($\text{W}$)**; $1\text{ HP} = 746\text{ W}$).
* **Commercial Unit of Electrical Energy:** **Kilowatt-hour ($\text{kWh}$)** = **1 Unit**:
  $$1\text{ kWh} = 1000\text{ W} \times 3600\text{ s} = \mathbf{3.6 \times 10^6\text{ J}}$$
* **Joule's Law of Heating:** Heat produced in a resistor:
  $$H = I^2 R t = V I t = \frac{V^2}{R} t$$

#### Heating Elements, Fuse, Bulb & Domestic Wiring (India)
* **Electric Fuse:** Safety device connected in **series with the Live wire**; works on **heating effect of current**. Made of an alloy of **Tin ($\text{Sn} \approx 63\%$) + Lead ($\text{Pb} \approx 37\%$)** having **high resistance and LOW melting point**. *(MCB — Miniature Circuit Breaker works on magnetic/thermal trip and is reusable).*
* **Electric Bulb Filament:** Made of **Tungsten ($\text{W}$)** — **highest melting point among metals ($\approx 3380^\circ\text{C}\text{–}3422^\circ\text{C}$)**. Bulb is filled with **inert gases (Argon + Nitrogen)** at low pressure to prevent oxidation and slow down tungsten evaporation.
* **Heater / Iron / Toaster Coil:** Made of **Nichrome** (alloy of Nickel + Chromium + Iron/Manganese) — **high resistivity, high melting point**, and does not oxidize easily when red hot.
* **Indian Domestic Supply:** **$220\text{ V}$, $50\text{ Hz}$ Alternating Current (AC)**.
* **Wire Colour Codes:**
  * **Live (Phase) Wire:** **Red** (traditional) / **Brown** (new IEC standard).
  * **Neutral Wire:** **Black** (traditional) / **Light Blue** (new IEC standard).
  * **Earth (Ground) Wire:** **Green** (or Green–Yellow).

#### Measuring Instruments (Galvanometer, Ammeter, Voltmeter, Shunt)

| Instrument | Purpose | Circuit Connection | Resistance | How Made from Galvanometer |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Galvanometer** | Detects presence & direction of small current | Series | Moderate | — |
| **Ammeter** | Measures electric current ($\text{A}$) | **Series** | **Very Low** *(Ideal = **Zero**)* | Connecting a **low-resistance Shunt in parallel** |
| **Voltmeter** | Measures potential difference ($\text{V}$) | **Parallel** | **Very High** *(Ideal = **Infinite**)* | Connecting a **high resistance in series** |

### 8.3 Magnetism & Electromagnetism

* **Magnetic Field ($B$):** **SI Unit:** **Tesla ($\text{T}$)** ($= \text{Wb/m}^2$); **CGS Unit:** **Gauss ($\text{G}$)** ($1\text{ T} = 10^4\text{ G}$).
* **Magnetic Flux ($\Phi$):** **SI Unit:** **Weber ($\text{Wb}$)**.
* **Magnetic Dip & Earth's Magnetism:** Angle of dip is **$0^\circ$ at the Magnetic Equator** and **$90^\circ$ at the Magnetic Poles**.

#### Classification of Magnetic Materials

| Property | Diamagnetic | Paramagnetic | Ferromagnetic |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Behaviour in Magnetic Field** | **Weakly repelled** | **Weakly attracted** | **Strongly attracted** |
| **Relative Permeability ($\mu_r$)** | $0 \le \mu_r < 1$ | $\mu_r > 1$ (slightly) | $\mu_r \gg 1$ (thousands) |
| **Susceptibility ($\chi$)** | Small **negative** | Small **positive** | Very large **positive** |
| **Temperature Effect** | Independent of $T$ | Follows **Curie's Law** ($\chi \propto 1/T$) | Becomes **paramagnetic above Curie Temperature ($T_C$)** |
| **Examples** | **Bismuth, Copper, Gold, Silver, Water, Mercury, Hydrogen, Quartz** | **Aluminium, Platinum, Oxygen ($\text{O}_2$), Sodium, Chromium, Manganese** | **Iron ($\text{Fe}$), Cobalt ($\text{Co}$), Nickel ($\text{Ni}$), Gadolinium ($\text{Gd}$), Alnico** |

* **Curie Temperature ($T_C$):** Temperature above which ferromagnetic materials lose permanent magnetism and turn paramagnetic (Iron $\approx 770^\circ\text{C}$, Cobalt $\approx 1130^\circ\text{C}$, Nickel $\approx 358^\circ\text{C}$).
* **Electromagnets (Temporary Magnets):** Use **Soft Iron** core (high permeability, low retentivity, low coercivity).
* **Permanent Magnets:** Use **Steel or Alnico** ($\text{Al + Ni + Co}$) (high retentivity, high coercivity).

#### Electromagnetism, EMI & Hand Rules
* **Oersted's Discovery (1820):** A current-carrying conductor produces a magnetic field around it.
* **Faraday's Laws of Electromagnetic Induction (EMI):** Changing magnetic flux linked with a circuit induces an EMF ($e = -d\Phi/dt$).
* **Lenz's Law:** The polarity/direction of induced current always **opposes the change in magnetic flux that produces it** $\rightarrow$ directly follows the **Law of Conservation of Energy**.
* **Fleming's Rules** *(Thumb = Motion/Force, Forefinger = Magnetic Field, Middle finger = Current)*:
  * **Fleming's Left-Hand Rule:** Gives direction of **force/motion** on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field $\rightarrow$ principle of **Electric Motor** (Electrical $\rightarrow$ Mechanical energy).
  * **Fleming's Right-Hand Rule:** Gives direction of **induced current** $\rightarrow$ principle of **Electric Generator / Dynamo** (Mechanical $\rightarrow$ Electrical energy).

#### AC vs DC, Rectifier, Inverter & Transformer
* **AC (Alternating Current):** Reverses direction periodically ($50\text{ Hz}$ in India $\implies$ reverses direction $100$ times/s; $60\text{ Hz}$ in USA). Can be stepped up/down easily, minimizing $I^2R$ heat losses in long-distance power transmission.
* **DC (Direct Current):** Flows in one constant direction ($0\text{ Hz}$). Source: Batteries, solar cells; used in electroplating, electronics.
* **Rectifier:** Converts **AC $\rightarrow$ DC** (uses semiconductor diodes).
* **Inverter:** Converts **DC $\rightarrow$ AC** (used in home UPS/backup systems).
* **Transformer:** Static device that steps up or steps down **AC voltage** based on **Mutual Induction**.
  * **CRITICAL EXAM RULE:** A transformer **works ONLY on AC, NEVER on DC** (steady DC gives zero flux change and would burn out the primary winding).
  * **Step-Up Transformer:** $N_s > N_p \implies V_s > V_p$ and $I_s < I_p$ (conserves power: $V_p I_p = V_s I_s$).
  * **Step-Down Transformer:** $N_s < N_p \implies V_s < V_p$ and $I_s > I_p$.
  * **Laminated Soft Iron Core:** Soft iron reduces hysteresis loss; **lamination** (thin insulated sheets) reduces **eddy current loss**.

### 8.4 Semiconductors & Electronics
* **Semiconductors:** Materials with conductivity between conductors and insulators (e.g., **Silicon ($\text{Si}$), Germanium ($\text{Ge}$)**). Forbidden energy gap $\approx 1\text{ eV}$ ($\text{Si} = 1.1\text{ eV}$, $\text{Ge} = 0.7\text{ eV}$). Resistance **decreases** with increasing temperature.
* **Intrinsic Semiconductor:** Pure $\text{Si}$ or $\text{Ge}$ ($n_e = n_h$).
* **Extrinsic Semiconductor (Doped):** Conductivity dramatically increased by adding impurity atoms (**Doping**):

| Type | Dopant Valency & Examples | Majority Charge Carriers | Minority Charge Carriers |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **n-type** | **Pentavalent** (Group 15: **Phosphorus, Arsenic, Antimony**) | **Electrons** | Holes |
| **p-type** | **Trivalent** (Group 13: **Boron, Aluminium, Indium, Gallium**) | **Holes** | Electrons |

* **Key Semiconductor Devices:**
  * **p–n Junction Diode:** Conducts in **forward bias**, blocks in **reverse bias** $\rightarrow$ used as a **Rectifier** ($\text{AC} \to \text{DC}$).
  * **Zener Diode:** Operated in **reverse breakdown** region $\rightarrow$ used as a **Voltage Regulator**.
  * **LED (Light Emitting Diode):** Forward-biased ($\text{GaAs}, \text{GaP}$); converts electrical energy $\rightarrow$ light.
  * **Solar Cell (Photovoltaic Cell):** Converts solar energy $\rightarrow$ DC electrical energy (made of **Silicon**).
  * **Transistor (BJT — NPN / PNP):** Invented by **Bardeen, Brattain & Shockley** (1956 Nobel); acts as an **amplifier** and **high-speed electronic switch**.

### 8.5 Modern Physics & Nuclear Energy

* **Cathode Rays (J.J. Thomson, 1897):** Stream of negatively charged **electrons** emitted from cathode in a discharge tube; travel in straight lines, deflected by electric and magnetic fields, produce X-rays on hitting heavy metals.
* **X-Rays (Wilhelm Roentgen, 1895):** Short-wavelength **EM waves**; **uncharged** (not deflected by $E$ or $B$ fields); stopped by bones and lead.
* **Photoelectric Effect:** Discovered by **Hertz**; explained by **Albert Einstein (1905)** using **Max Planck's quantum theory** ($E = h\nu$) $\rightarrow$ Einstein won the **1921 Nobel Prize in Physics** for this (NOT for Relativity!).
  * Electrons are ejected only if incident light frequency $\nu \ge$ **threshold frequency ($\nu_0$)**.
  * **Proves particle nature of light (Photons)**.
  * Alkali metals (**Cesium**, Potassium, Sodium) have low work function and show photoelectric effect with visible light.
  * Kinetic energy of photoelectrons depends on **frequency**; photocurrent depends on **intensity**.

#### Radioactivity & $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ Comparison
* Discovered by **Henri Becquerel (1896)** in Uranium salts; term coined and **Polonium & Radium** discovered by **Marie & Pierre Curie**.
* Spontaneous nuclear decay of unstable heavy nuclei ($Z > 82$); **unaffected by temperature, pressure, or chemical combination**.
* **SI Unit:** **Becquerel ($\text{Bq}$)** ($= 1\text{ decay/s}$). Other units: **Curie ($\text{Ci}$)** $= 3.7 \times 10^{10}\text{ Bq}$; **Rutherford ($\text{Rd}$)** $= 10^6\text{ Bq}$.

| Property | Alpha ($\alpha$) Rays | Beta ($\beta$) Rays | Gamma ($\gamma$) Rays |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature** | Helium nucleus ($_2^4\text{He}^{2+}$) | Fast electrons ($\beta^-$) or positrons ($\beta^+$) | High-energy **EM waves** (photons) |
| **Charge** | **$+2e$** | **$-1e$** (or $+1e$) | **$0$ (Neutral)** |
| **Mass** | $4\text{ u}$ (heaviest) | $\approx 1/1836\text{ u}$ (negligible) | Zero rest mass |
| **Speed** | $\sim 0.05c\text{–}0.1c$ (slowest) | Up to $0.99c$ | **$c = 3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$** |
| **Deflection in $E/B$ Field** | Deflected toward negative plate | Deflected toward positive plate (larger deflection) | **Undeflected** |
| **Ionizing Power** | **Highest** ($\sim 100\times \beta$) | Moderate | **Lowest** |
| **Penetrating Power** | **Lowest** (stopped by paper/skin) | Moderate (stopped by few mm of Al) | **Highest** (needs thick lead/concrete) |

* **Half-Life ($T_{1/2}$):** Time required for half of the radioactive nuclei in a sample to decay:
  $$T_{1/2} = \frac{0.693}{\lambda}, \qquad N = N_0 \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^n \quad \left(\text{where } n = \frac{t}{T_{1/2}}\right)$$
* **Radioactive Isotopes & Dating:**
  * **Carbon-14 ($\text{C-14}$, $T_{1/2} \approx 5730\text{ yrs}$):** **Radiocarbon dating** of **fossils, bones, wood, organic archaeological remains** (Willard Libby).
  * **Uranium-238 ($\text{U-238} \to \text{Pb-206}$, $T_{1/2} \approx 4.5 \times 10^9\text{ yrs}$):** Age of **Earth, rocks, and meteorites**.
  * **Cobalt-60:** Cancer radiotherapy ($\gamma$-rays).
  * **Iodine-131:** Thyroid disorder/cancer treatment.
  * **Sodium-24:** Detecting blood clots / blood circulation.
  * **Phosphorus-32:** Bone/skin disorders & leukemia.

#### Nuclear Fission vs Nuclear Fusion

| Feature | Nuclear Fission | Nuclear Fusion |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Process** | Heavy nucleus ($\text{U-235}, \text{Pu-239}$) splits into two lighter nuclei on **slow neutron** bombardment | Two light nuclei ($\text{H}$ isotopes: Deuterium + Tritium) fuse to form heavier $\text{He}$ nucleus |
| **Discoverer** | **Otto Hahn & Fritz Strassmann** (1938) | **Hans Bethe** (explained solar fusion, 1939) |
| **Conditions** | Room temperature (slow/thermal neutrons) | Extremely high temperature ($\sim 10^7\text{ K}$) & high pressure (**Thermonuclear**) |
| **Energy Released** | Huge ($\sim 200\text{ MeV}$ per U-235 fission) | **Even greater energy per unit mass** than fission |
| **Applications** | **Nuclear Reactor** (controlled), **Atom Bomb** (uncontrolled) | **Sun & Stars** (source of energy), **Hydrogen Bomb** (uncontrolled, triggered by a fission bomb) |

#### Nuclear Reactor & Indian Nuclear Power Stations
* **Key Components of a Nuclear Reactor:**
  * **Fuel:** $\text{U-235}$, $\text{Pu-239}$, $\text{U-233}$ (bred from India's abundant Thorium-232 reserves).
  * **Moderator** *(slows down fast neutrons to thermal speed)*: **Heavy Water ($\text{D}_2\text{O}$)**, **Graphite**, Ordinary water ($\text{H}_2\text{O}$).
  * **Control Rods** *(absorb excess neutrons to regulate/stop chain reaction)*: **Cadmium ($\text{Cd}$)** or **Boron ($\text{B}$)** rods.
  * **Coolant** *(extracts heat)*: Liquid Sodium ($\text{Na}$), Heavy water, Ordinary water, $\text{CO}_2$.
  * **Shielding:** Thick concrete & steel walls.
* **India's Landmark Nuclear Reactors (Exam Must-Know):**
  * **Father of Indian Nuclear Programme:** **Dr. Homi Jehangir Bhabha**.
  * **Apsara** (Trombay, Mumbai, **1956**): **India's & Asia's 1st nuclear research reactor** (swimming-pool type).
  * **Tarapur Atomic Power Station (TAPS, Maharashtra, 1969):** **India's 1st commercial nuclear power plant**.
  * **Kudankulam (Tamil Nadu):** **Largest nuclear power plant in India** (built with Russian collaboration).
  * **Other Operational Plants:** **Rawatbhata** (Rajasthan), **Kalpakkam / Madras** (Tamil Nadu), **Narora** (Uttar Pradesh), **Kakrapar** (Gujarat), **Kaiga** (Karnataka).

### 9.3 35 Solved SSC CHSL / CGL / GD Previous Year Questions (PYQs)

**Q1. At what temperature do the Celsius and Fahrenheit scales show the exact same reading?**
(A) $0^\circ$  
(B) $-273^\circ$  
(C) $-40^\circ$  
(D) $40^\circ$  
> **Answer: (C) $-40^\circ$**  
> **Explanation:** Putting $C = F = x$ in $\frac{C}{5} = \frac{F-32}{9}$ gives $9x = 5x - 160 \implies 4x = -160 \implies x = -40^\circ$.

**Q2. Water has maximum density at which temperature?**
(A) $0^\circ\text{C}$  
(B) $4^\circ\text{C}$  
(C) $-4^\circ\text{C}$  
(D) $100^\circ\text{C}$  
> **Answer: (B) $4^\circ\text{C}$**  
> **Explanation:** Due to anomalous expansion of water between $0^\circ\text{C}$ and $4^\circ\text{C}$, water has minimum volume and maximum density ($1\text{ g/cm}^3$ or $1000\text{ kg/m}^3$) at $4^\circ\text{C}$.

**Q3. Steam at $100^\circ\text{C}$ causes more severe burns than boiling water at $100^\circ\text{C}$ because:**
(A) Steam is a gas  
(B) Steam has higher specific heat  
(C) Steam contains extra latent heat of vaporization  
(D) Steam exerts higher pressure  
> **Answer: (C) Steam contains extra latent heat of vaporization**  
> **Explanation:** Steam at $100^\circ\text{C}$ carries an extra $540\text{ cal/g}$ ($2260\text{ J/g}$) of latent heat compared to water at $100^\circ\text{C}$.

(A) Decreases the boiling point of water  
(B) Increases the boiling point of water  
(C) Increases the specific heat of water  
(D) Decreases the latent heat of steam  
> **Answer: (B) Increases the boiling point of water**  
> **Explanation:** Trapped steam increases pressure inside the cooker, raising water's boiling point to $\sim 120^\circ\text{C}$.

**Q5. Which of the following modes of heat transfer does NOT require any material medium?**
(A) Conduction  
(B) Convection  
(C) Radiation  
(D) Both Conduction and Convection  
> **Answer: (C) Radiation**  
> **Explanation:** Radiation transfers heat via electromagnetic waves and travels through vacuum (e.g., Sun's heat reaching Earth).

(A) Decreases  
(B) Increases  
(C) Remains unchanged  
(D) First decreases then remains constant  
> **Answer: (B) Increases**  
> **Explanation:** The refrigerator's compressor does work $W$ and rejects total heat $Q_1 = Q_2 + W$ into the same room, causing a net temperature rise.

(A) Zeroth Law  
(B) First Law  
(C) Second Law  
(D) Third Law  
> **Answer: (A) Zeroth Law**  
> **Explanation:** Zeroth law deals with thermal equilibrium and establishes temperature; First defines internal energy, Second defines entropy.

(D) Isochoric expansion

**Q9. What is the effect of an increase in pressure on the speed of sound in air at constant temperature?**

(C) Remains unchanged  
(D) Becomes zero

**Q10. What is the minimum distance required between a listener and a reflecting surface to hear a distinct echo in air (at $\sim 22^\circ\text{C}$)?**

**Q31. Albert Einstein was awarded the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics for:**
(A) Special Theory of Relativity  
(B) General Theory of Relativity  
(C) Photoelectric Effect  
(D) Mass-Energy Equivalence  
> **Answer: (C) Photoelectric Effect**  
> **Explanation:** The Nobel Committee cited his services to theoretical physics and especially his discovery of the law of the photoelectric effect.

**Q32. Which radiation has the highest penetrating power?**
(A) Alpha rays  
(B) Beta rays  
(C) Gamma rays  
(D) Cathode rays  
> **Answer: (C) Gamma rays**  
> **Explanation:** Gamma rays are uncharged high-energy photons with minimal ionization and maximum penetration.

**Q33. In a nuclear reactor, Heavy Water ($\text{D}_2\text{O}$) or Graphite is used as a:**
(A) Coolant  
(B) Fuel  
(C) Control rod  
(D) Moderator  
> **Answer: (D) Moderator**  
> **Explanation:** Moderators slow down fast-moving fission neutrons into thermal neutrons; Cadmium/Boron act as control rods.

**Q34. Which was India's and Asia's first nuclear reactor?**
(A) Tarapur  
(B) Apsara  
(C) Cirus  
(D) Dhruva  
> **Answer: (B) Apsara**  
> **Explanation:** Apsara (1956, Trombay) was India's and Asia's first nuclear research reactor; Tarapur (1969) was the first commercial nuclear power station.

**Q35. Which instrument is used to measure the relative humidity of air?**
(A) Hydrometer  
(B) Hygrometer  
(C) Barometer  
(D) Anemometer  
> **Answer: (B) Hygrometer**  
> **Explanation:** Hygrometer measures atmospheric humidity, whereas Hydrometer measures the relative density (specific gravity) of liquids.
