# 1. Ancient India

## 1.1 Indus Valley Civilization (IVC)
- **Period**: 3300–1300 BCE | **Mature Phase**: 2600–1900 BCE | **Age**: Bronze Age.
- **Geographical Extent**:
  - **Northernmost Site**: Manda (Jammu & Kashmir, on Chenab River).
  - **Southernmost Site**: Daimabad (Maharashtra, on Pravara/Godavari River).
  - **Easternmost Site**: Alamgirpur (Uttar Pradesh, on Hindon River).
  - **Westernmost Site**: Sutkagan Dor (Balochistan, Pakistan, on Dasht River).
  - **Total Spread**: Over 1.3 million sq. km across northwest India, Pakistan, and northeastern Afghanistan (Shortugai).

## 1.2 Vedic Period (1500–500 BCE)

```
Vedic Timeline:
1500 BCE ────────── Early Vedic / Rig Vedic (Pastoral, Egalitarian) ────────── 1000 BCE
1000 BCE ────────── Later Vedic (Settled Agriculture, Iron, Rigid Varna) ────── 500 BCE
```

### Comparative Analysis: Early Vedic vs. Later Vedic

| Feature | Early Vedic Period (1500–1000 BCE) | Later Vedic Period (1000–500 BCE) |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary Region** | *Sapta Sindhu* (Land of 7 Rivers: Indus, Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, Beas, Sutlej, Saraswati) | *Aryavarta* (Ganga-Yamuna Doab, Kosala, Kuru, Panchala, Videha) |
| **Economy** | Pastoral economy; wealth measured in cattle (**Gau**); battles fought for cows (**Gavishti**) | Sedentary agricultural economy; rice (*Vrihi*), wheat (*Godhuma*), barley (*Yava*) |
| **Metals Known** | Copper / Bronze (*Ayas*) | Iron known extensively as **Krishna Ayas** / *Shyama Ayas* (revolutionized farming) |
| **Social Structure** | Clan-based (*Kula*, *Grama*, *Vis*, *Jana*); flexible Varna based on occupation | Birth-based rigid 4 Varnas (*Brahmana, Kshatriya, Vaishya, Shudra*); Gotra institutionalized |
| **Position of Women** | High status; attended *Sabha* & *Samiti*; participated in Upanayana; scholars like **Gargi, Maitreyi, Apala, Lopamudra** | Drastic decline; excluded from assemblies; child marriage emerged; dowry appeared |
| **Political Bodies** | **Sabha** (assembly of elders/nobles), **Samiti** (general folk assembly), **Vidatha** (clan assembly) | King's power became absolute and hereditary (*Samrat, Ekarat*); *Vidatha* completely vanished |
| **Deities** | Natural forces: **Indra** (*Purandara* - 250 hymns), **Agni** (200 hymns), **Varuna** (Rta/cosmic order) | Trinity emerged: **Prajapati** (creator), **Rudra** (destroyer), **Vishnu** (preserver) |
| **Key Sacrifices** | Simple domestic offerings; soma juice | Elaborate, expensive royal sacrifices: **Ashvamedha** (horse sacrifice), **Rajasuya** (consecration), **Vajapeya** (chariot race) |

### The Four Vedas and Ancillary Literature
1. **Rig Veda**: Oldest religious text in the world. 1,028 hymns (*Suktas*) arranged in **10 Mandalas** (Books).
   - *Mandala III*: Contains the sacred **Gayatri Mantra** composed by sage Vishvamitra dedicated to the sun deity Savitr.
   - *Mandala VII*: Describes the famous **Battle of Ten Kings** (*Dasrajan War*) on the banks of River Parushni (Ravi), where King Sudas of the Bharata clan defeated a coalition of 10 kings.
   - *Mandala IX*: Dedicated entirely to **Soma** (the sacred hallucinogenic plant/deity).
   - *Mandala X*: Contains the **Purusha Sukta**, the earliest textual mention of the four-fold Varna system originating from the Cosmic Being (Purusha).
   - Recited by the priest: **Hotri**.
2. **Sama Veda**: Book of chants and melodies (*Saman*). The root source of Indian classical music. 1,549 verses set to musical meters. Recited by: **Udgatri**.
3. **Yajur Veda**: Book of sacrificial rituals, procedures, and formulas. Divided into **Shukla Yajur Veda** (White/pure hymns) and **Krishna Yajur Veda** (Black/commentary + hymns). Recited by: **Adhvaryu**.
4. **Atharva Veda**: Non-Aryan magical charms, healing spells, medicinal formulas, and warding off evil spirits. Named after sage Atharvan. Mentions *Sabha* and *Samiti* as twin daughters of Prajapati. Supervised by: **Brahma**.
5. **Upanishads**: 108 philosophical texts (*Vedanta* = end of Vedas) dealing with *Brahman* (Universal Soul) and *Atman* (Individual Soul).
   - **Mundaka Upanishad**: Source of India's national motto **"Satyameva Jayate"** (Truth Alone Triumphs).
   - **Brihadaranyaka Upanishad**: Contains the famous prayer *"Tamaso Ma Jyotirgamaya"* and dialogue between sage Yajnavalkya and Gargi.
   - **Chandogya Upanishad**: Mentions the first three Ashramas and the dialogue of Tat Tvam Asi.
   - **Katha Upanishad**: Contains the profound dialogue on life and death between **Nachiketa and Yama**.

## 1.3 Buddhism and Jainism

### Buddhism
- **Founder**: Siddhartha Gautama (Buddha / Shakyamuni / Tathagata), born in 563 BCE at **Lumbini** (near Kapilavastu, Nepal) in the Shakya Kshatriya clan.
- **Parents**: Father Suddhodana (Chief of Shakya republic), Mother Mahamaya (Koliyan dynasty, died 7 days after birth), raised by stepmother Gautami (Prajapati Gautami — first woman admitted to Sangha).
- **Great Renunciation (*Mahabhinishkramana*)**: Left home at age 29 on his horse Kanthaka with charioteer Channa after witnessing Four Sights: an old man, a diseased person, a dead body, and an ascetic.
- **Enlightenment (*Nirvana / Sambodhi*)**: At age 35 under a Pipal tree (*Bodhi Vriksha*) on the banks of River **Niranjana** (Falgu) at **Bodh Gaya** (Bihar).
- **First Sermon (*Dharma Chakra Pravartana*)**: Delivered to 5 disciples at the Deer Park in **Sarnath** (near Varanasi).
- **Death (*Mahaparinirvana*)**: Passed away in 483 BCE at age 80 at **Kushinagar** (UP), the capital of the Mallas.
- **Core Doctrines**:
  - **Four Noble Truths (*Arya Satyani*)**: Life is full of sorrow (*Dukkha*); Desire is the cause of sorrow (*Samudaya*); Sorrow can end by eliminating desire (*Nirodha*); The way out is the Eightfold Path (*Magga*).
  - **Ashtangika Marga (Eightfold Path)**: Right View, Right Resolve, Right Speech, Right Action, Right Livelihood, Right Effort, Right Mindfulness, Right Concentration.
  - **Three Jewels (*Triratna*)**: Buddha, Dhamma, Sangha.
  - Rejection of Vedic infallibility, animal sacrifices, and caste supremacy. Believed in Karma and rebirth but rejected the existence of a permanent soul (*Anatta* / soullessness).

#### The Four Buddhist Councils (Crucial Exam Table)
| Council | Year | Place | Patron King & Dynasty | President | Major Outcome & Literature |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1st** | 483 BCE | Sattapanni Cave, **Rajagriha** | **Ajatashatru** (Haryanka) | Mahakassapa | Compilation of **Vinaya Pitaka** (monastic rules, by Upali) and **Sutta Pitaka** (Buddha's teachings, by Ananda) |
| **2nd** | 383 BCE | **Vaishali** | **Kalashoka** (Shishunaga) | Sabakami | First schism into **Sthaviravadins** (elders/orthodox) and **Mahasanghikas** (reformists) |

| **4th** | 72 CE | Kundalvana, **Kashmir** | **Kanishka** (Kushana) | Vasumitra (Vice: Ashvaghosha) | Formal split into **Mahayana** (idol worship of Buddha, Sanskrit scriptures) and **Hinayana** (orthodox, symbolic worship, Pali scriptures); *Mahavibhasha Sastra* compiled |

### Jainism
- **Tradition**: 24 Tirthankaras (ford-makers).
  - **1st Tirthankara**: **Rishabhadeva** (Adinatha) — Symbol: **Bull** (mentioned in Rig Veda alongside Arishtanemi).
  - **23rd Tirthankara**: **Parshvanatha** — Symbol: **Serpent** (prince of Banaras who introduced 4 core vows).
  - **24th Tirthankara**: **Vardhamana Mahavira** — Symbol: **Lion**.
- **Vardhamana Mahavira (540–468 BCE)**: Born at **Kundagrama** near Vaishali (Bihar) in the Jnatrika Kshatriya clan. Father Siddhartha; Mother Trishala (Lichchhavi princess, sister of King Chetaka); Wife Yashoda; Daughter Anojja/Priyadarshena.
- **Attainment of Kaivalya (Supreme Knowledge)**: At age 42 under a Sal tree on the banks of River **Rijupalika** near Jimbhikagrama. Became *Jina* (conqueror) and *Kevalin*.
- **Death**: Attained Nirvana at age 72 at **Pavapuri** (near Nalanda, Bihar) in 468 BCE at the palace of King Sastipal.
- **Core Principles**:
  - **Five Great Vows (*Pancha Mahavrata*)**:
    1. *Ahimsa* (Non-injury to any living being — paramount rule)
    2. *Satya* (Truth)
    3. *Asteya* (Non-stealing)
    4. *Aparigraha* (Non-attachment / non-possession)
    5. *Brahmacharya* (Celibacy — **added by Mahavira**; the first 4 were given by Parshvanatha)
  - **Three Jewels (*Triratna*)**: Samyak Darshana (Right Faith), Samyak Jnana (Right Knowledge), Samyak Charitra (Right Conduct).
  - **Syadvada / Anekantavada**: Theory of plurality of spiritual reality and conditional/relative viewpoints.
  - Strict asceticism: Extreme non-violence, starvation to death (*Sallekhana / Santhara* practiced by Chandragupta Maurya at Shravanabelagola).
- **The Great Schism**:
  - Around 300 BCE, a terrible 12-year famine in Magadha led **Bhadrabahu** and Chandragupta Maurya to migrate to South India (Karnataka). Those who stayed in the north were led by **Sthulabhadra**.
  - **Digambaras** (led by Bhadrabahu): "Sky-clad" (naked); reject clothes; believe women cannot attain liberation without rebirth as men.
  - **Shvetambaras** (led by Sthulabhadra): "White-clad"; wear white robes; believe women can attain liberation (regard 19th Tirthankara Mallinatha as a woman).
- **Jain Councils**:
  - **1st Council (300 BCE)** at **Pataliputra**, presided by **Sthulabhadra** — compiled 12 Angas to replace lost 14 Purvas.
  - **2nd Council (512 CE)** at **Vallabhi** (Gujarat), presided by **Devardhi Kshamasramana** — final compilation of Jain Agamas in Ardhamagadhi Prakrit.

### Magadha Dynasties Timeline
1. **Haryanka Dynasty (544–412 BCE)**:
   - **Bimbisara (544–492 BCE)**: Founder; used matrimonial alliances (Kosala, Lichchhavi, Madra) and conquest (annexed Anga). Built Rajagriha. Contemporary of Buddha and Mahavira. Sent royal physician **Jivaka** to cure King Pradyota of Avanti.
   - **Ajatashatru (492–460 BCE)**: Murdered his father Bimbisara. Conquered Vaishali using innovative war engines: **Mahashilakantaka** (catapult throwing large stones) and **Rathamusala** (chariot with attached spinning blades). Convened 1st Buddhist Council at Rajagriha.
   - **Udayin (460–444 BCE)**: Built the new capital city of **Pataliputra** at the confluence of the Ganga and Son rivers.
2. **Shishunaga Dynasty (412–344 BCE)**:
   - **Shishunaga**: Destroyed the 100-year rivalry with Avanti by annexing it.
   - **Kalashoka (Kakavarna)**: Transferred capital permanently to Pataliputra; organized 2nd Buddhist Council at Vaishali (383 BCE).
3. **Nanda Dynasty (344–321 BCE)**:
   - **Mahapadma Nanda**: Founder; first non-Kshatriya empire builder (*Ugrasena*). Destroyed all Kshatriya clans and assumed titles **Ekarat** (sole emperor) and *Sarva-Kshatrantaka* (destroyer of all Kshatriyas).
   - **Dhana Nanda**: Immensely wealthy and tyrannical ruler. Alexander the Great invaded India during his reign (326 BCE). Alexander fought King Porus at the **Battle of Hydaspes** (Jhelum River, 326 BCE), but his weary soldiers mutinied at the Beas River and refused to advance against the mighty Nanda army. Dhana Nanda was overthrown by Chandragupta Maurya and Chanakya in 321 BCE.


### Key Rulers and Achievements
- **Chandragupta Maurya (322–297 BCE)**:
  - Founded the dynasty with the assistance of **Chanakya (Vishnugupta / Kautilya)**, author of the political treatise **Arthashastra** (consisting of 15 *Adhikaranas* / books, detailing the **Saptanga Theory** of the state: Swami, Amatya, Janapada, Durga, Kosha, Danda, Mitra).
  - In 305 BCE, defeated **Seleucus I Nicator** (Macedonian satrap). By treaty, Seleucus ceded eastern satrapies (Arachosia/Kandahar, Gedrosia/Balochistan, Paropamisadae/Kabul) and sent ambassador **Megasthenes** to the court of Pataliputra.
  - Megasthenes wrote **Indica**, recording that Indian society was divided into **7 classes** (Philosophers, Farmers, Soldiers, Herdsmen, Artisans, Magistrates, Councillors) and that slavery did not exist in India.
  - Towards the end of his life, Chandragupta embraced Jainism under sage Bhadrabahu, migrated to **Shravanabelagola** (Karnataka), and starved to death by the ritual of *Sallekhana*.
- **Bindusara (297–268 BCE)**:
  - Called **Amitraghata** (Slayer of Foes) by the Greeks (*Amitrochates*).
  - Patronized the **Ajivika** sect (founded by Makkhali Gosala).
  - Received foreign envoys: **Deimachus** (from Syrian King Antiochus I, who sent dried figs and wine, but refused to send a sophist/philosopher) and **Dionysius** (from Ptolemy Philadelphus of Egypt).
- **Ashoka the Great (268–232 BCE)**:
  - Interregnum of 4 years (273–268 BCE) following Bindusara's death; Buddhist text *Mahavamsa* states Ashoka killed 99 brothers to seize the throne.
  - **Kalinga War (261 BCE)**: Fought in his 8th regnal year. The carnage and bloodshed moved him deeply, leading to the abandonment of **Bherighosha** (war drums) in favor of **Dhammaghosha** (drums of righteousness). Converted to Buddhism under monk **Upagupta**.
  - **Dhamma Policy**: Secular ethical code advocating non-violence, truthfulness, obedience to parents and teachers, humane treatment of servants/slaves, and religious tolerance.
  - Created a new class of moral welfare officers: **Dhamma Mahamattas**.
  - Sent his son **Mahendra** and daughter **Sanghamitra** to Ceylon (Sri Lanka) with a sapling of the original Bodhi tree.
  - Convened the **3rd Buddhist Council** at Pataliputra in 250 BCE.

### Ashokan Inscriptions & Edicts
- Deciphered for the first time in **1837** by **James Prinsep** (civil servant & antiquarian of the Asiatic Society).
- Inscriptions refer to Ashoka by titles: **Devanampiya** (Beloved of the Gods) and **Piyadasi** (Of Pleasing Appearance).
- Only 4 inscriptions explicitly mention the name **Ashoka**: **Maski, Gujjara, Nittur, and Udegolam**.
- **Scripts & Languages**:
  - Languages used: Prakrit (majority), Greek, and Aramaic.
  - Scripts: **Brahmi** (read left-to-right, majority), **Kharosthi** (read right-to-left, in northwest sites Shahbazgarhi and Mansehra), Greek and Aramaic (in Afghanistan: Kandahar bilingual edict).
- **Major Rock Edicts (Selected Essentials)**:
  - *MRE I*: Prohibition of animal sacrifice.
  - *MRE II*: Medical care for humans and animals; mentions South Indian kingdoms (Chola, Pandya, Satyaputra, Keralaputra).
  - *MRE III & IV*: Respect to parents, Brahmins; replacement of Bherighosha with Dhammaghosha.
  - *MRE V*: Appointment of Dhamma Mahamattas.
  - *MRE XII*: Religious tolerance and harmony among all sects.
  - *MRE XIII*: **Exhaustive account of the Kalinga War** (261 BCE) and conquest of Dhamma; mentions five Greek contemporary kings (Antiochus II of Syria, Ptolemy II of Egypt, Antigonus of Macedonia, Magas of Cyrene, Alexander of Epirus).
- **Sarnath Lion Capital**: Four back-to-back Asiatic lions standing on an abacus carved with an elephant, a galloping horse, a bull, and a lion, separated by 24-spoke Dharma Chakras. Adopted as the **National Emblem of India** on 26 January 1950.

### Mauryan Administrative Hierarchy
- **Samaharta**: Chief collector of revenue (in charge of state assessment and taxation).
- **Sannidhata**: Chief custodian of the state treasury and royal storehouses.
- **Akshapataladhyaksha**: Accountant General.
- **Sitadhyaksha**: Superintendent of crown lands (*Sita* land).
- **Panyadhyaksha**: Superintendent of commerce and market regulation.
- **Pautavadhyaksha**: Superintendent of weights and measures.
- **Navadhyaksha**: Superintendent of shipping and waterways.
- **Vyavaharika / Dharmastha**: Judges of civil courts (*Dharmasthiya*).
- **Pradeshta**: Judges of criminal courts (*Kantakashodhana*).
- **Espionage System**: Run by *Mahamatyapasarpa*; secret agents divided into **Sanstha** (stationed/stationary spies) and **Sanchara** (wandering spies).
- **Decline**: Last Mauryan emperor **Brihadratha** was assassinated in **185 BCE** by his Brahmin commander-in-chief **Pushyamitra Sunga**, who founded the Sunga Dynasty.

## 1.6 Post-Mauryan Period & The Sangam Age

### Post-Mauryan Dynasties (North & Deccan)
1. **Sunga Dynasty (185–73 BCE)**:
   - Founded by **Pushyamitra Sunga** after slaying Brihadratha. Revived Vedic sacrifices (*Ashvamedha* performed twice, officiated by great grammarian **Patanjali**, author of *Mahabhasya*).
   - Renovated and enlarged the **Sanchi Stupa** with stone railings (*Vedika*).
   - Kalidasa's drama **Malavikagnimitram** is based on the love story of Agnimitra (Pushyamitra's son) and Malavika.
2. **Satavahana Dynasty (1st Century BCE – 3rd Century CE)**:
   - Also called **Andhras** in the Puranas. Capital: **Pratishthana** (Paithan, Maharashtra) on River Godavari. Founded by **Simuka**.
   - **Gautamiputra Satakarni (106–130 CE)**: Greatest ruler. Claimed to be the *Ekabrahmana* (peerless Brahmin) and destroyer of Sakas, Yavanas, and Pahlavas. His conquests are documented in the **Nasik Cave Inscription** written by his mother **Gautami Balasri**. Defeated Saka ruler Nahapana.
   - **Yajna Sri Satakarni**: Issued unique coins featuring images of **ships and vessels**, indicating maritime commerce.
   - First Indian rulers to issue **inscriptions recording land grants to Buddhist monks and Brahmins** with tax immunities.
   - Built the famous rock-cut caves and chaityas at **Karle, Bhaja, and Kanheri**, and stupas at **Amaravati and Nagarjunakonda**.
3. **Kushana Empire (1st–3rd Century CE)**:
   - Branch of the Yuezhi nomadic tribe from Central Asia.
   - **Kujula Kadphises**: First king; issued copper coins imitating Roman coins.
   - **Vima Kadphises**: First to issue extensive **gold coins** in India, bearing Shaivite symbols (Lord Shiva with trident and Nandi bull).
   - **Kanishka the Great (78–101 CE)**:
     - Ascended the throne in **78 CE**, which marks the commencement of the **Saka Era** (adopted as the National Calendar of India in 1957).
     - Capitals: **Purushapura** (Peshawar) and **Mathura**.
     - Convened the **4th Buddhist Council** at Kundalvana in Kashmir.
     - Great royal court scholars: **Ashvaghosha** (wrote *Buddhacharita* and *Saundarananda*), **Vasumitra**, **Nagarjuna** (propounder of Madhyamika philosophy / theory of relativity, "Indian Einstein"), and **Charaka** (father of Indian medicine, author of *Charaka Samhita*).
     - Controlled the prestigious **Silk Route** passing through Central Asia.
     - Two major schools of sculpture flourished: **Gandhara Art** (Greco-Roman influence, grey/blue schist stone, realistic human features) and **Mathura Art** (indigenous style, spotted red sandstone, spiritual calm).

### The Sangam Age (South India: 3rd Century BCE – 3rd Century CE)
- Named after the assemblies of Tamil poets called **Sangam**, patronized by the Pandya kings of Madurai.
- **Three Dynasties (*Muvendar*)**:
  1. **Cheras**: Ruled Kerala and western Tamil Nadu. Capital: **Vanji** (Karur). Emblem: **Bow and Arrow**. Famous ruler: **Senguttuvan** (the Red Chera), who established the **Pattini Cult** (worship of Kannagi as the ideal chaste wife) using stones brought from the Himalayas.
  2. **Cholas**: Ruled Cauvery delta and northern Tamil Nadu. Capital: **Uraiyur** (inland, cotton trade) and **Puhar** (Kaveripattinam, port). Emblem: **Tiger**. Famous ruler: **Karikala Chola**, who built the **Kallanai Dam** (Grand Anicut) across the Cauvery River, one of the oldest water-regulation structures in the world.
  3. **Pandyas**: Ruled southernmost Tamil Nadu. Capital: **Madurai**. Port: **Korkai** (pearl fisheries). Emblem: **Carp / Fish**. Prominent ruler: **Neduncheliyan** (won the Battle of Talaiyalanganam; hero of Silappadikaram tragedy).
- **Major Sangam Epics & Texts**:
  - **Tolkappiyam**: Authored by Tolkappiyar; oldest extant work of Tamil grammar and poetics.
  - **Silappadikaram** (*The Jewelled Anklet*): Composed by Jain monk **Ilango Adigal**; epic narrative of Kovalan, Kannagi, and Madhavi.
  - **Manimekalai**: Written by grain merchant **Chithalai Chathanar**; sequel to Silappadikaram tracing the spiritual journey of Kovalan and Madhavi's daughter Manimekalai, who embraced Buddhism.
  - **Tirukkural**: Written by saint **Tiruvalluvar**; celebrated compendium of 1,330 couplets on Aram (ethics), Porul (wealth), and Inbam (love). Known as the "Bible of the Tamil Land".

## 1.7 The Gupta Empire (319–550 CE)
Known as the **Classical Age** or **Golden Age of Ancient India** due to unmatched achievements in science, mathematics, astronomy, Sanskrit literature, and temple architecture.

```
Gupta Imperial Lineage:
Sri Gupta (Founder) ──► Ghatotkacha ──► Chandragupta I (319 CE) ──► Samudragupta ──► Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya) ──► Kumaragupta I ──► Skandagupta
```

### Rulers and Accomplishments
- **Sri Gupta & Ghatotkacha**: Early rulers holding the feudatory title of *Maharaja*.
- **Chandragupta I (319–335 CE)**: First to assume the imperial title of **Maharajadhiraja**. Founded the **Gupta Era** (319–320 CE). Contracted a matrimonial alliance with the powerful Lichchhavi clan by marrying Princess **Kumaradevi**; issued famous gold "King and Queen" type coins.
- **Samudragupta (335–375 CE)**:
  - Celebrated as the **"Napoleon of India"** by historian V. A. Smith for his unbeaten military conquests across Aryavarta, Dakshinapatha (12 kings captured and liberated under *Grahana-Moksha-Anugraha*), and forest kingdoms (*Atavika*).
  - Achievements recorded in chaste Sanskrit in the **Allahabad Pillar Inscription (*Prayag Prashasti*)**, composed by court poet **Harisena** in the *Champu* kavya style (mix of prose and verse).
  - Assumed titles: **Kaviraja** (King of poets), *Param-Bhagavata*, and *Sarva-rajo-chhetta*.
  - Issued 6 types of gold coins: Archer, Battle-axe, Tiger-slayer, Ashvamedha, Lyrist (depicting him **playing the Veena**), and Standard type.
- **Chandragupta II / Vikramaditya (375–415 CE)**:
  - Eradicated the last remnants of Saka rule in Western India; assumed the epithet **Sakari** (conqueror of Sakas) and **Vikramaditya**.
  - Conferred with the **Iron Pillar of Delhi** (at Mehrauli), which has withstood rust for over 1,600 years, praising King "Chandra".
  - Made **Ujjain** his secondary cultural capital.
  - Chinese Buddhist pilgrim **Fa-Hien (Faxian)** visited India during his reign (399–412 CE), describing a peaceful society with minimal crime and vegetarianism.
  - Court embellished by the **Navratnas (Nine Gems)**:
    1. **Kalidasa**: Peerless poet and dramatist (*Abhijnanashakuntalam, Meghaduta, Raghuvamsa, Kumarasambhava, Malavikagnimitram, Ritusamhara*).
    2. **Varahamihira**: Astrologer and astronomer (*Brihat Samhita, Panchasiddhantika*).
    3. **Amarasimha**: Sanskrit lexicographer (wrote *Amarakosha*).
    4. **Dhanvantari**: Royal physician (Father of Ayurveda).
    5. **Vararuchi**: Grammarian (*Prakrita Prakasha*).
    6. **Shanku**: Master architect (*Shilpashastra*).
    7. **Kshapanaka**: Astrologer.
    8. **Ghatakarpara**: Sculptor and architect.
    9. **Vetala Bhatta**: Magician and writer (*Mantrashastra*).
- **Kumaragupta I (415–455 CE)**: Assumed title *Mahendraditya*. Founded the internationally renowned **Nalanda Mahavihara (University)** in modern Bihar. Worshipped Lord Kartikeya; issued peacock-type gold coins.
- **Skandagupta (455–467 CE)**: The last great emperor. Successfully defended India by repulsing the fierce invasion of the **White Hunas** (led by Toramana and Mihirakula). Repaired the dam of **Sudarshana Lake** in Kathiawar through governor Parnadatta (recorded in the **Junagarh Rock Inscription**).

### Scientific and Cultural Milestones
- **Aryabhata**: Court mathematician and astronomer from Pataliputra; authored **Aryabhatiya** and *Surya Siddhanta*. Established that the Earth rotates on its axis and revolves around the Sun; formulated equations for eclipses; calculated the exact value of Pi ($\pi \approx 3.1416$); introduced the decimal system and the concept of zero (*Shunya*).
- **Brahmagupta**: Great mathematician and astronomer; authored **Brahmasphutasiddhanta**; established mathematical laws for zero, negative numbers, and gravity centuries before Newton.
- **Temple Architecture**: The Gupta period marked the birth of structural stone temples in the **Nagara Style** with a *Garbhagriha* (sanctum sanctorum), *Mandapa*, and *Shikhara*. Key examples: **Dashavatara Temple** at Deogarh (Jhansi, UP — early depiction of Sheshashayi Vishnu), **Bhitargaon Temple** (Kanpur, earliest temple made entirely of baked bricks).

## 1.8 Harshavardhana and Post-Gupta Dynasties

### Harshavardhana (606–647 CE)
- Belonged to the **Pushyabhuti / Vardhana Dynasty** of Thaneshwar (Haryana).
- Ascended the throne at age 16 after the murder of his elder brother Rajyavardhana by Sasanka (Gauda king of Bengal) and his brother-in-law Grahavarman (Maukhari king of Kannauj) by the Malwa king.
- United the kingdoms of Thaneshwar and Kannauj; transferred the capital to **Kannauj** (Uttar Pradesh).
- **Chinese Pilgrim Xuanzang (Hiuen Tsang)**: Traveled across India between 629 and 645 CE during Harsha's reign; known as the "Prince of Pilgrims"; recorded his observations in **Si-Yu-Ki** (*Buddhist Records of the Western World*). Spent several years studying Buddhist philosophy at Nalanda University under Chancellor **Shilabhadra**.
- Organized two grand religious gatherings: the **Kannauj Assembly** (to honor Xuanzang and promote Mahayana Buddhism) and the **Maha Moksha Parishad** at Prayag (held every five years, where Harsha donated all his royal wealth).
- Harsha was a skilled dramatist and author of three classic Sanskrit plays: **Ratnavali**, **Priyadarsika**, and **Nagananda**.
- Patronized court poet **Banabhatta**, who authored **Harshacharita** (biography of Harsha) and **Kadambari** (one of the earliest Sanskrit prose novels).
- **Battle of Narmada River (618–619 CE)**: Harsha attempted to expand his empire south of the Vindhyas into the Deccan, but was decisively defeated on the banks of the Narmada by the Western Chalukya king **Pulakeshin II** (recorded in Pulakeshin's **Aihole Inscription**).

## 1.9 Major Early South Indian Kingdoms

```
South Indian Triad:
Chalukyas of Badami (Vatapi, Karnataka) ◄── Wars ──► Pallavas of Kanchi (Tamil Nadu)
                                                        │
                                               Imperial Cholas of Tanjore (Navy, Local Govt)
```

### 1. Chalukyas of Badami (Vatapi) (543–753 CE)
- Founded by **Pulakeshin I** with capital at **Vatapi** (Badami, Bagalkot district, Karnataka).
- **Pulakeshin II (610–642 CE)**: Greatest emperor; defeated Harshavardhana on the Narmada River. Court poet **Ravikirti** composed the eulogistic **Aihole Inscription** on the Meguti Jain temple. Defeated by Pallava king Narasimhavarman I in 642 CE at the Battle of Manimangala.
- Architectural heritage: Rock-cut cave temples of Badami and structural temple clusters at **Aihole** ("Cradle of Indian Temple Architecture") and **Pattadakal** (UNESCO World Heritage Site; features the Virupaksha and Mallikarjuna temples).

### 2. Pallavas of Kanchipuram (4th–9th Century CE)
- Capital: **Kanchipuram** (Tamil Nadu).
- **Mahendravarman I (600–630 CE)**: Scholar-king; composed the satirical Sanskrit farce **Mattavilasa Prahasana**; pioneer of rock-cut cave temples in Tamil Nadu (*Mandagapattu inscription*).
- **Narasimhavarman I / Mamalla (630–668 CE)**: Defeated Pulakeshin II, captured Vatapi, and assumed the title **Vatapikonda**. Founded the port city of **Mamallapuram (Mahabalipuram)** and commissioned the world-famous monolithic rock-cut **Pancha Ratha** temples and the bas-relief **"Descent of the Ganga / Arjuna's Penance"**.
- **Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha)**: Built the structural stone **Shore Temple** at Mahabalipuram and the **Kailasanatha Temple** at Kanchipuram.

### 3. Imperial Cholas of Thanjavur (850–1279 CE)
- Founded by **Vijayalaya Chola** in 850 CE after capturing Thanjavur from the Muttarayars.
- **Rajaraja I (985–1014 CE)**:
  - Greatest military and naval organizer of the Chola empire.
  - Conquered northern Sri Lanka (Anuradhapura); captured the Maldives.
  - Built the monumental **Brihadisvara Temple (Rajarajesvara Temple)** at **Thanjavur** in 1010 CE (UNESCO World Heritage Site), dedicated to Lord Shiva; features a 216-foot granite Shikhara and a single 80-ton granite capstone (*Kumbam*).
- **Rajendra I (1014–1044 CE)**:
  - Son of Rajaraja I; conquered all of Sri Lanka and captured its King Mahinda V.
  - Led a daring expedition across the Ganga into Bengal, defeating Mahipala I of the Pala dynasty; assumed the title **Gangaikondachola** (Conqueror of the Ganga) and founded the new capital city **Gangaikondacholapuram** along with an enormous 16-mile artificial irrigation lake (*Chola Gangam*).
  - Possessed the most formidable naval fleet in Asia; launched a massive trans-oceanic naval expedition against the **Srivijaya Empire** (Sumatra, Java, Malay Peninsula), capturing its capital Kadaram and opening maritime trade routes to China.
- **Chola Local Self-Government**:
  - Detailed in the celebrated **Uttaramerur Inscriptions** (919 and 921 CE) issued by King **Parantaka I**.
  - Villages were autonomous republics. Two main assemblies:
    - **Ur**: General assembly of the village tax-paying landholders.
    - **Sabha / Mahasabha**: Assembly of learned Brahmins in *Brahmadeya* (tax-free gift villages).
  - **Kudavolai System**: Democratic lottery election system for village executive committees (*Variyams*: Tank committee, Garden committee, Gold committee, Annual committee). Candidate names were written on palm leaves, placed into an earthen pot (*Kudam*), and picked out by a young boy.
- **Bronze Sculptures**: The Cholas perfected the lost-wax bronze casting technique, producing the iconic image of **Nataraja** (Shiva as the Cosmic Dancer of the universe: dancing on the dwarf of ignorance *Apasmara*, holding the damaru of creation in upper right hand and agni of destruction in upper left hand, surrounded by a ring of cosmic flames).

# 2. Medieval India

## 2.1 Early Medieval Invasions (712–1206 CE)
- **Arab Invasion of Sindh (712 CE)**: Led by 17-year-old **Muhammad bin Qasim** under orders from Hajjaj (Governor of Iraq). Defeated Raja Dahir of the Chach dynasty at the Battle of Raor. First introduction of the **Jizya tax** on non-Muslims in the Indian subcontinent.
- **Mahmud of Ghazni (998–1030 CE)**: Turkish ruler of the Ghaznavid dynasty in Afghanistan. Conducted **17 plundering expeditions** into Northern India between 1000 and 1027 CE. Major targets included Mathura, Kannauj (1018), and the infamous sack of the wealthy **Somnath Temple** in Gujarat (1025 CE, during the reign of Solanki king Bhima I).
  - Famous scholar **Al-Biruni** accompanied Mahmud to India and authored **Kitab-al-Hind / Tahqiq-i-Hind** (encyclopedic account of Indian society, sciences, and geography).
  - Court poet **Firdausi** wrote the Persian epic **Shahnameh**; historian **Utbi** wrote *Kitab-i-Yamini*.
- **Muhammad Ghori (Mu'izz ad-Din Muhammad) (1173–1206 CE)**:
  - **First Battle of Tarain (1191 CE)**: Ghori was thoroughly routed and wounded by the Rajput confederacy led by **Prithviraj Chauhan (Prithviraj III)** of Ajmer.
  - **Second Battle of Tarain (1192 CE)**: Ghori returned with a massive cavalry force; defeated and captured Prithviraj Chauhan. Established permanent Muslim rule in Delhi.
  - **Battle of Chandawar (1194 CE)**: Ghori defeated **Raja Jaichand** of Kannauj (Gahadavala dynasty).
  - His trusted commander **Ikhtiyar-ud-din Bakhtiyar Khalji** marched east, destroyed the ancient university centers of **Nalanda and Vikramashila** (1193–1200 CE), and conquered Bengal and Bihar.
  - Ghori returned to Ghazni, appointing his loyal slave-general **Qutbuddin Aibak** as his viceroy in Delhi.

## 2.2 The Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526 CE)

```
Five Dynasties of Delhi Sultanate:
1. Mamluk / Slave (1206–1290) ──► 2. Khalji (1290–1320) ──► 3. Tughlaq (1320–1414) ──► 4. Sayyid (1414–1451) ──► 5. Lodi (1451–1526)
```

### 1. Mamluk / Slave Dynasty (1206–1290 CE)
- **Qutbuddin Aibak (1206–1210 CE)**:
  - Founded the Sultanate; assumed the title *Malik* and *Sipahsalar* (refused to strike coins or read Khutbah in his name).
  - Known as **Lakh Baksh** (Giver of Lakhs) for his generous charity.
  - Began construction of the **Qutub Minar** in Delhi (dedicated to the Sufi saint Khwaja Qutbuddin Bakhtiyar Kaki); built the **Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque** (Delhi, first mosque in North India) and **Adhai Din Ka Jhonpra** (Ajmer).
  - Died suddenly in 1210 at Lahore from injuries sustained after falling from his horse while playing **Chaugan** (Polo).
- **Shamsuddin Iltutmish (1211–1236 CE)**:
  - The **real founder** of the Delhi Sultanate. Made **Delhi** the permanent capital instead of Lahore.
  - Obtained a formal deed of investiture (*Mansur*) from the Abbasid Caliph of Baghdad.
  - Completed the construction of the Qutub Minar up to its 4th storey.
  - Introduced the two hallmark coins of the Sultanate: the **Tanka** (pure silver, 175 grains) and the **Jital** (copper).
  - Organized the **Iqta System**: Land revenues assigned to officers in lieu of cash salaries to collect taxes and maintain troops.
  - Formed the **Turkan-i-Chahalgani** (The Forty / Chalisa), a powerful oligarchy of 40 trusted Turkish slave nobles.
  - Protected India from Mongol devastation in 1221 by tactfully refusing asylum to Jalal-ud-din Mangabarni (Crown prince of Khwarazm), who was being pursued by **Genghis Khan**.
- **Razia Sultana (1236–1240 CE)**:
  - Nominated by her father Iltutmish; **first and only Muslim female ruler of medieval Delhi**.
  - Abandoned the purdah/veil, wore male attire (Qaba/tunic and Kulah/cap), held open court, and rode war elephants.
  - Appointed an Abyssinian slave, Jamal-ud-din Yaqut, to the high office of *Amir-i-Akhur* (Superintendent of Royal Stables), provoking resentment among orthodox Turkish nobles.
  - Turkish nobles revolted under Altunia (Governor of Bhatinda); Razia married Altunia to reclaim the throne, but both were defeated and assassinated near **Kaithal** (Haryana) in 1240.
- **Ghiyasuddin Balban (1266–1287 CE)**:
  - Broke the stranglehold of the *Turkan-i-Chahalgani* (The Forty) by poisoning and executing its leaders.
  - Propounded the **Divine Right Theory of Kingship**: King is the shadow of God on Earth (*Zill-i-Ilahi*) and the recipient of divine light (*Niyabat-i-Khudai*).
  - Instituted rigid Persian court etiquette: **Sijdah** (prostration before the Sultan) and **Paibos** (kissing the Sultan's feet).
  - Introduced the celebration of the Persian spring New Year festival of **Nauroz**.
  - Followed the uncompromising **"Blood and Iron" (*Lahu aur Loha*) policy** to ruthlessly eradicate the Mewati brigands and recalcitrant Rajput chiefs.
  - Established the first independent central military department: **Diwan-i-Arz**, headed by *Ariz-i-Mumalik*.

### 2. Khalji Dynasty (1290–1320 CE)
- **Jalaluddin Khalji (1290–1296 CE)**: Founded the dynasty by overthrowing the Mamluks; advocated a tolerant policy towards the Hindu majority ("state should be based on the willing support of the governed"). Murdered at Kara (Kaushambi) by his ambitious nephew and son-in-law, Alauddin.
- **Alauddin Khalji (1296–1316 CE)**:
  - Assumed the exalted title **Sikandar-i-Sani** (Alexander the Second).
  - First Sultan to maintain a **permanent standing army** paid directly in cash from the royal treasury.
  - Introduced the practices of **Dagh** (branding of cavalry horses to prevent substitution) and **Chehra / Huliya** (descriptive descriptive rolls of each soldier).
  - **Market Control & Price Regulation**: Fixed prices of all commodities from food grains to cloth, horses, and cattle. Created three specialized markets in Delhi supervised by two high officials: **Diwan-i-Riyasat** (Minister of Commerce) and **Shahna-i-Mandi** (Market Superintendent). Established state granaries to prevent hoarding.
  - **Land Revenue Reforms**: First Sultan to order the actual measurement of arable land (*Paimash* / *Biswa*); fixed state revenue at **half (50%) of gross agricultural produce**. Abolished traditional hereditary tax-collector privileges (Khuts, Muqaddams, Chaudharis).
  - **Conquests of Northern India**: Gujarat (1299), Ranthambore (1301 - Hammiradeva), Chittor (1303 - siege against Rana Ratan Singh; Padmavati performed Jauhar), Malwa (1305).
  - **Deccan & South Indian Expeditions**: Entrusted to his trusted slave-general **Malik Kafur (Hazar Dinari)**, who subjugated Devagiri (Yadavas - Ramachandra), Warangal (Kakatiyas - Prataparudra, who surrendered the Koh-i-Noor diamond), Dwarasamudra (Hoysalas - Ballala III), and Madurai (Pandyas). Alauddin extracted heavy tribute and allegiance without direct annexation.
  - **Architectural Monuments**: Built the magnificent **Alai Darwaza** (southern gateway to Qutub complex, first true horseshoe arch and dome in India), **Siri Fort**, **Hauz Khas**, and the **Jamat Khana Mosque**.
  - **Court Patronage**: Patronized the legendary poet and scholar **Amir Khusrau** (*Tuti-e-Hind* / Parrot of India, inventor of the Sitar and Qawwali musical style).

### 3. Tughlaq Dynasty (1320–1414 CE)
- **Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq (Ghazi Malik) (1320–1325 CE)**: Founded the dynasty; built the massive fortified city of **Tughlaqabad** in Delhi. Died when a wooden victory pavilion collapsed at Afghanpur.
- **Muhammad bin Tughlaq (Jauna Khan) (1325–1351 CE)**:
  - Highly educated in mathematics, astronomy, logic, and medicine, but lacked practical statesmanship; known in history as the **"Wise Fool"** or a **"Mixture of Opposites"**.
  - **Five Disastrous Projects**:
    1. *Taxation in the Doab (1326)*: Arbitrarily increased land revenue in the fertile Ganga-Yamuna Doab during a severe famine, causing peasant rebellion.
    2. *Transfer of Capital (1327)*: Ordered the entire population and administration to migrate from Delhi to **Daulatabad (Devagiri)** in Maharashtra to control the south, only to order a return march to Delhi two years later due to lack of water and northern defense vulnerabilities.
    3. *Introduction of Token Currency (1329–1330)*: Issued bronze and copper coins having the same value as silver Tankas without setting up a state minting monopoly, leading to widespread counterfeiting in every household until the Sultan redeemed all counterfeit copper with genuine silver from the treasury.
    4. *Khurasan Expedition (1330)*: Recruited an army of 370,000 soldiers and paid them a full year's advance salary to conquer Iraq and Khurasan, but disbanded it when the geopolitical alliance collapsed.
    5. *Qarachil Expedition (1333)*: Launched an expedition into the Kumaon Himalayas (Uttarakhand) to counter Chinese encroachment; the army was destroyed by treacherous terrain and torrential rains.
  - Created a dedicated Department of Agriculture: **Diwan-i-Kohi** to bring uncultivated land under farming through loans (*Taccavi*).
  - **Ibn Battuta**: Famous Moroccan globetrotter arrived in India in **1333 CE**; appointed Chief Qazi of Delhi by Muhammad bin Tughlaq for 8 years; later sent as ambassador to China; authored the travelogue **Kitab-ur-Rihla**.
- **Firoz Shah Tughlaq (1351–1388 CE)**:
  - Followed a policy of conciliation towards the nobility and orthodox Ulema.
  - Imposed **Jizya on Brahmins** for the first time.
  - Pioneer of public works and canal irrigation: Constructed extensive irrigation canals from the Yamuna to Hissar, and from the Sutlej to the Ghaggar (*Haqq-i-Sharb* / irrigation tax of 10% levied).
  - Founded numerous new towns: **Firozabad** (Delhi), **Hissar**, **Fatehabad** (Haryana), and **Jaunpur** (UP, named in memory of Jauna Khan / Muhammad bin Tughlaq).
  - Established welfare institutions: **Diwan-i-Khairat** (charity bureau for poor girls' marriages), **Dar-ul-Shafa** (free charitable hospital), and **Diwan-i-Bandagan** (department of slaves, maintaining 180,000 royal slaves).
  - Transported two Ashokan monolithic stone pillars from Topra (Haryana) and Meerut to Delhi.
  - Wrote his royal autobiography in Persian: **Futuhat-i-Firoz Shahi**.
- **Timur's Invasion (1398 CE)**: Central Asian conqueror **Timur (Tamerlane)** invaded India during the reign of the last Tughlaq sultan, Nasiruddin Mahmud. He sacked and massacred Delhi in 1398, dealing a death blow to the Sultanate.

### 4. Sayyid Dynasty (1414–1451 CE)
- Founded by **Khizr Khan**, appointed by Timur as Governor of Multan, Lahore, and Dipalpur. Claimed descent from the Prophet Muhammad.
- Court chronicler Yahya bin Ahmad Sirhindi authored **Tarikh-i-Mubarak Shahi** (history of the reign of Mubarak Shah).

### 5. Lodi Dynasty (1451–1526 CE) — First Afghan Dynasty
- **Bahlul Lodi (1451–1489 CE)**: Founder; treated Afghan nobles as equals (*Masnad-i-Ali*).
- **Sikandar Lodi (1489–1517 CE)**: Greatest of the Lodis. Founded the city of **Agra in 1504** and made it his capital in 1506. Introduced the standard measurement unit **Gaz-i-Sikandari** (32 digits/30 inches) for land surveying. Penned Persian poetry under the pseudonym **Gulrukhi**.
- **Ibrahim Lodi (1517–1526 CE)**: Arrogant and uncompromising ruler who alienated his Afghan nobility. Daulat Khan Lodi (Governor of Punjab) and Rana Sanga of Mewar invited Babur of Kabul to overthrow him.
  - Defeated and slain at the **First Battle of Panipat on 21 April 1526**. Ibrahim Lodi was the **only Sultan of Delhi to die on the battlefield**.

### Sultanate Administrative Nomenclature
- **Wazir**: Prime Minister and Finance Minister (head of *Diwan-i-Wizarat*).
- **Ariz-i-Mumalik**: Head of the Military Department (*Diwan-i-Arz*).
- **Dabir-i-Khas**: Head of Royal Correspondence (*Diwan-i-Insha*).
- **Diwan-i-Risalat**: Department of Religious and Foreign Affairs.
- **Barid-i-Mumalik**: Head of the State Intelligence and Postal Agency.
- **Qazi-ul-Quzat**: Chief Justice and Legal Arbiter.
- **Muqti / Wali**: Provincial Governor in charge of an *Iqta*.
- **Amil**: Executive revenue collector in a *Pargana* (sub-district).

## 2.3 Vijayanagara and Bahmani Kingdoms

### 1. The Vijayanagara Empire (1336–1646 CE)
- Founded in **1336 CE** on the southern banks of the **Tungabhadra River** by two brothers, **Harihara I and Bukka I**, who had served under the Kakatiyas and Kampili. Inspired by spiritual guru **Sage Vidyaranya** of Sringeri matha. Capital: **Hampi** (Vijayanagara, Karnataka).
- **Four Ruling Dynasties**:
  1. *Sangama Dynasty (1336–1485)*: Harihara I, Bukka I, Devaraya I, Devaraya II.
  2. *Saluva Dynasty (1485–1505)*: Saluva Narasimha.
  3. *Tuluva Dynasty (1505–1570)*: Vira Narasimha, Krishnadeva Raya, Achyuta Raya, Sadashiva Raya.
  4. *Aravidu Dynasty (1570–1646)*: Tirumala, Venkata II.
- **Krishnadeva Raya (1509–1529 CE)**:
  - Greatest emperor of Vijayanagara (Tuluva dynasty). Assumed titles: **Andhra Bhoja**, *Abhinava Bhoja*, *Yavana-rajya-sthapana-acharya*.
  - Maintained friendly diplomatic relations with the Portuguese Governor Alfonso de Albuquerque to secure superior Arab and Persian war horses.
  - Conquered Udayagiri, Kondavidu, and defeated the Gajapatis of Odisha and the Sultan of Bijapur.
  - Brilliant scholar-poet: Authored **Amuktamalyada** (masterpiece in Telugu on statecraft and the life of Andal) and **Jambavati Kalyanam** (in Sanskrit).
  - Court patron of the **Ashtadiggajas** (Eight Telugu literary luminaries):
    - **Allasani Peddana** (*Andhra Kavita Pitamaha*, author of *Manucharitam*).
    - **Tenali Ramakrishna** (legendary wit and scholar, author of *Panduranga Mahatmyam*).
  - Built the **Hazara Rama Temple** and the **Vittala Temple** (with its 56 musical pillars and iconic Stone Chariot) at Hampi. Founded the new suburban township of **Nagalapuram** (named after his mother Nagala Devi).
- **Foreign Chroniclers to Vijayanagara (Exam Favorites)**:
  - **Nicolo de Conti** (Italian, visited 1420 during Devaraya I).
  - **Abdur Razzaq** (Ambassador of Shah Rukh of Persia, visited 1443 during Devaraya II; described Vijayanagara as one of the most magnificent cities in the world).
  - **Domingo Paes** and **Duarte Barbosa** (Portuguese travelers, visited during Krishnadeva Raya).
  - **Fernao Nuniz** (Portuguese horse-trader, visited 1535 during Achyuta Raya).
- **Battle of Talikota (Battle of Rakkasa-Tangadi) (23 January 1565)**:
  - The coalition armies of four Deccan Sultanates (Bijapur, Golconda, Ahmednagar, and Bidar) united to destroy Vijayanagara.
  - Vijayanagara forces led by prime minister **Aliya Rama Raya** were completely routed; Rama Raya was captured and executed. Hampi was plundered, burned, and reduced to ruins.

### 2. The Bahmani Kingdom (1347–1527 CE)
- Founded in **1347 CE** by Afghan noble **Alauddin Hasan Bahman Shah** (Zafar Khan / Hasan Gangu), who revolted against Muhammad bin Tughlaq. First capital: **Gulbarga** (Ahsanabad), later shifted to **Bidar** in 1424.
- Prime Minister **Mahmud Gawan**: Brilliant administrator who divided the kingdom into 8 provinces (*Tarafs*); built the famous three-storey **Madrasa of Mahmud Gawan** at Bidar in 1472.
- In the late 15th century, the Bahmani kingdom disintegrated into **Five Independent Deccan Sultanates**:
  1. **Bijapur** (Adil Shahi dynasty, founded 1489 by Yusuf Adil Shah) — Built the **Gol Gumbaz** (mausoleum of Muhammad Adil Shah, world's second-largest dome without pillar support, famous for its Whispering Gallery).
  2. **Golconda** (Qutb Shahi dynasty, founded 1512 by Quli Qutb Shah) — Famous for diamond mines (Kollur); Muhammad Quli Qutb Shah built the **Charminar** (Hyderabad) in 1591 to commemorate the eradication of plague.
  3. **Ahmednagar** (Nizam Shahi dynasty, founded 1490 by Malik Ahmad).
  4. **Bidar** (Barid Shahi dynasty, founded 1528 by Ali Barid).
  5. **Berar** (Imad Shahi dynasty, founded 1490 by Fathullah Imad-ul-Mulk).

## 2.4 The Mughal Empire (1526–1857 CE)

```
The Imperial Mughals:
Babur (1526–30) ──► Humayun (1530–40, 1555–56) ──► Akbar (1556–1605) ──► Jahangir (1605–27) ──► Shah Jahan (1628–58) ──► Aurangzeb (1658–1707)
```

### 1. Babur (Zahir-ud-din Muhammad) (1526–1530 CE)
- **Lineage**: Descendant of **Timur** (5th generation on father Umar Shaikh Mirza's side, ruler of Fergana) and **Genghis Khan** (14th generation on mother Qutlugh Nigar Khanum's side).
- **Invasions of India**:
  - Captured Kabul in 1504; invaded the Punjab region multiple times between 1519 and 1524.
  - **First used gunpowder and heavy artillery** in the subcontinent during the siege of Bhera and Bajaur in 1519.
- **The Four Decisive Battles**:
  1. **First Battle of Panipat (21 April 1526)**: Babur defeated **Ibrahim Lodi**, capturing Delhi and Agra. Employed the **Tulghuma system** (flanking movement) and the Ottoman/Rumi cart method (**Araba**), tying carts together with rawhide ropes to shelter matchlock gunners. Artillery commanded by master gunners **Ustad Ali** and **Mustafa**.
  2. **Battle of Khanwa (16 March 1527)**: Fought near Fatehpur Sikri against **Rana Sanga of Mewar** and his Afghan allies (Hasan Khan Mewati, Mahmud Lodi). Babur proclaimed the battle a holy war (**Jihad**), smashed his royal wine cups, swore off liquor, and assumed the title of **Ghazi** (victorious warrior of the faith) after defeating the Rajput confederacy.
  3. **Battle of Chanderi (1528)**: Defeated Rajput chief **Medini Rai** of Malwa and captured the fortress of Chanderi.
  4. **Battle of Ghagra (May 1529)**: Fought near the confluence of the Ganga and Ghagra rivers in Bihar against the joint Afghan forces led by **Mahmud Lodi** and Nusrat Shah of Bengal. This was the **first battle in medieval India fought simultaneously on land and water**.
- **Literary & Cultural Heritage**:
  - Authored his candid, celebrated autobiography **Tuzuk-i-Baburi** (Baburnama) in his native **Chagatai Turkish** language (later translated into Persian by Abdur Rahim Khan-i-Khana).
  - Introduced the Persian-style symmetrically designed terraced garden: **Charbagh System** (four-fold garden divided by running water channels).
  - Constructed the **Aram Bagh** (Ram Bagh) at Agra, the **Kabuli Bagh Mosque** at Panipat (Haryana), and the **Jami Masjid** at Sambhal (UP).
  - Died in December 1530 at Agra. First buried in the Aram Bagh (Agra), but his remains were later transferred to **Bagh-e-Babur** in Kabul (Afghanistan) according to his personal will.

---

### 2. Humayun (Nasir-ud-din Muhammad) (1530–1540, 1555–1556 CE)
- Succeeded Babur at age 22. Divided the empire among his three ambitious brothers: **Kamran** (Kabul and Kandahar), **Askari** (Sambhal), and **Hindal** (Alwar/Mewat).
- Threatened by **Bahadur Shah of Gujarat** (who conquered Malwa and besieged Chittor) and the rising power of Afghan chief **Sher Shah Suri** in Bihar and Bengal.
- **Two Decisive Defeats against Sher Shah Suri**:
  1. **Battle of Chausa (26 June 1539)**: Humayun was caught off-guard on the banks of the Ganga and narrowly escaped drowning on his horse with the help of a water-carrier (*Bhishti*) named Nizam. Sher Shah formally assumed the title of *Farid-ud-din Sher Shah*.
  2. **Battle of Kanauj / Bilgram (17 May 1540)**: Humayun's forces were utterly routed without a pitched battle. Humayun was driven into exile and lived as a fugitive in Sindh, Rajasthan, and Persia for 15 years.
- **Restoration of the Empire (1555 CE)**:
  - With the military assistance of Persian ruler **Shah Tahmasp**, Humayun recaptured Kabul and Kandahar.
  - Following the collapse of the Sur dynasty, Humayun invaded India, defeated Sikandar Suri at the **Battle of Sirhind (1555 CE)**, and re-occupied Delhi and Agra in July 1555.
- **Tragic Death**: In January 1556, while descending the steep stairs of his personal library and astronomical observatory (**Sher Mandal** inside Purana Qila, Delhi) after hearing the call to evening prayer, he tripped and sustained fatal head injuries. Historian Stanley Lane-Poole remarked: *"Humayun tumbled through life, and tumbled out of it."*
- **Literature & Tomb**:
  - Sister **Gulbadan Begum** authored the historical biography **Humayun-Nama** at the request of nephew Akbar.
  - **Humayun's Tomb (Delhi)**: Commissioned in 1565 by his chief queen and widow **Bega Begum (Haji Begum)** and executed during Akbar's reign (1565–1572). Designed by Persian architect **Mirak Mirza Ghiyas**; red sandstone garden-tomb with a double dome of white marble; UNESCO World Heritage Site; direct architectural inspiration for the Taj Mahal.

---

### 2.5 The Sur Empire / Sher Shah Suri (1540–1545 CE)
- **Original Name**: Farid Khan. Conferred the title **Sher Khan** by Bahar Khan Lohani (Governor of Bihar) after slaying a tiger single-handedly.
- Crowned at Delhi after defeating Humayun at Kanauj (1540). Established the **Second Afghan Empire** in India.
- **Administrative Genius**:
  - Built the legendary **Sadak-e-Azam** (now known as the **Grand Trunk Road / GT Road**) running from Sonargaon (Bengal) through Delhi and Lahore to Attock and Peshawar on the Indus River.
  - Constructed **1,700 Sarais** (fortified rest houses) every 2 Kos (approx. 4 miles) along major imperial highways with separate lodging and water provisions for Hindus and Muslims; functioned as mail staging stations (*Dak Chowkis*).
  - Reformed the currency: Discontinued debased coins and introduced the standardized silver coin **Rupiya** (weighing 178 grains of pure silver), which became the precursor to the modern Indian Rupee, alongside the copper coin **Dam** (380 grains, exchange ratio: 1 Rupiya = 64 Dams).
  - **Revenue System**: Introduced the **Zabt / Bandobast** system based on land surveying; peasants granted written title deeds (**Patta**) and signed deed agreements (**Qabuliyat**) fixing land taxes; state share was 1/3rd of the average produce.
  - Maintained strict law and order through collective local responsibility: local village headmen (**Muqaddams**) were held personally accountable for finding thieves or compensating stolen property.
- **Architecture**:
  - Built the **Purana Qila** (Old Fort, Delhi) and its exquisite **Qila-i-Kuhna Mosque**.
  - Built the massive **Rohtas Fort** (Punjab, Pakistan) to check Gakhar tribes and Mongol threats.
  - Built his own magnificent octagonal **Mausoleum at Sasaram** (Bihar), constructed in red sandstone in the middle of a square artificial lake.
- **Death**: Died on 22 May 1545 during the siege of **Kalinjar Fort** (Bundelkhand) when a rocket shell (*Uqqa*) rebounded off the fort wall and ignited an adjacent gunpowder depot.

---

### 3. Akbar the Great (Jalal-ud-din Muhammad) (1556–1605 CE)
- Born on 15 October 1542 at **Amarkot** (Sindh) in the fortress of Hindu chief Rana Prasad.
- Crowned at **Kalanaur** (Gurdaspur, Punjab) at age 13 in February 1556 on an improvised brick throne.
- **Regency of Bairam Khan (1556–1560 CE)**: Bairam Khan served as *Wakil-us-Sultanat* (regent) and assumed the title of *Khan-i-Khanan*.
  - **Second Battle of Panipat (5 November 1556)**: The Mughal army under Bairam Khan faced the Afghan-Hindu general **Hemu (Hemchandra Vikramaditya)**, who had captured Delhi and Agra. Hemu's massive war elephant army was poised for victory when a stray arrow struck his eye, rendering him unconscious; his army panicked and routed; Hemu was captured and executed.
  - Ended Bairam Khan's regency in 1560; Bairam was assassinated en route to Mecca at Patan (Gujarat).
- **Petticoat Government (1560–1562 CE)**: Influenced by foster mother Maham Anaga and foster brother Adham Khan. In 1562, Akbar executed Adham Khan and took complete control of state affairs.
- **Social Reforms & Religious Policy**:
  - 1562: Banned the enslavement of prisoners of war.
  - 1563: Abolished the discriminatory **Pilgrim Tax** on Hindus.
  - 1564: **Abolished the Jizya Tax** on non-Muslim subjects.
  - Married **Harkha Bai** (popularly known as Jodha Bai, daughter of Raja Bharmal of Amber/Jaipur) in 1562.
  - 1575: Constructed the **Ibadat Khana** (House of Worship) at Fatehpur Sikri for theological discussions among Sunni Muslims; in 1578, threw it open to scholars of all faiths (Hindus, Jains, Zoroastrians, Christians, Buddhists).
  - 1579: Promulgated the **Mahzar-Nama (Infallibility Decree)**, drafted by Sheikh Mubarak, making Akbar the supreme arbiter (*Imam-i-Adil*) in religious disputes.
  - 1582: Propounded **Din-i-Ilahi** (*Tauhid-i-Ilahi* / Divine Monotheism), a syncretic ethical system based on the philosophy of **Sulh-i-Kul** (universal peace). Raja **Birbal was the only Hindu who accepted Din-i-Ilahi**.
  - Started the **Ilahi Samvat** (solar calendar) in 1583.
- **Military Campaigns & Territorial Expansion**:
  - Conquest of Malwa (1561), Gondwana (1564 - heroism of Rani Durgavati), Chittor (1568 - siege against Rana Udai Singh; heroic resistance by Jaimal and Patta), Ranthambore (1569).
  - **Conquest of Gujarat (1572–1573)**: Highly strategic maritime province; Akbar saw the Arabian Sea for the first time at Khambhat (Cambay); built the colossal **Buland Darwaza** at Fatehpur Sikri to commemorate this victory.
  - **Battle of Haldighati (18 June 1576)**: Mughal army led by **Raja Man Singh of Amber** and Asaf Khan fought **Maharana Pratap of Mewar** (aided by Afghan commander Hakim Khan Sur and Bhil tribal chief Rana Punja). Maharana Pratap's faithful steed **Chetak** died after jumping across a stream; Pratap continued guerrilla warfare from the Aravalli hills and recaptured most of Mewar before his death in 1597.
  - Annexation of Bengal (1576), Kashmir (1586), Sindh (1591), Balochistan (1595), Kandahar (1595), Ahmednagar (1600 - heroic defense by Chand Bibi), and Asirgarh fortress in Khandesh (1601 - Akbar's final military conquest, opened by "golden keys").
- **Navratnas (The Nine Gems of Akbar's Court)**:
  1. **Raja Birbal (Mahesh Das)**: Chief advisor, celebrated wit, and poet laureate; given the title *Kavi Priya*; died fighting the Yusufzai Afghan tribes in the northwest frontier (1586).
  2. **Tansen (Ramtanu Pandey)**: Legendary classical music genius; title *Mian*; developed Ragas like *Darbari Kanada, Megh Malhar, Deepak*.
  3. **Abul Fazl**: Chief historian and ideologue; authored **Akbarnama** and **Ain-i-Akbari**; murdered in 1602 by Bir Singh Deo Bundela at the instigation of Prince Salim (Jahangir).
  4. **Faizi**: Elder brother of Abul Fazl; Persian poet laureate; translated Bhaskaracharya's Sanskrit mathematical treatise **Lilavati** into Persian.
  5. **Raja Todar Mal**: Finance and revenue wizard; introduced the **Dahsala System** (1580); standardized land survey rods.
  6. **Raja Man Singh**: Grandson of Raja Bharmal of Amber; commander-in-chief of the imperial forces.
  7. **Abdul Rahim Khan-i-Khana**: Son of Bairam Khan; celebrated Hindi and Persian poet (famous for his *Dohas*); translated Babur's memoirs into Persian.
  8. **Fakir Aziao-Din**: Chief mystic and religious advisor.
  9. **Mulla Do-Piyaza**: Chief counsellor and diplomat.

---

### 4. Jahangir (Nur-ud-din Muhammad Salim) (1605–1627 CE)
- Ascended the throne in October 1605 at Agra; adopted the title **Jahangir** ("Conqueror of the World").
- **Zanjir-i-Adl (Chain of Justice)**: Installed a pure gold chain measuring 80 feet with 60 bells connecting the Shah Burj of Agra Fort to a stone post on the banks of the Yamuna River, enabling any aggrieved subject to ring for immediate royal justice.
- Executed the **Fifth Sikh Guru, Guru Arjan Dev** in 1606 for providing blessings and financial help to his rebel son Prince Khusrau.
- **Nur Jahan (Mehr-un-Nisa)**:
  - Married Jahangir in May 1611; conferred the titles *Nur Mahal* (Light of the Palace) and **Nur Jahan** (Light of the World).
  - Exceptionally intelligent, artistic, and politically dominant; formed the powerful court faction **Nur Jahan Junta** (comprising herself, her father Mirza Ghiyas Beg / Itmad-ud-Daulah, brother Asaf Khan, and Prince Khurram). Coins and imperial decrees (*Firmans*) were issued bearing her name alongside the Emperor.
- **Foreign Envoys from England**:
  - **Captain William Hawkins** (1608): Emissary of the British East India Company carrying a letter from King James I; spoke fluent Persian; Jahangir granted him a mansab of 400 and the title **English Khan**, but refused factory concessions due to Portuguese influence.
  - **Sir Thomas Roe** (1615–1619): Official Ambassador of King James I to the Mughal Court; accompanied Jahangir to Ajmer, Mandu, and Ahmedabad; successfully secured royal charters permitting the English to erect factories at Surat, Agra, and Ahmedabad.
- **Golden Era of Mughal Painting**:
  - Jahangir possessed extraordinary connoisseurship of painting, claiming he could identify the artist of any brushstroke.
  - Shifted emphasis from illustrated manuscripts to **miniature portraiture, albums (*Muraqqa*), and natural studies** (birds, flowers, animals).
  - Famous painters: **Ustad Mansur** (world-renowned naturalist: painted the Siberian crane, dodo, Bengal Florican), **Abul Hasan** (master of portraiture; awarded title *Nadir-uz-Zaman*), **Bishan Das** (sent to Persia to sketch the Shah's portrait), **Govardhan**, and **Manohar**.
- **Autobiography & Works**: Authored **Tuzuk-i-Jahangiri** (memoirs in Persian); mutamid Khan later completed its concluding chapters in **Iqbalnama-i-Jahangir**.
- **Tomb**: Passed away at Rajouri in 1627; buried at **Shahdara** (Lahore, on the banks of River Ravi) in a mausoleum commissioned by Nur Jahan.

---

### 5. Shah Jahan (Shihab-ud-din Muhammad Khurram) (1628–1658 CE)
- Proclaimed Emperor in January 1628 at Agra with the support of father-in-law **Asaf Khan**. Married Arjumand Banu Begum (**Mumtaz Mahal**), who died in 1631 during the birth of their 14th child (Gauhar Ara) at Burhanpur.
- His reign is universally hailed as the **Golden Age of Mughal Architecture**.
- **Territorial Campaigns**:
  - Annexed Ahmednagar (1633); forced Bijapur and Golconda to accept Mughal suzerainty and sign subsidiary treaties (1636).
  - Sent Prince Aurangzeb as Viceroy of the Deccan (1636–1644 and 1652–1657).
  - Disastrous campaigns in Central Asia (Balkh and Badakhshan, 1646) and repeated failed sieges of **Kandahar** (1649, 1652, 1653), which was permanently lost to the Safavids of Persia.
- **Masterpieces of Architecture**:
  1. **Taj Mahal (Agra)**: Commenced in 1631 and completed in 1648 (with ancillary gardens and gateway completed by 1653) on the banks of the Yamuna River in white Makrana marble. Chief architect: **Ustad Ahmad Lahori**; calligrapher: **Amanat Khan Shirazi**; master dome builder: **Ismail Khan**.
  2. **Shahjahanabad (Delhi)**: Founded in 1638 as the new imperial capital city, transferring the court from Agra in 1648.
  3. **Red Fort (Lal Qila, Delhi)**: Built between 1639 and 1648 in red sandstone on the Yamuna. Encloses the **Diwan-i-Aam** and the **Diwan-i-Khas** (embellished with the famous Persian couplet: *"Agar firdaus bar roo-e zameen ast, hamin ast-o hamin ast-o hamin ast"* — If there is paradise on earth, it is here, it is here, it is here).
  4. **Peacock Throne (*Takht-i-Taus*)**: Commissioned by Shah Jahan and crafted by goldsmith Bebadal Khan over 7 years; encrusted with rubies, diamonds, and pearls; featured the legendary **Koh-i-Noor diamond** set into one of the peacocks.
  5. **Jama Masjid (Delhi)**: Completed in 1656; largest stone congregational mosque in India.
  6. **Moti Masjid (Pearl Mosque, Agra Fort)**: Built entirely in spotless white marble.
  7. **Shalimar Gardens (Lahore)**: Masterpiece of Mughal garden design laid out in 1641.
  8. **Musamman Burj (Agra Fort)**: Exquisite octagonal marble pavilion built by Shah Jahan facing the Taj Mahal.
- **War of Succession (1657–1658 CE)**:
  - When Shah Jahan fell critically ill in September 1657, a fierce war of succession broke out among his four sons:
    - **Dara Shikoh** (eldest son): Liberal philosopher; disciple of Sufi saint Mian Mir; translated 50 Upanishads into Persian under the title **Sirr-i-Akbar**; authored **Majma-ul-Bahrain** ("The Confluence of the Two Seas" — finding spiritual unity between Hinduism and Islam).
    - **Shah Shuja** (second son): Governor of Bengal.
    - **Aurangzeb** (third son): Religious puritan and brilliant military strategist; Viceroy of Deccan.
    - **Murad Baksh** (youngest son): Governor of Gujarat.
  - **Key Battles**:
    - *Battle of Dharmat (April 1658)*: Aurangzeb and Murad defeated the imperial army led by Jaswant Singh of Marwar.
    - *Battle of Samugarh (May 1658)*: Aurangzeb decisively routed Dara Shikoh's forces near Agra.
    - *Battle of Deorai (1659)*: Final defeat of Dara Shikoh; Dara was captured, paraded in chains through Delhi, and executed on grounds of apostasy.
  - Aurangzeb deposed his father Shah Jahan and imprisoned him in the **Musamman Burj of Agra Fort**, where he spent his final 8 years attended by daughter **Jahanara**, gazing at the Taj Mahal, until his death in 1666.

---

### 6. Aurangzeb (Muhi-ud-din Muhammad Alamgir) (1658–1707 CE)
- Crowned at Delhi under the title **Alamgir** ("Conqueror of the World"). Called **Zinda Pir** (Living Saint) by orthodox Muslims due to his austere lifestyle, sewing caps, and copying Qurans to pay for his personal meals.
- **Territorial Zenith & The Deccan Ulcer**:
  - Reached the **maximum territorial extent of the Mughal Empire**, stretching from Kashmir to Jinji (Tamil Nadu) and from the Hindukush to Chittagong.
  - Spent the final **25 years of his life (1681–1707)** fighting in the Deccan without ever returning to Delhi.
  - Annexed **Bijapur (1686)** and **Golconda (1687)**.
  - Historians note: *"The Deccan ulcer ruined Aurangzeb just as the Spanish ulcer ruined Napoleon."*
- **Religious Orthodoxy & Social Changes**:
  - Appointed moral censors: **Muhtasibs** to enforce Islamic morality and prohibit wine, gambling, and prostitution.
  - Banned court music, royal astrology, Jharokha Darshan (balcony appearances), Tuladan (weighing against gold), and the celebration of Nauroz and Holi in court.
  - Abolished the solar Ilahi calendar and discontinued inscribing the Quranic Kalma on coins to prevent defilement.
  - **1669**: Issued imperial orders for the demolition of Hindu temples, resulting in the destruction of the Kashi Vishwanath temple (Varanasi) and the Keshavdeva temple (Mathura).
  - **1679**: **Re-imposed the Jizya tax** on non-Muslim subjects after a lapse of 115 years, along with commercial pilgrim taxes.
  - Ordered the compiled code of Islamic jurisprudence: **Fatwa-i-Alamgiri** by a council of legal scholars.
- **Conflicts with Sikhs, Rajputs, and Marathas**:
  - **Sikhs**: Executed the **Ninth Sikh Guru, Guru Tegh Bahadur** in 1675 at Chandni Chowk (Delhi) for refusing to convert to Islam (Gurdwara Sis Ganj Sahib marks the site). In response, the **Tenth Guru, Guru Gobind Singh**, founded the **Khalsa Panth** in 1699 at Anandpur Sahib to fight Mughal tyranny.
  - **Rajputs**: Annexation attempts of Marwar after the death of Jaswant Singh led to the 30-year Rajput rebellion led by **Durgadas Rathore**.
  - **Marathas**: Constant conflict with **Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj**. Sent maternal uncle Shaista Khan (1660), whom Shivaji ambushed at Pune in 1663. Sent **Mirza Raja Jai Singh of Amber**, who besieged Purandar and compelled Shivaji to sign the **Treaty of Purandar (1665)**. Shivaji visited Agra court in 1666, was placed under house arrest, and escaped in sweet/fruit baskets. In 1689, Aurangzeb captured and brutally executed Shivaji's son **Chhatrapati Sambhaji**.
- **Architecture**:
  - Built the **Badshahi Mosque** in Lahore (1673), the largest mosque of the era.
  - Built the **Moti Masjid** (Pearl Mosque) in marble inside the Red Fort, Delhi.
  - Built the **Bibi Ka Maqbara** at Aurangabad (Maharashtra), constructed in 1660 by his son Prince Azam Shah in memory of his mother **Dilras Banu Begum (Rabia-ud-Daurani)**; designed by architect Ataullah; known as the *"Dakkani Taj"* or *"Poor Man's Taj"*.
- Died in March 1707 at Ahmednagar at age 88. Buried in an unadorned open-air grave at **Khuldabad** (near Aurangabad).

### 7. Later Mughals (1707–1857 CE) & The Decline
Following Aurangzeb's death in 1707, the empire rapidly disintegrated under weak rulers:

| Ruler | Reign | Key Events & Historical Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Bahadur Shah I (Shah Alam I)** | 1707–1712 | Eldest son of Aurangzeb; ascended at age 63; known as **Shah-i-Bekhabar** (Heedless King) by Khafi Khan for his careless administration; released Shahu from Mughal captivity; made peace with Guru Gobind Singh. |
| **Jahandar Shah** | 1712–1713 | Incompetent prince; first puppet ruler dominated by noble **Zulfiqar Khan**; introduced the ruinous **Ijarah (revenue-farming)** system; known as the "Lumpen Fool". |
| **Farrukhsiyar** | 1713–1719 | Placed on throne by the **Sayyid Brothers** (Abdullah Khan and Husain Ali Khan), celebrated in history as the **"King Makers"**. Executed Sikh leader **Banda Singh Bahadur** (1716). In **1717**, issued the infamous **Golden Farman** to the English East India Company granting duty-free trade concessions in Bengal for an annual payment of ₹3,000 (termed the *Magna Carta of the Company*). Murdered by the Sayyid Brothers in 1719. |
| **Muhammad Shah ("Rangeela")** | 1719–1748 | Pleasure-loving monarch. Overthrew the Sayyid Brothers with the aid of Nizam-ul-Mulk. | **Shah Alam II** | 1759–1806 | Long, tragic reign. Lived outside Delhi for 12 years. Fought the **Battle of Buxar (1764)** alongside Mir Qasim and Shuja-ud-Daula; signed the **Treaty of Allahabad (1765)** granting the British East India Company the legal **Diwani rights (revenue collection)** over Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa. Fought the **Third Battle of Panipat (1761)** during his reign. In **1803**, British General Lord Lake captured Delhi, reducing the Mughal Emperor to an imperial pensioner of the British. |
| **Akbar II** | 1806–1837 | Pensioner of the British. Bestowed the title **"Raja"** on socio-religious reformer **Ram Mohan Roy** in 1831 and sent him to England to lobby for an increase in the royal pension. |
| **Bahadur Shah II (Bahadur Shah Zafar)** | 1837–1857 | **The last Mughal Emperor**. Renowned Urdu poet writing under the pen-name **Zafar**. Chosen as the symbolic titular leader and Emperor of all India during the **Great Revolt of 1857**. Following the suppression of the revolt, captured by Major William Hodson at Humayun's Tomb, tried for treason, stripped of his crown, and exiled to **Rangoon (Burma)**, where he died in 1862. Marked the formal end of the Mughal Empire. |

## 2.6 Mughal Administration Systems & Economy

```
Mughal Administrative Tiers:
Empire (Badshah) ──► Suba (Subahdar) ──► Sarkar (Faujdar / Amalguzar) ──► Pargana (Shiqdar / Amil) ──► Gram / Village (Muqaddam / Patwari)
```

### 1. Central Administration Officials
- **Wazir / Diwan-i-Kul**: Prime Minister and supreme head of financial and revenue administration.
- **Mir Bakshi**: Head of military administration and intelligence; verified military musters, inspected troops, and issued appointment warrants (*Sarkhat*) for Mansabdars.
- **Sadr-us-Sudur**: Supreme ecclesiastical officer; supervised religious affairs, judicial appointments, and granted tax-free charitable land (*Madad-i-Ma'ash* / *Sayurghal*).
- **Mir Saman / Khan-i-Saman**: High steward in charge of the royal imperial household, personal palaces, provisions, and central manufacturing workshops (**Karkhanas**).
- **Qazi-ul-Quzat**: Chief Supreme Court Judge; administered Islamic Sharia law alongside provincial and district Qazis.
- **Muhtasib**: Censor of public morals; supervised weights, measures, market honesty, and compliance with Islamic practices.
- **Mir Atish / Darogha-i-Topkhana**: Head of the Imperial Artillery.

### 2. Provincial & Local Governance Hierarchy
- **Suba (Province)**: The empire was divided by Akbar in 1580 into 12 Subas, later expanded to 15 (after Deccan annexations), and reaching 21 under Aurangzeb.
  - **Subahdar / Sipahsalar**: Executive Governor, commander of provincial armed forces, and keeper of law and order.
  - **Provincial Diwan**: Independent of the Subahdar; reported directly to the central Diwan; controlled provincial finances and revenue.
  - **Bakshi**: Managed the provincial army logistics.
- **Sarkar (District)**:
  - **Faujdar**: Executive military administrator in charge of police and maintaining law and order in the district.
  - **Amalguzar**: Chief officer responsible for surveying and assessing land revenue collection.
  - **Kotwal**: Municipal police commissioner in charge of town security, controlling prisons, and checking night watches.
- **Pargana (Sub-district / Group of villages)**:
  - **Shiqdar**: Maintained law, order, and general administrative control.
  - **Amil / Munsif**: Revenue assessor and tax collector.
  - **Qanungo**: Keeper of official hereditary land survey and revenue records.
- **Gram (Village)**:
  - **Muqaddam**: Hereditary village headman responsible for local peace and revenue collection.
  - **Patwari**: Village accountant keeping records of peasant landholdings and crop yields.

### 3. The Mansabdari System
- Introduced by **Akbar in 1571 CE** as the bedrock of the civil service and military organization.
- Derived from the Mongolian decimal military system introduced by Genghis Khan.
- **Mansab** literally means "rank" or "position". It was **not hereditary**; appointment, promotion, and dismissal depended exclusively on the Emperor's will.
- Every officer was designated a dual rank:
  1. **Zat**: Determined personal standing in the imperial hierarchy and personal cash salary.
  2. **Sawar**: Fixed the exact number of mounted cavalrymen (*Sawar*) the officer was obligated to maintain for the state.
- **Dah-Bisti Rule**: For every 10 sawars maintained, the Mansabdar was required to keep 20 horses (ratio of 2 horses per sawar) to avoid animal fatigue in campaigns.
- Ranks ranged from 10 up to 10,000 (ranks above 5,000 were originally reserved exclusively for royal princes).
- **Innovations by Later Rulers**:
  - *Jahangir*: Introduced the **Du-Aspa Sih-Aspa** system (literally "two-horse, three-horse"), allowing selected nobles to maintain double or triple the cavalry strength without altering their Zat status.
  - *Shah Jahan*: Introduced the **Month-Scale System** (*Mahana Jagir*) due to shortfalls between the assessed revenue (*Jama*) and actual realization (*Hasil*).

### 4. The Jagirdari System & Land Revenue Classification
- **Jagir**: An assignment of the land revenue of a designated territory given to a Mansabdar in lieu of a cash salary.
- A Jagir was **strictly non-hereditary and transferable**; officers were routinely transferred to a different Jagir every 3 to 4 years to prevent them from building local feudal power.
- **Types of Jagirs**:
  - *Tankha Jagirs*: Standard transferable land assignments given in place of salary.
  - *Watan Jagirs*: Non-transferable, hereditary assignments granted to local rulers (such as Rajput Rajas) in their ancestral homelands.
  - *Mashrut Jagirs*: Conditional revenue grants made for specific military tasks.
  - *Khalisa Land*: Crown lands whose entire revenue went directly to the imperial treasury.
- **The Dahsala System (1580 CE)**:
  - Engineered by Akbar's finance minister **Raja Todar Mal** (assisted by Muzaffar Khan Turbati).
  - Calculated the average agricultural yield and average market prices of each crop over the preceding **10 years (1570–1580 CE)**.
  - One-third (1/3rd) of the average produce was fixed as the state's permanent revenue share.
  - Peasant tax could be paid in cash or kind, though cash was preferred.
- **Land Classification based on Cultivation**:
  1. **Polaj**: Land cultivated continuously every year; never left fallow; paid full revenue.
  2. **Parauti**: Land left fallow for 1 or 2 years to recover its natural fertility.
  3. **Chachar**: Land left fallow for 3 or 4 years.
  4. **Banjar**: Low-grade wasteland uncultivated for 5 or more years.

## 2.8 The Maratha Empire and Peshwas

```
Maratha Leadership:
Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj (1674–80) ──► Sambhaji ──► Rajaram ──► Shahu ──► Peshwa Supremacy (1713–1818)
```

### 1. Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj (1627/1630–1680 CE)
- Born at the hill fortress of **Shivneri** (Pune). Father: **Shahaji Bhonsle** (military jagirdar of Bijapur); Mother: **Jijabai**; Spiritual and moral mentor: **Guru Samarth Ramdas** and guardian **Dadaji Kondadeo**.
- Captured his first fort, **Torna**, at age 16 (1646); subsequently captured Raigad, Kondana (Sinhagad), and Purandar.
- **Encounter with Afzal Khan (1659)**: Bijapur sent veteran general Afzal Khan to crush Shivaji; at a private meeting below Pratapgad fort, Afzal Khan attempted to strangle Shivaji, who disemboweled him using concealed steel tiger claws (**Bagh Nakh**) and a dagger (*Bichuwa*).
- **Encounter with Shaista Khan (1663)**: Aurangzeb's viceroy occupied Pune; Shivaji made a daring midnight raid on Shaista Khan's harem quarters at Lal Mahal, cutting off Khan's fingers and forcing him to flee.
- Sacked the wealthy Mughal port of **Surat** twice: in **1664** and **1670**.
- **Treaty of Purandar (11 June 1665)**:
  - Signed between Shivaji and Mughal general **Mirza Raja Jai Singh of Amber**.
  - Shivaji surrendered **23 out of his 35 forts** to the Mughals, retaining 12 forts for himself.
  - Shivaji's son Sambhaji was granted a Mughal mansab of 5,000.
  - Shivaji visited Aurangzeb's court at Agra in May 1666; insulted by low mansab protocol, he protested openly, was placed under house arrest, and escaped hidden in baskets of sweets.
- **Coronation (1674 CE)**:
  - Formally crowned as independent sovereign monarch at **Raigad Fort** on 6 June 1674 by renowned Banaras scholar **Gaga Bhatt**.
  - Assumed the exalted titles **Chhatrapati** and *Haindava Dharmoddharak* (Protector of the Hindu Faith).
- **Ashta Pradhan (Council of Eight Ministers)**:
  1. **Peshwa (Mukhya Pradhan)**: Prime Minister; general administration and civil governance.
  2. **Amatya (Majumdar)**: Finance Minister; maintained public accounts.
  3. **Senapati (Sari Naubat)**: Commander-in-Chief of the armed forces.
  4. **Waqia-Navis (Mantri)**: Home Minister, intelligence, and court record keeper.
  5. **Sachiv (Shuru-Navis)**: Royal correspondence and checking state accounts.
  6. **Sumant (Dabir)**: Foreign Affairs Minister.
  7. **Nyayadhish**: Chief Justice.
  8. **Panditrao (Sadar)**: Religious head, charities, and moral grants.
- **Taxes**:
  - **Chauth**: 1/4th (25%) of the standard land revenue paid by non-Maratha neighboring states in exchange for protection from Maratha raids.
  - **Sardeshmukhi**: Additional 10% levy claimed by Shivaji as the hereditary supreme head (*Sar-Deshmukh*) of Maharashtra.
- **Guerrilla Warfare**: Pioneer of **Ganimi Kava** (swift guerrilla warfare tactics utilizing the hilly terrain of the Western Ghats).

### 2. The Era of the Peshwas (1713–1818 CE)
Following the release of Shahu by Bahadur Shah I, power shifted from the royal descendants to the hereditary Prime Ministers (**Peshwas**) based at **Pune**:
1. **Balaji Vishwanath (1713–1720 CE)**: First hereditary Peshwa; negotiated the 1719 treaty with the Sayyid Brothers securing Chauth rights over the Deccan.
2. **Baji Rao I (1720–1740 CE)**:
   - Greatest military cavalry strategist of the Marathas; proponent of Hindu-Pad-Padshahi.
   - Famously declared: *"Let us strike at the trunk of the withering tree, and the branches will fall of themselves."*
   - Won the **Battle of Palkhed (1728)** against the Nizam of Hyderabad, forcing the Treaty of Mungi-Shevgaon.
   - Snatched Salsette and Bassein from the Portuguese (1739).
   - Formed the **Maratha Confederacy**: Scindias of Gwalior, Holkars of Indore, Gaekwads of Baroda, Bhonsles of Nagpur, and Peshwas of Pune.
3. **Balaji Baji Rao (Nana Saheb) (1740–1761 CE)**:
   - Maratha power reached its territorial zenith, hoisting the saffron flag from **Attock (on the Indus) to Cuttack**.
   - **Third Battle of Panipat (14 January 1761)**:
     - The Maratha grand army led by **Sadashivrao Bhau** and crown prince **Vishwasrao** (with artillery commanded by Ibrahim Khan Gardi) faced the Afghan invader **Ahmad Shah Abdali** and his allies (Najib-ud-Daulah of Rohilkhand and Shuja-ud-Daula of Awadh).
     - Marathas suffered catastrophic defeat; 28,000 soldiers slain, including Bhau and Vishwasrao.
     - A banker's message to Nana Saheb read: *"Two pearls have been dissolved, 27 gold coins lost, and of the silver and copper the total cannot be cast up."* Nana Saheb died of grief shortly after.
     - Historian Sardesai noted: *"Panipat did not decide who was to rule India, but rather who was NOT to rule India"*, opening the doorway for the British East India Company.

# 3. Modern India & The Freedom Struggle

## 3.1 Advent of Europeans in India
- **Chronological Sequence of Arrival**: **Portuguese (1498) ➔ Dutch (1605) ➔ English (1608) ➔ Danes (1620) ➔ French (1664)**.
  *(Mnemonic: **P-D-E-D-F**)*

```
Arrival of European Trading Powers:
1498 (Portuguese) ──► 1605 (Dutch) ──► 1608 (English) ──► 1620 (Danes) ──► 1664 (French)
```

1. **The Portuguese**:
   - **Vasco da Gama**: Discovered the direct sea route to India around the Cape of Good Hope; landed at **Calicut** on **20 May 1498**; welcomed by the Hindu ruler **Zamorin (Samudri)**.
   - **Francisco de Almeida (1505–1509)**: First Portuguese Viceroy; initiated the **"Blue Water Policy" (*Cartaz System*)** aimed at naval supremacy in the Indian Ocean.
   - **Alfonso de Albuquerque (1509–1515)**: Real founder of Portuguese power in the East; **conquered Goa from the Sultan of Bijapur (Ismail Adil Shah) in 1510**; abolished Sati in Goa.
   - **Nino da Cunha (1529–1538)**: Transferred the Portuguese capital from Cochin to Goa in 1530.
   - First to bring printing presses (Goa, 1556) and cultivate **tobacco, potato, tomato, and chilli** in India.
2. **The Dutch (VOC - 1602)**:
   - Established their first Indian factory at **Masulipatnam** (Andhra Pradesh) in 1605.
   - Crushed by the British at the **Battle of Bedara (Chinsurah, Bengal) in 1759**, forcing the Dutch to abandon India and focus on the Indonesian spice islands.
3. **The English East India Company (1600)**:
   - Formed on 31 December 1600 under a Royal Charter granted by Queen Elizabeth I.
   - First factory established at **Surat** (1608/1612) following Captain Thomas Best's victory over the Portuguese at the Battle of Swally.
   - In 1639, Francis Day obtained land from the Raja of Chandragiri and constructed **Fort St. George** at Madras.
   - In 1668, acquired **Bombay** from King Charles II (who received it as royal dowry on marrying Catherine of Braganza of Portugal) for an annual rent of £10.
   - In 1690, **Job Charnock** founded Calcutta by amalgamating three villages (Sutanuti, Gobindapur, Kalikata); fortified it as **Fort William** in 1700.
4. **The French (Compagnie des Indes Orientales - 1664)**:
   - Founded by Finance Minister Colbert during the reign of King Louis XIV.
   - First French factory established at Surat (1668) by Caron; founded **Pondicherry** in 1674 under **François Martin**.
   - **The Three Carnatic Wars (Anglo-French Rivalry: 1746–1763)**:
     - *1st Carnatic War (1746–1748)*: Caused by Austrian War of Succession; Dupleix captured Madras; Battle of St. Thome; ended with Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle.
     - *2nd Carnatic War (1749–1754)*: Proxy war for succession in Hyderabad and Arcot; Robert Clive captured Arcot (1751); ended with Treaty of Pondicherry.
     - *3rd Carnatic War (1758–1763)*: **Battle of Wandiwash (22 January 1760)**: British General **Sir Eyre Coote** decisively crushed the French army under Count de Lally, permanently ending French imperial dreams in India; settled by the **Treaty of Paris (1763)**.

## 3.2 British Conquests and Major Battles
- **Battle of Plassey (23 June 1757)**:
  - Fought on the banks of the Bhagirathi River in Nadia district (Bengal).
  - British forces led by **Robert Clive** faced the young Nawab of Bengal, **Siraj-ud-Daulah**.
  - Siraj-ud-Daulah was betrayed by his commander-in-chief **Mir Jafar**, along with Rai Durlabh, Yar Latif, and banker **Jagat Seth**.
  - Siraj fled, was captured, and executed by Mir Jafar's son Miran.
  - Clive installed Mir Jafar as the puppet Nawab. Established the foundation of British dominion in India.
- **Battle of Buxar (22 October 1764)**:
  - Fought at Buxar (Bihar) between the British East India Company forces led by **Major Hector Munro** and the joint confederacy of:
    1. **Mir Qasim** (deposed Nawab of Bengal).
    2. **Shuja-ud-Daula** (Nawab of Awadh).
    3. **Shah Alam II** (Mughal Emperor).
  - Munro thoroughly defeated the combined army in a pitched battle, proving British military superiority.
  - Concluded by the **Treaty of Allahabad (16 August 1765)** signed by Robert Clive:
    - Mughal Emperor Shah Alam II granted the **Diwani (rights to collect land revenue)** of Bengal, Bihar, and Orissa to the Company in exchange for an annual tribute of ₹26 lakhs.
    - Clive instituted the **Dual Government in Bengal (1765–1772)**: Company held Diwani (revenue/power without responsibility), while the puppet Nawab held Nizamat (police/judicial administration without resources). Abolished by Warren Hastings in 1772.
- **Anglo-Mysore Wars**:
  - *1st War (1767–1769)*: Hyder Ali defeated the British; Treaty of Madras.
  - *2nd War (1780–1784)*: Hyder Ali died of cancer in 1782; son **Tipu Sultan** continued fighting; Treaty of Mangalore.
  - *3rd War (1790–1792)*: Lord Cornwallis defeated Tipu; **Treaty of Seringapatam (1792)**: Tipu ceded half his kingdom and gave two sons as hostages.
  - *4th War (1799)*: Lord Wellesley attacked Seringapatam; **Tipu Sultan died heroically defending his capital on 4 May 1799**. Mysore placed under the Wodeyar dynasty under Subsidiary Alliance.
- **Anglo-Maratha Wars**:
  - *1st War (1775–1782)*: Settled by the **Treaty of Salbai (1782)**, guaranteeing 20 years of peace.
  - *2nd War (1803–1805)*: Peshwa Baji Rao II signed the **Treaty of Bassein (1802)**, accepting Subsidiary Alliance; British defeated Scindia and Bhonsle.
  - *3rd War (1817–1818)*: Lord Hastings crushed the Pindaris and the Maratha confederacy; **Peshwaship was abolished**; Baji Rao II was pensioned off to Bithoor (Kanpur).
- **Anglo-Sikh Wars**:
  - *1st War (1845–1846)*: Fought after the death of Maharaja Ranjit Singh; British defeated the Khalsa army; **Treaty of Lahore (1846)**.
  - *2nd War (1848–1849)*: Lord Dalhousie annexed the Punjab completely on 29 March 1849; young Maharaja Duleep Singh was pensioned off to England, and the **Koh-i-Noor diamond** was surrendered to Queen Victoria.

## 3.3 Governor-Generals and Viceroys of India

### Governor-Generals of Bengal (1773–1833)
1. **Warren Hastings (1773–1785)**:
   - First Governor-General of Bengal (under **Regulating Act of 1773**).
   - Established the Supreme Court at Calcutta in 1774 with **Sir Elijah Impey** as First Chief Justice.
   - Abolished the Dual Government of Bengal (1772).
   - Sir William Jones founded the **Asiatic Society of Bengal** (1784).
   - Only Governor-General to face impeachment proceedings in British Parliament (led by Edmund Burke, later acquitted).
2. **Lord Cornwallis (1786–1793)**:
   - **Father of the Indian Civil Services (ICS)**.
   - Introduced the **Permanent Settlement of Bengal (*Zamindari Bandobast*) in 1793**: Zamindars recognized as permanent owners of land; state revenue share fixed permanently at 10/11ths, with Zamindars retaining 1/11th. Implemented the strict "Sunset Law".
   - Introduced the **Cornwallis Code** (1793) separating judicial from revenue functions.
   - Died in India; his tomb is at Ghazipur (UP).
3. **Lord Wellesley (1798–1805)**:
   - Called himself the *"Bengal Tiger"*.
   - Engineered the **Subsidiary Alliance System (*Sahayak Sandhi*)**: Indian rulers surrendered their foreign relations, maintained British troops at their expense, and expelled other Europeans.
   - **Order of States joining Subsidiary Alliance**: **Hyderabad (1798) ➔ Mysore (1799) ➔ Tanjore (1799) ➔ Awadh (1801) ➔ Peshwa (1802) ➔ Bhonsle (1803) ➔ Scindia (1804)**.
   - Established **Fort William College** at Calcutta (1800) to train young British civil servants.

### Governor-Generals of India (1833–1858)
1. **Lord William Bentinck (1828–1835)**:
   - **First official Governor-General of India** (under the **Charter Act of 1833**).
   - Passed the historic **Sati Abolition Regulation (Regulation XVII of December 1829)**, making Sati illegal and punishable as culpable homicide, assisted by **Raja Ram Mohan Roy**.
   - Suppressed the organized hereditary gang of assassins known as the **Thugs** through military officer **William Sleeman** (1830).
   - Approved **Macaulay's Minute on Education (1835)**, making English the official medium of higher instruction in India (*"Downward Filtration Theory"*).
2. **Lord Dalhousie (James Andrew Broun-Ramsay) (1848–1856)**:
   - Youngest Governor-General (age 36).
   - **Doctrine of Lapse (*Vigat Ka Siddhant*)**: Refused to recognize the political rights of adopted sons of Indian rulers who died without a natural male heir; annexed their states.
   - **Order of Annexation under Doctrine of Lapse**: **Satara (1848) ➔ Jaitpur & Sambalpur (1849) ➔ Baghat (1850) ➔ Udaipur (1852) ➔ Jhansi (1853) ➔ Nagpur (1854)**.
   - **Annexation of Awadh (1856)**: Annexed not through Lapse, but on the false pretext of "misgovernance and corruption" (*report by Outram*), deposing Nawab Wajid Ali Shah.
   - **Father of Indian Railways**: Laid the **first railway line in India from Bombay to Thane (34 km)**, operational on **16 April 1853**.
   - Laid the first electric telegraph line from **Calcutta to Agra** (1853).
   - Introduced uniform 2-pice postal rates under the **Post Office Act of 1854**.
   - Implemented **Charles Wood's Education Despatch (1854)**, celebrated as the *"Magna Carta of English Education in India"*.
   - Drafted the **Hindu Widows' Remarriage Act (1856)** (passed by Lord Canning), vigorously championed by Pandit **Ishwar Chandra Vidyasagar**.

### Viceroys of India (1858–1947)
1. **Lord Canning (1856–1862)**:
   - Last Governor-General of the Company and **First Viceroy of India** under the **Government of India Act 1858** (Queen Victoria's Proclamation read at Allahabad on 1 Nov 1858 transferring power to the British Crown).
   - Governor-General during the **Great Revolt of 1857**.
   - Established the first three modern universities at **Calcutta, Bombay, and Madras (1857)**.
   - Introduced the Indian Penal Code (IPC 1860, drafted by Macaulay) and Code of Criminal Procedure (1861).
   - Introduced the Portfolio System in administration.
2. **Lord Mayo (1869–1872)**:
   - Began the process of financial decentralization in India.
   - Conducted the **first census in Indian history (1872)**.
   - Founded Mayo College at Ajmer.
   - Only Viceroy to be **assassinated in office**, stabbed by Sher Ali Afridi (an Afghan convict) at Port Blair (Andaman Islands) in 1872.
3. **Lord Lytton (1876–1880)**:
   - Highly reactionary and repressive administrator.
   - Organized the lavish **Delhi Durbar of 1877** during the catastrophic Great Famine of 1876–1878, proclaiming Queen Victoria as *Kaisar-i-Hind*.
   - Enacted the **Vernacular Press Act of 1878** ("Gagging Act") to confiscate presses of Indian-language newspapers criticizing British policy (aimed particularly at Amrita Bazar Patrika).
   - Passed the **Arms Act of 1878**, making it a criminal offense for Indians to carry weapons without a license.
   - Reduced the maximum age limit for the ICS examination from 21 to **19 years** to handicap Indian candidates.
4. **Lord Ripon (1880–1884)**:
   - Revered by Indians as **"Ripon the Good"**; the most liberal Viceroy.
   - **Father of Local Self-Government in India** (famous Resolution of 1882 establishing municipal boards and rural district councils).
   - Repealed the hated Vernacular Press Act in 1882.
   - Conducted the **first regular, synchronous decennial census of India (1881)**.
   - Enacted the **First Factory Act of 1881** to prohibit child labor below 7 years and limit working hours.
   - Appointed the **Hunter Commission on Education (1882)**.
   - **Ilbert Bill Controversy (1883)**: Proposed by Law Member C. P. Ilbert to empower Indian judges to try European offenders; triggered a racist revolt by the Anglo-Indian community, forcing the government to withdraw the clause.
5. **Lord Dufferin (1884–1888)**:
   - Viceroy during the **foundation of the Indian National Congress (1885)**; later ridiculed the Congress as representing a *"microscopic minority"* of the people.
   - Third Anglo-Burmese War (1885): Annexed Upper Burma.
6. **Lord Curzon (1899–1905)**:
   - Energetic yet deeply imperialistic.
   - Ordered the **Partition of Bengal on 16 October 1905** on communal lines (creating East Bengal & Assam with Muslim majority and West Bengal with Hindu majority), sparking the historic **Swadeshi and Boycott Movement**.
   - Passed the **Ancient Monuments Preservation Act of 1904**; established the Archaeological Survey of India (ASI) on permanent footing under Director-General **Sir John Marshall**.
   - Set up the Police Commission under Andrew Fraser (1902) and Universities Act of 1904.
7. **Lord Minto II (1905–1910)**:
   - Passed the **Morley-Minto Reforms (Indian Councils Act of 1909)**, introducing **Separate Electorates for Muslims**, institutionalizing communalism in Indian politics.
8. **Lord Hardinge II (1910–1916)**:
   - Held the grand **Delhi Durbar of 1911** visited by King George V and Queen Mary.
   - **Annulled the Partition of Bengal** (1911).
   - **Transferred the capital of India from Calcutta to Delhi** (December 1911; operational from 1912).
   - Rash Behari Bose hurled a bomb at Hardinge's elephant carriage in Chandni Chowk (Delhi Conspiracy Case, 1912).
9. **Lord Chelmsford (1916–1921)**:
   - Passed the **Government of India Act 1919 (Montagu-Chelmsford Reforms)** introducing **Dyarchy** (dual governance: reserved and transferred subjects) in the provinces.
   - Enacted the draconian **Rowlatt Act (March 1919)**, permitting detention without trial.
   - Presided during the horrific **Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919)**.
10. **Lord Irwin (1926–1931)**:
    - Visit of the **Simon Commission (1928)**.
    - Promulgated the Deepavali Declaration (1929).
    - Civil Disobedience Movement and Dandi March (1930).
    - First Round Table Conference (1930).
    - Signed the **Gandhi-Irwin Pact on 5 March 1931**, leading to Congress participation in the 2nd Round Table Conference.
11. **Lord Linlithgow (1936–1944)**:
    - Longest tenure as Viceroy.
    - Provincial elections held under GoI Act 1935 (1937).
    - Dragged India into World War II without consulting Indian leaders (1939), prompting Congress ministries to resign en masse.
    - Delivered the **August Offer (1940)**; dispatched the **Cripps Mission (1942)**; crushed the **Quit India Movement (1942)**.
12. **Lord Mountbatten (March 1947 – June 1948)**:
    - Last Viceroy of British India and First Governor-General of independent India.
    - Declared the **Mountbatten Plan (June 3rd Plan)** for the partition of British India into India and Pakistan.
    - **Sir Cyril Radcliffe** appointed to demarcate the boundary lines (Radcliffe Line).
- **C. Rajagopalachari (June 1948 – January 1950)**: The **first and only Indian Governor-General of India** until the adoption of the Constitution on 26 January 1950.

## 3.4 The Revolt of 1857 (First War of Independence)

```
Revolt Outbreak:
Barrackpore (Mangal Pandey, 29 March) ──► Meerut Outbreak (10 May) ──► Delhi Capture (11 May) ──► Mass Spread
```

### Causes of the Uprising
- **Political Causes**: Aggressive expansion under Dalhousie's **Doctrine of Lapse** (annexation of Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur); unceremonious annexation of Awadh (1856) on grounds of misrule, throwing thousands of court retainers and soldiers out of work; discontinuance of pension to Nana Saheb (adopted son of Baji Rao II).
- **Economic Causes**: Ruinous revenue settlements (Permanent Settlement, Ryotwari, Mahalwari) leading to widespread peasant dispossession, rural indebtedness, and destruction of traditional Indian handicraft artisans by duty-free British manufactured goods.
- **Socio-Religious Causes**: Introduction of the Religious Disabilities Act (1850) protecting inheritance rights of Christian converts; Lex Loci Act; fear of forcible conversion by Christian missionaries; taxation of mosque and temple lands; resentment against Sati abolition and Widow Remarriage acts.
- **Military Causes**: Discrimination in promotions and pay (highest post an Indian could reach was *Subedar*); the **General Service Enlistment Act of 1856** requiring sepoys to serve overseas across the "Kala Pani" (which violated caste rules).
- **Immediate Trigger**: Introduction of the **Pattern 1853 Enfield Rifle**, whose paper cartridges had to be bitten off before loading. Rumors spread that the greasing grease was composed of a mixture of **cow fat** (sacred to Hindus) and **pig fat** (repugnant to Muslims).

### Key Timeline & Major Leaders
- **29 March 1857 (Barrackpore, Bengal)**: Young sepoy **Mangal Pandey** of the 34th Bengal Native Infantry mutinied and shot British officers Lieutenant Baugh and Sergeant-Major Hewson. He was hanged on 8 April 1857.
- **10 May 1857 (Meerut)**: 85 soldiers of the 3rd Native Cavalry refused to touch the greased cartridges and were court-martialled and chained. On 10 May, the sepoys broke open the jail, shot their British officers, and marched through the night to Delhi.
- **11–12 May 1857 (Delhi)**: Sepoys entered Delhi, seized the city, and proclaimed the aging Mughal Emperor **Bahadur Shah II (Zafar)** as the symbolic **Shahenshah-i-Hindustan** (Emperor of all India).

| Center of Revolt | Indian Leader(s) | British Military Suppressor |
|---|---|---|

## 3.8 The Gandhian Era & Mass Struggles

### Mohandas Karamchand Gandhi (1869–1948)
- Born: **2 October 1869** at Porbandar (Gujarat). Studied law in England; went to South Africa in 1893 to handle a legal suit for merchant Dada Abdulla.
- **South African Phase (1893–1914)**: Thrown out of a first-class train compartment at Pietermaritzburg station; founded the **Natal Indian Congress (1894)**; developed the technique of **Satyagraha** (Passive Resistance based on Truth and Non-violence); founded **Phoenix Settlement** (Durban, 1904) and **Tolstoy Farm** (Johannesburg, 1910); published newspaper *Indian Opinion*.
- **Return to India**: Landed at Bombay on **9 January 1915** (commemorated annually as **Pravasi Bharatiya Divas**). Met political guru **Gopal Krishna Gokhale**, who advised him to travel across India for one year before entering politics. Founded Sabarmati Ashram at Ahmedabad in 1917.

### Early Experiments in Satyagraha
1. **Champaran Satyagraha (1917, Bihar) — First Civil Disobedience**:
   - European planters forced peasants to cultivate Indigo on 3/20th of their land under the exploitative **Tinkathia System**.
   - Local peasant leader **Rajkumar Shukla** persuaded Gandhi to visit Champaran.
   - Government appointed a commission; abolished Tinkathia; planters refunded 25% of illegal exactions.
   - Rabindranath Tagore conferred the title **"Mahatma"** on Gandhi during this campaign.
2. **Ahmedabad Mill Strike (March 1918) — First Hunger Strike**:
   - Dispute between mill owners and workers over the withdrawal of the **Plague Bonus**.
   - Gandhi undertook a fast-unto-death; mill owners conceded and granted a **35% wage hike**.
3. **Kheda Satyagraha (March 1918, Gujarat) — First Non-Cooperation**:
   - Crops failed due to drought; revenue code mandated tax remission if yield was under 25%, but the government demanded full taxes.
   - Gandhi and **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** organized peasants to refuse tax payment; government issued secret orders to collect revenue only from those who could afford it.

### Major Mass Movements
- **Rowlatt Satyagraha (April 1919)**:
  - Passed by British government under the Anarchical and Revolutionary Crimes Act of 1919 (**Rowlatt Act**), empowering arrest and indefinite detention of any person without trial or legal representation (*"No Dalil, No Vakil, No Appeal"*).
  - Gandhi organized the Satyagraha Sabha and called for an all-India strike on 6 April 1919.
- **Jallianwala Bagh Massacre (13 April 1919, Baisakhi Day)**:
  - An unarmed crowd gathered at Jallianwala Bagh in Amritsar to peacefully protest the arrest of local leaders **Dr. Saifuddin Kitchlew** and **Dr. Satyapal**.
  - **Brigadier-General Reginald Dyer** blocked the sole narrow exit and ordered troops to open fire without warning; 379 killed and 1,200 wounded per official Hunter Committee report (actual deaths exceeded 1,000).
  - In protest, **Rabindranath Tagore renounced his British Knighthood** (*Sir*), and Gandhi surrendered his Boer War medal **Kaisar-i-Hind**.
- **Khilafat & Non-Cooperation Movement (1920–1922)**:
  - Initiated by the **Ali Brothers (Maulana Mohammad Ali and Shaukat Ali)** to protest the dismemberment of the Ottoman Empire (Turkey) and the removal of the Caliph (*Khalifa*).
  - Gandhi linked the Khilafat cause with Swaraj; Congress ratified the **Non-Cooperation Movement** at the Nagpur Session (1920).
  - Boycott of government schools, law courts, British cloth, foreign goods, titles, and honors; promotion of Charkha and Khadi.
  - **Chauri Chaura Incident (4 February 1922)**: An angry mob attacked and burned a police station at Chauri Chaura (Gorakhpur, UP), killing 22 policemen. Shocked by the violation of non-violence, **Gandhi unilaterally suspended the entire Non-Cooperation Movement on 12 February 1922** (Bardoli resolution).
- **Civil Disobedience Movement & Dandi March (1930–1934)**:
  - Triggered by Lord Irwin's refusal of Gandhi's 11-point ultimatum.
  - **Historic Dandi March**: Gandhi, along with **78 chosen followers**, set out on foot from **Sabarmati Ashram on 12 March 1930** and marched **240 miles (385 km)** across Gujarat, reaching the coastal village of **Dandi on 5 April 1930**. On the morning of **6 April 1930**, Gandhi picked up a lump of natural salt, breaking the British monopoly salt law and inaugurating the Civil Disobedience Movement.
  - Movement spread across the country:
    - *Tamil Nadu*: C. Rajagopalachari led the march from Tiruchirappalli to Vedaranyam.
    - *Kerala*: K. Kelappan marched from Calicut to Payyanur.
    - *North-West Frontier Province*: **Khan Abdul Ghaffar Khan (Badshah Khan / Frontier Gandhi)** mobilized the non-violent volunteer organization **Khudai Khidmatgar** (Servants of God, known as *Red Shirts*).
    - *Dharasana Salt Works (Gujarat)*: Sarojini Naidu, Imam Saheb, and Gandhi's son Manilal led peaceful volunteers who were brutally lathi-charged by police without retaliating.
  - **Gandhi-Irwin Pact (5 March 1931)**: Signed in Delhi; government agreed to release political prisoners not guilty of violence and permit coastal salt manufacturing; Congress agreed to suspend Civil Disobedience and attend the **Second Round Table Conference** in London.
- **The Round Table Conferences (London: 1930–1932)**:
  - *1st RTC (1930)*: Boycotted by Congress; attended by B. R. Ambedkar, Tej Bahadur Sapru, Muslim League.
  - *2nd RTC (1931)*: **Gandhi attended as the sole representative of the Congress**; failed due to communal electorate deadlocks.
  - *3rd RTC (1932)*: Poorly attended; Congress boycotted.
  - *(Note: **Dr. B. R. Ambedkar** and **Tej Bahadur Sapru** attended all three Round Table Conferences).*
- **Communal Award & Poona Pact (1932)**:
  - British Prime Minister **Ramsay MacDonald** announced the **Communal Award** in August 1932, granting **separate electorates to the Depressed Classes** (treating them as a separate minority from Hindus).
  - Gandhi, imprisoned in Yerwada Central Jail (Pune), went on an indefinite fast-unto-death against this division.
  - **Poona Pact (24 September 1932)**: Signed between **Dr. B. R. Ambedkar** and Hindu leaders (Madan Mohan Malaviya on behalf of Gandhi); abandoned separate electorates for depressed classes in exchange for **increasing reserved seats in provincial legislatures from 71 to 147**.
- **Quit India Movement ("August Kranti") (1942)**:
  - Sparked by the failure of the **Cripps Mission (March 1942)** (which Gandhi called a *"post-dated cheque on a crashing bank"*).
  - The All-India Congress Committee met at **Gowalia Tank Maidan (August Kranti Maidan), Bombay**, and passed the historic **Quit India Resolution on 8 August 1942**.
  - Gandhi delivered his stirring mantra to the nation: **"DO OR DIE" (*Karo ya Maro*)** — *"We shall either free India or die in the attempt."*
  - In the early hours of 9 August 1942, the British launched **Operation Zero Hour**, arresting all major leaders (Gandhi imprisoned at Aga Khan Palace, Pune; Nehru, Patel, Azad at Ahmednagar Fort).
  - **Aruna Asaf Ali** famously hoisted the Indian National Flag at Gowalia Tank Maidan following the arrests.
  - Spontaneous leaderless mass rebellion: Government buildings, post offices, and railway stations attacked; underground networks operated by **Ram Manohar Lohia, Jayaprakash Narayan, and Aruna Asaf Ali**; **Usha Mehta** operated an underground secret Congress radio station.
  - **Parallel Governments (*Prati Sarkar*) Established**:
    - *Ballia (UP)*: Led by Chittu Pandey (August 1942).
    - *Tamluk (Midnapore, Bengal)*: Tamralipta Jatiya Sarkar (December 1942 to 1944).
    - *Satara (Maharashtra)*: Led by Nana Patil and Y. B. Chavan (longest surviving: 1943–1946).

## 3.9 Constitutional Milestones & Independence (1940–1947)
- **August Offer (1940)**: Issued by Viceroy Linlithgow; proposed Dominion Status and post-war constituent assembly; rejected by Congress.
- **Individual Satyagraha (October 1940)**: Launched by Gandhi to assert freedom of speech against forced war participation. **First Satyagrahi: Acharya Vinoba Bhave**; **Second: Jawaharlal Nehru**; **Third: Brahma Dutt**.
- **Subhash Chandra Bose & The Indian National Army (INA / Azad Hind Fauj)**:
  - Netaji escaped house arrest in Calcutta in January 1941; traveled through Afghanistan and Russia to Germany.
  - INA was originally conceived in Malaya by **Captain Mohan Singh** (1942) with Indian PoWs.
  - In 1943, Netaji arrived in Singapore via submarine; took supreme command of the INA from Rash Behari Bose.
  - Formed the **Provisional Government of Free India (*Arzi Hukumat-e-Azad Hind*)** in Singapore on 21 October 1943.
  - Established the all-female **Rani of Jhansi Regiment** commanded by **Captain Lakshmi Sahgal**.
  - Slogans: **"Dilli Chalo"**, **"Jai Hind"**, and *"Give me blood, and I will give you freedom!"*
  - Re-named the Andaman and Nicobar Islands as **Shaheed Dweep** and **Swaraj Dweep**.
- **The Red Fort Trials (INA Trials) (November 1945)**:
  - British court-martial of three INA joint officers at the Red Fort: **Prem Kumar Sahgal (Hindu), Gurbaksh Singh Dhillon (Sikh), and Shahnawaz Khan (Muslim)**.
  - Defended by a distinguished joint legal defense team: **Bhulabhai Desai, Sir Tej Bahadur Sapru, Jawaharlal Nehru, and Asaf Ali**. Sentences were remitted due to nationwide public demonstrations.
- **Royal Indian Navy (RIN) Mutiny (18 February 1946)**:
  - Ratings on the signal ship **HMIS Talwar** in Bombay went on strike over racial discrimination, substandard food, and the arrest of B. C. Dutt (who wrote "Quit India" on the ship's side). Spread across 78 naval ships; ratings surrendered after appeals from Sardar Patel and Jinnah.
- **Cabinet Mission (March 1946)**:
  - Sent by British PM Clement Attlee; members: **Lord Pethick-Lawrence** (Secretary of State), **Sir Stafford Cripps**, and **A. V. Alexander**.
  - Rejected the demand for a sovereign Pakistan; recommended an undivided Indian Union with a three-tier provincial grouping and an elected **Constituent Assembly** to draft the constitution.
- **Direct Action Day (16 August 1946)**:
  - Called by Muslim League leader **M. A. Jinnah** to enforce the demand for Pakistan; triggered catastrophic communal riots ("The Great Calcutta Killings").
- **Interim Government (2 September 1946)**: Formed under the leadership of **Jawaharlal Nehru** as Vice-President of the Viceroy's Executive Council.
- **Constituent Assembly**: Held its historic first meeting on **9 December 1946**; temporary President: Dr. Sachchidananda Sinha; permanent President elected on 11 December: **Dr. Rajendra Prasad**.
- **Attlee's Declaration (20 February 1947)**: British PM Attlee announced in the House of Commons that the British would transfer power and quit India by **June 1948**; replaced Lord Wavell with **Lord Mountbatten**.
- **Mountbatten Plan (3 June 1947 Plan)**:
  - Formulated the partition of British India into two independent Dominions: **India and Pakistan**, with princely states given the option to join either dominion or remain independent.
  - Accepted by both the Congress and the Muslim League.
- **Indian Independence Act 1947**:
  - Passed by British Parliament on **18 July 1947**.
  - Ended British rule; created two sovereign dominions on **15 August 1947**; **Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel** (assisted by V. P. Menon) successfully integrated 562 princely states into the Indian Union.

# 5. SSC CHSL Previous Years Question Bank

### Q1. Which Indus Valley Civilization site has provided evidence of a unique three-part division of the city?
- **Answer**: **Dholavira** (Gujarat) ✅
- *Explanation*: Unlike most Harappan settlements divided into two parts (Citadel and Lower Town), Dholavira is uniquely divided into three fortified sections: Citadel, Middle Town, and Lower Town.

### Q2. Who among the following Mughal Emperors shifted the emphasis from illustrated manuscripts to albums and portrait painting?
- **Answer**: **Jahangir** ✅
- *Explanation*: Jahangir was a great connoisseur of art who encouraged individualized portraiture, natural history studies (painted by Ustad Mansur), and individual album pages (*Muraqqa*).

### Q3. The first battle of Panipat (1526) was fought between Babur and which Sultan of Delhi?
- **Answer**: **Ibrahim Lodi** ✅
- *Explanation*: Fought on 21 April 1526. Babur used Tulghuma tactics and artillery to decisively defeat Ibrahim Lodi, establishing the Mughal Empire.

### Q4. Which Mauryan ruler embraced Jainism and died through the ritual of Sallekhana at Shravanabelagola?
- **Answer**: **Chandragupta Maurya** ✅
- *Explanation*: Towards the end of his reign, Chandragupta abdicated the throne, accompanied Jain monk Bhadrabahu to Karnataka, and starved to death according to the Jain vow of Sallekhana.

### Q5. Who was the court poet of King Harshavardhana and authored the 'Harshacharita'?
- **Answer**: **Banabhatta** ✅
- *Explanation*: Banabhatta was the Asthana Kavi of Harsha; he authored *Harshacharita* (biography of Harsha) and *Kadambari*.

### Q6. The world-famous Shore Temple at Mahabalipuram was constructed during the reign of which Pallava ruler?
- **Answer**: **Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha)** ✅
- *Explanation*: Built during the early 8th century CE in dressed granite stone, inaugurating the Dravidian structural temple style.

### Q7. The Dahsala System of land revenue assessment during the Mughal period was developed by whom?
- **Answer**: **Raja Todar Mal** ✅
- *Explanation*: Introduced in 1580 under Akbar; calculated 10-year averages of crop yield and prices; state revenue share was fixed at 1/3rd.

### Q8. Who founded the Atmiya Sabha in 1814 and the Brahmo Samaj in 1828 in Calcutta?
- **Answer**: **Raja Ram Mohan Roy** ✅
- *Explanation*: Known as the "Father of Modern Indian Renaissance", he led the campaign resulting in the legal abolition of Sati in 1829.

### Q9. In which session of the Indian National Congress was the resolution for "Poorna Swaraj" (Complete Independence) passed?
- **Answer**: **Lahore Session (1929)** ✅
- *Explanation*: Presided over by Jawaharlal Nehru; 26 January 1930 was proclaimed Independence Day; the Tricolor was unfurled on the banks of River Ravi.

### Q10. Which foreign traveler visited India during the reign of Chandragupta II (Vikramaditya)?
- **Answer**: **Fa-Hien (Faxian)** ✅
- *Explanation*: Chinese Buddhist monk Fa-Hien visited India between 399 and 412 CE; documented peaceful society and Buddhist holy places.

### Q11. Who among the following laid the foundation of the city of Agra in 1504?
- **Answer**: **Sikandar Lodi** ✅
- *Explanation*: Sultan Sikandar Lodi founded Agra in 1504 and shifted the Sultanate capital there from Delhi in 1506.

### Q12. The historic 'Poona Pact' of September 1932 was signed between Mahatma Gandhi and which Indian leader?
- **Answer**: **Dr. B. R. Ambedkar** ✅
- *Explanation*: Signed at Yerwada Jail; replaced separate communal electorates for depressed classes with reserved seats in joint electorates.

### Q13. The famous iron pillar situated in the Qutub Minar complex in Delhi belongs to which ruler?
- **Answer**: **Chandragupta II (Gupta Dynasty)** ✅
- *Explanation*: Inscribed in Sanskrit in Brahmi script, it praises King "Chandra" (identified as Chandragupta II Vikramaditya) and has remained rust-free for over 1,600 years.

### Q14. Which Mughal monument was built by Nur Jahan in memory of her father Mirza Ghiyas Beg?
- **Answer**: **Tomb of Itmad-ud-Daulah (Agra)** ✅
- *Explanation*: Constructed between 1622 and 1628; first Mughal building constructed entirely of white marble with extensive Pietra Dura stone inlay.

### Q15. In which year was the Vernacular Press Act passed, and by which Viceroy?
- **Answer**: **1878 by Lord Lytton** ✅
- *Explanation*: Passed to muzzle Indian language newspapers criticizing British famine policies; later repealed by Lord Ripon in 1882.
