## Ancient India

### Indus Valley Civilization (3300–1300 BCE)
```
Also called: Harappan Civilization
Major Sites:
├── Harappa (Punjab, Pakistan) — First discovered; granary
├── Mohenjo-daro (Sindh, Pakistan) — Great Bath; Great Granary; Bronze Dancing Girl
├── Lothal (Gujarat, India) — Dockyard; rice cultivation; fire altars
├── Kalibangan (Rajasthan) — Fire altars; ploughed field
├── Dholavira (Gujarat) — Water reservoir; signboard with letters
├── Rakhigarhi (Haryana) — Largest Indian site
├── Chanhudaro (Pakistan) — Bead-making factory; no citadel
├── Banawali (Haryana) — Barley cultivation; oval-shaped settlement
└── Surkotada (Gujarat) — Horse bones found

Key Features:
- Town planning: Grid pattern, drainage system
- No temples found (secular society)
- Script: Not yet deciphered (Boustrophedon style)
- Agriculture: Wheat, barley, cotton, rice
- Animals: Humped bull (most common seal)
- Trade with Mesopotamia
- Decline: Possibly floods, climate change, Aryan invasion theories
```

### Vedic Period (1500–500 BCE)

#### Early Vedic / Rig Vedic Period (1500–1000 BCE)
```
Text: Rig Veda (oldest Veda; 1028 hymns; 10 Mandalas)
River: Saraswati (called Naditama = best of rivers)
Region: Sapta Sindhu (Land of Seven Rivers)
Society: Pastoral, semi-nomadic
Political unit: Jana (tribe)
Important rivers: Sindhu (Indus), Saraswati, Ganga
God: Indra (most mentioned; 250 hymns)
       Agni (fire god; 200 hymns)
       Varuna (water/sky god)
Women: Respected; could attend assemblies (Sabha, Samiti)
No caste system by birth (based on occupation)
```

#### Later Vedic Period (1000–500 BCE)
```
Texts: Sama Veda, Yajur Veda, Atharva Veda + Brahmanas, Aranyakas, Upanishads
Region: Moved to Ganga-Yamuna doab
Society: Agricultural; settled
Caste system became rigid (by birth)
King's power increased
Women's status declined
Iron was known (Krishna Ayas)
Important kingdoms: Kuru, Panchala, Kosala, Videha
```

### Buddhism and Jainism

#### Buddhism (Founded ~563 BCE)
```
Founder: Siddhartha Gautama (563–483 BCE)
Born: Lumbini (Nepal); Clan: Shakya; Father: Suddhodana
Enlightenment: Bodh Gaya (under Peepal/Bodhi tree)
First Sermon: Sarnath (Dharma Chakra Pravartana)
Death (Mahaparinirvana): Kushinagar

Four Noble Truths:
1. Life is suffering (Dukkha)
2. Cause of suffering is desire (Samudaya)
3. Suffering can end (Nirodha)
4. Path to end suffering (Magga) — Eightfold Path

Eightfold Path: Right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, concentration

Buddhist Councils:
1st: Rajagriha (483 BCE) — King Ajatashatru; Mahakassapa
2nd: Vaishali (383 BCE) — King Kalashoka
3rd: Pataliputra (250 BCE) — Ashoka; Moggaliputta Tissa
4th: Kashmir (72 CE) — Kanishka; Vasumitra → Mahayana split

Two sects: Hinayana (Theravada) & Mahayana
Tripitaka: Vinaya (rules), Sutta (teachings), Abhidhamma (philosophy)
```

#### Jainism
```
Founded by: Vardhamana Mahavira (24th Tirthankara)
Born: Kundagrama (Bihar); Died: Pavapuri (Bihar)
First Tirthankara: Rishabhadeva
23rd Tirthankara: Parshvanatha

Five Vows (Pancha Mahavratas):
1. Ahimsa (non-violence)
2. Satya (truth)
3. Asteya (non-stealing)
4. Aparigraha (non-possessiveness)
5. Brahmacharya (celibacy) — added by Mahavira

Three Jewels (Triratna): Right faith, Right knowledge, Right conduct
Two sects: Digambara (sky-clad) & Shvetambara (white-clad)

Jain Councils:
1st: Pataliputra (300 BCE) — Sthulabhadra
2nd: Vallabhi (512 CE) — Devardhigani
```

### Maurya Empire (321–185 BCE)
```
Founder: Chandragupta Maurya (with Chanakya/Kautilya)
Capital: Pataliputra
Chanakya wrote: Arthashastra (treatise on statecraft)

Chandragupta Maurya (321–297 BCE):
- Defeated Nanda dynasty
- Defeated Seleucus Nicator → Treaty; Megasthenes sent as ambassador
- Megasthenes wrote "Indica"
- Became Jain; died at Shravanabelagola

Bindusara (297–273 BCE):
- "Amitraghata" (slayer of enemies)
- Greek ambassador: Deimachus

Ashoka (273–232 BCE):
- Greatest Mauryan emperor
- Kalinga War (261 BCE) → turned to Buddhism
- Edicts inscribed on rocks and pillars
- Pillar at Sarnath → National emblem of India
- Spread Buddhism through missionaries
- Dhamma: Moral law for all
- Sent Mahendra and Sanghamitra to Sri Lanka

Decline: Last ruler Brihadratha killed by Pushyamitra Sunga
```

### Gupta Empire (320–550 CE) — "Golden Age of India"
```
Founder: Sri Gupta
Greatest: Chandragupta I, Samudragupta, Chandragupta II

Chandragupta I (320–335 CE): Married Lichhavi princess Kumaradevi
Samudragupta (335–375 CE): "Napoleon of India" (by V.A. Smith)
  - Allahabad Pillar inscription (by Harisena)
Chandragupta II / Vikramaditya (375–415 CE):
  - Defeated Shakas
  - Nine gems (Navaratnas) in court: Kalidasa, Varahamihira, etc.
  - Chinese pilgrim Fa-Hien visited

Achievements:
- Aryabhata: Calculated π, earth's rotation
- Kalidasa: Shakuntala, Meghaduta, Raghuvamsa
- Varahamihira: Brihat Samhita
- Nalanda University founded
- Iron Pillar of Delhi (rust-free!)
- Decimal system and zero concept developed
- Temple architecture: Dashavatara Temple (Deogarh)
```

## Medieval India

### Delhi Sultanate (1206–1526)
```
Five Dynasties:

1. Slave/Mamluk Dynasty (1206–1290):
   - Qutbuddin Aibak: Started Qutub Minar; Quwwat-ul-Islam mosque
   - Iltutmish: Completed Qutub Minar; introduced Iqta system, silver tanka
   - Razia Sultana: First female ruler of Delhi
   - Balban: Theory of Kingship; "Blood and Iron" policy

2. Khalji Dynasty (1290–1320):
   - Alauddin Khalji: Market reforms; conquered Deccan
   - Malik Kafur: Alauddin's general

3. Tughlaq Dynasty (1320–1414):
   - Muhammad bin Tughlaq: Shifted capital to Daulatabad; token currency
   - Firoz Shah Tughlaq: Irrigation canals; Firoz Shah Kotla
   - Ibn Battuta visited during Muhammad's reign

4. Sayyid Dynasty (1414–1451):
   - Claimed descent from Prophet Muhammad

5. Lodi Dynasty (1451–1526):
   - Ibrahim Lodi: Last Sultan; defeated by Babur at Panipat (1526)
```

### Mughal Empire (1526–1857)
```
Babur (1526–1530):
- First Battle of Panipat (1526) — defeated Ibrahim Lodi
- Battle of Khanwa (1527) — defeated Rana Sanga
- Wrote Baburnama (autobiography in Turkish)

Humayun (1530–1556):
- Lost to Sher Shah Suri; regained throne with Persian help
- Died falling from library stairs

Akbar (1556–1605):
- Greatest Mughal emperor
- Second Battle of Panipat (1556) — defeated Hemu
- Din-i-Ilahi (new religion)
- Mansabdari system
- Nine gems: Tansen, Birbal, Todar Mal, Abul Fazl, etc.
- Abul Fazl wrote Ain-i-Akbari & Akbarnama
- Built Fatehpur Sikri, Buland Darwaza
- Abolished Jaziya tax

Jahangir (1605–1627):
- "Chain of Justice" (Zanjir-i-Adl)
- Nur Jahan (wife) wielded great power
- Captain Hawkins and Sir Thomas Roe visited

Shah Jahan (1627–1658):
- "Prince of Builders"
- Built Taj Mahal, Red Fort, Jama Masjid
- Peacock Throne (Takht-i-Taus)
- Imprisoned by Aurangzeb

Aurangzeb (1658–1707):
- Last great Mughal
- Reimposed Jaziya
- Destroyed many temples
- Fought Marathas (Shivaji)
- Empire declined after his death
```
