## 1. Classical Dances of India & Renowned Exponents

### Foundational Treatises & Core Theory

* **Bharata Muni’s *Natya Shastra* (c. 200 BCE – 200 CE):**
  * Revered as the **Panchama Veda (Fifth Veda)**, created by Brahma by drawing elements from the four Vedas: **Pathya** (words) from *Rigveda*, **Abhinaya** (gestures/expression) from *Yajurveda*, **Geeta** (music/chant) from *Samaveda*, and **Rasa** (aesthetic sentiment) from *Atharvaveda*.
  * **Two Basic Aspects of Dance:**
    * **Tandava:** Masculine, vigorous, heroic, cosmic rhythm and movement (associated with **Lord Shiva**).
    * **Lasya:** Feminine, graceful, lyrical, soft expression of emotion (associated with **Goddess Parvati**).
  * **Three Elements of Classical Dance:**
    * **Nritta:** Pure, abstract, rhythmic dance movements devoid of dramatic expression or storytelling.
    * **Nritya:** Expressive dance combining rhythm with **Abhinaya** (facial expressions and hand gestures/**mudras**) to convey meaning and emotion.
    * **Natya:** Dramatic element combining dance, dialogue, and mime to enact a story or character.
  * **Navarasa (Nine Rasas / Aesthetic Sentiments):** Bharata Muni originally codified **8 Rasas** in *Natya Shastra*; the 9th Rasa (**Shanta Rasa**) was later established by **Abhinavagupta** (in *Abhinavabharati*):
    1. **Shringara** (Love / Beauty)
    2. **Hasya** (Laughter / Mirth)
    3. **Karuna** (Compassion / Sorrow)
    4. **Raudra** (Fury / Anger)
    5. **Veera** (Heroism / Valour)
    6. **Bhayanaka** (Terror / Fear)
    7. **Bibhatsa** (Disgust / Aversion)
    8. **Adbhuta** (Wonder / Amazement)
    9. **Shanta** (Peace / Tranquility)

* **Nandikeshvara’s *Abhinaya Darpana* (c. 2nd–5th century CE, "Mirror of Gesture"):**
  * Primary manual for solo classical dance technique (**Angika Abhinaya** — body movements, **Hasta Mudras**, head, eye, and neck movements).
  * Famous opening dictum: *"Yato hasta tato drishti, yato drishti tato manah, yato manah tato bhava, yato bhava tato rasa"* (Where the hand goes, the eyes follow; where the eyes go, the mind follows; where the mind goes, *bhava* arises; where *bhava* arises, *rasa* is born).
  * **Four Types of Abhinaya (Expression):**
    1. **Angika:** Physical movements of limbs (*Anga*, *Pratyanga*, *Upanga*) and *Mudras*.
    2. **Vachika:** Speech, song, poetry, and musical accompaniment.
    3. **Aharya:** Costume, jewellery, ornaments, stage decor, and make-up.
    4. **Sattvika:** Involuntary, genuine emotional states (tears, trembling, horripilation, change of colour).

### Recognized Classical Dance Forms of India

* **Sangeet Natak Akademi (SNA)** officially recognizes **8 Classical Dances**: **Bharatanatyam, Kathak, Kathakali, Kuchipudi, Odissi, Manipuri, Mohiniyattam, and Sattriya**.
* **Ministry of Culture (Government of India)** recognizes a **9th Classical Dance**: **Chhau** (Seraikella, Mayurbhanj, Purulia).

## 3. Traditional Folk Theatre, Puppetry & Martial Arts

### Traditional Folk Theatre of India (20+ Forms)

| Theatre Form | State / Region | Core Theme, Language & Key Exam Features |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Bhand Pather** | **Jammu & Kashmir** | Satirical, humorous folk theatre by the **Bhand** community; mimics social evils/officials. Music: **Surnai**, **Nagara**, **Dhol**. |
| **Swang (Saang)** | **Haryana, UP, Rajasthan** | Dialogue-driven musical folk theatre mixing prose & verse (*Ragini*). Two styles: **Rohtak** (Haryanvi) & **Hathras** (Brajbhasha). Key pioneer: **Kishan Lal Bhaat**, **Pt. Lakhmi Chand** ("Surya Kavi" of Haryana). |
| **Nautanki** | **Uttar Pradesh** (Kanpur, Hathras, Lucknow) | Operatic folk drama based on romantic/heroic tales (*Laila-Majnu*, *Sultana Daku*, *Harishchandra*). Principal percussion: **Nagara**; **Gulab Bai** (Padma Shri 1990) was first female artist. |
| **Raslila** | **Uttar Pradesh** (Mathura-Vrindavan) & **Manipur** | Sacred enactments of **Lord Krishna's** childhood & *raas* with Radha/Gopis; popularized by **Vallabhacharya** & **Nand Das** (16th c.). |

## 4. Indian Classical Music: Hindustani & Carnatic Traditions

### Origins & Foundational Texts
* ***Sama Veda*:** Earliest source of Indian music; hymns (*Samagana*) were chanted in musical notes (*Swaras*).
* ***Gandharva Veda*:** The **Upaveda** of the *Sama Veda*, considered the science of music and dance.
* ***Natya Shastra* (Bharata Muni, c. 200 BCE–200 CE):** Earliest surviving treatise covering dramaturgy, **22 Shrutis** (microtones), *Swaras*, *Rasa* theory, and the **4-fold classification of musical instruments**.
* ***Brihaddeshi* (Matanga Muni, c. 6th–8th century CE):** First treatise to explicitly define and use the term ***Raga***.
* ***Sangita Ratnakara* (Sarangadeva, 13th century CE):** Written in the Yadava court of Devagiri; known as ***Saptadhyayi*** (7 chapters); serves as the **bridge text** uniting both Hindustani and Carnatic music traditions.
* **Amir Khusrau (1253–1325 CE):** Celebrated as ***"Tuti-e-Hind"* (Parrot of India)**; court poet under Delhi Sultans (notably Alauddin Khalji); credited with the evolution of **Sitar** (from *Tritantri Veena*) and **Tabla** (splitting *Pakhawaj*), and pioneering/developing ***Khayal***, ***Qawwali***, ***Tarana***, and Persian-Indian *Ragas* (e.g., *Yaman*, *Kafi*, *Bahar*).

### Hindustani vs. Carnatic Classical Music

| Parameter | Hindustani Classical Music | Carnatic Classical Music |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Region & Origin** | **North India**; diverged post-13th century CE from common Vedic/Shastric roots | **South India** (Tamil Nadu, Karnataka, Andhra Pradesh, Telangana, Kerala); remained closer to ancient tradition |
| **External Influences** | Strong **Persian, Mughal & Central Asian** influences | **Indigenous**; largely free from Persian/Islamic influence |
| **Raga Classification** | **10 Thaats** (systematized by **Pandit V.N. Bhatkhande**); hundreds of Ragas | **72 Melakarta Ragas** (systematized by **Venkatamakhin** in *Chaturdandi Prakasika*) |
| **Time Theory (*Samaya*)** | Strictly bound to **time of day & season** (e.g., *Bhairav* at dawn, *Yaman* at dusk, *Malhar* in monsoon) | **No strict time-of-day** or seasonal restriction for Ragas |
| **Improvisation vs. Composition** | Greater scope for **improvisation** (~60–70% improvised); slower tempo progression | **Composition-centric** (Kriti-based); strict adherence to *Sahitya* (lyrics) and *Tala* |
| **Vocal vs. Instrumental** | Equal prominence to **both vocal and instrumental** traditions | Primarily **vocal-centric**; instruments imitate vocal nuances (*Gayaki Ang*) |
| **Tala System** | Flexible cyclic *Talas* (e.g., *Teental* 16, *Ektaal* 12, *Dadra* 6, *Jhaptal* 10) | Rigid **Suladi Sapta Tala** system (7 basic Talas × 5 Jatis = 35 Talas; *Adi Tala* 8 beats most common) |
| **Sub-styles / Gharanas** | **Gharana system** (Gwalior, Agra, Kirana, Patiala, etc.) | **No Gharana system**; uniform structural framework (*Bani* / *Sampradaya* lineages) |
| **Key Instruments** | **Sitar, Sarod, Tabla, Bansuri, Shehnai, Santoor, Sarangi, Pakhawaj** | **Veena (Saraswati), Mridangam, Violin, Ghatam, Nadaswaram, Kanjira, Morsing** |
| **Prominent Composers / Pioneers** | **Tansen, Baiju Bawra, Sadarang-Adarang, Amir Khusrau, V.N. Bhatkhande, V.D. Paluskar** | **Purandara Dasa**, **Trinity** (**Tyagaraja, Muthuswami Dikshitar, Syama Sastri**), **Annamacharya, Swathi Thirunal** |

### Carnatic Music: Components & Legendary Composers

#### Key Structural Components
* **Alapana:** Unmetered (no *Tala*) melodic improvisation exploring the Raga's mood and phrases (equivalent to Hindustani *Alap*).
* **Tanam:** Rhythmic improvisation without a formal *Tala* cycle, using syllables like *Ananta* or *Nom-Tom* (performed before *Pallavi* in *Ragam-Tanam-Pallavi*).
* **Varnam:** Opening warm-up/scholarly composition combining *Swara* and *Sahitya*; establishes the Raga's grammar (types: *Tana Varnam*, *Pada Varnam*).
* **Kriti / Kirtanam:** Backbone of Carnatic concerts with **3 mandatory sections**:
  1. **Pallavi:** First thematic refrain line (repeated after subsequent sections).
  2. **Anupallavi:** Second section exploring higher octaves (*Tara Sthayi*).
  3. **Charanam:** Concluding stanza(s) elaborating the theme and returning to *Pallavi*.
* **Tillana:** Brisk, rhythmic, concluding piece corresponding to Hindustani **Tarana**; uses rhythmic syllables (*nadirdim, tom*); heavily used in **Bharatanatyam** & **Kuchipudi**.

#### Landmark Composers
* **Purandara Dasa (1484–1564):** Revered as the ***"Pitamaha (Father/Grandfather) of Carnatic Music"***; Haridasa saint-composer (Vijayanagara Empire); framed the systematic teaching pedagogy (*Sarali Varisai*, *Janta Varisai*, *Alankaras*, *Geethams*) in **Raga Mayamalavagowla**; composed in **Kannada & Sanskrit**.
* **Trinity of Carnatic Music (18th–19th Century — all born in Tiruvarur, Tamil Nadu):**
  1. **Tyagaraja (1767–1847):** Composed primarily in **Telugu** (and Sanskrit) in devotion to **Lord Rama**; famous for ***Pancharatna Kritis*** (*Jagadanandakaraka*, *Endaro Mahanubhavulu*).
  2. **Muthuswami Dikshitar (1775–1835):** Composed exclusively in **Sanskrit** under the mudra ***Guruguha***; famous for ***Navagraha Kritis*** & *Kamalamba Navavarna Kritis*.
  3. **Syama Sastri (1762–1827):** Oldest of the Trinity; composed in **Telugu & Sanskrit** in praise of **Goddess Kamakshi** (mudra: *Syama Krishna*); master of complex *Tala* structures (*Swarajatis*).
* **Tallapaka Annamacharya (1408–1503):** Known as ***Pada Kavita Pitamaha***; earliest known Carnatic *Sankirtana* composer in **Telugu**; composed ~32,000 *Sankirtanas* on **Lord Venkateswara** of Tirumala.
* **Maharaja Swathi Thirunal (1813–1846):** Ruler of **Travancore** (Kerala); composed over 400 works across **Carnatic and Hindustani** traditions in Sanskrit, Malayalam, Hindi, Telugu, and Kannada.

## 7. 11 Classical Languages of India & National Cultural Institutions

### All 11 Classical Languages of India (Up to October 2024)

On **October 3, 2024**, the Union Cabinet approved Classical Language status for **5 new languages** (**Marathi, Pali, Prakrit, Assamese, and Bengali**), increasing the total from 6 to **11 Classical Languages**.

| # | Language | Year of Recognition | Language Family | Key Exam Facts |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |

| **2** | **Sanskrit** | **2005** | Indo-Aryan | Paninian *Ashtadhyayi*, Vedas, Epics, Classical Kavya & *Natya Shastra*. |

#### 4 Official Criteria for Classical Language Status (Linguistic Experts Committee, Ministry of Culture / Sahitya Akademi)
1. **High Antiquity:** Early texts/recorded history spanning **1,500–2,000 years**.
2. **Ancient Literature:** A body of ancient literature/texts considered a valuable heritage by generations of speakers.
3. **Original Literary Tradition:** The literary tradition must be **original** and not borrowed from another speech community.
4. **Distinctness:** Classical language and literature are **distinct from modern** forms (or there may be a discontinuity between the classical language and its later/modern forms).

*Benefits:* Creation of Centres of Excellence, UGC Professional Chairs in Central Universities, and major international awards for scholars.

## 8. Ancient Architecture: Harappan, Mauryan Pillars, Stupas & Sculpture Schools

### A. Harappan (Indus Valley) Architecture & Art (c. 2600–1900 BCE)
* **Urban Planning:** Orthogonal **grid pattern** (roads intersecting at 90°); city typically divided into a raised western **Citadel** (public/religious buildings) and a lower eastern **Lower Town** (residential).
* **Construction:** Standardized **burnt bricks** in **1 : 2 : 4** ratio (thickness : width : length); **covered underground drainage** with inspection manholes/cesspits; houses opened into side lanes rather than main streets.
* **Key Architectural Sites & Structures:**
  * **Mohenjo-daro (Indus River, Sindh):** **Great Bath** (waterproofed with bitumen/gypsum mortar), **Great Granary**, **Assembly Hall**, **Steatite Bearded Priest**, **Bronze Dancing Girl** (*lost-wax / cire perdue* technique), **Pashupati Seal** (proto-Shiva surrounded by elephant, tiger, rhino, buffalo, and two deer below).
  * **Harappa (Ravi River, Punjab):** Six granaries in two rows, **Red Sandstone Male Torso**, **R-37 cemetery**, **Mother Goddess** terracotta figurines.
  * **Lothal (Bhogava River, Gujarat):** World's earliest **tidal brick dockyard**, bead-making factory, **fire altars**, double burial, rice husk remains.
  * **Dholavira (Khadir Bet, Kutch, Gujarat):** Unique **three-tier division** (Citadel, Middle Town, Lower Town), sophisticated **stone water reservoirs**, **10-sign Indus signboard**; **40th UNESCO World Heritage Site (2021)**.
  * **Kalibangan (Ghaggar River, Rajasthan):** **Ploughed field** (furrow marks), **fire altars**, mud-brick houses, cylindrical seals.
  * **Rakhigarhi (Hisar, Haryana):** Largest Indus Valley site in India; **Banawali** (Haryana — terracotta plough model, radial streets).

## 10. Indian Temple Architecture (Nagara, Dravida, Vesara) & 45+ Famous Temples

### A. Structural Comparison: Nagara vs. Dravida vs. Vesara

| Feature | Nagara Style (North India) | Dravida Style (South India) | Vesara Style (Deccan / Karnataka) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Geography** | Himalayas to **Vindhyas** | **Krishna River** to Kanyakumari | Between **Vindhyas & Krishna** (Chalukya, Rashtrakuta, Hoysala) |
| **Ground Plan** | Square/cruciform; **Panchayatana** (1 main + 4 corner shrines) | Square/rectangular inside walled compound | **Stellate (star-shaped)** plan in Hoysala temples; apsidal/hybrid in Chalukya |
| **Platform (*Jagati*)** | High raised stone terrace (**Jagati**) common | **No Jagati**; built at ground level | Prominent **Jagati** matching star-shaped outline |
| **Tower** | **Curvilinear Shikhara** (*Rekha-prasad / Latina*, *Phamsana*, *Valabhi*) | **Stepped pyramidal Vimana** with horizontal tiers (*talas*) | Hybrid: Lower *Vimana* profile with Nagara-like vertical banding |
| **Crown** | Ribbed disc (**Amalaka**) topped by pot (**Kalasha**) | Octagonal/domical **Stupika (Shikhara)** + *Kalasha* | Domical/flattened *Kalasha* or circular *Amalaka*-like crown |
| **Boundary Wall & Gateway** | **Absent** (except Odisha & Khajuraho exceptions) | **High enclosure wall** with towering **Gopuram** gateways | Low/ornamental walls; moderate gateways |
| **Entrance Guardians** | River goddesses **Ganga & Yamuna** | Fierce **Dwarpalas** (gatekeepers) | Mix of *Dwarpalas* & scrollwork |
| **Water Tank** | Usually **absent** (except Solanki *Surya-Kund*) | **Mandatory Kalyani / Pushkarini** inside compound | Often near natural rivers/tanks |

**Three Nagara Shikhara Sub-Types:**
1. **Latina / Rekha-prasada:** Square base, inward-curving walls converging to a point (e.g., *Lingaraja*, *Kandariya Mahadeva*).
2. **Phamsana:** Shorter, broader base with straight-sloping pyramidal roof of horizontal slabs (e.g., *Jagmohan* of Konark).
3. **Valabhi:** Rectangular base with barrel-vaulted (wagon-vault) roof (e.g., *Teli ka Mandir* at Gwalior; *Vaital Deula* at Bhubaneswar).

### B. Regional Sub-Schools of Nagara Architecture

1. **Odisha (Kalinga) School:**
   * Sanctum = **Deul** (*Rekha-deula* curvilinear tower; *Pidha-deula* pyramidal roof; *Khakhara-deula* barrel-vault Shakti shrine like **Vaital Deula**).
   * Mandapa = **Jagamohana**, often accompanied by **Natamandira** (dance hall) & **Bhogamandapa** (offering hall).
   * Exterior walls lavishly sculpted, interior **plain**; **no interior pillars** (iron girders support roofs); **boundary wall present**; examples: **Lingaraja, Konark, Jagannath, Mukteshwar** ("Gem of Odisha architecture").
2. **Khajuraho (Chandela) School (MP):**
   * Both interior & exterior richly carved; high **Jagati** without boundary wall; **Panchayatana** layout; clustered **Uru-shringas** (miniature spires rising up the main Shikhara); sandstone; includes ~10% erotic sculptures; examples: **Kandariya Mahadeva, Lakshmana, Vishwanatha**. (**UNESCO WHS, 1986**).
3. **Solanki / Maru-Gurjara School (Gujarat & Rajasthan):**
   * Soft sandstone or white **Makrana marble**; stepped water tank (**Surya-Kund**) with miniature shrines (**Modhera**); ornamental **Torana** archway; intricately carved **Sabhamandapa** ceilings; examples: **Modhera Sun Temple, Dilwara Jain Temples (Mt. Abu), Rani ki Vav (Patan)**.

## 11. Indo-Islamic, Mughal & Modern/Colonial Architecture

### A. Core Architectural Features (Indo-Islamic Synthesis)
* **Arcuate vs. Trabeate:** Indigenous **Trabeate** used pillars, horizontal beams/lintels, and corbelled domes; Indo-Islamic **Arcuate** introduced the **true arch** (voussoirs + keystone), **true dome** (on squinches/pendentives), **minarets**, and **lime mortar**.
* **Arabesque:** Endless geometrical and stylized floral/vegetal motifs (human/animal figures avoided in religious structures).
* **Calligraphy:** Quranic verses in *Kufic*, *Naskh*, *Thuluth*, and *Nastaliq* scripts used decoratively; **foreshortening** (making upper letters larger so text looks uniform from the ground, e.g., Taj Mahal).
* **Pietra Dura (*Parchinkari*):** Inlay of semi-precious stones (lapis lazuli, carnelian, jasper, agate) into white marble.
* **Charbagh:** Four-quadrant Persian paradise garden divided by water channels (introduced by **Babur** at *Aram Bagh*, Agra; perfected in Humayun's Tomb & Taj Mahal).
* **Jali:** Pierced stone lattice screens allowing ventilation, diffused light, and privacy (e.g., *Salim Chishti Tomb*, *Sidi Saiyyed Mosque*).
* **Batter:** Thick inward-sloping walls for strength and fortress appearance — signature of **Tughlaq architecture**.
* **Double Dome:** Hollow gap between inner ceiling and outer shell giving towering exterior height without distorting interior proportion — pioneered in **Lodi period** and perfected by **Mughals**.
* **Rajput Adoptions:** **Chhatris** (domed kiosks), **Jharokhas** (overhanging balconies), and **Chhajjas** (sloping eaves).

### The 4 Kumbh Mela Sites, Rivers & Astronomical Trigger

Kumbh Mela rotates across **4 sacred sites** in India every **12 years** based on the orbital positions of **Jupiter (*Brihaspati*)**, **Sun (*Surya*)**, and **Moon (*Chandra*)** (mythologically linked to drops of *Amrita* from *Samudra Manthan*).

| # | Site (State) | Sacred River | Local Name | Astronomical Trigger (Jupiter & Sun) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Prayagraj** (Uttar Pradesh) | **Triveni Sangam** (Confluence of **Ganga, Yamuna & mythical Saraswati**) | Kumbh / Maha Kumbh | **Jupiter** in **Taurus (*Vrishabha*)** (or Aries) AND **Sun & Moon** in **Capricorn (*Makara*)** during *Magha* month. |
| **2** | **Haridwar** (Uttarakhand) | **Ganga** | Kumbh Mela | **Jupiter** in **Aquarius (*Kumbha*)** AND **Sun** in **Aries (*Mesha*)**. |
| **3** | **Ujjain** (Madhya Pradesh) | **Shipra** (Kshipra) | **Simhastha** Kumbh | **Jupiter** in **Leo (*Simha*)** AND **Sun** in **Aries (*Mesha*)** (or *Vaishakha* full moon). |
| **4** | **Nashik–Trimbakeshwar** (Maharashtra) | **Godavari** | **Simhastha** Kumbh | Both **Jupiter** AND **Sun** simultaneously in **Leo (*Simha*)**. |

#### Kumbh Types & Maha Kumbh 2025 Note
- **Ardh Kumbh Mela:** Held every **6 years** (only at **Haridwar** and **Prayagraj**).
- **Purna Kumbh Mela:** Held every **12 years** at each of the 4 sites.
- **Maha Kumbh Mela (144-Year Cycle):** Held once every **12 Purna Kumbhs (144 years)** exclusively at **Prayagraj**.
- **Maha Kumbh 2025 (Prayagraj):** Held from **13 January 2025 (Paush Purnima) to 26 February 2025 (Maha Shivaratri)** — spanning **45 days** at Triveni Sangam.

## 14. Indian Calendar Systems

### 1. National Calendar of India (Saka Era — 78 CE)
- **Origin:** Founded by **Kushan Emperor Kanishka** in **78 CE** (to commemorate his accession).
- **Calendar Reform Committee (1952):** Headed by eminent astrophysicist **Dr. Meghnad Saha** under CSIR to unify 30+ regional calendars.
- **Official Adoption:** Adopted on **March 22, 1957** (*1 Chaitra 1879 Saka Era*).
- **Usage:** Used alongside the Gregorian calendar for the *Gazette of India*, All India Radio (AIR) broadcasts, and official Government of India communications.
- **Structure:** **365 days** (366 in a leap year), **12 months** starting with **Chaitra** and ending with **Phalguna**.
  - **1 Chaitra** = **March 22** in a normal year, and **March 21** in a Gregorian **leap year** (when Chaitra has **31 days** instead of 30).
  - **Formula:** $\text{Gregorian Year} = \text{Saka Year} + 78$.

## 17. Last 10 Years SSC CHSL / CGL / GD Art & Culture PYQs (46 Solved Questions)

### A. Gharanas & Hindustani/Carnatic Music

**Q1. Which of the following gharanas is considered the oldest among all Khayal gharanas of Hindustani classical music?**
- A) Agra Gharana
- B) Gwalior Gharana
- C) Kirana Gharana
- D) Patiala Gharana
- **Answer:** **B) Gwalior Gharana**
- **Explanation:** **Gwalior Gharana** (patronized under Raja Man Singh Tomar and later evolved by Nathan Pir Baksh & Haddu/Hassu Khan) is the oldest Khayal gharana; Agra, Kirana, and Patiala branched out later.

**Q2. Pandit Bhimsen Joshi, who received the Bharat Ratna in 2008, belonged to which Hindustani classical gharana?**
- A) Jaipur-Atrauli Gharana
- B) Rampur-Sahaswan Gharana
- C) Kirana Gharana
- D) Mewati Gharana
- **Answer:** **C) Kirana Gharana**
- **Explanation:** **Pandit Bhimsen Joshi** (and **Gangubai Hangal**, **Abdul Karim Khan**) belonged to the **Kirana Gharana** (named after Kairana in UP), known for *swara* purity and slow-tempo *aalap*.

**Q3. Ustad Bade Ghulam Ali Khan was a legendary vocalist of which gharana?**
- A) Patiala Gharana
- B) Banaras Gharana
- C) Gwalior Gharana
- D) Bhendibazaar Gharana
- **Answer:** **A) Patiala Gharana**
- **Explanation:** **Ustad Bade Ghulam Ali Khan** (also Begum Akhtar's contemporary in Thumri/Khayal) was the foremost exponent of the **Patiala Gharana** (founded by Ustad Fateh Ali Khan & Ali Baksh).

**Q4. Kishori Amonkar and Mallikarjun Mansur are celebrated vocalists associated with which gharana?**
- A) Kirana Gharana
- B) Agra Gharana
- C) Jaipur-Atrauli Gharana
- D) Senia Gharana
- **Answer:** **C) Jaipur-Atrauli Gharana**
- **Explanation:** Founded by **Ustad Alladiya Khan**, the **Jaipur-Atrauli Gharana** is famed for complex *ragas* and intricate *laya*; **Kishori Amonkar**, **Mallikarjun Mansur**, and **Kesarbai Kerkar** belong to it.

**Q5. Ustad Allauddin Khan founded which prominent instrumental gharana of Hindustani music?**
- A) Maihar Gharana
- B) Etawah (Imdadkhani) Gharana
- C) Lucknow Gharana
- D) Farrukhabad Gharana
- **Answer:** **A) Maihar Gharana**
- **Explanation:** **Baba Allauddin Khan** founded the **Maihar Gharana** (MP) and trained legends like **Pt. Ravi Shankar** (Sitar), **Ustad Ali Akbar Khan** (Sarod), **Annapurna Devi** (Surbahar), and **Pt. Pannalal Ghosh** (Flute).

### B. Padma Awardee Dancers & Musicians

**Q6. Guru Kelucharan Mohapatra, the first dancer from Odisha to receive the Padma Vibhushan (2000), is credited with reviving which classical dance?**
- A) Kathakali
- B) Odissi
- C) Kuchipudi
- D) Manipuri
- **Answer:** **B) Odissi**
- **Explanation:** **Kelucharan Mohapatra** won Padma Shri (1974), Padma Bhushan (1988), and **Padma Vibhushan (2000)** for reviving and codifying **Odissi** dance; he also played the *Mardala*.

**Q7. Who was the first classical dancer to be awarded the Padma Vibhushan (1977) and was praised globally for Bharatanatyam's *Abhinaya*?**
- A) Rukmini Devi Arundale
- B) T. Balasaraswati
- C) Sonal Mansingh
- D) Mrinalini Sarabhai
- **Answer:** **B) T. Balasaraswati**
- **Explanation:** **T. Balasaraswati** received Padma Bhushan (1957) and **Padma Vibhushan (1977)**—the first classical dancer to earn the Padma Vibhushan; **Sonal Mansingh** (2003) was the second woman dancer.

**Q8. Sonal Mansingh, awarded the Padma Vibhushan in 2003, is an exponent of Bharatanatyam and which other classical dance form?**
- A) Kathak
- B) Manipuri
- C) Odissi
- D) Mohiniyattam
- **Answer:** **C) Odissi**
- **Explanation:** **Sonal Mansingh** received Padma Bhushan (1992) and **Padma Vibhushan (2003)** for her mastery over both **Bharatanatyam** and **Odissi**.

**Q9. Pandit Birju Maharaj, who belonged to the Kalka-Bindadin (Lucknow) Gharana of Kathak, was conferred the Padma Vibhushan in which year?**
- A) 1976
- B) 1986
- C) 1996
- D) 2002
- **Answer:** **B) 1986**
- **Explanation:** **Pt. Birju Maharaj** (Brijmohan Mishra) won the **Padma Vibhushan in 1986** and also won the National Film Award for Best Choreography (*Vishwaroopam*, 2013).

**Q10. Which legendary Kathak dancer won the Padma Shri in 1973 and famously declined the Padma Bhushan, stating she deserved nothing less than the Bharat Ratna?**
- A) Sitara Devi
- B) Shovana Narayan
- C) Kumudini Lakhia
- D) Roshan Kumari
- **Answer:** **A) Sitara Devi**
- **Explanation:** **Sitara Devi**, known as *"Nritya Samragini"* (by Rabindranath Tagore), received **Padma Shri (1973)** and Sangeet Natak Akademi Award (1969), declining the Padma Bhushan in 2002.

**Q11. Teejan Bai, renowned exponent of the *Pandavani* folk singing style of Chhattisgarh, received the Padma Vibhushan in which year?**
- A) 2003
- B) 2015
- C) 2019
- D) 2021
- **Answer:** **C) 2019**
- **Explanation:** **Teejan Bai** (*Kapalik* style of Pandavani, narrating Bhima's Mahabharata tales with a *Tambura*) won Padma Shri (1988), Padma Bhushan (2003), and **Padma Vibhushan (2019)**.

**Q12. Which Bharatanatyam scholar and creator of *"Bharatanrityam"* received the Padma Vibhushan in 2024?**

- D) Leela Samson
- **Answer:** **B) Padma Subrahmanyam**
- **Explanation:** Both **Padma Subrahmanyam** (Padma Shri 1981, Padma Bhushan 2003) and **Vyjayanthimala Bali** received the **Padma Vibhushan in 2024**; Padma Subrahmanyam researched the 108 *Karanas* of *Natyashastra* and created *Bharatanrityam*.

**Q13. Yamini Krishnamurthy, honoured with Padma Shri (1968), Padma Bhushan (2001), and Padma Vibhushan (2016), is celebrated for Bharatanatyam and which other dance form?**

### C. Musical Instruments & Maestros

**Q16. Pandit Shivkumar Sharma, who passed away in 2022, is credited with elevating which Kashmiri folk instrument to the Indian classical concert stage?**
- A) Sarangi
- B) Santoor
- C) Rabab
- D) Sitar
- **Answer:** **B) Santoor**
- **Explanation:** **Pt. Shivkumar Sharma** (along with **Pt. Bhajan Sopori**, known as *"Saint of Santoor"*) brought the 100-string **Santoor** (*Shata Tantri Veena*) into Hindustani classical music.

**Q17. Which iconic Carnatic percussionist popularized the *Ghatam* (clay pot) globally and won a Grammy Award in 1991?**
- A) Palghat Mani Iyer
- B) T. H. Vinayakram
- C) Umayalpuram K. Sivaraman
- D) Karaikudi Mani
- **Answer:** **B) T. H. Vinayakram**
- **Explanation:** **Thetakudi Harihara (Vikku) Vinayakram** won the Grammy (for *Planet Drum* with Mickey Hart & Zakir Hussain) and Padma Bhushan (2014) for the **Ghatam**.

**Q18. Ustad Amjad Ali Khan and Ustad Ali Akbar Khan are legendary maestros of which stringed musical instrument?**
- A) Sarod
- B) Rudra Veena
- C) Sarangi
- D) Surbahar
- **Answer:** **A) Sarod**
- **Explanation:** **Ustad Amjad Ali Khan** (Senia-Bangash Gharana) and **Ustad Ali Akbar Khan** (Maihar Gharana) are two of India's greatest **Sarod** players.

**Q19. Pandit Vishwa Mohan Bhatt won a Grammy Award (1994, *A Meeting by the River*) for his self-invented 19-string modified slide guitar called:**
- A) Chitra Veena
- B) Mohan Veena
- C) Vichitra Veena
- D) Gottuvadhyam
- **Answer:** **B) Mohan Veena**
- **Explanation:** **Pt. Vishwa Mohan Bhatt** (Jaipur) created the **Mohan Veena** (a modified Hawaiian guitar with sympathetic strings) and won Padma Shri (2002) and Padma Bhushan (2017).

**Q20. Ustad Asad Ali Khan and Zia Mohiuddin Dagar are associated with which ancient Dhrupad instrument?**
- A) Pakhawaj
- B) Rudra Veena
- C) Esraj
- D) Bansuri
- **Answer:** **B) Rudra Veena**
- **Explanation:** **Rudra Veena** (*Been*) is the primary plucked string instrument of **Dhrupad**; **Ustad Asad Ali Khan** (Khandarbani style) and **Zia Mohiuddin Dagar** (Dagarvani) were its foremost exponents.

**Q21. Which bowed string instrument is Ustad Bundu Khan, Pandit Ram Narayan, and Ustad Sultan Khan associated with?**
- A) Violin
- B) Sarangi
- C) Dilruba
- D) Kamaycha
- **Answer:** **B) Sarangi**
- **Explanation:** **Pt. Ram Narayan** elevated the **Sarangi** from an accompanying instrument to a solo classical concert instrument; **Ustad Sultan Khan** and **Ustad Sabri Khan** are also famed Sarangi maestros.

### D. Tribal & Folk Festivals of India

**Q22. Which tribal festival of Telangana, celebrated biennially by the Koya tribe in Mulugu district, is Asia's largest tribal gathering?**
- A) Bathukamma
- B) Nagoba Jatara
- C) Medaram Jatara (Sammakka Saralamma Jatara)
- D) Bonalu
- **Answer:** **C) Medaram Jatara (Sammakka Saralamma Jatara)**
- **Explanation:** Held every two years in **Medaram (Mulugu)** near Eturnagaram Wildlife Sanctuary, it honours mother-daughter tribal heroines **Sammakka and Saralamma** who fought Kakatiya tax tyranny.

**Q23. Which Indian festival holds the distinction of being the longest Dussehra celebration in the world, spanning 75 days?**
- A) Kullu Dussehra
- B) Mysuru Dasara
- C) Bastar Dussehra
- D) Kota Dussehra
- **Answer:** **C) Bastar Dussehra**
- **Explanation:** Celebrated in **Jagdalpur (Chhattisgarh)** for **75 days** (*Hareli* to *Ashwin Shukla Trayodashi*), it worships local deity **Goddess Danteshwari** without burning Ravana's effigy.

**Q24. Jonbeel Mela, famous as the only fair in India where the traditional *barter system* is still practiced, is held in which state?**
- A) Arunachal Pradesh
- B) Assam
- C) Tripura
- D) Nagaland
- **Answer:** **B) Assam**
- **Explanation:** Held every January in **Morigaon district (Assam)** by the **Tiwa (Lalung)** community alongside hill tribes (Karbi, Khasi, Jaintia), commodities like ginger, turmeric, and dry fish are bartered without money.

**Q25. The *Wangala* festival, popularly known as the "100 Drums Festival", is the most important post-harvest festival of which tribe?**
- A) Khasi
- B) Jaintia
- C) Garo
- D) Ao Naga
- **Answer:** **C) Garo**
- **Explanation:** Celebrated in **Meghalaya** by the **Garo** tribe in honour of **Misi Saljong** (Sun God), featuring 100 *Dama* drums and traditional *Rugala* rituals.

**Q26. Match the following Naga tribes with their respective major festivals:**
1. Ao — a) Sekrenyi
2. Angami — b) Moatsu
3. Konyak — c) Tokhu Emong
4. Lotha — d) Aoling
- A) 1-b, 2-a, 3-d, 4-c
- B) 1-a, 2-b, 3-c, 4-d
- C) 1-b, 2-d, 3-a, 4-c
- D) 1-c, 2-a, 3-d, 4-b
- **Answer:** **A) 1-b, 2-a, 3-d, 4-c**
- **Explanation:** **Moatsu** = **Ao**; **Sekrenyi** = **Angami**; **Aoling** = **Konyak**; **Tokhu Emong** = **Lotha**.

**Q27. *Sarhul*, a spring festival marking the New Year and worship of the Sal tree, is predominantly celebrated in which state?**
- A) Odisha
- B) Jharkhand
- C) Chhattisgarh
- D) Madhya Pradesh
- **Answer:** **B) Jharkhand**
- **Explanation:** Celebrated by **Oraon, Munda, Ho, and Santhal** tribes of **Jharkhand** during *Chaitra*; *Pahan* (village priest) predicts rainfall using earthen water pots.

**Q28. *Nuakhai*, an agricultural festival welcoming the newly harvested rice crop (*Nua* = new, *Khai* = food), is primarily celebrated in:**
- A) Western Odisha
- B) Coastal Karnataka
- C) Upper Assam
- D) Vidarbha, Maharashtra
- **Answer:** **A) Western Odisha**
- **Explanation:** Celebrated the day after Ganesh Chaturthi in **Western Odisha** (Sambalpur, Bolangir, Kalahandi), offering *Nabanna* (new rice) to **Goddess Samaleswari**.

**Q29. *Bhumchu*, in which monks open a sealed sacred water vase to forecast the state's fortune for the upcoming year, is held at Tashiding Monastery in:**
- A) Ladakh
- B) Himachal Pradesh
- C) Sikkim
- D) Arunachal Pradesh
- **Answer:** **C) Sikkim**
- **Explanation:** Held at **Tashiding Monastery (West Sikkim)** on the 14th–15th day of the first Tibetan lunar month; a brimful vase signifies prosperity, while low or overflowing water signals scarcity or strife.

**Q30. *Herath*, celebrated uniquely with *Watuk Nath* puja by Kashmiri Pandits, corresponds to which pan-Indian festival?**
- A) Makar Sankranti
- B) Maha Shivaratri
- C) Navratri
- D) Sharad Purnima
- **Answer:** **B) Maha Shivaratri**
- **Explanation:** **Herath** is derived from Sanskrit *Hararatri* (Night of Shiva) and is the premier religious festival of Kashmiri Pandits.

### E. Temple Dynasties & Rock-Cut Caves

**Q31. The Brihadeeswarar (Rajarajeswara) Temple at Thanjavur, completed around 1010 CE, was built by which Chola ruler?**
- A) Rajendra Chola I
- B) Rajaraja Chola I
- C) Kulottunga I
- D) Vijayalaya Chola
- **Answer:** **B) Rajaraja Chola I**
- **Explanation:** **Rajaraja Chola I** built the granite **Brihadeeswarar Temple** at Thanjavur; his son **Rajendra I** built **Gangaikonda Cholapuram**; **Rajaraja II** built **Airavatesvara** at Darasuram.

**Q32. The monolithic Kailasanatha Temple (Cave 16) at Ellora, carved top-down from a single basalt rock, was commissioned by which Rashtrakuta king?**
- A) Dantidurga
- B) Dhruva
- C) Krishna I
- D) Amoghavarsha I
- **Answer:** **C) Krishna I**
- **Explanation:** **Krishna I** (reigned c. 756–773 CE) of the **Rashtrakuta dynasty** commissioned the monolithic Dravida-style **Kailasa Temple (Cave 16)** at Ellora.

**Q33. The famous Khajuraho temples in Madhya Pradesh, known for Nagara-style *Shikharas* and erotic sculptures, were built by which dynasty?**
- A) Paramara
- B) Chandela
- C) Solanki
- D) Pratihara
- **Answer:** **B) Chandela**
- **Explanation:** The **Chandela** rulers of Bundelkhand built ~85 temples at Khajuraho between 950 and 1050 CE (notably **Kandariya Mahadeva** under Vidyadhara).

**Q34. Which Solanki (Chaulukya) ruler built the Sun Temple at Modhera in Gujarat (1026–27 CE)?**
- A) Mulraja I
- B) Bhima I
- C) Siddharaja Jayasimha
- D) Kumarapala
- **Answer:** **B) Bhima I**
- **Explanation:** **Bhima I** of the Chaulukya (Solanki) dynasty built the **Modhera Sun Temple** on the banks of the Pushpavati river; his queen **Udayamati** later built **Rani ki Vav** at Patan.

**Q35. The Shore Temple at Mahabalipuram (Mamallapuram) was constructed under which Pallava ruler?**
- A) Mahendravarman I
- B) Narasimhavarman I (Mamalla)
- C) Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha)
- D) Nandivarman II
- **Answer:** **C) Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha)**
- **Explanation:** While **Narasimhavarman I** carved the monolithic *Pancha Rathas*, the structural **Shore Temple** (700–728 CE) and **Kailasanatha Temple (Kanchipuram)** were built by **Narasimhavarman II (Rajasimha)**.

**Q36. The Barabar Caves in Bihar, the oldest surviving rock-cut caves in India, were donated by Emperor Ashoka to which sect?**
- A) Digambara Jains
- B) Theravada Buddhists
- C) Ajivikas
- D) Pashupata Shaivites
- **Answer:** **C) Ajivikas**
- **Explanation:** **Ashoka** and his grandson **Dasharatha** excavated the **Barabar** (Lomas Rishi, Sudama, Karna Chaupar, Vishva Zopri) and **Nagarjuni** caves for the **Ajivika** ascetics (founded by Makkhali Gosala).

**Q37. The Udayagiri and Khandagiri rock-cut caves near Bhubaneswar (Odisha) were excavated during the reign of King Kharavela of which dynasty?**
- A) Mahameghavahana (Chedi) Dynasty
- B) Satavahana Dynasty
- C) Bhaumakara Dynasty
- D) Somavamshi Dynasty
- **Answer:** **A) Mahameghavahana (Chedi) Dynasty**
- **Explanation:** Built for **Jain** monks in the 2nd–1st century BCE; the **Hathigumpha inscription** of **King Kharavela** (Chedi/Mahameghavahana dynasty) is located in Udayagiri.

### F. Puppetry, Paintings & Martial Arts

**Q38. Which traditional shadow puppetry form belongs to Karnataka?**
- A) Tholu Bommalata
- B) Togalu Gombeyaata
- C) Ravanachhaya
- D) Tholpavakoothu
- **Answer:** **B) Togalu Gombeyaata**
- **Explanation:** Shadow puppetry states: **Togalu Gombeyaata** = Karnataka; **Tholu Bommalata** = Andhra Pradesh; **Ravanachhaya** = Odisha; **Tholpavakoothu** = Kerala.

**Q39. *Pavakoothu* is a traditional glove puppetry form of which state, heavily influenced by Kathakali?**
- A) Tamil Nadu
- B) Kerala
- C) West Bengal
- D) Odisha
- **Answer:** **B) Kerala**
- **Explanation:** **Pavakoothu** (glove puppetry, Kerala) uses puppets decorated like Kathakali characters; **Putul Nach** (rod puppetry) is from West Bengal/Assam; **Kathputli** (string) is from Rajasthan.

**Q40. Which traditional folk painting style uses natural dyes on palm-leaf or cloth (*Patta*) in Odisha and depicts stories of Lord Jagannath?**
- A) Kalamkari
- B) Pattachitra
- C) Pichwai
- D) Phad
- **Answer:** **B) Pattachitra**
- **Explanation:** **Odisha Pattachitra** (also done in West Bengal) uses tamarind-paste-treated cloth (*Patta*) and natural pigments; **Pichwai** (Nathdwara, Rajasthan) depicts Shrinathji; **Phad** (Bhilwara, Rajasthan) depicts Pabuji/Devnarayan.

**Q41. *Warli* paintings, known for geometric white-pigment triangles, circles, and squares on mud walls, originate from which state?**
- A) Rajasthan
- B) Bihar
- C) Maharashtra
- D) Jharkhand
- **Answer:** **C) Maharashtra**
- **Explanation:** **Warli** folk art is practiced by the Warli tribe of **Maharashtra** (Palghar/Thane); **Madhubani (Mithila)** is from Bihar; **Gond** is from Madhya Pradesh.

**Q42. Match the following Indian traditional martial arts with their states:**
1. Kalaripayattu — a) Manipur
2. Silambam — b) Kerala
3. Thang-Ta — c) Punjab
4. Gatka — d) Tamil Nadu
- A) 1-b, 2-d, 3-a, 4-c
- B) 1-b, 2-a, 3-d, 4-c
- C) 1-d, 2-b, 3-a, 4-c
- D) 1-a, 2-d, 3-b, 4-c
- **Answer:** **A) 1-b, 2-d, 3-a, 4-c**

### G. UNESCO Sites, ICH & Calendars

**Q43. Which cultural site from Assam became India's 43rd UNESCO World Heritage Site in July 2024?**
- A) Majuli River Island
- B) Sivasagar Rang Ghar
- C) Moidams – the Mound-Burial System of the Ahom Dynasty
- D) Kamakhya Temple Complex
- **Answer:** **C) Moidams – the Mound-Burial System of the Ahom Dynasty**
- **Explanation:** Located at **Charaideo**, the **Ahom Moidams** became India's **43rd UNESCO World Heritage Site** (and the **1st cultural site from Northeast India**) in **2024**.

**Q44. Which is the ONLY site in India inscribed under the *Mixed* (both Cultural and Natural) category of UNESCO World Heritage Sites?**
- A) Nanda Devi and Valley of Flowers
- B) Western Ghats
- C) Khangchendzonga National Park
- D) Sundarbans National Park
- **Answer:** **C) Khangchendzonga National Park**
- **Explanation:** Located in **Sikkim** and inscribed in **2016**, **Khangchendzonga National Park** is India's sole **Mixed** UNESCO World Heritage Site.

**Q45. Which traditional dance of Gujarat became India's 15th element on the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage (ICH) list in December 2023?**
- A) Dandiya Raas
- B) Garba
- C) Tippani
- D) Bhavai
- **Answer:** **B) Garba**
- **Explanation:** **Garba of Gujarat** was inscribed in **2023** (15th ICH element), following **Durga Puja in Kolkata (2021 — 14th)** and **Kumbh Mela (2017 — 13th)**.

**Q46. The Indian National Calendar based on the Saka Era was officially adopted on March 22, 1957, on the recommendation of a committee chaired by:**
- A) P. C. Mahalanobis
- B) Meghnad Saha
- C) C. V. Raman
- D) Homi J. Bhabha
- **Answer:** **B) Meghnad Saha**
- **Explanation:** The **Calendar Reform Committee (1952)** chaired by astrophysicist **Dr. Meghnad Saha** recommended the Saka-based National Calendar (**1 Chaitra 1879 Saka = March 22, 1957**).
