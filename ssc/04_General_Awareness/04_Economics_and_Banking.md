## Basic Concepts & Sectors of Economy

### Core Definitions
- **Economics:** Study of allocation of scarce resources among alternative uses.
- **Adam Smith:** Known as the **"Father of Modern Economics"** (Book: *The Wealth of Nations*, 1776).
- **Microeconomics:** Studies individual economic units (consumers, firms, households). Father: Alfred Marshall.
- **Macroeconomics:** Studies aggregate economic behavior (inflation, national income, unemployment). Father: John Maynard Keynes (*General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money*, 1936).

### Sectors of the Economy
```
1. Primary Sector (Direct extraction from nature)
   └── Agriculture, Forestry, Fishing, Mining & Quarrying

2. Secondary Sector (Manufacturing & Processing / Value addition)
   └── Manufacturing, Construction, Electricity, Gas & Water Supply

3. Tertiary Sector (Services)
   └── Trade, Hotels, Transport, Communication, Banking, Insurance, Real Estate
   * Largest contributor to India's GDP (~54%)

4. Quaternary Sector: Knowledge-based services (IT, R&D, Financial consulting)
5. Quinary Sector: Highest level decision-making (CEOs, government leaders, top scientists)
```

### Types of Economic Systems
| Type | Ownership | Motive | Examples |
|------|-----------|--------|----------|
| **Capitalist / Market Economy** | Private entities | Profit maximization (Laissez-faire) | USA, UK |
| **Socialist / Command Economy** | State / Government | Social welfare, equitable distribution | Former USSR, Cuba |
| **Mixed Economy** | Both Public and Private | Profit + Social Welfare | **India** |

## National Income Accounting

### Key Aggregates
```
GDP (Gross Domestic Product):
= Total money value of all final goods and services produced within the domestic territory of a country in a financial year.

GNP (Gross National Product):
= GDP + NFIA (Net Factor Income from Abroad)
= GDP + (Income earned by Indians abroad - Income earned by foreigners in India)

Depreciation (Consumption of Fixed Capital):
Gross - Depreciation = Net
├── NDP = GDP - Depreciation
└── NNP = GNP - Depreciation

National Income (NI):
= NNP at Factor Cost (NNP_FC)
= NNP at Market Price - Indirect Taxes + Subsidies
```

### Important Concepts:
- **Per Capita Income:** $\text{Per Capita Income} = \frac{\text{Total National Income}}{\text{Total Population}}$
- **Nominal GDP:** Evaluated at current market prices (includes inflation).
- **Real GDP:** Evaluated at constant base year prices (excludes inflation; true measure of growth).
- **GDP Deflator:** Ratio of Nominal GDP to Real GDP: $\text{GDP Deflator} = \frac{\text{Nominal GDP}}{\text{Real GDP}} \times 100$.
- **Base Year for India's GDP:** **2011–12** (set by CSO/NSO).
- **Calculated by:** National Statistical Office (**NSO**), under the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI).

## Inflation, Deflation & Business Cycles

### Types of Inflation
- **Demand-Pull Inflation:** Caused when aggregate demand exceeds aggregate supply ("Too much money chasing too few goods").
- **Cost-Push Inflation:** Caused by increase in prices of inputs (wages, raw materials, crude oil prices).
- **Stagflation:** High inflation combined with stagnant economic growth and high unemployment.
- **Deflation:** Persistent decrease in general price level (negative inflation).
- **Disinflation:** Slowing down of the rate of inflation (e.g., inflation drops from 7% to 4%).
- **Headline Inflation:** Overall inflation figure based on entire consumer basket (volatile items included).
- **Core Inflation:** Headline inflation minus volatile items like food and fuel.

### Measuring Inflation in India
| Indicator | Base Year | Published By | Key Focus |
|-----------|-----------|--------------|-----------|
| **CPI (Consumer Price Index)** | **2012** | NSO (MoSPI) | Retail level price change; used by RBI for inflation targeting (**4% ± 2%**) |
| **WPI (Wholesale Price Index)** | **2011-12** | Office of Economic Adviser (Ministry of Commerce) | Wholesale/bulk transactions; does **not** include services! |

## Monetary Policy & Reserve Bank of India (RBI)

### About RBI
- **Established:** **April 1, 1935** under the RBI Act, 1934 based on the **Hilton Young Commission** (Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance).
- **Nationalized:** **January 1, 1949**.
- **Headquarters:** Mumbai (originally Calcutta from 1935 to 1937).
- **First Governor:** Sir Osborne Smith.
- **First Indian Governor:** C.D. Deshmukh.
- **Note Issuing Authority:** Sole right to issue currency notes (except ₹1 note and coins, which are issued by the Ministry of Finance and bear the signature of the **Finance Secretary**; other notes carry the **RBI Governor's** signature).

### Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)
- Formed under the RBI Act (amended in 2016).
- **Composition:** 6 members (3 from RBI including Governor as ex-officio Chairperson + 3 appointed by Central Government).
- **Mandate:** Maintain consumer inflation (CPI) within target of **4% with a tolerance band of ± 2%** (i.e., 2% to 6%).

### Monetary Policy Instruments
```
Quantitative Instruments (Control overall volume of credit):
├── Repo Rate: Rate at which RBI lends short-term money to commercial banks against government securities.
├── Reverse Repo Rate: Rate at which RBI borrows money from commercial banks.
├── Standing Deposit Facility (SDF): Liquidity absorption without providing collateral securities.
├── Marginal Standing Facility (MSF): Overnight lending window for banks in emergency at higher rate.
├── Bank Rate: Rate at which RBI lends long-term funds without collateral.
├── Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR): Percentage of net demand and time liabilities (NDTL) banks must keep with RBI in cash.
└── Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR): Percentage of NDTL banks must maintain with themselves in liquid assets (Gold, Cash, Govt Securities).

Qualitative Instruments (Direct flow of credit to specific sectors):
├── Margin requirements
├── Moral suasion
├── Credit rationing
└── Direct action
```

> **Rule:** To curb inflation (Tight/Dear money policy) $\rightarrow$ RBI **increases** policy rates (Repo, CRR). To boost economic growth (Easy/Cheap money policy) $\rightarrow$ RBI **decreases** policy rates.

## Fiscal Policy & Union Budget Terms

### Budget Architecture
Presented under **Article 112** as the "Annual Financial Statement".

```
Union Budget
├── Revenue Budget
│   ├── Revenue Receipts: Tax Revenue (Direct + Indirect) + Non-tax Revenue (Interest, Dividends, Fees)
│   └── Revenue Expenditure: Interest payments, Subsidies, Defence salaries/pensions (creates no assets)
└── Capital Budget
    ├── Capital Receipts: Disinvestment, Recovery of loans, Market borrowings (creates liabilities/reduces assets)
    └── Capital Expenditure: Construction of roads, bridges, machinery, acquisition of assets (creates capital assets)
```

### Deficit Formulas
- **Revenue Deficit** = $\text{Revenue Expenditure} - \text{Revenue Receipts}$
- **Fiscal Deficit** = $\text{Total Expenditure} - \text{Total Receipts (excluding borrowings)}$
  *(Indicates total borrowing requirement of the government!)*
- **Primary Deficit** = $\text{Fiscal Deficit} - \text{Interest Payments}$

### Important Funds of India
- **Consolidated Fund of India (Article 266(1)):** All revenues, loans, and recoveries flow here. Parliament's approval needed to withdraw funds.
- **Public Account of India (Article 266(2)):** PF, small savings, postal deposits. Executive approval suffices.
- **Contingency Fund of India (Article 267):** ₹30,000 Cr corpus placed at disposal of the President for unforeseen emergencies.

## Indian Banking Structure & Financial Markets

### Banking Classification
- **Scheduled Commercial Banks (SCBs):** Listed in the 2nd Schedule of RBI Act, 1934. Includes Public Sector Banks (e.g., SBI, PNB, BoB), Private Banks (HDFC, ICICI), Foreign Banks, and Regional Rural Banks (RRBs).
- **RRBs:** First established on **October 2, 1975** (Prathama Bank, Moradabad) based on Narasimham Committee.
- **Payments Banks:** Can accept deposits up to ₹2 Lakh; **cannot issue credit cards or give loans** (e.g., Airtel Payments Bank, India Post Payments Bank).
- **Small Finance Banks (SFBs):** Provide basic banking & credit to unserved/underserved sections (e.g., AU Small Finance Bank).
- **NPA (Non-Performing Asset):** A loan or advance where interest or principal remains overdue for a period of **90 days**.

### Financial Markets
- **Money Market:** Market for short-term funds (<1 year). Regulated by **RBI**.
  - *Instruments:* Treasury Bills (T-Bills issued for 91, 182, 364 days), Call Money (1 day), Commercial Paper, Certificate of Deposit.
- **Capital Market:** Market for long-term funds (>1 year). Regulated by **SEBI** (Securities and Exchange Board of India; est. 1988, statutory status 1992).
  - *Stock Exchanges:* Bombay Stock Exchange (BSE, est. 1875 — oldest in Asia; index: SENSEX), National Stock Exchange (NSE, est. 1992; index: NIFTY 50).

## Taxation System & GST

### Direct vs Indirect Taxes
| Parameter | Direct Tax | Indirect Tax |
|-----------|------------|--------------|
| Incidence & Impact | Fall on the same person (cannot be shifted) | Fall on different persons (can be shifted to consumer) |
| Examples | Income Tax, Corporate Tax, Capital Gains Tax | GST, Customs Duty, Excise on petroleum/alcohol |
| Nature | **Progressive** (richer pay higher percentage) | **Regressive** (same burden on rich and poor) |
| Administered by | CBDT (Central Board of Direct Taxes) | CBIC (Central Board of Indirect Taxes and Customs) |

### Goods and Services Tax (GST)
- **Implemented on:** **July 1, 2017** (celebrated as GST Day).
- **Constitutional Amendment:** **101st Amendment Act, 2016** (Article 279A).
- **First state to ratify:** **Assam**.
- **Nature:** Destination-based, comprehensive indirect tax on consumption.
- **Tax Slabs:** 0%, 5%, 12%, 18%, 28%.
- **GST Council (Article 279A):** Chaired by the **Union Finance Minister**; members are state finance ministers.
- **Exempt from GST:** Petroleum products, Alcoholic liquor for human consumption, Electricity.

## Five-Year Plans & NITI Aayog

### Important Five-Year Plans (1951–2017)
| Plan | Duration | Model / Focus | Highlights |
|------|----------|---------------|------------|
| **1st FYP** | 1951–56 | **Harrod-Domar Model** | Focus on agriculture, irrigation, dams (Bhakra Nangal, Hirakud) |
| **2nd FYP** | 1956–61 | **P.C. Mahalanobis Model** | Focus on rapid industrialization & heavy industries (Bhilai, Durgapur, Rourkela steel plants) |
| **3rd FYP** | 1961–66 | Gadgil Yojana | Agriculture & wheat production; set back by Indo-China (1962) & Indo-Pak (1965) wars |
| **Plan Holidays** | 1966–69 | Three annual plans | Devaluation of Rupee; Green Revolution launched (1966) |
| **4th FYP** | 1969–74 | Ashok Rudra & A.S. Manne | Growth with stability; Nationalization of 14 major banks (1969); Pokhran-I (1974) |
| **5th FYP** | 1974–79 | D.P. Dhar Model | Slogan: **"Garibi Hatao"** (Poverty eradication) & self-reliance; terminated by Janata Govt |
| **Rolling Plan** | 1978–80 | Gunnar Myrdal concept | Introduced by Morarji Desai government |
| **6th FYP** | 1980–85 | Investment Yojana | Nationalization of 6 more banks (1980); NABARD established (1982) |
| **7th FYP** | 1985–90 | Pranab Mukherjee | Speedier food-grain growth; Blue Revolution |
| **8th FYP** | 1992–97 | John W. Miller Model | **LPG Reforms (1991)**: Liberalization, Privatization, Globalization (PM: Narasimha Rao; FM: Dr. Manmohan Singh) |
| **12th FYP** | 2012–17 | Final FYP | Theme: "Faster, Sustainable and More Inclusive Growth" |

### NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India)
- **Formed:** **January 1, 2015** (replaced the 65-year-old Planning Commission).
- **Nature:** Non-constitutional, non-statutory extra-constitutional think tank.
- **Chairperson:** **Prime Minister of India** (ex-officio).
- **Vice-Chairperson:** Appointed by PM (Rank of Cabinet Minister).
- **Governing Council:** Comprises PM, all State Chief Ministers, and Lt. Governors of UTs (promotes **Cooperative Federalism**).

## Last 10 Years PYQs with Explanations

### PYQ 1 — SSC CHSL 2024
**Q.** Which organization replaced the Planning Commission of India on January 1, 2015?
- A) Finance Commission
- B) NITI Aayog
- C) National Development Council
- D) Central Statistical Office
- **Answer:** **B) NITI Aayog** ✅
- *Explanation:* National Institution for Transforming India (NITI Aayog) was formed via cabinet resolution on Jan 1, 2015 with PM as Chairperson.

### PYQ 2 — SSC CHSL 2023
**Q.** In India, which authority has the sole right to issue currency notes other than one rupee note?
- A) Ministry of Finance
- B) State Bank of India
- C) Reserve Bank of India
- D) SEBI
- **Answer:** **C) Reserve Bank of India** ✅
- *Explanation:* Under Section 22 of the RBI Act 1934, RBI issues all banknotes except ₹1 note and coins, which are issued by the Ministry of Finance.

### PYQ 3 — SSC CHSL 2023
**Q.** What does "Stagflation" refer to in economics?
- A) High inflation combined with rapid economic growth
- B) Low inflation combined with high employment
- C) High inflation combined with high unemployment and stagnant growth
- D) Persistent deflation
- **Answer:** **C) High inflation combined with high unemployment and stagnant growth** ✅
- *Explanation:* Stagflation is a condition of slow economic growth, high unemployment, and rising prices (inflation).

### PYQ 4 — SSC CHSL 2022
**Q.** Which amendment to the Constitution of India introduced the Goods and Services Tax (GST)?
- A) 100th Amendment
- B) 101st Amendment
- C) 102nd Amendment
- D) 103rd Amendment
- **Answer:** **B) 101st Amendment Act, 2016** ✅
- *Explanation:* The 101st Constitutional Amendment Act introduced GST from July 1, 2017.

### PYQ 5 — SSC CHSL 2022
**Q.** The Second Five-Year Plan (1956–1961) of India was based on which economic model?
- A) Harrod-Domar Model
- B) Mahalanobis Model
- C) Gadgil Model
- D) Solow Model
- **Answer:** **B) Mahalanobis Model** ✅
- *Explanation:* Formulated by Prof. P.C. Mahalanobis focusing on rapid industrialization and heavy capital goods.

### PYQ 6 — SSC CHSL 2021
**Q.** The rate at which the Reserve Bank of India provides short-term liquidity to commercial banks against government securities is called:
- A) Reverse Repo Rate
- B) Bank Rate
- C) Repo Rate
- D) Marginal Standing Facility
- **Answer:** **C) Repo Rate** ✅
- *Explanation:* Repo Rate (Repurchasing Option) is the rate at which commercial banks borrow from RBI against government securities.

### PYQ 7 — SSC CHSL 2020
**Q.** Who was the first Indian Governor of the Reserve Bank of India?
- A) Sir Osborne Smith
- B) C.D. Deshmukh
- C) Manmohan Singh
- D) Raghuram Rajan
- **Answer:** **B) C.D. Deshmukh** ✅
- *Explanation:* Sir Osborne Smith was the first governor (1935), while C.D. Deshmukh was the first Indian governor (1943–1949).

### PYQ 8 — SSC CHSL 2019
**Q.** Who is known as the "Father of Economics"?
- A) Karl Marx
- B) John Maynard Keynes
- C) Adam Smith
- D) Amartya Sen
- **Answer:** **C) Adam Smith** ✅
- *Explanation:* Adam Smith authored *The Wealth of Nations* (1776) and is regarded as the Father of Modern Economics.

### PYQ 9 — SSC CHSL 2018
**Q.** In which year were 14 major commercial Indian banks nationalized for the first time?
- A) 1949
- B) 1955
- C) 1969
- D) 1980
- **Answer:** **C) 1969** ✅
- *Explanation:* Prime Minister Indira Gandhi nationalized 14 major commercial banks on July 19, 1969. 6 more banks were nationalized in 1980.

### PYQ 10 — SSC CHSL 2017
**Q.** Which institution publishes the "World Economic Outlook" report?
- A) World Bank
- B) International Monetary Fund (IMF)
- C) United Nations
- D) World Economic Forum
- **Answer:** **B) International Monetary Fund (IMF)** ✅
- *Explanation:* IMF publishes the *World Economic Outlook* and *Global Financial Stability Report*. World Bank publishes *World Development Report*.
