## 1. Fundamental Concepts, Microeconomics & Economic Systems

### 1.1 Origin, Definitions & Branches of Economics
- **Etymology:** Derived from the Greek word *Oikonomia* (*oikos* = household + *nomos* = management), meaning "household management."
- **Father of Economics:** **Adam Smith** (Scottish economist). Famous book: ***An Inquiry into the Nature and Causes of the Wealth of Nations*** (**1776**). Defined economics as the *"Science of Wealth"* and advocated **Laissez-faire** (minimal government intervention, guided by the **"Invisible Hand"** of market forces).
- **Key Definitions of Economics (SSC Favorites):**

| Definition Type | Economist | Famous Work & Year | Core Idea |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Wealth Definition** | **Adam Smith** | *The Wealth of Nations* (1776) | Economics studies the nature and causes of wealth of nations. |
| **Welfare Definition** | **Alfred Marshall** | *Principles of Economics* (1890) | Study of mankind in the ordinary business of life; focuses on material welfare, not just wealth. |
| **Scarcity Definition** | **Lionel Robbins** | *An Essay on the Nature and Significance of Economic Science* (1932) | Study of human behaviour as a relationship between **unlimited ends (wants)** and **scarce means** with **alternative uses**. |
| **Growth Definition** | **Paul A. Samuelson** | *Economics* (1948) | Dynamic study of how society allocates scarce resources over time for sustainable growth and distribution. |

- **Microeconomics vs. Macroeconomics:**
  - The terms **"Micro"** (*mikros* = small) and **"Macro"** (*makros* = large) were first coined by Norwegian economist **Ragnar Frisch** in **1933** (who also won the first Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences in 1969 alongside Jan Tinbergen).
  - **Father of Macroeconomics:** **John Maynard Keynes** (British economist). Famous book: ***The General Theory of Employment, Interest and Money*** (**1936**), written in response to the **Great Depression (1929)**. Advocated active fiscal policy / government intervention to boost aggregate demand.

| Basis | Microeconomics ("Price Theory") | Macroeconomics ("Income & Employment Theory") |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Scope** | Individual units: a consumer, firm, household, or industry. | Economy as a whole: national aggregates. |
| **Core Tools** | Individual demand and supply. | Aggregate Demand ($AD$) and Aggregate Supply ($AS$). |
| **Main Objective** | Price determination & optimal resource allocation. | National income, employment, inflation control, economic growth. |
| **Key Assumption** | Macro variables (like national income) remain constant (*ceteris paribus*). | Micro distribution remains constant. |
| **Examples** | Price of wheat, wage of a worker, demand for smartphones. | GDP, inflation (CPI/WPI), RBI monetary policy, fiscal deficit. |

### 1.2 Scarcity, Opportunity Cost & Production Possibility Curve (PPC)
- **Scarcity:** The central economic problem arises because **human wants are unlimited**, while **resources are limited (scarce)** and have **alternative uses**. Scarcity exists even in the richest countries.
- **Three Central Problems of an Economy:** *(1) What to produce and in what quantities? (2) How to produce? (labour-intensive vs. capital-intensive) (3) For whom to produce? (distribution of income).*
- **Opportunity Cost:** The value of the **next best alternative forgone** when making a choice.
  - *Exam Exception:* **Free goods** (e.g., sunlight, air) have **zero opportunity cost**. Resources with only one specific use (no alternative use) also have **zero opportunity cost**.
- **Production Possibility Curve (PPC) / Production Possibility Frontier (PPF) / Transformation Curve:**
  - Shows all maximum possible combinations of **two goods** an economy can produce with **given fixed resources and technology**, assuming full and efficient utilization.
  - **Shape:** **Concave to the origin** because of **Increasing Marginal Opportunity Cost (MOC)** / Marginal Rate of Transformation ($MRT = \frac{\Delta \text{Loss}}{\Delta \text{Gain}}$) — resources are not equally efficient in producing both goods.
  - *Exception:* If $MOC$ is constant, PPC is a **downward-sloping straight line**. If $MOC$ decreases, PPC is **convex**.
  - **Points relative to PPC:**
    - **On the PPC:** Full and efficient utilization of resources.
    - **Inside the PPC:** **Underutilization / inefficiency / unemployment** of resources.
    - **Outside the PPC:** **Unattainable** combinations with current resources and technology.
  - **Shifts in PPC:** Shifts **rightward (outward)** with economic growth, technological advancement, or increase in resources (e.g., *Skill India*, FDI); shifts **leftward (inward)** due to war, natural disasters, or resource depletion.

### 1.3 The 5 Sectors of the Economy & India's Structural Context

| Sector | Colloquial Name | Core Activities | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Primary Sector** | **Red-Collar** (Agriculture & Allied) | Direct extraction/harvesting of natural resources. | Farming, dairy/livestock, forestry, fishing, mining & quarrying. |
| **2. Secondary Sector** | **Blue-Collar** (Manufacturing & Industry) | Processing raw materials into finished/semi-finished goods. | Manufacturing, steel/textiles, construction, electricity, gas & water supply. |
| **3. Tertiary Sector** | **White / Pink-Collar** (Services) | Intangible services supporting production & consumers. | Banking, insurance, transport, trade, hotels, telecom, healthcare, education. |
| **4. Quaternary Sector** | **White-Collar** (Knowledge Economy) | Intellectual, R&D, and information-based services. | Software/IT R&D, data analytics, scientific research, consultancy, fintech. |
| **5. Quinary Sector** | **Gold-Collar** (Top Decision Makers) | Highest-level policy & executive decision-making. | CEOs, top bureaucrats, judges, cabinet ministers, senior scientists. |

> **India's GDP (GVA) vs. Employment Structural Paradox (Economic Survey & PLFS Context):**
> - **Share in India's GVA / GDP:** **Tertiary (Services)** is the largest contributor (**~54%**), followed by **Secondary (Industry)** (**~25%–28%**), and **Primary (Agriculture & Allied)** (**~15%–18%**).
> - **Share in Employment (PLFS):** **Primary (Agriculture)** remains the **largest employer (~42%–43%)**, followed by **Tertiary (~30%)**, and **Secondary (~24%–26%)**.
> - **SSC Takeaway:** Unlike developed nations (which moved Agriculture $\rightarrow$ Manufacturing $\rightarrow$ Services), India bypassed a large manufacturing boom and jumped to a **services-led economy**. Consequently, agriculture employs over 42% of workers while producing under 18% of output — causing **low per-worker productivity** and **disguised unemployment**.

### 1.4 Types of Economic Systems & Open vs. Closed Economy

| Feature | Capitalist Economy (Market / Laissez-faire) | Socialist Economy (Command / Planned) | Mixed Economy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ownership of Resources** | Private ownership. | State / public ownership. | Co-existence of both **public and private sectors**. |
| **Primary Motive** | Profit maximization. | Social welfare. | Profit (private) + Social welfare (public). |
| **Decision Mechanism** | **Price Mechanism** (Demand & Supply). | **Central Planning Authority**. | Price mechanism regulated by government planning. |
| **Consumer Sovereignty** | High (consumer is king). | Restricted (state decides output). | Moderate to High (regulated). |
| **Intellectual Origin** | Adam Smith (*Wealth of Nations*, 1776). | Karl Marx & Friedrich Engels (*Das Kapital*, 1867). | J.M. Keynes (inspired modern welfare state). |

- **Open Economy vs. Closed Economy:**
  - **Open Economy:** Engages in international trade (exports & imports) and capital flows with the rest of the world ($X - M \neq 0$). Here, **$GDP \neq GNP$**. Almost all modern economies (including India post-1991) are open economies.
  - **Closed Economy:** Zero foreign trade or external factor flows ($X = 0, M = 0$; $NFIA = 0$). Here, **$GDP = GNP$**.

### 1.5 Microeconomics High-Yield Concepts

#### A. Demand, Supply & Equilibrium Price
- **Law of Demand:** *Ceteris paribus* (other things remaining equal), when the price ($P$) of a good rises, its quantity demanded ($Q_d$) falls, and vice versa — an **inverse relationship**. Demand curve slopes **downward** (left to right) due to the Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility, Substitution Effect, and Income Effect.
  - **Exceptions to Law of Demand (Upward-sloping demand curve):** (1) **Giffen Goods**, (2) **Veblen Goods** (status symbols), (3) Fear of future price rise / hoarding, (4) Emergencies/War, (5) Ignorance (judging quality by higher price).
  - **Movement along Demand Curve (Change in $Q_d$):** Caused *only* by change in the **own price** of the good (*Extension* = price falls, $Q_d$ rises; *Contraction* = price rises, $Q_d$ falls).
  - **Shift of Demand Curve (Change in Demand):** Caused by **non-price factors** — consumer income, price of related goods, tastes, population, expectations (*Rightward shift* = increase; *Leftward shift* = decrease).
- **Law of Supply:** *Ceteris paribus*, when price ($P$) rises, quantity supplied ($Q_s$) rises — a **direct relationship**. Supply curve slopes **upward**.
  - **Exceptions to Law of Supply:** Perishable goods (vegetables, milk), rare/antique items (Mona Lisa), auction/distress sales, agricultural output (weather-dependent), and **Backward-Bending Labour Supply Curve** (at very high wage rates, workers prefer leisure over extra hours).
- **Market Equilibrium:** Occurs where **$Q_d = Q_s$**, determining **Equilibrium Price** and **Equilibrium Quantity**.
  - **Price Ceiling (Maximum Price):** Set **below** equilibrium price (e.g., rent control, essential medicines) $\rightarrow$ creates **excess demand (shortage)** and black marketing.
  - **Price Floor (Minimum Support Price / Minimum Wage):** Set **above** equilibrium price (e.g., MSP for crops) $\rightarrow$ creates **excess supply (surplus)**.

#### B. Price Elasticity of Demand ($E_d$)
$$E_d = -\frac{\%\text{ Change in Quantity Demanded}}{\%\text{ Change in Price}} = -\frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta P} \times \frac{P}{Q}$$

| Type of Elasticity | Value of $\|E_d\|$ | Demand Curve Shape | Everyday Examples & Exam Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Perfectly Elastic** | $E_d = \infty$ | **Horizontal** (parallel to X-axis) | Goods under **Perfect Competition**; slightest price rise drops demand to zero. |
| **Relatively Elastic** | $E_d > 1$ | **Flatter** downward slope | **Luxury goods** (ACs, cars, gold jewellery), goods with close substitutes (Pepsi/Coke). |
| **Unitary Elastic** | $E_d = 1$ | **Rectangular Hyperbola** | Total Expenditure ($P \times Q$) remains **constant** when price changes (e.g., moderate-priority goods like mid-range apparel). |
| **Relatively Inelastic** | $E_d < 1$ | **Steeper** downward slope | **Necessities** (milk, fuel/petrol, electricity, toothpaste), addictive goods (cigarettes). |
| **Perfectly Inelastic** | $E_d = 0$ | **Vertical** (parallel to Y-axis) | **Life-saving drugs (insulin)**, **salt**, matchboxes (demand unchanged regardless of price). |

- **Key Determinants of $E_d$:** Availability of close substitutes (more substitutes $\rightarrow$ higher elasticity), nature of good (necessity = inelastic; luxury = elastic), share of income spent (tiny share like salt/matchbox = inelastic), time period (long run = more elastic), postponement of consumption.
- **Other Elasticities (Quick Formulas):**
  - **Income Elasticity ($E_y = \frac{\%\Delta Q}{\%\Delta Y}$):** $E_y > 0$ = **Normal good**; $0 < E_y < 1$ = Necessity; $E_y > 1$ = **Luxury good**; $E_y < 0$ = **Inferior good**.
  - **Cross Price Elasticity ($E_c = \frac{\%\Delta Q_x}{\%\Delta P_y}$):** $E_c > 0$ = **Substitutes** (Tea & Coffee); $E_c < 0$ = **Complements** (Car & Petrol); $E_c = 0$ = **Unrelated goods**.

#### C. Types of Goods (High-Frequency SSC Distinctions)

| Type of Good | Core Definition & Behaviour | Classic Exam Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Normal Good** | Demand **increases** as income increases ($E_y > 0$); obeys Law of Demand. | Branded clothing, fresh fruits, electronics. |
| **Inferior Good** | Demand **decreases** as income increases ($E_y < 0$) because consumers shift to better alternatives. | Coarse grains (jowar/bajra vs. wheat), cheap local footwear, second-hand clothes. |
| **Giffen Good** | Named after **Sir Robert Giffen**. A special **inferior staple good** with **no close substitutes** where a price rise *increases* demand (**upward-sloping demand curve**). Negative income effect outweighs substitution effect. | Bajra/coarse bread for ultra-poor households (historically Irish potatoes / bread). **Rule: All Giffen goods are inferior goods, but all inferior goods are NOT Giffen goods.** |
| **Veblen Good** | Named after **Thorstein Veblen** (*The Theory of the Leisure Class*, 1899). Luxury status symbols exhibiting **conspicuous consumption** / **Snob Appeal** — higher price makes them *more* desirable. | Diamonds, luxury watches (Rolex), vintage cars, designer bags. |
| **Substitute Goods** | Used in place of each other (**Competitive Demand**); price of X $\uparrow \implies$ demand for Y $\uparrow$. | Tea & Coffee, Pepsi & Coke, Uber & Ola. |
| **Complementary Goods** | Used together (**Joint Demand**); price of X $\uparrow \implies$ demand for Y $\downarrow$. | Car & Petrol, Pen & Ink, Bread & Butter, Printer & Cartridge. |
| **Public Good** | **Non-rivalrous** (one person's use doesn't reduce others') and **Non-excludable** (cannot stop non-payers $\rightarrow$ **Free-Rider Problem**). | National defence, street lighting, lighthouse, public parks. |
| **Private Good** | Both **rivalrous** and **excludable**. | Food, clothing, personal mobile phone. |
| **Club Good** | **Excludable** but **non-rivalrous** (until congested). | Netflix/OTT subscription, golf clubs, toll roads, private gyms. |
| **Common-Pool Resource** | **Rivalrous** but **non-excludable** $\rightarrow$ leads to **"Tragedy of the Commons"** (Garrett Hardin). | Open-sea fisheries, groundwater, public grazing pastures. |
| **Merit Good** | Coined by **Richard Musgrave**. Socially desirable goods that individuals under-consume if left to market; subsidized/provided by state. | Education, primary healthcare, vaccination. |

#### D. Utility Theory
- **Utility:** Want-satisfying power of a commodity (subjective; measured in hypothetical units called **Utils**).
  - **Cardinal Utility** (Alfred Marshall): Utility can be measured in numbers ($1, 2, 3\dots$).
  - **Ordinal Utility** (J.R. Hicks & R.G.D. Allen): Utility can only be *ranked* using **Indifference Curves** (convex to origin, downward sloping, never intersect; higher IC = higher satisfaction).
- **Total Utility ($TU$) vs. Marginal Utility ($MU$):**
  $$TU = \sum MU \qquad \text{and} \qquad MU_n = TU_n - TU_{n-1} = \frac{\Delta TU}{\Delta Q}$$
- **Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility (Gossen's First Law):** As a consumer consumes successive units of a commodity continuously, the additional satisfaction ($MU$) from each extra unit goes on diminishing.
  - **Key Relationship ($TU$ & $MU$):**
    1. When $MU > 0$, $TU$ increases at a diminishing rate.
    2. When **$MU = 0$**, **$TU$ is at its Maximum** (**Point of Satiety**).
    3. When $MU < 0$ (negative), $TU$ starts falling.
- **Law of Equi-Marginal Utility (Gossen's Second Law):** A consumer maximizes total utility when the last rupee spent on every good yields equal marginal utility: $\frac{MU_x}{P_x} = \frac{MU_y}{P_y} = MU_m$.
- **Paradox of Value (Water-Diamond Paradox — Adam Smith):** Water has immense *value-in-use* ($TU$) but low price because its supply is abundant ($MU$ is very low). Diamonds have low *value-in-use* but huge *value-in-exchange* (price) because they are scarce ($MU$ is very high).

| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Lorenz Curve & Gini Coefficient** | **Max O. Lorenz** (1905); **Corrado Gini** (1912) | Measures **income or wealth inequality**. Diagonal $45^\circ$ line = Line of Perfect Equality. Further the Lorenz curve bows away, higher the inequality. **Gini Coefficient** $= \frac{\text{Area between equality line & Lorenz curve}}{\text{Total area under equality line}}$. ## 2. National Income Accounting (GDP, GNP, NNP, Per Capita)

### 2.1 Core National Income Formulas & Conversions
All national income conversions rely on **three golden bridges**:
1. **Domestic $\leftrightarrow$ National:** $\text{National} = \text{Domestic} + \mathbf{NFIA}$
   - **NFIA** (*Net Factor Income from Abroad*) $=$ Factor income earned by normal residents from abroad $-$ Factor income paid to non-residents within domestic territory.
   - *Note:* India's NFIA is typically **negative** (outflows of interest/dividends/profits on foreign investments exceed inflows), so **GDP > GNP** for India.
2. **Gross $\leftrightarrow$ Net:** $\text{Net} = \text{Gross} - \mathbf{Depreciation}$ (*Consumption of Fixed Capital*).
3. **Factor Cost $\leftrightarrow$ Market Price:** $\text{Market Price (MP)} = \text{Factor Cost (FC)} + \mathbf{NIT}$
   - **NIT** (*Net Indirect Taxes*) $= \text{Indirect Taxes} - \text{Subsidies}$.

| Aggregate | Exact Formula | Exam Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$GDP_{MP}$** | Market value of all **final goods & services** produced within the **domestic territory** in a financial year. | Headline size of economy; includes foreigners working inside India, excludes Indians working abroad. |
| **$GDP_{FC}$** | $GDP_{MP} - NIT$ | Measures factor income generated domestically (gross). |
| **$GNP_{MP}$** | $GDP_{MP} + NFIA$ | Output produced by **normal residents** of a country regardless of location. |
| **$NDP_{MP}$** | $GDP_{MP} - \text{Depreciation}$ | Net domestic output at market prices. |
| **$NDP_{FC}$** | $GDP_{MP} - \text{Depreciation} - NIT$ | **Domestic Income** (sum of factor incomes generated within domestic boundaries). |
| **$NNP_{MP}$** | $GNP_{MP} - \text{Depreciation}$ | Net national output at market prices. |
| **$NNP_{FC}$** | $NNP_{MP} - NIT = GNP_{FC} - \text{Depreciation} = NDP_{FC} + NFIA$ | **NATIONAL INCOME (NI)** — the true measure of a nation's income! |
| **Per Capita Income (PCI)** | $\dfrac{\text{National Income } (NNP_{FC})}{\text{Mid-year Total Population}}$ | Standard indicator of average living standard (ignores income distribution). |

### 2.2 Factor Cost vs. Basic Price vs. Market Price & GVA (Post-2015 NAS Revision)
In January 2015, India aligned its National Accounts Statistics with the UN System of National Accounts (**SNA 2008**):
- **Headline GDP** is measured at **Market Prices ($GDP_{MP}$)**, while **sector-wise output** (Agriculture, Industry, Services) is measured as **Gross Value Added (GVA) at Basic Prices**.
- **GVA (Gross Value Added):** $\text{GVA} = \text{Value of Output} - \text{Intermediate Consumption}$.
- **Distinction between Production Taxes & Product Taxes:**
  - *Production Taxes/Subsidies:* Independent of volume of production (e.g., land revenue, stamp & registration fees, professional tax; input subsidies to farmers/small units).
  - *Product Taxes/Subsidies:* Paid/received per unit of product (e.g., GST, excise duty, customs duty; food/fertilizer/petroleum subsidies).
- **Exact Linking Formulas:**
  $$\text{GVA at Basic Prices} = \text{GVA at Factor Cost} + (\text{Production Taxes} - \text{Production Subsidies})$$
  $$\text{GDP at Market Prices} = \sum \text{GVA at Basic Prices} + (\text{Product Taxes} - \text{Product Subsidies})$$

### 2.3 Personal Income, Disposable Income, Nominal vs. Real GDP & Deflator
- **Personal Income (PI):** Actual income received by households/individuals from all sources (including transfer payments, excluding corporate retained earnings & corporate taxes):
  $$PI = NI - \text{Undistributed Corporate Profits} - \text{Corporate Tax} - \text{Social Security Contributions} + \text{Transfer Payments}$$
- **Personal Disposable Income (PDI / DPI):** Income actually available for households to spend or save:
  $$PDI = PI - \text{Personal Direct Taxes (Income Tax)} - \text{Misc. Govt Fees/Fines} = \text{Consumption} + \text{Savings}$$
- **Nominal GDP vs. Real GDP:**
  - **Nominal GDP (GDP at Current Prices):** Valued at current year's prevailing prices. Increases with both higher output *and* inflation.
  - **Real GDP (GDP at Constant / Base Year Prices):** Valued at a fixed base year's prices. Reflects **true physical output growth** (adjusted for inflation).
- **GDP Deflator:** Comprehensive measure of price inflation covering **all domestically produced goods and services** (unlike CPI/WPI which use fixed baskets and exclude/limit services or capital goods).
  $$\text{GDP Deflator} = \frac{\text{Nominal GDP}}{\text{Real GDP}} \times 100$$
- **Base Year & Statistical Agency:**
  - **Compiling Agency:** **NSO (National Statistical Office)** under **MoSPI** (*Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation*). *(Note: NSO was formed in May 2019 by merging CSO and NSSO).*
  - **Base Year:** For over a decade (Jan 2015–2026), India's National Accounts base year was **2011–12** (widely tested in SSC exams; previously 2004–05). On **February 27, 2026**, MoSPI/NSO rolled out the new National Accounts series updating the GDP base year to **2022–23** (incorporating GST, MCA-21, ASUSE, and PLFS data).
- **Green GDP:** Adjusts conventional GDP for environmental degradation and natural resource depletion:
  $$\text{Green GDP} = \text{Traditional GDP} - \text{Cost of Environmental Degradation \& Resource Depletion}$$
- **Purchasing Power Parity (PPP):** Theory by **Gustav Cassel** comparing currencies via a common basket of goods rather than market exchange rates (popularized by *The Economist's* **Big Mac Index**). India is the **3rd largest economy in the world by PPP** (after China and the USA).

### 2.4 Three Methods of Measuring National Income

| Method | Alternative Name | Core Formula | Key Precautions & Exclusions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Product / Value Added Method** | Output / Inventory Method | $GDP_{MP} = \sum \text{GVA}_{MP}$ where $\text{GVA} = \text{Value of Output} - \text{Intermediate Consumption}$ | **Exclude** intermediate goods (to avoid **double counting**), second-hand goods (only broker's commission included), and unpaid domestic services. **Include** imputed rent of owner-occupied houses & own-account production. |
| **2. Income Method** | Factor Payment Method | $NDP_{FC} = \text{Compensation of Employees (Wages)} + \text{Operating Surplus (Rent + Interest + Profit)} + \text{Mixed Income of Self-Employed}$ | **Exclude** transfer payments (pensions, scholarships, gifts), windfall gains (lotteries), illegal income, and sale of financial assets (shares/bonds). Add $NFIA$ to get $NNP_{FC}$. |
| **3. Expenditure Method** | Income Disposal Method | $GDP_{MP} = C + I + G + (X - M)$ *(Private Consumption + Gross Investment + Govt Final Expenditure + Net Exports)* | **Exclude** expenditure on intermediate goods, second-hand goods, and financial securities (shares/debentures). |

## 3. Inflation, Business Cycles, Poverty & Unemployment

### 3.1 Inflation: Concepts, Effects & Complete Typology
- **Inflation:** A sustained, appreciable rise in the general price level of goods and services over time, resulting in a **fall in the purchasing power (real value) of money**.
- **Who Gains & Who Loses During Inflation?**
  - **Gainers:** **Debtors / Borrowers** (repay loans with cheaper money), producers/businessmen, equity shareholders, variable-income groups.
  - **Losers:** **Creditors / Lenders**, **fixed-income earners** (salaried employees, pensioners), bondholders, cash savers.

| Type of Inflation | Definition / Key Characteristics |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Creeping Inflation** | Mild, safe, and desirable for growth: **< 3% per annum** (encourages investment). |
| **Walking / Trotting** | Moderate inflation: **3% to 10% per annum** (warning signal for central bank). |
| **Running Inflation** | Rapid rise: **10% to 20% per annum** (hurts fixed-income and middle class). |
| **Galloping / Hopping** | Very high double- or triple-digit inflation (**20% to 100%+ per year**); severely erodes savings. |
| **Hyperinflation** | Out-of-control price surge (**> 50% per month** per Philip Cagan); currency loses all trust (e.g., Weimar Germany 1923, Zimbabwe 2008, Venezuela). |
| **Demand-Pull Inflation** | **"Too much money chasing too few goods."** Caused by excess Aggregate Demand ($AD > AS$) due to rising income, deficit financing, or cheap credit. |
| **Cost-Push Inflation** | Driven by rising **production costs** ($AS$ shifts left) — higher wages (**Wage-Push**), crude oil shocks, higher input prices, or indirect tax hikes. |
| **Structural Inflation** | Common in developing economies like India; caused by supply bottlenecks, poor storage/cold-chains, cartelization/hoarding, and inelastic food supply. |
| **Bottleneck Inflation** | Coined by **W.W. Rostow**. Occurs when supply falls sharply while demand remains constant (usually at or near full capacity/output). |
| **Headline vs. Core Inflation** | **Headline Inflation:** Total inflation across the entire CPI basket (including food & fuel). **Core Inflation:** Headline inflation **minus volatile Food and Fuel/Energy prices** (measures underlying long-term trend). |
| **Stagflation** | **Stagnation + Inflation:** Simultaneous occurrence of **high inflation, high unemployment, and slow/negative GDP growth** (e.g., 1973 OPEC oil shock). |
| **Deflation** | **Negative inflation rate** — a persistent *fall* in the general price level (increases debt burden, causes recessionary spiral). |
| **Disinflation** | A **slowdown in the rate of inflation** (e.g., inflation drops from 7% to 4% — prices still rise, just more slowly). |
| **Reflation** | Deliberate fiscal/monetary stimulus by government/central bank to **raise prices and revive output** from deflation or recession. |
| **Shrinkflation** | Reducing product weight/quantity/size while keeping the retail price unchanged (e.g., biscuit pack shrinks from 100g to 80g at ₹10). |
| **Skewflation** | Asymmetric price rise concentrated in **one or a few specific items** (e.g., sudden spike in onion or tomato prices) while general prices stay stable. |
| **Inflation Tax (Seigniorage)** | Not a legal tax, but the erosion of real purchasing power of cash held by the public when government prints excess money to finance deficits. |

### 3.2 Business Cycles (Trade Cycles)
Recurring fluctuations in aggregate economic activity (GDP, employment, investment) around the long-term growth trend:
1. **Expansion / Boom:** Rising GDP, high employment, rising wages, strong credit growth.
2. **Peak:** Maximum output capacity reached; inflationary pressures build.
3. **Recession:** Slowdown in economic activity; technically defined as **two consecutive quarters of negative real GDP growth**.
4. **Depression:** Severe, prolonged recession with mass unemployment and deflation (e.g., **Great Depression, 1929–33**).
5. **Trough:** Lowest point of the cycle before recovery begins.
6. **Recovery:** Output, confidence, and employment begin rising again.
- **Recovery Shapes:** **V-shaped** (sharp fall, rapid rebound), **U-shaped** (prolonged trough before recovery), **W-shaped** (double-dip recession), **L-shaped** (stagnant growth after crash), **K-shaped** (unequal recovery where some sectors/rich boom while others lag — e.g., post-COVID divergence).

### 3.4 Poverty Estimation Committees in India & Multidimensional Poverty (MPI)
- **Absolute vs. Relative Poverty:**
  - **Absolute Poverty:** Inability to meet a minimum biological/basic threshold (calories, income, consumption expenditure — measured by **Headcount Ratio**). Used in developing countries like India.
  - **Relative Poverty:** Income disparity compared to the rest of society (measured by **Lorenz Curve** & **Gini Coefficient**).

| **Suresh Tendulkar Committee** | **2009** | **Shifted away from calorie norm**; adopted **Mixed Reference Period (MRP)**; used uniform **urban basket**; included **private health & education** spending. *(2011–12 prices)*. | **₹816 / month** (~₹27/day) <br> **25.7%** | **₹1,000 / month** (~₹33/day) <br> **13.7%** | **21.9%** *(~26.9 crore)* |

- **NITI Aayog National Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI):**
  - **Methodology:** **Alkire–Foster Methodology** (in partnership with OPHI & UNDP).
  - **Formula:** $\text{MPI} = H \times A$, where **$H$ = Headcount Ratio** (% of population multidimensionally poor) and **$A$ = Intensity of Poverty** (average % of deprivations suffered by the poor).
  - **Structure:** **3 Equally Weighted Dimensions** ($1/3$ each) and **12 Indicators** (India added **Maternal Health** and **Bank Account** to the global 10-indicator MPI):
    1. **Health ($1/3$):** Nutrition ($1/6$), Child & Adolescent Mortality ($1/12$), Maternal Health ($1/12$).
    2. **Education ($1/3$):** Years of Schooling ($1/6$), School Attendance ($1/6$).
    3. **Standard of Living ($1/3$):** Cooking Fuel, Sanitation, Drinking Water, Electricity, Housing, Assets, Bank Account ($1/21$ each).
  - **Key Headcount Ratio ($H$) Figures:**
    - **NFHS-4 (2015–16) to NFHS-5 (2019–21):** Dropped from **24.85% $\rightarrow$ 14.96%** (~13.5 crore escaped MPI).
    - **NITI Aayog Discussion Paper (Jan 2024):** Dropped from **29.17% (2013–14) $\rightarrow$ 11.28% (2022–23)** — lifting **~24.82 crore people** out of multidimensional poverty (largest absolute reductions in UP, Bihar, MP, Rajasthan).

### 3.5 Types of Unemployment, Measurement & PLFS (Very High-Frequency SSC Topic)

| Type of Unemployment | Core Concept & Cause | Classic SSC Examples & Keywords |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Disguised (Hidden) Unemployment** | More workers are engaged than actually required; **Marginal Productivity of Labour ($MPL$) is ZERO** (removing excess workers causes no fall in output). | **Indian Agriculture** (e.g., 5 family members working on a small farm that needs only 2). Concept linked to **Joan Robinson**. |
| **Structural Unemployment** | Long-term mismatch between **worker skills/location** and available jobs due to structural/technological shifts. | Handloom weavers displaced by automated textile mills; lack of AI/digital skills. |
| **Frictional Unemployment** | Short-term, **voluntary/transitional** unemployment as workers switch jobs or freshers search for their first job. | Exists even at "full employment"; also called **Search Unemployment**. |
| **Cyclical Unemployment** | Caused by **economic recession / downturns** due to **deficient aggregate demand** (Keynesian concept). | Layoffs in IT, auto, or real estate during the 2008 global crisis or recessions. |
| **Seasonal Unemployment** | Workers are employed only during specific seasons of the year and idle during off-seasons. | Sowing/harvesting farm labourers, hill-station tourism workers, sugar-mill workers. |
| **Underemployment** | *Visible:* Working fewer hours than desired (part-time wanting full-time). *Invisible:* Working in jobs **below one's qualification/skill level**. | An engineering graduate working as a delivery rider or clerk. |
| **Educated Unemployment** | Degree/diploma holders willing to work at prevailing wages fail to find suitable employment. | High among urban youth & graduates in India. |
| **Natural Rate of Unemployment** | Sum of **Frictional + Structural** unemployment when an economy is at full employment (zero cyclical unemployment). | Baseline minimum unemployment in any dynamic economy. |

- **Measurement Approaches in India (Bhagwati Committee on Unemployment, 1973 / NSSO):**
  1. **Usual Status (US — Principal + Subsidiary):** Reference period = **365 days** preceding the survey. Measures **chronic / open long-term unemployment**.
  2. **Current Weekly Status (CWS):** Reference period = **7 days**. A person is unemployed if they did not work even for **1 hour on any single day** during the reference week.
  3. **Current Daily Status (CDS):** Tracks activity status for **each day** of the 7-day reference week in **half-day units**. Considered the **most comprehensive measure** because it captures both open unemployment and **underemployment**.
- **PLFS (Periodic Labour Force Survey):**
  - Launched by **NSSO / NSO (MoSPI)** in **April 2017** (replacing the quinquennial Employment-Unemployment Surveys). Provides **quarterly** urban estimates and **annual** rural+urban estimates.
  - **Key PLFS Ratios:**
    - **LFPR (Labour Force Participation Rate):** $\dfrac{\text{Employed + Unemployed (Labour Force)}}{\text{Total Population (age 15+)}} \times 100$
    - **WPR (Worker Population Ratio):** $\dfrac{\text{Employed Persons}}{\text{Total Population}} \times 100$
    - **UR (Unemployment Rate):** $\dfrac{\text{Unemployed Persons}}{\text{Labour Force}} \times 100$

### 4.3 The 1991 LPG Economic Reforms (New Economic Policy)
- **Background / Crisis:** By June 1991, India faced a severe **Balance of Payments (BoP) crisis** — foreign exchange reserves fell to ~$1.2 billion (barely 2–3 weeks of imports), fiscal deficit hit ~8.4% of GDP, inflation surged above 13%, aggravated by the Gulf War oil shock and collapse of the USSR. India pledged gold to the Bank of England/UBS and sought an **IMF Structural Adjustment Loan**.
- **Architects:** Prime Minister **P.V. Narasimha Rao** and Finance Minister **Dr. Manmohan Singh**.
- **New Industrial Policy:** Announced on **July 24, 1991**, anchored on three pillars (**LPG**):

| Pillar | Key Policy Measures & Exam Highlights |
| :--- | :--- |
| **1. Liberalisation** *(Removing controls & License Raj)* | - **Abolition of Industrial Licensing** except for 18 sensitive industries (progressively reduced to **5 today**: alcohol, cigarettes, hazardous chemicals, industrial explosives, defence/aerospace electronics). <br> - **Abolition of MRTP Act (1969) asset threshold** in 1991; later replaced by **Competition Act, 2002** (**CCI** set up 2003). <br> - **Banking & Tax Reforms:** **Narasimham Committee (1991)** (SLR/CRR cuts, private banks allowed); **Raja Chelliah Committee (1991)** (tax rate rationalization). <br> - **Rupee Devaluation (July 1991)** in two steps (~18–20%) & shift to market-determined exchange rate (**LERMS 1992 $\rightarrow$ Unified Exchange Rate 1993**). |
| **2. Privatisation** *(Reducing public-sector monopoly)* | - Industries reserved exclusively for the **Public Sector reduced from 17 to 8** in 1991, later 3, and **only 2 today** (**Atomic Energy** and **Railway Operations**). <br> - **Disinvestment** of government equity in PSUs (1991–92 onwards; Rangarajan Committee on Disinvestment 1993) and granting **Navratna / Miniratna / Maharatna** autonomy. |
| **3. Globalisation** *(Integrating with world economy)* | - Sharp reduction in import tariffs (**Chelliah Committee**) and dismantling of Quantitative Restrictions (QRs). <br> - Automatic **FDI approval** initially up to **51%** in 34 high-priority industries (later raised to 74%/100% across most sectors). <br> - Replaced restrictive **FERA (1973)** with investor-friendly **FEMA (1999, effective 2000)**. <br> - India became a founding member of the **WTO (Jan 1, 1995)**. |

### 4.4 NITI Aayog (National Institution for Transforming India)
- **Established:** **January 1, 2015** by a **Union Cabinet Resolution**, replacing the 65-year-old Planning Commission.
- **Nature:** **Non-constitutional, non-statutory** executive **Think Tank** of the Government of India.
- **Core Philosophy:** **"Bottom-Up" approach** (replacing Planning Commission's "Top-Down" model); promotes **Cooperative Federalism** (Centre + States as *Team India*) and **Competitive Federalism** (ranking states via indices).
- **Critical Distinction from Planning Commission:** NITI Aayog has **NO power to allocate funds** to Ministries or States (fund allocation rests with the **Ministry of Finance** and **Finance Commission**).

#### Organisational Structure of NITI Aayog

## 5. Money, Monetary Policy & Reserve Bank of India (RBI)

### 5.1 Money: Definition, Legal Tender & Functions

**Money** is anything that is generally accepted as a medium of exchange, a measure of value, a store of value, and a standard of deferred payments.

* **Fiat Money:** Money issued by order of the government/central bank without intrinsic value (e.g., modern currency notes and coins).
* **Fiduciary Money:** Money accepted as a medium of exchange based on trust between payer and payee, not by legal compulsion (e.g., cheques, bank drafts, bills of exchange).
* **Legal Tender Money:** Money that cannot be legally refused in settlement of debt.
  * **Unlimited Legal Tender:** All RBI banknotes (₹2 and above) and the **₹1 currency note** (can be used to pay any amount).
  * **Limited Legal Tender (Coinage Act, 2011):** Coins of **₹1 and above** are legal tender up to **₹1,000**; **50 paise** coins are legal tender up to **₹10** only. *(Coins below 50 paise were demonetized in June 2011).*
* **Plastic Money:** Debit cards, credit cards, prepaid cards.
* **Near Money:** Highly liquid financial assets easily convertible into cash (e.g., Treasury Bills, Fixed Deposits, Commercial Paper).
* **Hot Money:** Short-term foreign capital that moves rapidly across borders seeking higher interest rates or speculative gains (e.g., FPI/FII).
* **Gresham's Law:** *"Bad money drives out good money"* — when two forms of commodity money have the same face value by law but different intrinsic values, people hoard the "good" (higher intrinsic value) money and spend the "bad" money.

#### Functions of Money

| Category | Function | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Primary Functions** | **1. Medium of Exchange** | Eliminates the problem of *double coincidence of wants* in the barter system. |
| | **2. Measure of Value (Unit of Account)** | Expresses the value of all goods and services in a single monetary unit. |
| **Secondary Functions** | **3. Store of Value** | Allows purchasing power to be stored for future use (wealth retention). |
| | **4. Standard of Deferred Payments** | Facilitates borrowing, lending, and future contractual payments. |
| | **5. Transfer of Value** | Enables easy transfer of purchasing power across places and persons. |
| **Contingent Functions** | Basis of Credit, Distribution of National Income, Equalization of Marginal Utilities | Supports modern banking credit creation and factor payments (rent, wages, interest, profit). |

### 5.2 Money Supply Measures ($M_0, M_1, M_2, M_3, M_4$)

Money supply is a **stock concept** (measured at a specific point in time), published by the **RBI** (originally adopted on the recommendations of the Second Working Group on Money Supply, 1977).

| Measure | Popular Name | Exact Formula / Components | Key Exam Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$M_0$** | **Reserve Money / High-Powered Money / Monetary Base** | $\text{Currency in Circulation} + \text{Bankers' Deposits with RBI} + \text{'Other' Deposits with RBI}$ | Controlled directly by RBI; foundation for credit creation. |
| **$M_1$** | **Narrow Money** | $\text{Currency with Public} + \text{Demand Deposits with Banks (CASA)} + \text{'Other' Deposits with RBI}$ | **Most liquid** monetary aggregate; excludes inter-bank deposits and cash held by banks. |
| **$M_2$** | — | $M_1 + \text{Savings Deposits with Post Office Savings Banks}$ | Narrower than $M_3$; slightly less liquid than $M_1$. |
| **$M_3$** | **Broad Money** *(Aggregate Monetary Resources)* | $M_1 + \text{Time Deposits (FD/RD) with the Banking System}$ | **Most commonly used** measure of money supply in India. |
| **$M_4$** | — | $M_3 + \text{All Deposits with Post Office Savings Organizations}$ *(excluding NSC)* | **Least liquid** and widest measure. |

* **Liquidity Order:** $M_1 > M_2 > M_3 > M_4$
* **Components Note:**
  * *Currency with Public* = Currency in Circulation (notes + coins) $-$ Cash in hand with banks.
  * *'Other' Deposits with RBI* = Deposits held by RBI on behalf of foreign central banks, IMF, World Bank, DFIs, etc. (excludes Govt. of India & commercial banks' deposits).
  * **National Savings Certificates (NSC)** are **excluded** from $M_4$.
* **Money Multiplier ($m$):**
  $$m = \frac{\text{Broad Money } (M_3)}{\text{Reserve Money } (M_0)} = \frac{1 + c}{c + r}$$
  where $c$ = Currency-to-Deposit Ratio and $r$ = Reserve-to-Deposit Ratio. In simplified banking theory (assuming zero cash leakage, $c = 0$):
  $$m = \frac{1}{\text{CRR}} \quad \text{or} \quad \frac{1}{\text{LRR}}$$
* **New Monetary Aggregates (Y.V. Reddy Working Group, 1998):** Replaced post office deposits with term borrowings and Certificates of Deposit: $NM_1$, $NM_2$, $NM_3$ (excludes foreign currency non-resident repatriable deposits) and Liquidity Aggregates ($L_1, L_2, L_3$).

### 5.3 Reserve Bank of India (RBI): History, Structure & Note Issue

#### Historical Milestones
* **Recommendation:** **Hilton Young Commission (1926)** (formally the *Royal Commission on Indian Currency and Finance*) recommended establishing a central bank. *(Dr. B.R. Ambedkar's book "The Problem of the Rupee: Its Origin and Its Solution", 1923, also heavily influenced the Commission).*
* **Statutory Backing:** **Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934**.
* **Establishment:** **April 1, 1935** (initially as a private shareholders' bank with a paid-up share capital of ₹5 crore).
* **Headquarters Shift:** Initially in **Calcutta**; permanently shifted to **Mumbai** in **1937**.
* **Nationalization:** **January 1, 1949** (under the *Reserve Bank (Transfer to Public Ownership) Act, 1948*), making it 100% Government-owned.
* **RBI Accounting Year:** Changed from July–June to **April–March** starting FY 2020–21 (per **Bimal Jalan Committee** recommendations) to align with the Government's fiscal year.

#### Organizational Structure
* **Central Board of Directors (up to 21 members):**
  * **1 Governor** + up to **4 Deputy Governors** (appointed for up to 5 years, eligible for reappointment).
  * **2 Government Officials** (from Ministry of Finance).
  * **10 Directors** nominated by the Central Government from various fields.
  * **4 Directors** nominated from the 4 Local Boards (**Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, New Delhi**).

| **1st Indian Governor** | **Sir C.D. Deshmukh** | 1943–1949 (3rd Governor; oversaw RBI's nationalization in 1949) |
| **Longest-serving Governor** | **Benegal Rama Rau** | 1949–1957 (~7.5 years) |

#### Currency Note Issue & Printing Infrastructure
* **Sole Right of Note Issue:** **Section 22 of RBI Act, 1934** empowers RBI to issue banknotes of denominations ₹2, ₹5, ₹10, ₹20, ₹50, ₹100, ₹200, ₹500, ₹2,000 *(Note: ₹2,000 note was introduced in Nov 2016 and withdrawn from circulation in May 2023 under the "Clean Note Policy", though it remains legal tender).*
* **Minimum Reserve System (MRS):** Adopted in **1956** (replacing the earlier Proportional Reserve System).
  * RBI must maintain a minimum backing of **₹200 crore** at all times:
    * **Gold (coin & bullion):** Minimum **₹115 crore**
    * **Foreign Currency / Foreign Securities:** Minimum **₹85 crore**
* **Exam Exception — ₹1 Note & All Coins:**
  * Issued by the **Ministry of Finance (Government of India)** under the **Coinage Act, 2011** (earlier Currency Ordinance, 1940 & Coinage Act, 1906), **NOT** by the RBI.
  * The **₹1 currency note** bears the signature of the **Finance Secretary** of India.
  * All notes of **₹2 and above** bear the signature of the **RBI Governor**.
  * RBI acts as the **distributing agent** for ₹1 notes and coins on behalf of the Government.
* **4 Currency Printing Presses in India:**
  1. **Nashik** (Maharashtra) — *SPMCIL* (Govt. of India)
  2. **Dewas** (Madhya Pradesh) — *SPMCIL* (Govt. of India)
  3. **Mysuru** (Karnataka) — *BRBNMPL* (Wholly-owned RBI subsidiary)
  4. **Salboni** (West Bengal) — *BRBNMPL* (Wholly-owned RBI subsidiary)
* **4 Government Mints (Coins — all under SPMCIL):** **Mumbai, Kolkata, Hyderabad, Noida**. *(Paper Mill: Hoshangabad, MP — Security Paper Mill; Bank Note Paper Mill India Pvt Ltd in Mysuru).*

### 5.4 Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) — 2016 Amendment

* **Origin & Legal Basis:** Recommended by the **Urjit Patel Committee (2014)**; institutionalized via the **Finance Act, 2016** amending **Section 45ZB of the RBI Act, 1934** (Flexible Inflation Targeting Framework). First MPC met in October 2016.
* **Composition (6 Members):**
  * **3 RBI Members:** RBI Governor (*ex-officio* Chairperson) + Deputy Governor in charge of Monetary Policy + 1 RBI Officer nominated by the Central Board.
  * **3 External Members:** Appointed by the Central Government for a **4-year term** (**not eligible for reappointment**).
* **Voting & Quorum:**
  * Quorum = **4 members**.
  * Decisions taken by **majority vote** (each member has 1 vote).
  * In case of a **tie**, the **RBI Governor has a second/casting vote** *(Governor has NO veto power)*.
* **Frequency:** Must meet at least **4 times a year** (currently meets **bi-monthly**, i.e., 6 times a year).
* **Inflation Target:** **CPI (Combined)** target of **4% ± 2%** (tolerance band: **2% to 6%**).
  * **Failure Condition:** If average CPI inflation remains **above 6% or below 2% for 3 consecutive quarters**, RBI fails its mandate and must submit a written report to the Central Government explaining the reasons, remedial actions, and estimated time to return to target.

### 5.5 Quantitative & Qualitative Monetary Policy Tools

#### A. Quantitative (General) Instruments — Control *Volume* of Credit

| Tool | Statutory Basis & Mechanism | Purpose / Effect | Current Snapshot *(Oct 2026 MPC)* & Key Facts |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Repo Rate** *(Repurchase Option)* | Rate at which **RBI lends short-term funds** (overnight to 14 days) to commercial banks against **collateral (G-Secs)** under LAF. | Policy benchmark rate. ↑ Repo → borrowing costlier → **controls inflation** (Dear Money Policy). ↓ Repo → **boosts growth** (Cheap Money Policy). | **5.50%** *(Stance: Calibrated Tightening)* |
| **Fixed Reverse Repo Rate** | Rate at which **RBI borrows/absorbs liquidity** from banks against G-Sec collateral on a fixed basis. | Absorbs excess liquidity from banking system. | **3.35%** *(Largely superseded by SDF as LAF floor since April 2022)* |
| **Standing Deposit Facility (SDF)** | Introduced **April 2022** (Urjit Patel Committee idea, amended Sec 17 of RBI Act). Allows RBI to absorb overnight liquidity **WITHOUT collateral**. | Acts as the **floor of the LAF corridor** (fixed **25 bps below Repo Rate**). | **5.25%** |
| **Marginal Standing Facility (MSF)** | Introduced **2011**. Penal overnight borrowing window for Scheduled Commercial Banks when inter-bank liquidity dries up; banks can dip up to **2% into their SLR** securities. | Acts as the **ceiling of the LAF corridor** (fixed **25 bps above Repo Rate**). | **5.75%** |
| **Bank Rate** | **Section 49 of RBI Act, 1934**. Rate at which RBI buys/rediscounts bills of exchange or lends **long-term without collateral**. | Aligned with MSF rate; acts as **penal rate** on banks for CRR/SLR shortfalls. | **5.75%** |
| **Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR)** | **Section 42(1) of RBI Act, 1934**. Minimum % of **NDTL** (Net Demand & Time Liabilities) that banks must keep as **cash with RBI**. | Direct liquidity tool. Banks earn **0% interest** on CRR balances. No statutory floor/ceiling since 2007 amendment. | **3.00%** |
| **Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR)** | **Section 24 of Banking Regulation Act, 1949**. Minimum % of **NDTL** banks must maintain with **themselves** in liquid assets (**Cash, Gold, or Approved Govt. Securities**). | Ensures bank solvency & creates captive demand for G-Secs. Statutory ceiling = **40%**. | **18.00%** |
| **Open Market Operations (OMO)** | Outright **purchase or sale of Government Securities (G-Secs)** by RBI in the open market. | **Buy G-Secs** → injects liquidity. **Sell G-Secs** → absorbs liquidity. | Permanent liquidity management tool (unlike LAF, which is temporary). |
| **LTRO / TLTRO** | **Long-Term Repo Operations** (1–3 years tenor) at the prevailing Repo Rate against G-Secs. | Lowers cost of long-term funds for banks; **TLTRO** targets specific stressed sectors (NBFCs, corporates). | Introduced by RBI in Feb 2020. |
| **Market Stabilization Scheme (MSS)** | Introduced **April 2004**. RBI issues special **MSS T-Bills / Dated Securities** on behalf of Govt. (proceeds parked in separate Govt. account, cannot be spent). | **Sterilizes** excess domestic liquidity caused by large foreign capital inflows. | Govt. bears the interest cost (unlike Reverse Repo, where RBI bears cost). |

> **LAF Corridor Summary:** $\text{SDF (Floor: Repo } - 0.25\%) < \text{Policy Repo Rate} < \text{MSF (Ceiling: Repo } + 0.25\%)$

#### B. Qualitative (Selective) Instruments — Control *Direction* of Credit

| Tool | Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Margin Requirements** | Difference between market value of collateral and loan amount ($100\% - \text{LTV}$). Increasing margin restricts speculative credit (e.g., against stocks/commodities). |
| **Moral Suasion** | Informal persuasion, advisories, and meetings by RBI urging banks to follow desired lending norms without legal compulsion. |
| **Rationing of Credit** | Fixing upper ceilings on credit limits for specific sectors or categories of borrowers. |
| **Consumer Credit Regulation** | Adjusting minimum down payments and maximum EMI tenors for consumer durables. |
| **Direct Action** | Penalizing non-compliant banks (fines, refusal of rediscounting, or cancelling banking licence). |
| **Priority Sector Lending (PSL)** | Mandates banks to allocate a specified % of **ANBC** (Adjusted Net Bank Credit) or **CEOBSE** (whichever is higher) to vital/underserved sectors (Agriculture, MSMEs, Export Credit, Education, Housing, Social Infrastructure, Renewable Energy, Weaker Sections). |

| :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| **Domestic Commercial Banks & Foreign Banks (≥20 branches)** | **40%** | **18%** *(incl. 10% Small & Marginal Farmers)* | **7.5%** | **12%** |
| **Regional Rural Banks (RRBs)** | **75%** | **18%** *(incl. 10% SMF)* | **7.5%** | **15%** |
| **Small Finance Banks (SFBs)** | **60%** *(harmonized from 75%)* | **18%** *(incl. 10% SMF)* | **7.5%** | **12%** |

## 6. Indian Banking Structure, Financial Markets & Reforms

### 6.1 Banking History & Landmark Milestones

* **First Bank in India:** **Bank of Hindustan (1770)**, established in Calcutta by Alexander & Co. *(Failed in 1832).*
* **First Bank with Limited Liability managed by Indians:** **Oudh Commercial Bank (1881)**, Faizabad.
* **First Swadeshi Bank (wholly owned & managed by Indians):** **Punjab National Bank (1894)**, Lahore (co-founded by Lala Lajpat Rai).
* **Central Bank of India (1911):** First Indian commercial bank wholly owned and managed by Indians (founded by Sir Sorabji Pochkhanawala).
* **Evolution of SBI (Presidency Banks → Imperial Bank → SBI):**
  * Three Presidency Banks: **Bank of Calcutta (1806; renamed Bank of Bengal in 1809)**, **Bank of Bombay (1840)**, **Bank of Madras (1843)**.
  * Merged on **January 27, 1921** to form the **Imperial Bank of India** (acted as quasi-central bank until 1935).
  * Nationalized on **July 1, 1955** as **State Bank of India (SBI)** under the SBI Act, 1955 on the recommendation of the **A.D. Gorewala Committee** (*All-India Rural Credit Survey Committee*).
  * SBI Subsidiaries (1959 Act): 8 associate banks created → reduced to 5 → all 5 Associate Banks + **Bharatiya Mahila Bank** merged into SBI on **April 1, 2017**.
* **Bank Nationalization:**
  * **Phase I (July 19, 1969):** **14** major private commercial banks with deposits ≥ **₹50 crore** were nationalized *(4th Five-Year Plan, PM Indira Gandhi)*.
  * **Phase II (April 15, 1980):** **6** more private commercial banks with deposits ≥ **₹200 crore** were nationalized *(New Bank of India later merged into PNB in 1993)*.
* **Mega-Merger of PSBs (2019–2020):** Reduced the number of Public Sector Banks from 27 (in 2017) to **12 PSBs** (effective **April 1, 2020**):
  * *Vijaya Bank & Dena Bank* → **Bank of Baroda** (2019)
  * *Oriental Bank of Commerce & United Bank of India* → **Punjab National Bank (PNB)**
  * *Syndicate Bank* → **Canara Bank**
  * *Andhra Bank & Corporation Bank* → **Union Bank of India**
  * *Allahabad Bank* → **Indian Bank**
* **Lead Bank Scheme (1969):** Introduced by RBI on recommendations of **D.R. Gadgil Study Group** and **F.K.F. Nariman Committee** (*"Area Approach"*: each district assigned to a lead bank to coordinate rural credit).
* **Regional Rural Banks (RRBs):**
  * Established on **October 2, 1975** (via RRB Ordinance, 1975; later **RRB Act, 1976**) on recommendation of the **M. Narasimham Working Group (1975)**.
  * **First RRB:** **Prathama Bank** (Moradabad, UP; sponsored by Syndicate Bank).
  * **Ownership Structure:** **50% Central Govt : 15% State Govt : 35% Sponsor Bank**.
  * *Exam Exception:* **Goa and Sikkim** have **no RRBs**.

### 6.4 NPCI, Digital Payments, Asset Quality & Deposit Insurance

#### NPCI & Digital Payment Systems
* **NPCI (National Payments Corporation of India):** Established in **2008** (HQ: Mumbai) as a not-for-profit umbrella entity by **RBI & IBA** under the **Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007**.
* **Major NPCI Products:** **UPI** (2016), **RuPay** (2012), **IMPS** (2010 — instant 24×7 transfer up to ₹5 lakh), **BHIM** (2016), **AePS** (Aadhaar Enabled Payment System), **NETC FASTag**, **NACH**, **UPI 123PAY** (feature phones), **UPI Lite**.
* **NEFT vs RTGS (Both operated by RBI, available 24×7×365):**
  * **NEFT (National Electronic Funds Transfer):** Half-hourly batch settlement; **No minimum or maximum limit** (starts at ₹1).
  * **RTGS (Real Time Gross Settlement):** Instantaneous one-to-one settlement for high-value transactions; **Minimum limit = ₹2 lakh**, **No upper limit**.
* **CBDC (e₹ — Digital Rupee):** Legal tender in digital form issued by RBI (piloted in Wholesale `e₹-W` and Retail `e₹-R` in late 2022).

#### NPA, Recovery Laws & Deposit Insurance
* **NPA (Non-Performing Asset):** A loan or advance where interest and/or principal installment remains **overdue for more than 90 days**.
  * *Agricultural Loan Exception:* Overdue for **2 crop seasons** (short-duration crops) or **1 crop season** (long-duration crops).
  * *Categories:* **Sub-standard** (NPA ≤ 12 months) → **Doubtful** (NPA > 12 months) → **Loss Asset** (identified as uncollectible).
* **SARFAESI Act, 2002:** *Securitisation and Reconstruction of Financial Assets and Enforcement of Security Interest Act* — empowers banks to seize and auction secured collateral of defaulters **without court intervention**. *(Does NOT apply to agricultural land or loans below ₹1 lakh).* Led to **ARCs** (Asset Reconstruction Companies) and **CERSAI**.
* **IBC, 2016 (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code):** Time-bound resolution (**180 days + 90 days = 270 days**, max 330 days incl. litigation).
  * Adjudicating Authorities: **NCLT** (for companies/LLPs) and **DRT** (for individuals/partnerships). Regulator: **IBBI** (Insolvency and Bankruptcy Board of India).
* **DICGC (Deposit Insurance and Credit Guarantee Corporation):** Est. **1978** (merger of DIC 1962 & CGCI 1971), wholly-owned RBI subsidiary. Insures bank deposits up to **₹5 lakh per depositor per bank** (raised from ₹1 lakh in Feb 2020) covering principal + interest across savings, current, FD, and RD accounts.

### 6.5 Money Market vs Capital Market & Regulators

| Feature | Money Market | Capital Market |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Maturity / Tenure** | **Short-term** ($\le 1$ year; up to 364 days) | **Long-term** ($> 1$ year; equity & long-term debt) |
| **Regulator** | **RBI** | **SEBI** |
| **Key Instruments** | T-Bills, Commercial Paper (CP), Certificate of Deposit (CD), Call/Notice/Term Money, Commercial Bills, TREPS | Equity Shares, Preference Shares, Debentures, Corporate & Govt. Bonds, Mutual Funds, Derivatives |

#### Key Money Market Instruments
* **Treasury Bills (T-Bills):** Short-term sovereign debt issued **ONLY by the Central Government** (State Govts cannot issue T-Bills). Issued at a **discount** and redeemed at face value (**zero-coupon**). Tenors: **91 days, 182 days, 364 days** (Min: ₹25,000). *(Cash Management Bills / CMBs have maturity < 91 days).*
* **Call, Notice & Term Money (Inter-bank market):**
  * **Call Money:** **1 day** (overnight)
  * **Notice Money:** **2 to 14 days**
  * **Term Money:** **15 days to 1 year**
* **Commercial Paper (CP):** Unsecured money market promissory note issued by high-rated corporates/PDs/AFIs (7 days to 1 year; min ₹5 lakh).
* **Certificate of Deposit (CD):** Negotiable instrument issued by Scheduled Commercial Banks (7 days to 1 year; min ₹5 lakh) and All-India FIs (1 to 3 years).

#### Capital Market Institutions & Jargon
* **SEBI (Securities and Exchange Board of India):** Set up in **1988** (non-statutory); given statutory powers on **Jan 30, 1992** via **SEBI Act, 1992**; HQ: **Mumbai**.
* **BSE (Bombay Stock Exchange):** Est. **1875** (*Native Share & Stock Brokers' Association*) — **Oldest stock exchange in Asia**. Benchmark Index: **SENSEX** (Sensitive Index of **30** companies; base year 1978–79 = 100).
* **NSE (National Stock Exchange):** Est. **1992** (on **Pherwani Committee** recommendation; trading began 1994). Benchmark Index: **NIFTY 50** (**50** companies; base year 1995 = 1000).
* **Primary vs Secondary Market:**
  * **Primary Market (New Issue Market):** Direct sale of securities by issuers via **IPO** (Initial Public Offering — first-time public listing), **FPO** (Follow-on Public Offer — additional shares by listed firm), Rights Issue, Private Placement.
  * **Secondary Market:** Trading of already-issued securities among investors on exchanges (BSE/NSE).
* **Market Terms:**
  * **Bull:** Optimistic investor expecting prices to **rise** (buys now to sell higher later).
  * **Bear:** Pessimistic investor expecting prices to **fall** (sells now/short-sells to buy back lower).
  * **Stag:** Short-term speculator who applies in an **IPO** solely to sell immediately on listing day for a quick premium.
  * **Demat Account:** Dematerialized electronic account holding shares, managed by Depositories — **NSDL** (1996) and **CDSL** (1999).
  * **Mutual Funds:** Pooled investment funds regulated by **SEBI** (AMFI is industry body); e.g., Equity, Debt, Hybrid, ETFs, **ELSS** (tax-saving under Sec 80C).
* **Insurance & Pension Regulators:**
  * **IRDAI** *(Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority of India)*: Est. **1999** (IRDA Act, 1999; **Malhotra Committee**); HQ: **Hyderabad**.
  * **PFRDA** *(Pension Fund Regulatory and Development Authority)*: Est. **2003** (statutory via PFRDA Act, 2013); HQ: **New Delhi**; regulates **NPS** & **APY**.

### 7.2 Revenue vs Capital Budget & All 5 Deficit Formulas

| Budget Head | Receipts (Inflow) | Expenditure (Outflow) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Revenue Budget** *(Short-term, recurring; NO asset/liability change)* | **Revenue Receipts:** Neither create liability nor reduce assets.<br>• *Tax Revenue:* Direct + Indirect taxes.<br>• *Non-Tax Revenue:* Interest, PSU dividends, fees, fines, grants. | **Revenue Expenditure:** Neither creates assets nor reduces liability.<br>• Salaries, pensions, interest payments *(largest item)*, subsidies, defence revenue, grants to states. |
| **Capital Budget** *(Long-term; changes asset/liability position)* | **Capital Receipts:** Either create liability OR reduce assets.<br>• *Debt-creating:* Market loans, external loans, small savings.<br>• *Non-debt:* **Disinvestment** proceeds, recovery of loans. | **Capital Expenditure (CapEx):** Creates physical/financial assets OR reduces liability.<br>• Highways, railways, hospitals, defence equipment, loans to states, loan repayments. *(Highest economic multiplier).* |

#### All 5 Deficit Formulas (Crucial for SSC!)

1. **Revenue Deficit:**
   $$\text{Revenue Deficit} = \text{Revenue Expenditure} - \text{Revenue Receipts}$$
   *(Indicates government is dissaving / borrowing to meet day-to-day consumption).*
2. **Effective Revenue Deficit (introduced FY 2011–12):**
   $$\text{Effective Revenue Deficit} = \text{Revenue Deficit} - \text{Grants for Creation of Capital Assets}$$
3. **Fiscal Deficit:**
   $$\text{Fiscal Deficit} = \text{Total Expenditure} - (\text{Revenue Receipts} + \text{Non-Debt Capital Receipts})$$
   *(Equals the **total borrowing requirement** of the government from all sources).*
4. **Primary Deficit:**
   $$\text{Primary Deficit} = \text{Fiscal Deficit} - \text{Interest Payments}$$
   *(Shows current year's borrowing excluding legacy debt-servicing; zero Primary Deficit means borrowing is only to pay past interest).*
5. **Monetized Deficit:**
   $$\text{Monetized Deficit} = \text{Net RBI Credit to Central Government}$$
   *(Borrowing directly from RBI via printing currency / ad-hoc T-bills; automatic monetization was abolished in 1997 and replaced by Ways & Means Advances — WMA).*

#### FRBM Act, 2003
* **Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management (FRBM) Act, 2003** (effective July 2004): Mandated institutional fiscal discipline — originally targeted **0% Revenue Deficit** and **3% Fiscal Deficit** of GDP.
* **N.K. Singh FRBM Review Committee (2017):** Recommended a **60% Debt-to-GDP target** (**40% Centre + 20% States**), 3% Fiscal Deficit glide path, and an **Escape Clause** (±0.5% deviation allowed during war, national calamity, or severe structural reforms).

### 7.5 Taxation & Goods and Services Tax (GST)

#### Types of Taxation Systems
* **Progressive Tax:** Tax rate **increases** as income rises (reduces inequality; e.g., *Income Tax*).
* **Regressive Tax:** Effective tax burden (% of income) is **higher on the poor** than the rich (e.g., *Indirect taxes / GST* on essentials).
* **Proportional Tax (Flat Tax):** Tax rate remains **constant** regardless of income level.
* **Digressive Tax:** Tax rate is progressive up to a certain income limit, after which it becomes **flat/proportional**.
* **Laffer Curve:** Inverted-U curve showing that beyond an optimal tax rate, higher tax rates **reduce** total tax revenue due to disincentives and evasion.
* **Tobin Tax:** Tax on short-term spot currency conversions to curb speculative volatility.

#### Direct vs Indirect Taxes

| Feature | Direct Tax | Indirect Tax |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Incidence & Impact** | Falls on the **same person** (cannot be shifted). | **Shifted** to the final consumer. |
| **Nature** | Generally **Progressive**. | Generally **Regressive**. |
| **Administering Board** | **CBDT** *(Central Board of Direct Taxes)* | **CBIC** *(Central Board of Indirect Taxes & Customs)* |
| **Examples** | Income Tax, Corporate Tax, Capital Gains Tax, Securities Transaction Tax (STT) | GST, Customs Duty, Central Excise *(on petroleum/liquor)*, Stamp Duty |

#### Goods and Services Tax (GST)
* **Concept:** Destination-based, multi-stage **Value Added Tax (VAT)** ("One Nation, One Tax"). Removes cascading effect (*tax on tax*) via Input Tax Credit (ITC).
* **Committees & Firsts:** Recommended by **Vijay Kelkar Task Force (2003)**; Empowered Committee chaired by **Asim Dasgupta**.
  * **1st Country in World:** **France (1954)**.
  * **1st State in India to ratify GST Bill:** **Assam** (followed by Bihar).
* **Constitutional Basis:** **101st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2016** (122nd Amendment Bill).
  * **Article 279A:** **GST Council**
  * **Article 246A:** Concurrent power of Parliament & State Legislatures to legislate on GST
  * **Article 269A:** Levy & collection of **IGST** on inter-state trade
* **Launch Date:** **July 1, 2017** (midnight launch in Parliament).
* **GST Council (Art 279A):**
  * **Chairperson:** Union Finance Minister; Members: Union MoS (Finance) + State Finance/Taxation Ministers.
  * **Voting:** Every decision requires a **3/4th (75%) majority** of weighted votes cast (**Centre = 1/3rd weightage**, **All States combined = 2/3rd weightage**).
* **4 GST Components:**
  1. **CGST:** Collected by Centre on **intra-state** supplies.
  2. **SGST:** Collected by State on **intra-state** supplies.
  3. **UTGST:** Collected by UTs without legislature (Andaman & Nicobar, Chandigarh, Dadra & Nagar Haveli and Daman & Diu, Ladakh, Lakshadweep).
  4. **IGST:** Collected by **Centre** on **inter-state** supplies & imports, then apportioned to the **destination/consuming State**.
* **GST Slabs — Original vs Rationalized Structure:**
  * *Original 2017–2025 Structure (often tested in older SSC PYQs):* **0% (Exempt), 5%, 12%, 18%, 28%** (+ Compensation Cess on sin/luxury goods; special **0.25%** on rough diamonds, **3%** on gold/silver/jewellery).
  * *Rationalized Structure (56th GST Council Overhaul):* Consolidated into **0% (Nil)** *(incl. individual health & life insurance, rotis/parathas, UHT milk, paneer, school supplies)*, **5% (Merit rate)** *(absorbed most 12% items + daily-use soaps, toothpaste, agri machinery)*, **18% (Standard rate)** *(cement, small cars & motorcycles ≤350cc, consumer durables)*, and **40% (Luxury/Demerit rate)** *(replaced 28% + cess on SUVs, >350cc bikes, tobacco, pan masala, aerated drinks, online gaming/casinos)*.
* **Items Outside GST:**
  1. **Alcohol for human consumption** *(permanently excluded by Constitution — State Excise + VAT)*.
  2. **5 Petroleum Products** (Crude oil, Petrol, Diesel, ATF, Natural Gas — outside GST until GST Council recommends a date).
  3. **Electricity** & **Real Estate (sale of land/completed buildings)**.

## 8. Balance of Payments, Foreign Trade, PSUs, International Orgs & Schemes

### 8.1 Balance of Payments (BoP) & Foreign Exchange

**Balance of Payments (BoP)** is a systematic record of all economic transactions between **residents** and **non-residents** of a country during a given year, prepared on **double-entry bookkeeping** (Credit `+`, Debit `−`; always balances in accounting terms).

| Account | Sub-Components | Examples & Exam Facts |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Current Account** *(Flow of goods, services, income, transfers)* | **a) Visible Trade (Merchandise / Balance of Trade — BoT)** | Exports − Imports of physical goods. *(India traditionally runs a **Trade Deficit**).* |
| | **b) Invisibles** | **Services** (IT/software, tourism — India has surplus), **Primary Income** (profits, dividends, interest), **Secondary Income** (private **remittances** — *India is #1 globally*, grants, gifts). |
| **2. Capital Account** *(Changes in assets & liabilities)* | **a) Foreign Investment (Non-Debt)** | **FDI** ($\ge 10\%$ equity, long-term, management control, stable) vs **FPI / FII** ($< 10\%$ equity, stock/bond markets, volatile/"hot money"). |
| | **b) Loans & Borrowings (Debt)** | External Commercial Borrowings (**ECBs**), sovereign/bilateral loans. |
| | **c) Banking Capital** | NRI deposits (NRE, FCNR accounts). |
| **3. Official Reserve Account** | Change in **RBI Forex Reserves** | Balances any Current + Capital surplus/deficit. |

#### Exchange Rate Concepts & Reforms
* **Devaluation vs Depreciation:**
  * **Devaluation:** Deliberate downward adjustment of currency value by the **government/central bank** under a **fixed** exchange rate *(India devalued Rupee in **1949**, **1966**, and **July 1991**)*.
  * **Depreciation:** Market-driven fall in currency value due to demand–supply forces under a **floating** exchange rate.
  * *(Opposite: **Revaluation** = official increase; **Appreciation** = market-driven increase).*
* **Rupee Convertibility:**
  * **Full Current Account Convertibility:** Achieved in **August 1994** (IMF Article VIII).
  * **Capital Account Convertibility:** **Partial** in India; roadmap examined by **S.S. Tarapore Committee I (1997) & II (2006)**.
* **FERA (1973) → FEMA (1999):**
  * **FERA (Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1973):** Strict, restrictive law treating violations as **criminal offences** ("conserve forex").
  * **FEMA (Foreign Exchange Management Act, 1999; effective June 1, 2000):** Replaced FERA; treats violations as **civil offences** ("manage forex & facilitate external trade").
* **4 Components of India's Forex Reserves (held by RBI):**
  1. **Foreign Currency Assets (FCA)** *(Largest component)*
  2. **Gold Reserves**
  3. **Special Drawing Rights (SDRs)** with IMF
  4. **Reserve Tranche Position (RTP)** in the IMF
