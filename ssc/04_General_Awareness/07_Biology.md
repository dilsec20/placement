## 1. Cell Biology: Structure, Organelles & Cell Division

The **cell** is the fundamental structural, functional, and biological unit of all living organisms. The study of cell structure and function is called **Cytology** (modern term: **Cell Biology**).

### 1.1 Discovery Timeline & Cell Theory

| Year | Scientist | Key Discovery / Contribution | SSC Exam Fact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1665** | **Robert Hooke** | Observed dead cork cells (*Quercus suber*) under a self-designed compound microscope; coined the term **"Cell"** (*cellula* = little room) | Published in his book ***Micrographia*** (1665); saw only empty cell walls |
| **1674** | **Anton van Leeuwenhoek** | First observed **living cells** (bacteria, protozoa, spermatozoa, RBCs) using an improved single-lens microscope | Called them *animalcules*; known as **Father of Microbiology** |
| **1831** | **Robert Brown** | Discovered the **Nucleus** inside orchid root cells | Key milestone distinguishing eukaryotic organization |
| **1838** | **Matthias Schleiden** (Botanist) | Stated that all plants are composed of cells | Co-founder of **Cell Theory** |
| **1839** | **Theodor Schwann** (Zoologist) | Stated that all animals are composed of cells; noted animal cells have a thin outer plasma membrane and lack a cell wall | Co-founder of **Cell Theory** (*Cellula-e-cellula* was added later) |
| **1839** | **J. E. Purkinje** | Coined the term **Protoplasm** for the living fluid content of the cell | Earlier called *sarcode* by Dujardin (1835); Huxley called it *"Physical basis of life"* |
| **1855** | **Rudolf Virchow** | Expanded Cell Theory with ***"Omnis cellula-e-cellula"*** | Means all new cells arise only from **pre-existing cells** |

#### Core Tenets of Modern Cell Theory
1. All living organisms are composed of one or more cells and their products.
2. The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life.
3. All cells arise from pre-existing cells (Virchow).
4. Cells contain hereditary information (DNA) passed from parent to daughter cells.

> **Exam-Tested Exceptions to Cell Theory:**
> - **Viruses:** Acellular entities; behave as inert crystals outside a host and replicate only inside living host cells.
> - **Mature Mammalian RBCs & Phloem Sieve Tube Elements:** Lack a nucleus at maturity yet perform vital physiological functions.
> - **Coenocytic Fungi (e.g., *Rhizopus*) & *Vaucheria*:** Multinucleated without cross-walls (syncytial/coenocytic condition).

#### Cell Size & Shape Extremes
- **Smallest living cell:** *Mycoplasma gallisepticum* / **PPLO** (Pleuropneumonia-Like Organism), size $\approx 0.1\text{–}0.3\ \mu\text{m}$; lacks a cell wall and can survive without oxygen.
- **Largest single cell:** **Ostrich egg** ($\approx 130\text{–}170\text{ mm}$).
- **Longest animal cell:** **Neuron (Nerve cell)** (up to $\approx 1\text{ m}$).
- **Longest plant cell:** Bast fibre of **Ramie (*Boehmeria nivea*)** / Jute.
- **Human cells:** Largest = **Ovum** (egg cell); Smallest = **Sperm cell** (head $\approx 4\text{–}5\ \mu\text{m}$; mature RBC $\approx 7\ \mu\text{m}$ in diameter).

### 1.2 Prokaryotic vs Eukaryotic Cells

| Feature | Prokaryotic Cell | Eukaryotic Cell |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Representative Organisms** | Bacteria, Cyanobacteria (BGA), *Mycoplasma*, Archaea | Protists, Fungi, Plants, Animals |
| **Cell Size** | Small ($0.1\text{–}5\ \mu\text{m}$) | Larger ($10\text{–}100\ \mu\text{m}$) |
| **Nuclear Membrane & Nucleolus** | **Absent**; genetic material lies free in cytoplasm as **Nucleoid** (incipient nucleus) | **Present**; true membrane-bound nucleus with nucleolus |
| **DNA / Chromosomes** | Single, **circular**, double-stranded DNA; **naked** (no histone proteins) | Multiple, **linear** dsDNA molecules associated with **histone proteins** |
| **Extra-chromosomal DNA** | **Plasmids** (small circular DNA) often present | Present inside **Mitochondria** and **Plastids** |
| **Membrane-bound Organelles** | **Absent** (no mitochondria, ER, Golgi, lysosomes, plastids) | **Present** (Mitochondria, ER, Golgi, Lysosomes, Plastids in plants, etc.) |
| **Ribosomes** | **70S** ($50\text{S} + 30\text{S}$) free in cytoplasm | **80S** ($60\text{S} + 40\text{S}$) in cytoplasm/ER; **70S** inside mitochondria & chloroplasts |
| **Cell Wall** | Present (except *Mycoplasma*); made of **Peptidoglycan (Murein)** in bacteria | Present in plants (**Cellulose**) & fungi (**Chitin**); **Absent** in animal cells |
| **Respiration Site** | Plasma membrane infoldings called **Mesosomes** | Cytoplasm (glycolysis) & **Mitochondria** (Krebs cycle, ETS) |
| **Cell Division** | **Binary fission / Amitosis** (no spindle fibres) | **Mitosis** and **Meiosis** (spindle apparatus formed) |
| **Flagella** | Made of **Flagellin** protein ($9+0$ or simple filament) | Made of **Tubulin** protein in **$9+2$** microtubule arrangement |

### 1.3 Plant Cell vs Animal Cell

| Feature | Plant Cell | Animal Cell |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cell Wall** | **Present** (rigid, made of Cellulose) | **Absent** |
| **Outermost Layer** | Cell Wall | Plasma Membrane |
| **Shape** | Fixed, usually rectangular/polygonal | Irregular/flexible, usually round or oval |
| **Plastids** | **Present** (Chloroplasts, Chromoplasts, Leucoplasts) | **Absent** *(Exception: **Euglena** possesses chloroplasts)* |
| **Vacuoles** | **Single, large central vacuole** (up to $90\%$ of cell volume), bounded by **Tonoplast** | **Absent** or very small, temporary vacuoles |
| **Centrosome & Centrioles** | **Absent** in higher plants (present in some lower plants/algae) | **Present** (essential for spindle formation) |
| **Golgi Apparatus** | Scattered discrete units called **Dictyosomes** | Well-developed localized complex near nucleus |
| **Lysosomes** | Rare (vacuoles perform hydrolytic functions) | **Abundant** |
| **Reserve Food** | **Starch** (stored in amyloplasts) | **Glycogen** and fat droplets |
| **Cytokinesis** | By **Cell Plate** formation (**centrifugal**: centre $\rightarrow$ periphery) | By **Cleavage Furrow** formation (**centripetal**: periphery $\rightarrow$ centre) |

### 1.4 Plasma Membrane: Fluid Mosaic Model

The **Plasma Membrane** (plasmalemma) is a thin, living, elastic, **selectively permeable** boundary surrounding the cytoplasm.

- **Fluid Mosaic Model (S. J. Singer & Garth L. Nicolson, 1972):** Most widely accepted model, described as ***"Protein icebergs in a sea of lipids."***
  - **Phospholipid Bilayer:** Amphipathic molecules with **hydrophilic (polar) heads** facing outward and **hydrophobic (non-polar) fatty acid tails** facing inward. Provides **quasi-fluid nature** enabling lateral movement of proteins, endocytosis, cell growth, and secretion.
  - **Membrane Proteins:**
    - **Intrinsic (Integral / Transmembrane) Proteins:** Partially or totally buried in the bilayer; act as channels, carriers, and pumps.
    - **Extrinsic (Peripheral) Proteins:** Attached loosely to the outer or inner surface; act as enzymes or structural anchors.
  - **Cholesterol:** Embedded in animal cell membranes to regulate membrane fluidity and mechanical stability (absent in plant/bacterial membranes).
  - **Carbohydrates (Glycoproteins & Glycolipids):** Present exclusively on the **outer surface** (glycocalyx) for cell-to-cell recognition.

### 1.5 Diffusion vs Osmosis & Tonicity

| Feature | Diffusion | Osmosis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Definition** | Net movement of molecules/ions from **higher concentration to lower concentration** | Net movement of **solvent (water)** from **lower solute concentration (higher water potential) to higher solute concentration (lower water potential)** |
| **Semi-permeable Membrane** | **Not required** | **Essential / Mandatory** |
| **Medium / State** | Occurs in **solids, liquids, and gases** | Occurs only in **liquid medium** (solvent movement only) |
| **Direction** | Both solute and solvent particles can move | Only **solvent (water)** molecules move across the membrane |
| **Biological Examples** | Exchange of $O_2$ and $CO_2$ in alveoli & stomata; spreading of perfume | Absorption of water by plant root hairs; swelling of raisins in water |

#### Effects of Tonicity on Living Cells

| External Solution Type | Solute Concentration (Outside vs Inside) | Direction of Water Flow | Effect on Animal Cell (e.g., RBC) | Effect on Plant Cell |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hypotonic** | Lower outside (higher water potential) | **Endosmosis** (Water enters cell) | Swells and **bursts (Lysis / Haemolysis)** | Becomes **Turgid** (cell wall resists bursting by exerting wall pressure) |
| **Isotonic** | Equal inside and outside | **No net movement** (dynamic equilibrium) | Retains normal shape | Becomes **Flaccid** (incipient plasmolysis) |
| **Hypertonic** | Higher outside (lower water potential) | **Exosmosis** (Water leaves cell) | Shrinks and wrinkles (**Crenation**) | Protoplasm shrinks away from cell wall (**Plasmolysis**) |

> **Reverse Osmosis (RO):** When external hydrostatic pressure applied on the concentrated solution exceeds its **osmotic pressure**, solvent moves in reverse (from higher solute concentration to lower solute concentration) through a semi-permeable membrane. Used in **water purification and desalination**.

### 1.7 Cell Division: Cell Cycle, Mitosis & Meiosis

#### A. The Cell Cycle
The sequence of events by which a cell duplicates its genome, synthesizes cell constituents, and divides into two daughter cells.

1. **Interphase ($\approx 95\%$ of cycle duration; metabolically most active preparatory phase):**
   - **$G_1$ Phase (Gap 1 / Post-mitotic):** Cell grows in size; synthesizes RNA, proteins, and most organelles; DNA content = $2C$, chromosome number = $2n$.
   - **$S$ Phase (Synthesis):** **DNA replication** occurs (DNA content doubles from **$2C \rightarrow 4C$**, chromosome number **$2n$ remains unchanged**); histone proteins synthesized; **centrioles duplicate** in animal cells.
   - **$G_2$ Phase (Gap 2 / Pre-mitotic):** Synthesis of **tubulin** protein for spindle fibres; final preparation for division.
   - **$G_0$ Phase (Quiescent Stage):** Cells exit the cell cycle temporarily or permanently (e.g., **neurons** and **cardiac muscle cells** remain metabolically active without dividing).
2. **M-Phase (Mitotic / Meiotic Phase):**
   - **Karyokinesis:** Division of the nucleus.
   - **Cytokinesis:** Division of the cytoplasm.

---

#### B. Mitosis (Equational Division)
- Discovered and named by **Walther Flemming (1882)** in salamander larvae.
- Occurs in **somatic (body) cells**; produces **2 genetically identical diploid ($2n$) daughter cells** from one diploid ($2n$) parent cell.

| Phase | Key Events (PMAT) | SSC Exam Highlights |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Prophase** | Chromatin condenses into distinct chromosomes (each with 2 sister chromatids joined at centromere); **nuclear membrane and nucleolus disappear**; centrioles migrate to opposite poles forming mitotic spindle | Longest phase of mitosis; initiation of spindle assembly |
| **2. Metaphase** | Chromosomes align at the **equatorial plate (metaphase plate)**; spindle fibres attach to **kinetochores** on centromeres | **Best stage to study chromosome number, morphology, and karyotype** |
| **3. Anaphase** | Centromeres split; **sister chromatids separate** and move to opposite poles | **Shortest phase** of mitosis; chromosomes assume **V, L, J, or I** shapes depending on centromere position (Metacentric, Sub-metacentric, Acrocentric, Telocentric) |
| **4. Telophase** | Chromosomes reach poles and decondense into chromatin; **nuclear envelope and nucleolus reappear** | Called **"Reverse Prophase"**; followed by **Cytokinesis** |

- **Significance of Mitosis:** Growth of multicellular organisms, repair and regeneration of damaged tissues, replacement of worn-out cells (skin, gut lining, blood), asexual reproduction, maintenance of nucleo-cytoplasmic ratio and genetic stability.

---

#### C. Meiosis (Reductional Division)
- Term coined by **Farmer & Moore (1905)** (observed earlier by Oscar Hertwig & Van Beneden).
- Occurs in **germ cells / meiocytes** during gametogenesis; involves **1 DNA replication** followed by **2 nuclear divisions** (Meiosis I & Meiosis II), yielding **4 genetically distinct haploid ($n$) daughter cells**.

##### 1. Meiosis I (Reductional Division: $2n \rightarrow n$)
- **Prophase I (Longest & most complex phase — 5 Substages):**
  1. **Leptotene:** Chromosomes condense into thin thread-like structures (*bouquet stage*).
  2. **Zygotene:** Homologous chromosomes pair longitudinally (**Synapsis**) via the **synaptonemal complex**, forming **Bivalents (Tetrads)**.
  3. **Pachytene:** **Crossing Over** occurs — exchange of genetic segments between **non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes**, mediated by the enzyme **Recombinase** at recombination nodules. **Primary source of genetic variation.**
  4. **Diplotene:** Dissolution of synaptonemal complex; homologous chromosomes begin separating but remain attached at X-shaped points called **Chiasmata**. *(Arrested in human primary oocytes from fetal life until ovulation).*
  5. **Diakinesis:** **Terminalisation of chiasmata**; nuclear membrane and nucleolus disappear; spindle forms.
- **Metaphase I:** Bivalents align at the equatorial plate in two parallel rows.
- **Anaphase I:** **Homologous chromosomes separate** to opposite poles (**centromeres do NOT divide**; chromosome number halves from $2n \rightarrow n$).
- **Telophase I:** Two haploid nuclei formed (each chromosome still has two chromatids).
- **Interkinesis:** Short resting gap between Meiosis I and II; **NO DNA replication** occurs.

##### 2. Meiosis II (Equational Division — identical to Mitosis)
- **Prophase II $\rightarrow$ Metaphase II $\rightarrow$ Anaphase II** (centromeres split and sister chromatids separate) $\rightarrow$ **Telophase II** $\rightarrow$ **4 haploid ($n$) cells**.

#### D. Mitosis vs Meiosis Comparison Table

| Feature | Mitosis | Meiosis |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Site of Occurrence** | **Somatic cells** | **Germ cells / Meiocytes** (gonads, anthers, ovules) |
| **Number of Divisions** | One (Equational) | Two (Meiosis I = Reductional; Meiosis II = Equational) |
| **Daughter Cells Produced** | **2 diploid ($2n$)** cells | **4 haploid ($n$)** cells |
| **Chromosome Number** | Remains **constant** ($2n \rightarrow 2n$) | Reduced to **half** ($2n \rightarrow n$) |
| **Synapsis & Crossing Over** | **Absent** | **Present** (Synapsis at Zygotene; Crossing over at **Pachytene**) |
| **Centromere Splitting** | Occurs in Anaphase | Does **not** occur in Anaphase I; occurs in **Anaphase II** |
| **Genetic Composition** | Daughter cells are **identical** to parent and each other | Daughter cells are **genetically different** (variation) |
| **Biological Role** | Growth, repair, tissue replacement, asexual reproduction | **Gamete formation**, maintains species chromosome number, drives evolution |

## 2. Biomolecules, Genetics & Evolution

### 2.1 Biomolecules

Living cells contain inorganic elements/compounds (water $\approx 70\text{–}90\%$, minerals) and four major classes of organic **biomolecules**: Carbohydrates, Proteins, Lipids, and Nucleic Acids.

#### A. Carbohydrates
- General formula: $\text{C}_n(\text{H}_2\text{O})_m$ (hydrates of carbon); primary and quickest source of energy (**$\approx 4\text{ kcal/g}$**); monomers linked by **glycosidic bonds**.
- **Classification:**

| Category | Sub-type / Examples | Composition / Monomers | Key SSC Facts & Sources |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Monosaccharides** *(Simple sugars; cannot be hydrolysed)* | **Ribose** ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_5$), **Deoxyribose** ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_4$) | Pentose sugars ($5\text{C}$) | Backbone of RNA (Ribose) and DNA (Deoxyribose) |
| | **Glucose** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$) | Hexose ($6\text{C}$) | **Blood sugar / Grape sugar / Dextrose**; instant energy fuel |
| | **Fructose** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$) | Hexose ($6\text{C}$, ketohexose) | **Fruit sugar**; **sweetest naturally occurring sugar** (in honey & fruits) |
| | **Galactose** ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$) | Hexose ($6\text{C}$) | Component of milk sugar (lactose) and brain glycolipids |
| **Disaccharides** ($\text{C}_{12}\text{H}_{22}\text{O}_{11}$) | **Sucrose** | $\text{Glucose} + \text{Fructose}$ | **Cane sugar / Table sugar**; **non-reducing sugar**; transported in plant phloem |
| | **Lactose** | $\text{Glucose} + \text{Galactose}$ | **Milk sugar** (digested by lactase; deficiency causes lactose intolerance) |
| | **Maltose** | $\text{Glucose} + \text{Glucose}$ | **Malt sugar** (formed during starch digestion by amylase) |
| **Polysaccharides** *(Complex; tasteless, insoluble)* | **Starch** | Polymer of $\alpha$-Glucose (Amylose + Amylopectin) | Primary **plant food reserve**; gives **blue-black colour** with iodine solution |
| | **Glycogen** | Branched polymer of $\alpha$-Glucose | **"Animal Starch"**; stored in **liver and skeletal muscles**; red-brown with iodine |
| | **Cellulose** | Unbranched polymer of $\beta$-Glucose | Plant cell wall; **most abundant organic polymer on Earth**; undigested by humans (roughage/fibre) due to lack of cellulase |
| | **Chitin** | Polymer of $N$-acetylglucosamine | **2nd most abundant organic polymer**; fungal cell wall & **arthropod exoskeleton** |
| | **Inulin** | Polymer of Fructose | Stored in *Dahlia* roots; used clinically to measure Glomerular Filtration Rate (GFR) |

> **Artificial Sweeteners (Non-nutritive):** **Saccharin** (~300–500× sweeter than sucrose), **Aspartame** (~100–200×, unstable on cooking, avoided in PKU), **Sucralose** (~600×, heat-stable), **Alitame** (~2000×).

#### B. Proteins & Enzymes
- Term **Protein** coined by **J. J. Berzelius (1838)** (from Greek *proteios* = of primary importance); studied by Mulder.
- Energy yield: **$\approx 4\text{ kcal/g}$**. Composed of C, H, O, N (and often S, P).
- **Monomers:** **20 standard Amino Acids** linked covalently by **Peptide Bonds** ($-\text{CO}-\text{NH}-$).
  - **Essential Amino Acids (9):** Cannot be synthesized by the human body; must come from diet — **P**henylalanine, **V**aline, **T**hreonine, **T**ryptophan, **I**soleucine, **M**ethionine, **H**istidine, **L**eucine, **L**ysine *(Arginine is semi-essential: essential for growing children)*.
  - **Non-Essential Amino Acids (11):** Synthesized by the body (e.g., Glycine — simplest amino acid, Alanine, Glutamic acid).
- **Important Proteins:**
  - **Collagen:** Most abundant protein in the **animal world** (connective tissue, tendons, skin).
  - **RuBisCO** (Ribulose-1,5-bisphosphate carboxylase-oxygenase): Most abundant protein in the **whole biosphere**.
  - **Keratin:** Fibrous protein in hair, nails, horns, hooves, feathers.
  - **Haemoglobin:** Quaternary globular protein (4 polypeptide chains + 4 $Fe^{2+}$ haem groups); transports $O_2$.
  - **Fibroin:** Protein in natural silk; **Casein:** Milk protein; **Gluten:** Wheat protein; **Actin & Myosin:** Muscle contraction proteins.
- **Enzymes (Biocatalysts):**
  - Term **Enzyme** coined by **Willy Kühne (1878)**; first enzyme isolated was **Urease** (Sumner, 1926); Eduard Buchner discovered *Zymase* in yeast.
  - **Golden Rule & Exception:** *All enzymes are proteins*, **EXCEPT Ribozymes** (catalytic RNA molecules, discovered by Thomas Cech & Sidney Altman).
  - **Mechanism:** Lower the **activation energy** of biochemical reactions without being consumed; show substrate specificity (**Lock & Key Model** by Emil Fischer, 1894; **Induced Fit Model** by Daniel Koshland, 1958).
  - Optimum human enzyme temperature: **$\sim 37^\circ\text{C}$** (denatured above $50\text{–}60^\circ\text{C}$, except *Taq* polymerase from *Thermus aquaticus* used in PCR).

---

#### C. Lipids
- Water-insoluble, organic-solvent-soluble biomolecules (fats, oils, waxes, phospholipids, steroids).
- **Highest dietary energy yield:** **$\approx 9\text{ kcal/g}$** (more than double carbohydrates/proteins).
- **Simple Lipids (Triglycerides):** Esters of **1 Glycerol + 3 Fatty Acids** (linked by ester bonds).
  - **Saturated Fatty Acids:** Only single C–C bonds; solid at room temperature (e.g., butter, ghee, animal fat, palmitic acid).
  - **Unsaturated Fatty Acids:** One or more C=C double bonds; liquid at room temperature (e.g., mustard oil, olive oil, oleic & linoleic acids).
  - **Trans Fats:** Formed by partial hydrogenation of vegetable oils (e.g., vanaspati/margarine); raise **LDL ("bad" cholesterol)** and lower **HDL ("good" cholesterol)**, increasing cardiovascular risk.
- **Compound Lipids:** **Phospholipids** (e.g., Lecithin) form biological membranes.
- **Derived Lipids:** **Cholesterol** (a sterol; precursor of Vitamin D, bile salts, testosterone, oestrogen, cortisol; absent in plants).

---

#### D. Nucleic Acids
- Discovered by **Friedrich Miescher (1869)** in pus cell nuclei (named *Nuclein*); Richard Altman (1889) coined **Nucleic Acid**.
- Polymers of **Nucleotides** linked by **$3'\text{–}5'$ phosphodiester bonds**.
  - **$\text{Nucleoside} = \text{Pentose Sugar} + \text{Nitrogenous Base}$**
  - **$\text{Nucleotide} = \text{Pentose Sugar} + \text{Nitrogenous Base} + \text{Phosphate Group } (\text{H}_3\text{PO}_4)$**
- **Nitrogenous Bases:**
  - **Purines (Double-ring, 9-membered):** **Adenine (A)** and **Guanine (G)**.
  - **Pyrimidines (Single-ring, 6-membered):** **Cytosine (C)**, **Thymine (T)** *(DNA only)*, and **Uracil (U)** *(RNA only)*.

### 2.2 DNA vs RNA, Molecular Structure & Central Dogma

#### DNA vs RNA Comparison Table

| Feature | DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid) | RNA (Ribonucleic Acid) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pentose Sugar** | **2-Deoxyribose** ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_4$; lacks O at $2'$ C) | **Ribose** ($\text{C}_5\text{H}_{10}\text{O}_5$; has $-OH$ at $2'$ C) |
| **Nitrogenous Bases** | **A, G, C, Thymine (T)** | **A, G, C, Uracil (U)** (Uracil replaces Thymine) |
| **Strands** | Usually **Double-stranded (dsDNA)** | Usually **Single-stranded (ssRNA)** |
| **Location in Eukaryotes** | Nucleus (chromatin), Mitochondria, Chloroplasts | Nucleolus, Cytoplasm, Ribosomes |
| **Stability** | Chemically **more stable** (lacks reactive $2'\text{-OH}$; T resists deamination) $\rightarrow$ ideal genetic storage | Chemically **less stable**, mutates faster |
| **Primary Function** | Stores and transmits hereditary genetic information | Protein synthesis (**mRNA, tRNA, rRNA**); genetic material in retroviruses/many plant viruses |
| **SSC Exceptions** | **ssDNA** occurs in bacteriophage **$\phi \times 174$** | **dsRNA** occurs in **Reovirus** and **Rotavirus** |

- **3 Functional Types of Non-Genetic RNA:**
  1. **rRNA (Ribosomal RNA):** Most abundant ($\sim 80\%$ of cellular RNA); structural & catalytic component of ribosomes.
  2. **tRNA (Transfer RNA):** Smallest RNA ($\sim 15\%$); **"Adapter molecule"** (clover-leaf 2D shape) carrying specific amino acids to ribosomes via its anticodon.
  3. **mRNA (Messenger RNA):** Least abundant ($\sim 5\%$); carries genetic codons from DNA to ribosomes for protein synthesis.

#### Watson & Crick Double-Helix Model (1953) & Chargaff's Rule
- **James Watson & Francis Crick (1953)** proposed the double-helix structure of B-DNA using X-ray diffraction data of **Rosalind Franklin** and **Maurice Wilkins** *(Nobel Prize 1962: Watson, Crick & Wilkins)*.
- **Key Dimensions of B-DNA:**
  - Two **antiparallel** polynucleotide strands ($5' \rightarrow 3'$ and $3' \rightarrow 5'$) with a sugar-phosphate backbone outside and bases stacked inside.
  - **Complementary Base Pairing:**
    - **$\text{Adenine (A)} = \text{Thymine (T)}$** (**2 hydrogen bonds**)
    - **$\text{Guanine (G)} \equiv \text{Cytosine (C)}$** (**3 hydrogen bonds** — higher GC content = higher melting temperature $T_m$)
  - Diameter of helix = **$2\text{ nm}$ ($20\ \text{\AA}$)**.
  - Pitch (length of one complete turn) = **$3.4\text{ nm}$ ($34\ \text{\AA}$)**, containing **$10\text{ base pairs}$**.
  - Distance between two adjacent base pairs = **$0.34\text{ nm}$ ($3.4\ \text{\AA}$)**.
- **Chargaff's Rule (Erwin Chargaff, 1950):** In double-stranded DNA, the molar amount of Purines equals Pyrimidines:
  $$[\text{A}] = [\text{T}], \quad [\text{G}] = [\text{C}], \quad \frac{[\text{A}] + [\text{G}]}{[\text{T}] + [\text{C}]} = 1$$
  *(Exception: Does NOT apply to single-stranded DNA or RNA).*

#### Central Dogma & Genetic Code
- **Central Dogma of Molecular Biology (Francis Crick, 1958):** Unidirectional flow of genetic information:
  $$\text{DNA} \xrightarrow{\text{Replication (DNA Polymerase)}} \text{DNA} \xrightarrow{\text{Transcription (RNA Polymerase)}} \text{mRNA} \xrightarrow{\text{Translation (Ribosomes)}} \text{Protein}$$
  - **DNA Replication** is **semiconservative** (each daughter DNA has 1 old parental strand + 1 new strand), proved experimentally by **Meselson & Stahl (1958)** using $^{15}\text{N}$ in *E. coli*.
  - **Reverse Transcription (Teminism):** Discovered by **Howard Temin & David Baltimore (1970)** in retroviruses (e.g., **HIV**, Rous Sarcoma Virus):
    $$\text{RNA} \xrightarrow{\text{Reverse Transcriptase}} \text{cDNA}$$
- **Genetic Code:**
  - Set of **$64\text{ triplet codons}$** ($4^3$): **$61\text{ sense codons}$** code for 20 amino acids; **$3\text{ Stop / Nonsense codons}$** (**UAA** *Ochre*, **UAG** *Amber*, **UGA** *Opal*) terminate translation.
  - **Start / Initiation Codon:** **AUG** (codes for **Methionine** in eukaryotes; $N$-formylmethionine in prokaryotes).
  - **Properties:** Triplet, **universal** (with minor mitochondrial/protozoan exceptions), **non-overlapping**, **comma-less**, **unambiguous** (1 codon = 1 amino acid), and **degenerate** (one amino acid can have multiple codons, e.g., Leucine/Arginine/Serine have 6 codons; only Methionine [AUG] and Tryptophan [UGG] have 1 codon).
  - **Har Gobind Khorana:** Synthesized artificial RNA homopolymers & copolymers with defined sequences to decipher the genetic code; shared the **1968 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine** with Marshall Nirenberg and Robert Holley.

### 2.3 Mendelian Genetics, Sex Determination & Genetic Disorders

#### A. Mendel's Laws of Inheritance
- **Gregor Johann Mendel** (*Father of Genetics*) conducted hybridization experiments on the Garden Pea (**_Pisum sativum_**, $2n = 14$) for 7 years (1856–1863; published 1865).

##### 7 Contrasting Traits Studied by Mendel in *Pisum sativum*

| S.No. | Character | Dominant Trait | Recessive Trait |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Stem Height** | Tall | Dwarf |
| **2** | **Flower Colour** | Violet / Purple | White |
| **3** | **Flower Position** | Axial | Terminal |
| **4** | **Pod Shape** | Inflated / Full | Constricted |
| **5** | **Pod Colour** | Green | Yellow |
| **6** | **Seed Shape** | Round | Wrinkled |
| **7** | **Seed Colour** | Yellow | Green |

##### Mendel's 3 Laws & Key Cross Ratios
1. **Law of Dominance:** In a heterozygous condition ($Tt$), only the **dominant allele** ($T$) expresses itself phenotypically in the $F_1$ generation, masking the recessive allele ($t$).
   - *Exceptions:*
     - **Incomplete Dominance:** Neither allele is completely dominant; $F_1$ shows an intermediate phenotype (e.g., *Mirabilis jalapa* / Four o'clock plant and *Antirrhinum* / Snapdragon: $\text{Red} \times \text{White} \rightarrow \text{Pink } F_1$; $F_2$ phenotypic & genotypic ratio both = **$1:2:1$**).
     - **Co-dominance:** Both alleles express equally and independently in heterozygotes (e.g., **AB blood group** — $I^A I^B$, **Roan coat colour** in cattle).
     - **Multiple Allelism:** More than two alleles govern a single gene in a population (e.g., **ABO blood group** has 3 alleles: $I^A, I^B, i$).
2. **Law of Segregation (Law of Purity of Gametes):** Allele pairs separate (segregate) cleanly during gamete formation so each gamete receives only one allele without blending. **Universally valid — NO known exception.**
3. **Law of Independent Assortment:** Alleles of two or more different genes assort independently of one another during gamete formation.
   - *Exception:* **Linkage** — genes located close together on the same chromosome tend to be inherited together (discovered by **T. H. Morgan** in *Drosophila melanogaster*).

##### Standard Cross Ratios (Must Memorize)
- **Monohybrid Cross ($Tt \times Tt$):**
  - $F_2$ **Phenotypic Ratio** = **$3 : 1$** ($3\text{ Tall} : 1\text{ Dwarf}$)
  - $F_2$ **Genotypic Ratio** = **$1 : 2 : 1$** ($1\ TT : 2\ Tt : 1\ tt$)
- **Dihybrid Cross ($RrYy \times RrYy$):**
  - $F_2$ **Phenotypic Ratio** = **$9 : 3 : 3 : 1$**
  - $F_2$ **Genotypic Ratio** = **$1 : 2 : 1 : 2 : 4 : 2 : 1 : 2 : 1$**
- **Test Cross ($F_1 \text{ hybrid} \times \text{Homozygous recessive}$):** Used to determine unknown genotype — yields **$1:1$** (monohybrid) and **$1:1:1:1$** (dihybrid).

#### B. Human Chromosomes & Sex Determination
- **Human Chromosome Number:** **$46\text{ chromosomes}$ ($23\text{ pairs}$)** per somatic diploid cell (established by Tjio & Levan, 1956).
  - **22 pairs (44) Autosomes** (identical in males and females).
  - **1 pair (2) Allosomes / Sex Chromosomes:** **XX** in females, **XY** in males.
- **Sex Determination in Humans (XX–XY Mechanism):**
  - Females are **homogametic** (produce only **$22 + X$** ova).
  - Males are **heterogametic** (produce $50\%$ **$22 + X$** and $50\%$ **$22 + Y$** sperms).
  - **The father's sperm determines the biological sex of the child** ($X\text{-sperm} \rightarrow \text{XX girl}$; $Y\text{-sperm} \rightarrow \text{XY boy}$). The **SRY gene** on the Y chromosome triggers testis development.
  - *Other Mechanisms:* **ZW–ZZ** in **Birds** (female is heterogametic ZW); **XX–XO** in **Grasshoppers**; **Haplodiploidy** in **Honeybees** (unfertilized haploid egg $n=16$ develops into male drone via parthenogenesis; fertilized diploid egg $2n=32$ becomes female worker/queen).

### 2.4 Evolution

#### A. Origin of Life & Major Theories of Evolution
- **Oparin–Haldane Theory (Chemical Evolution):** Life originated abiogenetically from inorganic molecules in Earth's early reducing atmosphere (no free $O_2$).
  - **Miller–Urey Experiment (1953):** Stanley Miller & Harold Urey simulated primitive Earth using **$\text{CH}_4$, $\text{NH}_3$, $\text{H}_2$, and $\text{H}_2\text{O}$ vapour** at $800^\circ\text{C}$ with electric sparks $\rightarrow$ synthesized **amino acids** (glycine, alanine, aspartic acid).
- **Lamarckism (Jean-Baptiste de Lamarck, *Philosophie Zoologique*, 1809):**
  - Theory of **Use and Disuse of Organs** and **Inheritance of Acquired Characters** (e.g., giraffe neck elongation, loss of limbs in snakes).
  - *Disproved by:* **August Weismann** (*Theory of Continuity of Germplasm*), who cut tails of mice for 21 generations and still got tailed offspring.
- **Darwinism (Charles Darwin, *On the Origin of Species*, 1859):**
  - Voyage on **HMS *Beagle***; observed adaptive radiation in **Darwin's Finches** on the **Galápagos Islands**.
  - Theory of **Natural Selection** (Alfred Russel Wallace independently reached the same conclusion; **"Survival of the Fittest"** phrase coined by **Herbert Spencer**).
  - Core principles: Overproduction $\rightarrow$ Struggle for existence $\rightarrow$ Heritable variations $\rightarrow$ Natural selection $\rightarrow$ Speciation.
- **Mutation Theory (Hugo de Vries, 1901):** Worked on Evening Primrose (*Oenothera lamarckiana*); proposed that evolution proceeds via sudden, large, heritable changes called **mutations / saltation**.
- **Modern Synthetic Theory (Neo-Darwinism):** Combines Darwinian natural selection with Mendelian genetics, mutations, genetic drift, gene flow, and reproductive isolation.

## 3. Classification of Living Organisms (Five Kingdoms, Plantae & Animalia)

### 3.1 Taxonomy & Binomial Nomenclature
- **Taxonomy** (term coined by **A. P. de Candolle**): Science of identification, nomenclature, and classification of organisms.
- **Carl Linnaeus** (**Father of Taxonomy**): Wrote ***Systema Naturae*** (1735, animal nomenclature) and ***Species Plantarum*** (1753, plant nomenclature).
- **Binomial Nomenclature:** Every species is assigned a two-word Latinized scientific name:
  1. First word = **Genus** (starts with **Capital** letter).
  2. Second word = **Species / Specific Epithet** (starts with **lowercase** letter).
  - Printed in *italics* (or underlined separately when handwritten). Examples: *Homo sapiens* (Human), *Mangifera indica* (Mango), *Rana tigrina* (Indian Bullfrog), *Triticum aestivum* (Wheat), *Oryza sativa* (Rice).
- **7 Obligate Taxonomic Categories (Descending Order):**
  $$\text{Kingdom} \rightarrow \text{Phylum (Division in plants)} \rightarrow \text{Class} \rightarrow \text{Order} \rightarrow \text{Family} \rightarrow \text{Genus} \rightarrow \text{Species}$$
  - **Species** is the **lowest / basic unit** of classification (Biological Species Concept by **Ernst Mayr**: group of actually or potentially interbreeding populations producing fertile offspring).
- **Evolution of Kingdom Systems:**
  - **2 Kingdoms (Linnaeus, 1758):** Plantae, Animalia.
  - **3 Kingdoms (Ernst Haeckel, 1866):** Added **Protista**.
  - **4 Kingdoms (Copeland, 1956):** Added **Monera**.
  - **5 Kingdoms (R. H. Whittaker, 1969):** Monera, Protista, Fungi, Plantae, Animalia.
  - **3 Domains / 6 Kingdoms (Carl Woese, 1990):** Based on **16S rRNA** sequences — Domains **Archaea**, **Bacteria**, and **Eukarya** (splits Monera into Archaebacteria & Eubacteria).

### 3.2 Whittaker's Five Kingdom Classification (1969)

Based on **cell structure, body organization, mode of nutrition, reproduction, and phylogenetic relationships**:

| Feature | 1. Monera | 2. Protista | 3. Fungi | 4. Plantae | 5. Animalia |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Cell Type** | **Prokaryotic** | **Eukaryotic** | **Eukaryotic** | **Eukaryotic** | **Eukaryotic** |
| **Cell Wall** | Present (**Peptidoglycan**; *absent in Mycoplasma*) | Present in some (cellulose/silica) or absent | Present (**Chitin** / fungal cellulose) | Present (**Cellulose**) | **Absent** |
| **Nuclear Membrane** | **Absent** | Present | Present | Present | Present |
| **Body Organization** | **Unicellular** | **Unicellular** | Multicellular / loose tissue *(Exception: **Yeast** is unicellular)* | Tissue / Organ | Tissue / Organ / Organ-system |
| **Mode of Nutrition** | Autotrophic (chemo/photo) or Heterotrophic (saprophytic/parasitic) | Autotrophic (photosynthetic) or Heterotrophic | **Heterotrophic** (**Saprophytic** or Parasitic; absorptive) | **Autotrophic** (Photosynthetic) *(Exceptions: Insectivorous & parasitic plants)* | **Heterotrophic** (**Holozoic** / ingestive) |
| **Stored Food** | Glycogen / Volutin | Starch / Glycogen / Oil | **Glycogen & Oil** | **Starch** | **Glycogen & Fat** |
| **Classic Examples** | Bacteria (*E. coli, Rhizobium*), Cyanobacteria (*Nostoc, Anabaena*), *Mycoplasma*, Archaebacteria | *Amoeba, Paramecium, Euglena, Plasmodium*, Diatoms, Dinoflagellates | Yeast (*Saccharomyces*), *Penicillium*, Mushroom (*Agaricus*), *Rhizopus* | Algae, Bryophytes, Pteridophytes, Gymnosperms, Angiosperms | Sponges, Worms, Insects, Molluscs, Fishes, Birds, Mammals |

#### High-Yield SSC Facts on Monera, Protista, Fungi & Acellular Forms
- **Monera:**
  - **Bacterial Shapes:** **Coccus** (spherical), **Bacillus** (rod-shaped), **Vibrio** (comma-shaped, e.g., *Vibrio cholerae*), **Spirillum** (spiral).
  - **Blue-Green Algae (Cyanobacteria):** Prokaryotic photosynthetic organisms (NOT true algae); *Nostoc* and *Anabaena* fix atmospheric nitrogen in specialized cells called **Heterocysts**.
  - **Archaebacteria:** Survive extreme habitats — **Methanogens** (marshy areas & cattle rumen; produce **biogas/$CH_4$**), **Halophiles** (salty lakes), **Thermoacidophiles** (hot sulphur springs). Cell wall lacks peptidoglycan.
- **Protista:**
  - **Diatoms (Chrysophytes):** Cell wall impregnated with **silica** (indestructible $\rightarrow$ forms **diatomaceous earth**); chief producers in oceans.
  - **Dinoflagellates:** *Gonyaulax* causes **"Red Tides"** in sea.
- **Fungi (Study = Mycology):**
  - **Yeast (*Saccharomyces cerevisiae*):** Unicellular fungus used in bread-making (releases $CO_2$) and brewing alcohol (fermentation).
  - ***Penicillium notatum*:** Source of **Penicillin** (first antibiotic, discovered by **Alexander Fleming, 1928**).
  - **Lichens:** Symbiotic association between **Algae (Phycobiont — prepares food)** and **Fungi (Mycobiont — provides shelter & absorbs water/minerals)**; **natural bio-indicators of $\text{SO}_2$ air pollution** (do not grow in polluted areas).
  - **Mycorrhiza:** Symbiotic association of fungi with roots of higher plants (e.g., *Pinus*).
- **Excluded from Five Kingdoms (Acellular):**
  - **Viruses** (D. J. Ivanovsky 1892; Beijerinck; W. M. Stanley crystallized TMV in 1935) — DNA or RNA core + protein coat (**Capsid**).
  - **Viroids** (T. O. Diener, 1971) — Free, low-molecular-weight infectious **RNA without a protein coat** (e.g., Potato Spindle Tuber disease).
  - **Prions** (Stanley Prusiner) — Infectious **abnormally folded proteins** without nucleic acid (cause **Mad Cow Disease / BSE** and Creutzfeldt–Jakob Disease in humans).

### 3.3 Plant Kingdom (Eichler's Classification)

A. W. Eichler divided **Plantae** into two subkingdoms:
1. **Cryptogamae** *(Seedless, non-flowering, hidden reproductive organs)*: **Thallophyta, Bryophyta, Pteridophyta**.
2. **Phanerogamae** *(Seed-bearing)*: **Gymnosperms, Angiosperms**.

#### 1. Thallophyta (Algae — Study = Phycology)
- Undifferentiated **thallus** body (no true roots, stems, or leaves); predominantly aquatic; **no vascular tissues**; **no embryo stage** after fertilization.

| Class | Common Name | Major Pigments | Stored Food | Cell Wall | Classic Examples & Economic Importance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Chlorophyceae** | **Green Algae** | **Chlorophyll $a, b$** | **Starch** (with pyrenoids) | Cellulose + Pectose | *Spirogyra* ("Pond Silk"), *Chlamydomonas*, *Volvox*, ***Chlorella*** (protein-rich unicellular alga used as **space food**) |
| **Phaeophyceae** | **Brown Algae** | Chlorophyll $a, c$ + **Fucoxanthin** | **Laminarin & Mannitol** | Cellulose + **Algin** | *Sargassum*, ***Laminaria*** (rich source of **Iodine**), *Fucus*, **Kelps** (giant marine algae) |
| **Rhodophyceae** | **Red Algae** | Chlorophyll $a, d$ + **r-Phycoerythrin** | **Floridean Starch** | Cellulose, pectin & polysulphate esters | ***Gelidium* & *Gracilaria*** (source of **Agar-agar** used in microbial culture & ice creams), *Porphyra* |

#### 2. Bryophyta ("Amphibians of the Plant Kingdom")
- Live on damp soil/rocks but **depend on water for fertilization** (flagellated male gametes swim to archegonia).
- Lack true roots, stems, leaves (possess root-like **rhizoids**); **lack vascular tissues (no xylem/phloem)**; dominant phase is haploid **gametophyte**.
- **Examples:**
  - **Mosses:** *Funaria*, ***Sphagnum*** (**Peat Moss** — absorbs up to 18× its weight in water; used as packing material for live plants).
  - **Liverworts:** *Marchantia*, *Riccia*; **Hornworts:** *Anthoceros*.

#### 3. Pteridophyta (First Vascular Cryptogams / "Botanical Snakes")
- First terrestrial plants with **true roots, stems, leaves**, and **vascular tissues (xylem and phloem)** *(Note: xylem lacks vessels and phloem lacks companion cells)*.
- Dominant phase is diploid **sporophyte**; still need water for fertilization.
- **Examples:** **Ferns** (*Pteris, Dryopteris, Adiantum* — walking fern), *Equisetum* (Horsetail), *Selaginella*, ***Azolla*** (tiny aquatic fern hosting *Anabaena* for nitrogen fixation in rice/paddy fields).

#### 4. Gymnosperms (Naked-Seeded Plants)
- Ovules are **not enclosed by an ovary wall**; **no fruit** is formed, so seeds remain **naked** (borne on cones/strobili).
- Woody, evergreen perennials; xylem lacks vessels *(Exception: Gnetales like Gnetum, Ephedra, Welwitschia)*.
- **Examples:**
  - ***Cycas*** (*Living fossil*; coralloid roots with $N_2$-fixing cyanobacteria).
  - ***Pinus*** (Mycorrhizal roots; winged pollen causes *"sulphur shower"*; source of turpentine oil & chilgoza pine nuts from *Pinus gerardiana*).
  - ***Sequoia sempervirens*** (California Redwood — **tallest tree in the world**).
  - ***Ginkgo biloba*** (*Maidenhair tree* — living fossil).
  - ***Ephedra*** (Source of **Ephedrine** drug used to treat asthma/bronchitis).

#### 5. Angiosperms (Flowering / Covered-Seeded Plants)
- Largest and most diverse plant group; ovules enclosed inside **ovary** $\rightarrow$ seeds enclosed inside **fruits**.
- Possess **flowers** and exhibit **Double Fertilization** (unique to Angiosperms: 1st male gamete + egg $\rightarrow$ diploid **zygote $2n$**; 2nd male gamete + 2 polar nuclei $\rightarrow$ triploid **endosperm $3n$** for nourishment).
- **Smallest Angiosperm:** ***Wolffia*** (watermeal, $< 1\text{ mm}$); **Tallest Angiosperm:** ***Eucalyptus regnans*** ($> 100\text{ m}$).

### 3.4 Animal Kingdom (Non-Chordates & Chordates)

#### Key Classification Criteria
- **Level of Organization:** Cellular (Porifera) $\rightarrow$ Tissue (Cnidaria, Ctenophora) $\rightarrow$ Organ (Platyhelminthes) $\rightarrow$ Organ-system (Aschelminthes to Chordata).
- **Symmetry:** **Asymmetrical** (most sponges), **Radial** (Cnidaria, Ctenophora, adult Echinoderms), **Bilateral** (Platyhelminthes to Chordata).
- **Germ Layers:** **Diploblastic** (Ectoderm + Endoderm: Cnidaria, Ctenophora) vs **Triploblastic** (Ectoderm + Mesoderm + Endoderm: Platyhelminthes to Chordata).
- **Body Cavity (Coelom):** **Acoelomate** (no coelom: Porifera to Platyhelminthes), **Pseudocoelomate** (false coelom not lined by mesoderm: Aschelminthes), **Eucoelomate / Coelomate** (true mesoderm-lined coelom: Annelida to Chordata).

### 4.1 Digestive System

* **Alimentary Canal Path (~9 m):** Mouth (Buccal cavity) → Pharynx → Oesophagus (Peristalsis; **no digestion**) → Stomach (Cardia, Fundus, Body, Pylorus; forms acidic **Chyme**) → Small Intestine (Duodenum, Jejunum, Ileum; forms alkaline **Chyle**) → Large Intestine (Caecum + vestigial Vermiform Appendix, Colon [Ascending, Transverse, Descending, Sigmoid], Rectum) → Anus.
* **Teeth Characteristics:** **Thecodont** (embedded in jaw sockets), **Diphyodont** (two sets in life: milk & permanent), and **Heterodont** (4 types: Incisors [cutting], Canines [tearing], Premolars & Molars [chewing/grinding]).
  * **Adult Dental Formula (32 teeth):** `(2123 / 2123) × 2 = 32` → Incisors (8), Canines (4), Premolars (8), Molars (12; last 4 are **Wisdom teeth**, vestigial).
  * **Child Milk Dental Formula (20 teeth):** `(2102 / 2102) × 2 = 20` → **Premolars and 3rd molars (wisdom teeth) are absent** in children.
  * **Exam Fact:** Elephant tusks are modified **upper incisors**; carnivorous animals have well-developed canines.
* **Tooth Enamel:** The **hardest substance in the human body**; non-living outer covering secreted by **ameloblasts**; ~96% inorganic mineral, primarily **Calcium hydroxyapatite** (`Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂`). Beneath it lies **Dentine** (living, softer, bone-like).
* **Tongue Taste Buds:** Tip → Sweet; Sides (front) → Salty; Sides (back) → Sour; Back/Posterior → Bitter. (Umami receptors are distributed across the tongue).

### 4.2 Respiratory System

#### Aerobic vs Anaerobic Respiration

| Feature | Aerobic Respiration | Anaerobic Respiration (Muscles) | Anaerobic Respiration (Yeast / Plants) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Oxygen Requirement** | Present | Absent / Deficient (Vigorous exercise) | Absent (Fermentation) |
| **Site** | Cytoplasm (Glycolysis) + **Mitochondria** (Krebs cycle & ETC) | Cytoplasm | Cytoplasm |
| **Overall Equation** | `C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + 38 ATP` *(net 36–38 ATP)* | `C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2 C₃H₆O₃ (Lactic Acid) + 2 ATP` | `C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2 C₂H₅OH (Ethanol) + 2CO₂ + 2 ATP` |
| **End Products** | `CO₂` + `H₂O` | **Lactic Acid** (causes **muscle fatigue/cramps**; cleared in liver via Cori cycle) | **Ethanol** + `CO₂` (used in baking & brewing via *Saccharomyces cerevisiae*) |
| **Glycolysis Note** | Common first step in both aerobic & anaerobic (**EMP pathway** in cytoplasm; Glucose → 2 Pyruvate; net **2 ATP**) | Same glycolysis → Pyruvate reduced to Lactic acid | Same glycolysis → Pyruvate converted to Ethanol + `CO₂` |

* **Human Respiratory Pathway:** External Nostrils → Nasal Cavity → Pharynx → **Larynx** (Voice box / Adam's apple; guarded by **Epiglottis** to prevent food entering windpipe) → **Trachea** (supported by C-shaped cartilaginous rings) → Bronchi → Bronchioles → **Alveoli**.
  * **Alveoli:** ~300 million in both lungs; structural and functional unit of lungs; lined by **simple squamous epithelium**; site of `O₂` and `CO₂` exchange by **simple diffusion**. **Lecithin surfactant** prevents alveolar collapse.
  * **Pleura:** Double-layered protective membrane around lungs containing pleural fluid.
  * **Breathing Mechanism:** Normal rate = **12–16 breaths/min**. During **Inspiration**, diaphragm contracts (flattens) and external intercostal muscles pull ribs outward/upward → thoracic volume increases → pressure drops → air enters. **Expiration** is normally passive relaxation.
* **Transport of Gases:**
  * **Oxygen (`O₂`):** **~97%** transported as **Oxyhaemoglobin (`HbO₂`)** inside RBCs (1 Haemoglobin molecule binds max **4 `O₂`** molecules; Iron stays in **`Fe²⁺`** state); ~3% dissolved in plasma.
  * **Carbon Monoxide (CO) Exception:** CO has **~200–250× higher affinity** for Haemoglobin than `O₂`, forming stable **Carboxyhaemoglobin** (causes asphyxiation).
  * **Carbon Dioxide (`CO₂`):** **~70%** transported as **Bicarbonate ions (`HCO₃⁻`)** (catalyzed by **Carbonic anhydrase** in RBCs; **Chloride shift / Hamburger phenomenon**), **~20–25%** as **Carbaminohaemoglobin**, and **~7%** dissolved in plasma.
* **Respiratory Volumes & Capacities (Measured by Spirometer):**
  * **Tidal Volume (TV):** **~500 mL** (air inspired/expired during normal quiet breathing).
  * **Inspiratory Reserve Volume (IRV):** ~2,500–3,000 mL (extra air forcefully inhaled).
  * **Expiratory Reserve Volume (ERV):** ~1,000–1,100 mL (extra air forcefully exhaled).
  * **Residual Volume (RV):** ~1,100–1,200 mL (air remaining in lungs even after forced expiration; prevents lung collapse).
  * **Vital Capacity (VC):** `VC = TV + IRV + ERV` ≈ **3,500–4,600 mL** (max air breathed in/out after forced expiration/inspiration).
  * **Total Lung Capacity (TLC):** `TLC = VC + RV` ≈ **5,800–6,000 mL**.

### 4.3 Circulatory System & Blood

* **Discovery:** **William Harvey (1628)** discovered blood circulation.
* **Human Heart:** Myogenic, **4-chambered** (2 Atria + 2 Ventricles), located in thoracic cavity between lungs (mediastinum), enclosed in **Pericardium**.
  * **Chambers in Vertebrates (Exam Favourite):** Fish = **2** (single circulation, venous heart); Amphibians & most Reptiles = **3**; **Crocodiles, Birds & Mammals = 4**; Cockroach = **13** tubular chambers.
  * **Heart Valves:** **Tricuspid valve** (Right atrium ↔ Right ventricle); **Bicuspid / Mitral valve** (Left atrium ↔ Left ventricle); **Semilunar valves** (bases of Pulmonary artery & Aorta).
  * **Conduction System:** **SA Node** (Sinoatrial node, upper right atrium — **Natural Pacemaker**, ~70–75 beats/min) → **AV Node** (Atrioventricular node — Pacesetter) → **Bundle of His** → **Purkinje fibres**.
  * **Heart Sounds (Stethoscope — René Laennec):**
    * **Lubb (S1):** Closure of **AV valves** (Tricuspid & Bicuspid) at start of ventricular systole (longer, duller).
    * **Dubb (S2):** Closure of **Semilunar valves** at start of ventricular diastole (shorter, sharper).
  * **Cardiac Output:** Stroke Volume (~70 mL) × Heart Rate (~72/min) ≈ **5,040 mL (~5 L/min)**.
  * **ECG (Electrocardiogram — Willem Einthoven):** **P wave** = Atrial depolarization (contraction); **QRS complex** = Ventricular depolarization (contraction); **T wave** = Ventricular repolarization (relaxation).
  * **Blood Pressure (Sphygmomanometer):** Normal = **120 / 80 mmHg** (**Systolic** = 120 mmHg during ventricular contraction; **Diastolic** = 80 mmHg during ventricular relaxation; Pulse pressure = 40 mmHg). **Hypertension** ≥ 140/90 mmHg; **Hypotension** ≤ 90/60 mmHg.

#### Arteries vs Veins vs Capillaries

| Feature | Arteries | Veins | Capillaries |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Direction of Flow** | **Away from heart** to organs | **Towards heart** from organs | Connect arterioles to venules |
| **Wall & Lumen** | **Thick, elastic, muscular wall**; narrow lumen | **Thin wall**; wide lumen | **Single-cell thick** (Endothelium only) |
| **Valves** | **Absent** (except semilunar at heart exit) | **Present** (prevent backflow) | Absent |
| **Blood Type** | Oxygenated *(**Exception:** **Pulmonary artery** & Umbilical artery carry deoxygenated blood)* | Deoxygenated *(**Exception:** **Pulmonary vein** & Umbilical vein carry oxygenated blood)* | Site of gas, nutrient & waste exchange |

* **Double Circulation (Birds & Mammals):** Blood passes through the heart **twice** per complete circuit:
  1. **Pulmonary Circulation:** Right Ventricle → Pulmonary Artery → Lungs (oxygenation) → Pulmonary Veins → Left Atrium.
  2. **Systemic Circulation:** Left Ventricle *(thickest muscular wall)* → Aorta → Body tissues → Superior/Inferior Vena Cava → Right Atrium.
  * *(Hepatic Portal System: Hepatic portal vein carries nutrient-rich blood from intestine to liver before reaching heart).*

#### Blood Composition (Fluid Connective Tissue; pH 7.35–7.45; ~5–6 L)

* **1. Plasma (55% of blood):** ~90–92% water, ~7–8% plasma proteins (**Albumin** [osmotic balance], **Globulins** [antibodies/immunity], **Fibrinogen & Prothrombin** [clotting]), dissolved electrolytes, nutrients, hormones, wastes.
  * **Serum = Plasma − Clotting factors (Fibrinogen).**
* **2. Formed Elements (45% of blood):**

| Component | Count (per mm³) | Lifespan | Site of Origin & Destruction | Key Characteristics & Functions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **RBCs (Erythrocytes)** | 4.5–5.5 million | **120 days** | Origin: **Red Bone Marrow**; Graveyard: **Spleen** (& Liver) | Biconcave, **enucleated** in mammals *(Exceptions: **Camel & Llama** have nucleated RBCs)*; contain Haemoglobin (`Fe²⁺`) for `O₂`/`CO₂` transport |
| **WBCs (Leukocytes)** | 6,000–8,000 | Few hours to days (memory cells longer) | Bone marrow & Lymphoid organs | Nucleated, colourless; amoeboid movement (**diapedesis**); immune defense |
| **Platelets (Thrombocytes)** | 1.5–3.5 lakh | 7–10 days | Fragments of **Megakaryocytes** in bone marrow | Enucleated cell fragments; **blood clotting** (count drops sharply in **Dengue**) |

* **5 Types of WBCs:**
  * *Granulocytes:* **Neutrophils** (60–65% — **most abundant**, phagocytic), **Eosinophils** (2–3% — **allergic reactions** & parasitic infections), **Basophils** (0.5–1% — **least abundant**, secrete **Histamine, Serotonin, Heparin** for inflammation).
  * *Agranulocytes:* **Lymphocytes** (20–25% — **B-cells** [humoral/antibodies] & **T-cells** [cell-mediated immunity]), **Monocytes** (6–8% — **largest WBC**, differentiate into phagocytic **Macrophages**).

* **Blood Clotting Cascade (Coagulation):**
  1. Injured tissues & platelets release **Thromboplastin**, forming **Thrombokinase** complex.
  2. Inactive **Prothrombin** (made in liver) $\xrightarrow{\text{Thrombokinase},\ \text{Ca}^{2+}}$ Active **Thrombin**.
  3. Soluble **Fibrinogen** $\xrightarrow{\text{Thrombin}}$ Insoluble **Fibrin** threads (meshwork trapping blood cells = **Clot**).
  * **Essential Clotting Factors:** **Vitamin K** (required in liver to synthesize Prothrombin & factors II, VII, IX, X) and **Calcium ions (`Ca²⁺`)**.
  * **Anticoagulants:** **Heparin** (natural in human body; produced by mast cells/basophils/liver); **Hirudin** (leech saliva); **Sodium citrate / EDTA** (used in blood banks).

#### Blood Groups & Rh Factor

* **ABO System:** Discovered by **Karl Landsteiner (1900)**; AB group discovered by **Decastello & Sturli (1902)**.

| Blood Group | Antigen on RBC | Antibody in Plasma | Can Donate To | Can Receive From |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A** | A | anti-B | A, AB | A, O |
| **B** | B | anti-A | B, AB | B, O |
| **AB** *(Universal Recipient)* | A & B | **None** | AB | **A, B, AB, O** |
| **O** *(Universal Donor)* | **None** | anti-A & anti-B | **A, B, AB, O** | O |

* **Rh Factor:** Discovered by **Karl Landsteiner & Alexander Wiener (1940)** in the **Rhesus monkey** (*Macaca mulatta*). ~85% humans are **Rh⁺** (have D-antigen).
  * **True Universal Donor:** **O⁻** (O-negative). **True Universal Recipient:** **AB⁺** (AB-positive).
* **Erythroblastosis Fetalis (Haemolytic Disease of Newborn):** Occurs when an **Rh⁻ mother** carries an **Rh⁺ fetus**. First pregnancy is usually safe, but during first delivery fetal Rh⁺ RBCs enter maternal blood, stimulating **anti-Rh antibodies** in mother. In a **second Rh⁺ pregnancy**, maternal anti-Rh IgG antibodies cross the placenta and destroy fetal RBCs (severe anaemia & jaundice). Prevented by injecting **anti-Rh antibodies (RhoGAM)** to the mother immediately after the first birth.

### 4.4 Excretory System

#### Modes of Nitrogenous Excretion

| Mode | Excretory Product | Toxicity & Water Needed | Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ammonotelism** | **Ammonia (`NH₃`)** | Most toxic; maximum water | Bony fishes, Aquatic amphibians (tadpoles), Aquatic insects |
| **Ureotelism** | **Urea (`CO(NH₂)₂`)** *(made in Liver)* | Moderate toxicity; moderate water | **Mammals (Humans)**, Adult frogs, Marine cartilaginous fishes (**Sharks**) |
| **Uricotelism** | **Uric Acid** | Least toxic; minimum water (paste/pellets) | **Birds, Reptiles, Insects, Land snails** |

* **Excretory Organs in Lower Animals:** Flame cells / Protonephridia (Platyhelminthes/Planaria), Nephridia (Earthworm/Annelids), **Malpighian tubules** (Insects/Cockroach), Antennary / Green glands (Prawn/Crustaceans).
* **Human Excretory System:** Pair of **Kidneys** (bean-shaped, retroperitoneal; right kidney slightly lower due to liver; outer **Cortex** + inner **Medulla**), 2 Ureters, Urinary Bladder, Urethra.
* **Nephron (~1 million per kidney):** Structural and functional unit of kidney.
  1. **Bowman's Capsule & Glomerulus** (Malpighian Corpuscle): **Ultrafiltration** under high glomerular capillary hydrostatic pressure (afferent arteriole is wider than efferent arteriole). Filters everything except blood cells & large plasma proteins.
  2. **Proximal Convoluted Tubule (PCT):** Cuboidal brush-border epithelium; **maximum reabsorption (~70–80%)** of electrolytes & water, and **100% of glucose & amino acids**.
  3. **Loop of Henle:** Hairpin loop; Descending limb (permeable to water), Ascending limb (impermeable to water, actively transports electrolytes); establishes medullary osmolarity gradient (**Counter-current mechanism**) to **concentrate urine**.
  4. **Distal Convoluted Tubule (DCT):** Conditional reabsorption of `Na⁺` (**Aldosterone**) and water (**ADH / Vasopressin**); tubular secretion of `H⁺`, `K⁺`, `NH₃` for blood pH homeostasis.
  5. **Collecting Duct:** Final water reabsorption under ADH; empties into renal pelvis.
* **Urine Composition & Urochrome:** ~1–1.5 L/day; ~95% water, ~2% urea, ~2.6% salts/creatinine/uric acid; pH **~6.0** (slightly acidic). Pale yellow colour is due to **Urochrome** (pigment derived from haemoglobin/bilirubin breakdown). *Glycosuria* (glucose in urine) indicates Diabetes mellitus.
* **Dialysis (Haemodialysis):** Artificial kidney used during renal failure (**Uraemia** — toxic urea buildup). Blood is pumped through a semipermeable cellophane tube bathed in dialysing fluid (same osmotic composition as plasma, **without nitrogenous wastes**); urea diffuses out, and heparin-treated blood returns to the patient.
* **Kidney Stones (Renal Calculi):** Hard crystalline masses formed inside kidney, most commonly composed of **Calcium Oxalate (`CaC₂O₄`)**.

### 4.5 Nervous System & Sense Organs

* **Neuron (Nerve Cell):** Structural and functional unit of nervous system; **longest cell in the human body** (up to ~1 m); lacks centrosome/centrioles (**cannot divide** after birth).
  * **Parts:** **Cyton / Cell body** (contains **Nissl's granules** for protein synthesis), **Dendrites** (short branched processes carrying impulses *towards* cyton), **Axon** (long fibre carrying impulses *away* from cyton), **Myelin Sheath** (lipid insulating cover formed by **Schwann cells** in PNS; gaps called **Nodes of Ranvier** allow fast saltatory conduction).
  * **Synapse & Neurotransmitters:** Junction between axon terminal of one neuron and dendrite of next. Impulse crosses the synaptic cleft via chemical **Neurotransmitters** such as **Acetylcholine (ACh)**, **Dopamine**, **Serotonin**, **GABA**, and **Noradrenaline**.

#### Human Brain (~1,300–1,400 g; protected by Cranium, 3 Meninges [Dura, Arachnoid, Pia mater] & CSF)

| Division | Part | Key Functions (High-Yield SSC) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Forebrain** *(Prosencephalon)* | **Cerebrum** *(Largest part; ~2/3 of brain; hemispheres joined by **Corpus Callosum**)* | **Intelligence, memory, thinking, reasoning, willpower, voluntary actions, speech & sensory perception** |
| **Forebrain** | **Thalamus** | Major **relay station** for sensory and motor signals going to cerebral cortex |
| **Forebrain** | **Hypothalamus** | **Body temperature regulation (thermostat: 37°C / 98.6°F)**, **hunger, thirst, sleep-wake, emotions**; links nervous & endocrine systems |
| **Midbrain** *(Mesencephalon)* | **Corpora Quadrigemina** | **Visual and auditory reflexes**; connects forebrain & hindbrain |
| **Hindbrain** *(Rhombencephalon)* | **Cerebellum** *(2nd largest part; "Little brain")* | **Body balance, posture, equilibrium, and fine coordination of voluntary muscles** *(impaired by alcohol)* |
| **Hindbrain** | **Pons** | Bridge between brain parts; **pneumotaxic centre** (regulates breathing rhythm) |
| **Hindbrain** | **Medulla Oblongata** *(lowest part)* | **Involuntary vital functions:** heartbeat, breathing rate, blood pressure, swallowing, coughing, sneezing, vomiting |

*(Brain Stem = Midbrain + Pons + Medulla Oblongata).*

* **Peripheral & Autonomic Nervous System:**
  * **Cranial Nerves:** **12 pairs** (arise from brain; **Vagus [X]** is the longest cranial nerve, supplying viscera; **Optic [II]** for vision; **Olfactory [I]** for smell; **Auditory [VIII]** for hearing & balance).
  * **Spinal Nerves:** **31 pairs** (arise from spinal cord; all are mixed nerves: 8 Cervical, 12 Thoracic, 5 Lumbar, 5 Sacral, 1 Coccygeal).
  * **ANS:** **Sympathetic** ("Fight or Flight" — dilates pupil, raises heart rate) vs **Parasympathetic** ("Rest and Digest" — constricts pupil, slows heart rate).
* **Reflex Arc (Discovered by Marshall Hall):** Immediate, automatic involuntary response controlled primarily by the **Spinal Cord**:
  `Stimulus → Receptor → Sensory (Afferent) Neuron → Interneuron (Spinal Cord) → Motor (Efferent) Neuron → Effector (Muscle/Gland) → Response`

#### Sense Organs: Human Eye & Ear

* **Human Eye (Image on Retina is Real & Inverted):**
  * **Sclera & Cornea:** Outer fibrous coat; anterior transparent **Cornea** is avascular (no blood vessels) and is the **part transplanted in eye donation**.
  * **Choroid, Iris & Pupil:** Middle vascular layer; **Iris** (muscular diaphragm giving eye its colour) controls the size of the **Pupil** (aperture regulating light entry). Crystalline convex lens focuses light.
  * **Retina (Innermost layer):**
    * **Rods** (~120 million): Sensitive to dim light (**black & white / scotopic vision**); contain **Rhodopsin (Visual purple)** synthesized from **Vitamin A (Retinal)** + Opsin → Vitamin A deficiency causes **Night blindness (Nyctalopia)**.
    * **Cones** (~6–7 million): Sensitive to bright light & **colour vision** (Red, Green, Blue); contain **Iodopsin**; concentrated at **Fovea centralis (Yellow spot)** for sharpest vision.
    * **Blind Spot:** Point where optic nerve exits retina (no rods or cones → no image formed).
  * **Eye Defects:** **Myopia** (Short-sightedness → **Concave lens**); **Hypermetropia** (Long-sightedness → **Convex lens**); **Presbyopia** (Old age → **Bifocal lens**); **Astigmatism** (Uneven corneal curvature → **Cylindrical lens**); **Cataract** (Cloudy lens → Surgery).
* **Human Ear (Hearing + Balance/Equilibrium):**
  * **Outer Ear:** Pinna → Auditory canal → **Tympanic membrane (Eardrum)**.
  * **Middle Ear:** Contains **3 Ear Ossicles** arranged from eardrum inward that amplify sound ~20×: **Malleus** (Hammer) → **Incus** (Anvil) → **Stapes** (Stirrup — **smallest bone in the human body!**). **Eustachian tube** connects middle ear to pharynx to equalize air pressure.
  * **Inner Ear:** **Cochlea** (snail-like coiled organ housing **Organ of Corti** — **hearing**); **Vestibular apparatus** (3 Semicircular canals + Utricle & Saccule — **body balance & posture**).

## 5. Endocrine System, Nutrition, Vitamins & Diseases

### 5.1 Endocrine Glands & Hormones

* **Exocrine vs Endocrine:** Exocrine glands have ducts (Salivary, Sweat, Tear, Liver); **Endocrine glands are ductless** and pour **hormones** directly into blood. **Mixed (Heterocrine) glands:** Pancreas, Testes, Ovaries.
* **Father of Endocrinology:** Thomas Addison. **First hormone discovered:** **Secretin** (Bayliss & Starling, 1902).

| Gland | Location | Hormones Secreted | Primary Function | Hypo / Hypersecretion Disorders |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Hypothalamus** | Forebrain | Releasing & Inhibiting hormones (TRH, GnRH, GHRH, Somatostatin); synthesizes Oxytocin & ADH | **"Master of Master Gland"**; controls anterior pituitary | Disrupts pituitary & homeostasis |

| **Pituitary (Posterior)** | Posterior lobe | **Oxytocin** & **ADH (Vasopressin)** *(stored & released)* | **Oxytocin:** uterine contraction & milk ejection ("Birth/Love hormone"); **ADH:** water reabsorption in kidneys | **ADH Hypo:** **Diabetes Insipidus** (excessive dilute urine, *normal blood sugar*) |
| **Pineal Gland** | Dorsal forebrain | **Melatonin** | Regulates **24-hr circadian rhythm / sleep-wake cycle** ("Biological clock") | Sleep-wake disorders |

| **Testes** *(Leydig cells)* | Scrotum (Male) | **Testosterone** (Androgen) | Spermatogenesis; male secondary sexual characteristics | Hypogonadism |
| **Ovaries** | Pelvic cavity (Female) | **Estrogen** (Graafian follicle); **Progesterone** (Corpus luteum); Relaxin | **Estrogen:** female secondary characters; **Progesterone:** **"Pregnancy hormone"** (maintains endometrium) | Irregular menstruation / infertility |

## 6. Plant Physiology, Branches & Scientific Names

### 6.1 Plant Tissues

* **Meristematic Tissues** *(Actively dividing cells, thin cellulosic wall, dense cytoplasm, prominent nucleus, **no vacuoles**)*:
  * **Apical Meristem:** Root & shoot tips → **Primary growth** (increases length).
  * **Intercalary Meristem:** Base of leaves or internodes (e.g., grasses, bamboo) → elongation of internodes.
  * **Lateral Meristem:** Vascular cambium & cork cambium → **Secondary growth** (increases girth/thickness).
* **Permanent Tissues** *(Differentiated; lost power of division)*:
  * **Simple Permanent Tissues:**
    * **Parenchyma:** Living, thin-walled, large intercellular spaces; food storage. Special types: **Chlorenchyma** (contains chlorophyll → photosynthesis) and **Aerenchyma** (large air cavities → buoyancy in aquatic plants like lotus/hydrilla).
    * **Collenchyma:** Living, unevenly thickened corners (cellulose + pectin); provides **flexibility & mechanical support** (allows stems/leaves to bend without breaking).
    * **Sclerenchyma:** **Dead** at maturity, thick **lignified** cell walls, no protoplasm; provides hardness & rigidity (fibres & sclereids; e.g., **husk of coconut**, gritty texture of pear/guava, jute & hemp).
  * **Complex Permanent (Vascular) Tissues:**
    * **Xylem:** **Unidirectional** upward transport of **Water & Minerals** from roots to leaves. Elements: Tracheids (dead), Vessels (dead; *absent in most gymnosperms*), Xylem fibres (dead), and **Xylem parenchyma *(ONLY living element)***.
    * **Phloem:** **Bidirectional** transport (**Translocation**) of **Food (Sucrose)** from leaves to storage/growing regions. Elements: Sieve tubes (living, **enucleated** at maturity), Companion cells (living, nucleated, control sieve tubes), Phloem parenchyma (living), and **Phloem fibres / Bast fibres *(ONLY dead element; e.g., jute, flax, hemp)***.

### 6.2 Photosynthesis, Respiration & Transpiration

* **Photosynthesis:** `6CO₂ + 12H₂O —(Sunlight, Chlorophyll)→ C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂↑ + 6H₂O`
  * **Chlorophyll:** Contains central **Magnesium (`Mg²⁺`)** atom; absorbs **Blue & Red** wavelengths maximally and reflects Green (**highest rate in Red light**, lowest in Green).
  * **Light Reaction (Hill Reaction):** Occurs in **Grana (Thylakoid membranes)**; **photolysis of water** releases **`O₂`** (comes from **`H₂O`**, NOT `CO₂`); generates **ATP & NADPH**.
  * **Dark Reaction (Calvin / `C₃` Cycle):** Occurs in **Stroma**; light-independent enzymatic reduction of `CO₂` into glucose using ATP + NADPH; key enzyme is **RuBisCO** *(most abundant protein on Earth)*. *(`C₄` / Hatch–Slack plants like Maize, Sugarcane, Sorghum show Kranz anatomy).*
* **Plant Respiration:** Occurs **24×7** (`O₂` absorbed, `CO₂` released) through stomata, lenticels (woody stems), and root hairs. **Respiratory Quotient (`RQ = CO₂ evolved / O₂ consumed`):** Carbohydrates = `1.0`, Fats ≈ `0.7`, Proteins ≈ `0.8–0.9`, Anaerobic = `∞`.
* **Transpiration:** Evaporation of water vapour from aerial parts (~90% through **Stomata**, rest via Cuticle & Lenticels).
  * **Stomata:** Guarded by two **Guard cells** (kidney-shaped in dicots, dumbbell-shaped in monocots/grasses); opening/closing is regulated by **`K⁺` ion** transport & turgor pressure.
  * **Instrument:** Rate measured by **Ganong's Potometer**. Increases with temperature, wind, light; decreases with high humidity. Transpiration pull (Cohesion–Tension theory) drives **ascent of sap**.
  * **Guttation:** Exudation of liquid water droplets from leaf margins through **Hydathodes** due to positive root pressure.

## 7. Last 10 Years SSC CHSL / CGL Biology PYQs (35 Solved Questions)

**Q1. Which cell organelle is known as the "Suicide Bag" of the cell?** *(SSC CGL / CHSL)*
A) Mitochondria  B) Lysosome  C) Golgi apparatus  D) Ribosome
**Answer:** **B) Lysosome**
**Explanation:** Lysosomes contain hydrolytic digestive enzymes; when the cell is damaged, they burst and digest the cell itself (autolysis).

**Q2. Which cell organelle other than the nucleus contains its own DNA?** *(SSC CGL)*
A) Endoplasmic Reticulum  B) Golgi body  C) Mitochondria  D) Lysosome
**Answer:** **C) Mitochondria**
**Explanation:** Both **Mitochondria** and **Plastids (Chloroplasts)** are semi-autonomous organelles possessing their own circular DNA and 70S ribosomes.

**Q3. In the Five Kingdom Classification proposed by R.H. Whittaker (1969), prokaryotes are placed under which kingdom?** *(SSC CHSL / CGL)*
A) Protista  B) Fungi  C) Monera  D) Plantae
**Answer:** **C) Monera**
**Explanation:** **Monera** includes all unicellular prokaryotes (Bacteria, Cyanobacteria, Mycoplasma); **Protista** includes unicellular eukaryotes.

**Q4. Which nitrogenous base is present in RNA instead of Thymine?** *(SSC CGL / GD)*
A) Adenine  B) Guanine  C) Cytosine  D) Uracil
**Answer:** **D) Uracil**
**Explanation:** DNA contains A, T, G, C; RNA replaces Thymine (T) with **Uracil (U)**.

**Q5. Which phase of mitosis is best suited to study the morphology and number of chromosomes?** *(SSC CGL)*
A) Prophase  B) Metaphase  C) Anaphase  D) Telophase
**Answer:** **B) Metaphase**
**Explanation:** During **Metaphase**, chromosomes are maximally condensed and aligned neatly along the equatorial plate.

**Q6. What is the phenotypic ratio of a Mendelian monohybrid cross in the $F_2$ generation?** *(SSC CHSL / GD)*
A) 1:1  B) 3:1  C) 9:3:3:1  D) 1:2:1
**Answer:** **B) 3:1**
**Explanation:** In a monohybrid cross, the $F_2$ **phenotypic ratio is 3:1** and **genotypic ratio is 1:2:1** (9:3:3:1 is the dihybrid phenotypic ratio).

**Q7. Which gas was absent in Earth's primitive reducing atmosphere according to the Oparin–Haldane hypothesis?** *(SSC CGL)*
A) Methane  B) Ammonia  C) Hydrogen  D) Free Oxygen
**Answer:** **D) Free Oxygen**
**Explanation:** Primitive Earth had a reducing atmosphere (`CH₄`, `NH₃`, `H₂`, `H₂O` vapour) with **no free `O₂`**.

**Q8. Lichens are symbiotic associations between:** *(SSC CGL / CHSL)*
A) Bacteria and Fungi  B) Algae and Fungi  C) Fungi and Roots of higher plants  D) Protozoa and Algae
**Answer:** **B) Algae and Fungi**
**Explanation:** **Lichens** = Algae (Phycobiont) + Fungi (Mycobiont) and act as bioindicators of **`SO₂` air pollution**. *(Mycorrhiza = Fungi + higher plant roots).*

**Q9. In an ecological pyramid of energy, what percentage of energy is transferred from one trophic level to the next (Lindeman's Law)?** *(SSC CHSL / CGL)*
A) 1%  B) 10%  C) 50%  D) 90%
**Answer:** **B) 10%**
**Explanation:** Under **Lindeman's 10% Law**, only ~10% of energy passes to the next trophic level; ~90% is lost as heat/metabolism. The energy pyramid is always upright.

**Q10. Which organism is the smallest known living cell without a cell wall and can survive without oxygen?** *(SSC CGL)*
A) *Escherichia coli*  B) *Amoeba*  C) *Mycoplasma*  D) *Nostoc*
**Answer:** **C) *Mycoplasma***
**Explanation:** ***Mycoplasma*** (PPLO) lacks a cell wall (hence resistant to penicillin) and is the smallest free-living prokaryote.

**Q11. Which enzyme converts proteins into proteoses and peptones in the acidic medium of the stomach?** *(SSC CGL)*
A) Trypsin  B) Pepsin  C) Amylase  D) Lipase
**Answer:** **B) Pepsin**
**Explanation:** Inactive Pepsinogen is activated by gastric **HCl** (pH 1.5–2.0) into **Pepsin**, which initiates protein digestion in the stomach.

**Q12. Bile juice is produced by the ______ and stored in the ______, though it contains no enzymes.** *(SSC CHSL / GD)*
A) Pancreas, Duodenum  B) Liver, Gallbladder  C) Spleen, Liver  D) Stomach, Ileum
**Answer:** **B) Liver, Gallbladder**
**Explanation:** **Liver** secretes bile (stored in **Gallbladder**); its bile salts **emulsify fats** in the duodenum without containing any digestive enzymes.

**Q13. What is the adult human dental formula?** *(SSC CHSL / CGL)*
A) 2102/2102  B) 2123/2123  C) 2122/2122  D) 1123/1123
**Answer:** **B) 2123/2123**
**Explanation:** Adults have 32 permanent teeth (`(2123/2123) × 2`); children have 20 milk teeth (`(2102/2102) × 2`, lacking premolars and 3rd molars).

**Q14. Muscle fatigue during strenuous exercise occurs due to the accumulation of:** *(SSC CGL / CHSL / GD)*
A) Pyruvic acid  B) Citric acid  C) Lactic acid  D) Acetic acid
**Answer:** **C) Lactic acid**
**Explanation:** Under oxygen deficit, skeletal muscles perform anaerobic respiration converting glucose into **Lactic acid** + 2 ATP.

**Q15. Which blood vessel carries deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle to the lungs?** *(SSC CGL / CHSL)*

**Answer:** **C) Pulmonary artery**
**Explanation:** Arteries carry oxygenated blood except the **Pulmonary artery** (carries deoxygenated blood to lungs); veins carry deoxygenated blood except the **Pulmonary vein**.

**Q16. Which organ is called the "Graveyard of RBCs"?** *(SSC CGL / GD)*

**Answer:** **C) Spleen**

**Q17. Which mineral ion and vitamin are essential for blood clotting?** *(SSC CGL / CHSL)*

**Answer:** **B) `Ca²⁺` and Vitamin K**
**Explanation:** **Vitamin K** is required by the liver to synthesize prothrombin, while **`Ca²⁺`** activates prothrombin → thrombin to convert fibrinogen into insoluble fibrin.

**Q18. Erythroblastosis fetalis occurs when:** *(SSC CGL)*

**Answer:** **B) Rh⁻ mother carries Rh⁺ fetus**
**Explanation:** Maternal anti-Rh antibodies formed after the first Rh⁺ delivery cross the placenta in a subsequent Rh⁺ pregnancy and haemolyse fetal RBCs.

**Q19. Which part of the nephron is primarily responsible for ultrafiltration of blood?** *(SSC CHSL / CGL)*

**Answer:** **C) Glomerulus & Bowman's capsule**
**Explanation:** High pressure in the **Glomerulus** filters plasma into **Bowman's capsule** (ultrafiltration); maximum reabsorption occurs in PCT, and urine is concentrated in Loop of Henle.
