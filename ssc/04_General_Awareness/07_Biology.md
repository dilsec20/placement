## Cell Biology: Structure, Organelles & Genetics

### The Cell: Unit of Life
- **Discovered by:** **Robert Hooke** (1665, in cork slices; dead cells).
- **First living cell observed by:** **Antonie van Leeuwenhoek** (1674).
- **Cell Theory:** Proposed by **Matthias Schleiden** & **Theodor Schwann** (1839). Modified by **Rudolf Virchow** (*"Omnis cellula-e-cellula"* — all cells arise from pre-existing cells).

### Cell Organelles & Their Popular Nicknames
| Organelle | Nickname / Function | Key Facts |
|-----------|---------------------|-----------|
| **Mitochondria** | **"Powerhouse of the Cell"** | Generates cellular energy in form of **ATP** (Adenosine Triphosphate) via cellular respiration; has its own DNA and 70S ribosomes |
| **Ribosomes** | **"Protein Factories"** | Smallest organelle; site of protein synthesis; not bound by any membrane (found in both prokaryotes and eukaryotes) |
| **Lysosomes** | **"Suicidal Bags of the Cell"** | Contain rich hydrolytic digestive enzymes; digest worn-out cell parts and foreign invaders |
| **Golgi Apparatus** | **"Post Office / Traffic Police of Cell"** | Packaging, modification, and dispatch of proteins/lipids; discovered by Camillo Golgi |
| **Endoplasmic Reticulum (ER)** | "Transport Network" | Rough ER (has ribosomes; synthesizes proteins); Smooth ER (synthesizes lipids/steroids and detoxifies drugs) |
| **Chloroplasts** | **"Kitchen of the Plant Cell"** | Site of photosynthesis; contains green pigment **Chlorophyll** (contains **Magnesium** at its center) |
| **Nucleus** | **"Brain / Control Center of the Cell"** | Discovered by **Robert Brown** (1831); contains genetic material (Chromatin / DNA) |
| **Centrosome / Centriole** | "Cell Division Organizer" | Present **only in animal cells**; absent in higher plant cells |
| **Vacuole** | "Storage Sac" | Very large and central in plant cells (bounded by **Tonoplast** membrane); small/temporary in animal cells |
| **Cell Wall** | "Rigid Outer Boundary" | Found in plants (made of **Cellulose**), fungi (made of **Chitin**), bacteria (Peptidoglycan); **absent in animal cells** |

### DNA & Genetics
- **DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid):** Genetic blueprint of organisms.
  - Double helix model discovered by **James Watson and Francis Crick** (1953, Nobel Prize 1962).
  - Nitrogenous bases: Adenine ($A$), Thymine ($T$), Guanine ($G$), Cytosine ($C$). Base pairing: $A=T$, $G\equiv C$.
  - In RNA: Thymine is replaced by **Uracil ($U$)**.
- **Chromosomes in Humans:** **46 chromosomes (23 pairs)**.
  - 22 pairs of Autosomes + 1 pair of Allosomes / Sex Chromosomes ($XY$ in males, $XX$ in females).
- **Father of Genetics:** **Gregor Johann Mendel** (performed experiments on Garden Pea: *Pisum sativum*).

## Human Anatomy & Physiological Systems

### 1. Digestive System
```
Mouth (Salivary Amylase / Ptyalin digests starch into maltose)
  ↓
Oesophagus (Food moves by peristalsis; no digestion here)
  ↓
Stomach (Gastric juice: HCl kills bacteria + Pepsin breaks proteins + Mucus protects stomach lining)
  ↓
Small Intestine (Duodenum → Jejunum → Ileum)
  ├── Liver secretes BILE (stored in GALLBLADDER; emulsifies fats; contains no enzymes)
  ├── Pancreas secretes PANCREATIC JUICE (Trypsin for protein, Amylase for starch, Lipase for fats)
  └── Complete digestion occurs here; nutrients absorbed via microscopic finger-like VILLI
  ↓
Large Intestine (Absorbs water and salts; eliminates undigested faeces via rectum and anus)
```
- **Largest gland in the human body:** **Liver** (regenerates quickly; converts toxic ammonia to urea).

### 2. Circulatory System & Blood
- **Discovered by:** **William Harvey** (1628).
- **Human Heart:** 4 chambers (2 Atria, 2 Ventricles).
  - **Pacemaker of Heart:** **SA Node** (Sinoatrial node in right atrium) initiates heartbeat.
  - Normal heartbeat: **72 beats per minute**.
  - Normal blood pressure: **120/80 mmHg** (measured by **Sphygmomanometer**; 120 = Systolic, 80 = Diastolic).
  - **Arteries:** Carry oxygenated blood away from heart (Except **Pulmonary Artery** which carries deoxygenated blood to lungs). Thick, elastic walls, no valves.
  - **Veins:** Carry deoxygenated blood toward heart (Except **Pulmonary Vein** which carries oxygenated blood from lungs). Have valves to prevent backflow.

#### Blood Composition:
1. **Plasma ($55\%$):** Liquid matrix containing $90-92\%$ water, proteins (Albumin, Globulin, Fibrinogen for clotting).
2. **Formed Elements ($45\%$):**
   - **RBC (Erythrocytes):** Biconcave, no nucleus (in mammals, except camel and llama); contains **Hemoglobin** (iron-protein, binds oxygen); lifespan = **120 days**; formed in red bone marrow, destroyed in **Spleen** (known as the *"Graveyard of RBCs"*).
   - **WBC (Leucocytes):** Nucleated; fight infections (body's soldiers); lifespan = 2–4 days. Types: Monocytes (largest), Lymphocytes (produce antibodies), Neutrophils (most abundant phagocytes).
   - **Platelets (Thrombocytes):** Aid in **blood clotting**; lifespan = 7–10 days. Essential vitamin for clotting: **Vitamin K**; essential mineral: **Calcium ($Ca^{2+}$)**.

#### Blood Groups (Discovered by Karl Landsteiner):
| Blood Group | Antigens on RBC | Antibodies in Plasma | Can Donate To | Can Receive From |
|-------------|-----------------|----------------------|---------------|------------------|
| **A** | A | Anti-B | A, AB | A, O |
| **B** | B | Anti-A | B, AB | B, O |
| **AB** | A and B | None | AB only | A, B, AB, O (**Universal Recipient / Acceptor: $AB^+$**) |
| **O** | None | Anti-A and Anti-B | A, B, AB, O (**Universal Donor: $O^-$**) | O only |

### 3. Respiratory System
- Functional unit of lungs: **Alveoli** (site of gas exchange by diffusion).
- Normal respiratory rate: 12–16 breaths/min.

### 4. Excretory System
- Functional and structural unit of Kidney: **Nephron** ($\approx 1\text{ million}$ per kidney).
- Kidneys filter metabolic waste (urea, uric acid) from blood to form urine.
- **Dialysis (Artificial Kidney):** Works on the principle of **Osmosis and Diffusion**.
- Normal urine color is pale yellow due to the pigment **Urochrome** (breakdown of hemoglobin).

### 5. Nervous System
- Structural unit: **Neuron** (longest cell in the human body).
- **Brain Parts & Functions:**
  - **Cerebrum:** Largest part of brain; center of intelligence, memory, thoughts, consciousness, sensations.
  - **Cerebellum:** Controls and coordinates **body balance, equilibrium, and posture** (heavily affected by alcohol consumption!).
  - **Medulla Oblongata:** Controls **involuntary activities** (heartbeat, breathing, blood pressure, swallowing, vomiting, coughing).
  - **Hypothalamus:** Regulates **body temperature**, hunger, thirst, sleep, emotions, and controls the pituitary gland.

## Endocrine Glands & Hormones

| Gland | Location | Major Hormones | Function / Deficiency Disease |
|-------|----------|----------------|-------------------------------|
| **Pituitary Gland** | Base of brain | Growth Hormone (GH), TSH, ACTH, Oxytocin | Known as the **"Master Gland"**; Hypersecretion $\rightarrow$ Gigantism; Hyposecretion $\rightarrow$ Dwarfism |
| **Thyroid Gland** | Neck (trachea) | **Thyroxine** (contains **Iodine**) | Regulates basal metabolic rate (BMR); Deficiency of iodine $\rightarrow$ **Goitre** (swelling of neck); Cretinism in children |
| **Pancreas** | Abdomen (Islets of Langerhans) | **Insulin** ($\beta$-cells), **Glucagon** ($\alpha$-cells) | **Mixed gland** (both exocrine and endocrine); Insulin lowers blood sugar; Deficiency causes **Diabetes Mellitus** |
| **Adrenal Glands** | On top of kidneys | **Adrenaline** (Epinephrine) | **"Emergency Hormone" / "Fight or Flight" hormone**; rapidly increases heart rate, BP, glucose supply |
| **Pineal Gland** | Brain | Melatonin | Regulates circadian rhythm (24-hour sleep-wake cycle) |
| **Testes (Male)** | Scrotum | Testosterone | Male secondary sexual characteristics, sperm production |
| **Ovaries (Female)** | Pelvis | Estrogen, Progesterone | Female secondary sexual characteristics, menstrual cycle |

## Vitamins, Minerals & Deficiency Diseases

### Classification of Vitamins (Discovered by Casimir Funk, 1912)
- **Fat-Soluble Vitamins:** **A, D, E, K** (Stored in the liver and adipose tissue).
- **Water-Soluble Vitamins:** **B-Complex and Vitamin C** (Excreted in urine; need regular intake; destroyed by overcooking).

| Vitamin | Chemical Name | Main Dietary Sources | Deficiency Disease / Symptoms |
|---------|---------------|----------------------|--------------------------------|
| **Vitamin A** | **Retinol** | Carrot, papaya, mango, milk, fish liver oil | **Night Blindness (Nyctalopia)**, Xerophthalmia (dry cornea) |
| **Vitamin B1** | **Thiamine** | Whole cereals, yeast, unpolished rice | **Beriberi** (nerve damage, muscle wasting) |
| **Vitamin B2** | **Riboflavin** | Milk, eggs, green leafy vegetables | **Cheilosis** (cracking of corners of mouth), glossitis |
| **Vitamin B3** | **Niacin** (Nicotinic acid) | Meat, fish, peanuts | **Pellagra** (4 Ds: Dermatitis, Diarrhea, Dementia, Death) |
| **Vitamin B6** | Pyridoxine | Nuts, pulses, meat | Anemia, convulsions |
| **Vitamin B9** | **Folic Acid** | Green leafy vegetables, liver | Megaloblastic Anemia, neural tube birth defects |
| **Vitamin B12**| **Cyanocobalamin** (**Contains Cobalt!**) | Meat, fish, dairy (absent in plant foods) | **Pernicious Anemia**, neurological disorders |
| **Vitamin C** | **Ascorbic Acid** | Citrus fruits (Amla is richest source), lemon, orange | **Scurvy** (bleeding gums, delayed wound healing); destroyed by heat |
| **Vitamin D** | **Calciferol** (Sunshine vitamin) | Sunlight synthesis in skin, fish liver oil, milk | **Rickets** in children (soft/bowed bones), **Osteomalacia** in adults |
| **Vitamin E** | **Tocopherol** (Beauty vitamin) | Wheat germ oil, vegetable oils, almonds | Muscular weakness, **Infertility / Sterility**, premature aging |
| **Vitamin K** | **Phylloquinone** | Green leafy vegetables, intestinal bacteria | **Delayed blood clotting** / excessive bleeding from wounds |

### Important Minerals
- **Iron ($Fe$):** Component of hemoglobin; deficiency causes **Anemia**.
- **Iodine ($I$):** Synthesis of thyroxine; deficiency causes **Simple Goitre**.
- **Calcium ($Ca$) & Phosphorus ($P$):** Bone and teeth formation; muscle contraction.

## Infectious Diseases & Pathogens

### Pathogen Classification Chart
```
1. Bacterial Diseases
   ├── Tuberculosis (TB) — Mycobacterium tuberculosis (BCG vaccine)
   ├── Cholera — Vibrio cholerae (contaminated water)
   ├── Typhoid — Salmonella typhi (Widal Test confirms diagnosis)
   ├── Tetanus (Lockjaw) — Clostridium tetani (ATS injection)
   ├── Diphtheria, Pertussis (Whooping cough), Tetanus — DPT Vaccine
   ├── Plague (Black Death) — Yersinia pestis (rat flea vector)
   └── Leprosy (Hansen's Disease) — Mycobacterium leprae

2. Viral Diseases
   ├── Rabies (Hydrophobia) — Rhabdovirus (transmitted by bite of mad dog/cat; Pasteur vaccine)
   ├── Polio — Poliovirus (Oral Polio Vaccine by Albert Sabin; Injectable by Jonas Salk)
   ├── AIDS — HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus; attacks CD4+ T-helper cells; ELISA test)
   ├── Dengue (Breakbone fever) — Flavivirus (transmitted by female Aedes aegypti mosquito)
   ├── Chikungunya & Zika — Transmitted by Aedes mosquito
   ├── Chickenpox — Varicella-zoster virus
   ├── Smallpox — Variola virus (eradicating vaccine discovered by Edward Jenner — Father of Immunology)
   └── Hepatitis (Jaundice) — Hepatitis Virus (attacks liver)

3. Protozoan Diseases
   ├── Malaria — Plasmodium (transmitted by female Anopheles mosquito; treated with Quinine from Cinchona bark)
   ├── Kala-Azar (Black fever) — Leishmania donovani (transmitted by Sandfly)
   ├── Sleeping Sickness — Trypanosoma (transmitted by Tsetse fly)
   └── Amoebic Dysentery — Entamoeba histolytica

4. Fungal Diseases
   ├── Ringworm — Trichophyton / Microsporum
   └── Athlete's Foot — Tinea pedis
```

## Plant Physiology & Botany

### Plant Tissues
- **Xylem:** Complex permanent tissue responsible for **unidirectional transport of water and dissolved minerals** from roots to leaves.
- **Phloem:** Responsible for **bidirectional translocation of synthesized food (sucrose)** from leaves to storage organs.

### Photosynthesis
$$6CO_2 + 12H_2O \xrightarrow[\text{Chlorophyll}]{\text{Sunlight}} C_6H_{12}O_6 + 6O_2 \uparrow + 6H_2O$$
- Light reaction occurs in the **Grana** of chloroplasts (water is split $\rightarrow$ Oxygen released).
- Dark reaction (Calvin cycle) occurs in the **Stroma** of chloroplasts ($CO_2$ is reduced to glucose).

### Plant Hormones (Phytohormones)
| Hormone | Function |
|---------|----------|
| **Auxin** | Cell elongation, apical dominance, phototropism (discovered in oat coleoptiles) |
| **Gibberellins** | Stem elongation, breaking seed dormancy, bolting in plants |
| **Cytokinin** | Promotes rapid **cell division** (mitosis); delays leaf senescence |
| **Abscisic Acid (ABA)** | **Growth inhibitor / Stress hormone**; induces closing of stomata during drought |
| **Ethylene** | **Only gaseous plant hormone**; promotes commercial **ripening of fruits** |

## Last 10 Years PYQs with Detailed Explanations

### PYQ 1 — SSC CHSL 2024
**Q.** Which of the following cell organelles is known as the "Powerhouse of the cell"?
- A) Golgi body
- B) Lysosome
- C) Mitochondria
- D) Ribosome
- **Answer:** **C) Mitochondria** ✅
- *Explanation:* Mitochondria synthesize ATP (cellular energy currency) via oxidative phosphorylation and the Krebs cycle.

### PYQ 2 — SSC CHSL 2023
**Q.** Which vitamin contains the trace mineral Cobalt?
- A) Vitamin B1
- B) Vitamin B2
- C) Vitamin B6
- D) Vitamin B12
- **Answer:** **D) Vitamin B12 (Cyanocobalamin)** ✅
- *Explanation:* Vitamin B12 has a corrin ring containing a central Cobalt ion; its deficiency causes pernicious anemia.

### PYQ 3 — SSC CHSL 2023
**Q.** Which gland in the human body is referred to as both an exocrine and an endocrine (mixed) gland?
- A) Thyroid
- B) Pancreas
- C) Pituitary
- D) Adrenal
- **Answer:** **B) Pancreas** ✅
- *Explanation:* Pancreas acts as exocrine by secreting digestive enzymes into duodenum via duct, and endocrine by secreting insulin and glucagon directly into blood.

### PYQ 4 — SSC CHSL 2022
**Q.** Scurvy is caused due to the deficiency of which of the following vitamins?
- A) Vitamin A
- B) Vitamin B
- C) Vitamin C
- D) Vitamin D
- **Answer:** **C) Vitamin C (Ascorbic acid)** ✅
- *Explanation:* Vitamin C is required for collagen synthesis. Deficiency leads to spongy bleeding gums, skin spots, and slow healing of wounds.

### PYQ 5 — SSC CHSL 2022
**Q.** Which of the following blood groups is known as the Universal Recipient?
- A) Blood group $O^+$
- B) Blood group $O^-$
- C) Blood group $AB^+$
- D) Blood group $AB^-$
- **Answer:** **C) Blood group $AB^+$** ✅
- *Explanation:* $AB^+$ has both A and B antigens and Rh factor on RBCs with zero antibodies in plasma, so it can accept blood from any group. $O^-$ is Universal Donor.

### PYQ 6 — SSC CHSL 2021
**Q.** Which vector mosquito transmits the parasite that causes Malaria in humans?
- A) Male Anopheles mosquito
- B) Female Anopheles mosquito
- C) Female Aedes aegypti
- D) Culex mosquito
- **Answer:** **B) Female Anopheles mosquito** ✅
- *Explanation:* Female Anopheles mosquito transmits the protozoan parasite *Plasmodium*. Aedes transmits dengue and chikungunya.

### PYQ 7 — SSC CHSL 2020
**Q.** Which gaseous plant hormone is primarily responsible for the ripening of fruits?
- A) Auxin
- B) Gibberellin
- C) Ethylene
- D) Abscisic acid
- **Answer:** **C) Ethylene** ✅
- *Explanation:* Ethylene ($C_2H_4$) is a gaseous phytohormone that promotes fruit ripening, leaf abscission, and flower wilting.

### PYQ 8 — SSC CHSL 2019
**Q.** What is the normal average lifespan of human Red Blood Cells (RBCs)?
- A) 30 days
- B) 60 days
- C) 120 days
- D) 365 days
- **Answer:** **C) 120 days** ✅
- *Explanation:* RBCs live for approximately 120 days before being broken down and phagocytosed in the spleen (graveyard of RBCs).

### PYQ 9 — SSC CHSL 2018
**Q.** Which of the following tests is used for the definitive diagnosis of Typhoid fever?
- A) ELISA Test
- B) Widal Test
- C) Mantoux Test
- D) Western Blot Test
- **Answer:** **B) Widal Test** ✅
- *Explanation:* Widal test detects agglutinating antibodies against *Salmonella enterica* serotype Typhi. ELISA is used for HIV/AIDS.

### PYQ 10 — SSC CHSL 2017
**Q.** Which part of the human brain is responsible for maintaining posture and muscular balance?
- A) Cerebrum
- B) Cerebellum
- C) Medulla
- D) Hypothalamus
- **Answer:** **B) Cerebellum** ✅
- *Explanation:* Cerebellum coordinates voluntary muscle movements, maintaining posture, balance, and equilibrium.
