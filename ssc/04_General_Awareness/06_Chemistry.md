## Matter & Physical vs Chemical Changes

### Five States of Matter
1. **Solid:** Definite shape and volume; strong intermolecular attraction.
2. **Liquid:** Indefinite shape, definite volume; fluid.
3. **Gas:** Indefinite shape and volume; highly compressible.
4. **Plasma:** Ionized gas at extremely high temperatures (found in stars, Sun, neon signs, lightning).
5. **Bose-Einstein Condensate (BEC):** Formed by cooling a gas of extremely low density to super-low temperatures close to absolute zero (discovered by Satyendra Nath Bose and Albert Einstein).

### Phase Transitions
- **Sublimation:** Direct conversion from solid to gas without entering liquid state.
  - *Examples:* Camphor, Dry Ice (solid $CO_2$), Ammonium chloride ($NH_4Cl$), Naphthalene balls, Iodine.
- **Deposition:** Direct conversion from gas to solid (e.g., frost).
- **Latent Heat:** Heat absorbed/released during state change without temperature change.
  - *Latent heat of vaporization of water ($22.6 \times 10^5\text{ J/kg}$)* is very high $\rightarrow$ why steam causes worse burns than boiling water at $100^\circ\text{C}$!

### Physical vs Chemical Change
| Parameter | Physical Change | Chemical Change |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------------|
| New Substance | No new substance formed | New substance formed with distinct properties |
| Reversibility | Usually easily reversible | Usually irreversible |
| Examples | Melting of ice, boiling of water, dissolving sugar/salt in water, breaking glass, cutting wood | **Rusting of iron**, **burning of paper/candle**, digestion of food, **curdling of milk**, ripening of fruit |
| *Note* | **Burning of a candle** is BOTH physical (melting of wax) and chemical (burning of wax vapour). |

## Atomic Structure & Radioactivity

### Subatomic Particles
| Particle | Discoverer | Charge | Mass | Location |
|----------|------------|--------|------|----------|
| **Electron ($e^-$)** | **J.J. Thomson** (Cathode Ray experiment) | $-1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$ | $9.1 \times 10^{-31}\text{ kg}$ | Orbiting in shells |
| **Proton ($p^+$)** | **E. Goldstein** (discovered canal rays); named by **Rutherford** | $+1.6 \times 10^{-19}\text{ C}$ | $1.672 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$ | Inside nucleus |
| **Neutron ($n^0$)** | **James Chadwick** (1932) | $0$ (neutral) | $1.674 \times 10^{-27}\text{ kg}$ | Inside nucleus |

- **Nucleus of Atom:** Discovered by **Ernest Rutherford** (Gold Foil $\alpha$-particle scattering experiment). Contains protons and neutrons (collectively called **Nucleons**).

### Important Definitions
- **Atomic Number ($Z$):** Number of protons in the nucleus = Number of electrons (in neutral atom).
- **Mass Number ($A$):** Number of protons + Number of neutrons ($A = Z + N$).
- **Isotopes:** Same atomic number ($Z$), different mass number ($A$).
  - Hydrogen isotopes: Protium ($^1_1H$), Deuterium ($^2_1H$ — forms Heavy Water $D_2O$), Tritium ($^3_1H$ — radioactive!).
  - Carbon-14 ($^{14}C$): Used for **radiocarbon dating** of fossils.
  - Cobalt-60 ($^{60}Co$): Used in cancer radiation therapy.
  - Iodine-131 ($^{131}I$): Used in thyroid treatment.
- **Isobars:** Same mass number ($A$), different atomic number ($Z$).
  - Example: $^{40}_{18}Ar$, $^{40}_{19}K$, $^{40}_{20}Ca$.
- **Isotones:** Different elements having the same number of neutrons ($A - Z$).

## Periodic Table & Periodic Trends

### History of Periodic Table
- **Döbereiner's Triads (1817):** Middle element had average atomic weight of outer two (e.g., $Li, Na, K$).
- **Newlands' Law of Octaves (1864):** Every 8th element had similar properties (applicable only up to Calcium).
- **Mendeleev's Periodic Table (1869):** Arranged elements by increasing **Atomic Mass**. Left gaps for undiscovered elements (Eka-boron = Scandium, Eka-aluminium = Gallium, Eka-silicon = Germanium).
- **Modern Periodic Table (1913):** Designed by **Henry Moseley**. Arranged elements by increasing **Atomic Number**.
  - **7 Periods** (horizontal rows) and **18 Groups** (vertical columns).

### Important Groups
- **Group 1:** Alkali Metals ($Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr$) — soft, highly reactive, stored under kerosene (Sodium & Potassium).
- **Group 2:** Alkaline Earth Metals ($Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra$).
- **Groups 3 to 12:** Transition Elements (d-block metals; form colored compounds).
- **Group 16:** Chalcogens (Oxygen family / ore-forming elements).
- **Group 17:** Halogens ($F, Cl, Br, I, At$) — Fluorine is the most electronegative element; Bromine is the only liquid non-metal.
- **Group 18:** Noble / Inert Gases ($He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn$) — zero valency; Radon is radioactive and absent in normal atmosphere.

### Periodic Trends (Summary Table)
| Property | Left to Right across a Period | Top to Bottom down a Group |
|----------|-------------------------------|----------------------------|
| **Atomic Radius** | **Decreases** (nuclear charge pulls electrons closer) | **Increases** (extra electron shells added) |
| **Ionization Energy** | **Increases** (harder to pull electron) | **Decreases** (outer electron further away) |
| **Electron Affinity** | **Increases** (highest: **Chlorine**) | **Decreases** |
| **Electronegativity** | **Increases** (highest: **Fluorine**) | **Decreases** |
| **Metallic Character** | **Decreases** (metals $\rightarrow$ non-metals) | **Increases** |

## Chemical Bonding & Reactions

### Types of Bonds
1. **Electrovalent / Ionic Bond:** Complete transfer of electrons from metal to non-metal (e.g., $NaCl, MgO, CaCl_2$). High melting/boiling points, conduct electricity in molten/aqueous state.
2. **Covalent Bond:** Mutual sharing of electrons between non-metals (e.g., $CH_4, H_2O, CO_2$). Lower melting points, non-conductors.
3. **Hydrogen Bond:** Attraction between hydrogen and highly electronegative atom ($F, O, N$). Responsible for high boiling point of water and double helix structure of DNA.

### Types of Chemical Reactions
- **Combination:** $A + B \rightarrow AB$ (e.g., $CaO + H_2O \rightarrow Ca(OH)_2$ quicklime to slaked lime).
- **Decomposition:** $AB \rightarrow A + B$ (e.g., $2FeSO_4 \xrightarrow{\Delta} Fe_2O_3 + SO_2 + SO_3$).
- **Displacement:** $Fe + CuSO_4 \rightarrow FeSO_4 + Cu$ (Iron displaces copper from blue vitriol solution; solution turns green).
- **Redox:** Simultaneous Oxidation (loss of electrons / addition of oxygen) and Reduction (gain of electrons / addition of hydrogen).
- **Rusting of Iron:** Hydrated iron oxide ($Fe_2O_3 \cdot xH_2O$).
  - Requires **both Oxygen and Moisture**.
  - Weight of iron **increases** upon rusting!
  - **Prevention:** **Galvanization** (coating iron with a layer of **Zinc**).

## Acids, Bases, Salts & pH Scale

### Properties
- **Acids:** Sour taste, turn **Blue Litmus Red**, produce $H^+$ ions in water. pH $< 7$.
- **Bases:** Bitter taste, soapy feel, turn **Red Litmus Blue**, produce $OH^-$ ions in water. pH $> 7$.
- **Neutral:** pH $= 7$ (Pure distilled water).

### Naturally Occurring Acids
| Source | Natural Acid Present |
|--------|----------------------|
| Vinegar | Acetic Acid ($CH_3COOH$) |
| Sour Milk / Curd | Lactic Acid |
| Lemons, Oranges, Citrus Fruits | Citric Acid |
| Tamarind, Unripe Grapes | Tartaric Acid |
| Tomatoes | Oxalic Acid |
| Ant Sting / Nettle Leaf Sting | Methanoic Acid / Formic Acid ($HCOOH$) |
| Apples | Malic Acid |
| Gastric Juice (Human Stomach) | Hydrochloric Acid ($HCl$) |

### Important Chemical Salts
| Common Name | Chemical Name | Formula | Key Uses |
|-------------|---------------|---------|----------|
| **Baking Soda** | Sodium Hydrogen Carbonate | $NaHCO_3$ | Bakery (releases $CO_2$), fire extinguishers, antacid |
| **Washing Soda** | Sodium Carbonate Decahydrate | $Na_2CO_3 \cdot 10H_2O$ | Laundry, softening permanent hardness of water, glass making |
| **Bleaching Powder** | Calcium Oxychloride | $CaOCl_2$ | Disinfecting drinking water, bleaching cotton & linen |
| **Plaster of Paris (POP)** | Calcium Sulphate Hemihydrate | $CaSO_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}H_2O$ | Bone fractures cast, making statues, false ceilings |
| **Gypsum** | Calcium Sulphate Dihydrate | $CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$ | Mixed with cement to slow down its setting time |
| **Caustic Soda** | Sodium Hydroxide | $NaOH$ | Soap, paper, artificial fibres |
| **Common Salt** | Sodium Chloride | $NaCl$ | Food seasoning, food preservative |

## Metals, Non-Metals, Ores & Alloys

### Exceptional Properties
- **Liquid metal at room temp:** **Mercury ($Hg$)** (Gallium and Caesium melt in the palm of hand).
- **Liquid non-metal at room temp:** **Bromine ($Br_2$)**.
- **Soft metals that can be cut with a knife:** **Sodium ($Na$) and Potassium ($K$)**.
- **Best conductors of electricity:** **Silver ($Ag$)** > Copper ($Cu$) > Gold ($Au$) > Aluminium ($Al$).
- **Poorest metallic conductor of heat:** **Lead ($Pb$)**.
- **Most ductile & malleable metal:** **Gold ($Au$)** (1 gram of gold can be drawn into a 2 km long wire).
- **Non-metal with metallic luster:** **Iodine**.
- **Non-metal that conducts electricity:** **Graphite** (allotrope of carbon).

### Important Ores of Metals
| Metal | Important Ores |
|-------|----------------|
| **Aluminium ($Al$)** | **Bauxite** ($Al_2O_3 \cdot 2H_2O$), Cryolite ($Na_3AlF_6$) |
| **Iron ($Fe$)** | **Haematite** ($Fe_2O_3$), Magnetite ($Fe_3O_4$), Siderite ($FeCO_3$) |
| **Copper ($Cu$)** | **Copper Pyrites** ($CuFeS_2$), Malachite, Cuprite ($Cu_2O$) |
| **Mercury ($Hg$)** | **Cinnabar** ($HgS$) |
| **Lead ($Pb$)** | **Galena** ($PbS$) |
| **Zinc ($Zn$)** | **Zinc Blende** ($ZnS$), Calamine ($ZnCO_3$) |
| **Uranium ($U$)** | **Pitchblende** ($U_3O_8$) |
| **Thorium ($Th$)** | **Monazite sand** (found abundantly in Kerala beaches) |

### Important Alloys & Their Compositions
| Alloy | Composition | Key Applications |
|-------|-------------|------------------|
| **Brass** | Copper ($70\%$) + Zinc ($30\%$) | Utensils, musical instruments, cartridges |
| **Bronze** | Copper ($88\%$) + Tin ($12\%$) | Statues, medals, coins |
| **Solder** | Lead ($50\%$) + Tin ($50\%$) | Joining electric wires (low melting point) |
| **Stainless Steel** | Iron + Chromium ($18\%$) + Nickel ($8\%$) + Carbon | Cutlery, surgical instruments (anti-rust) |
| **German Silver** | Copper + Zinc + Nickel (**Contains NO Silver!**) | Ornaments, imitation jewelry |
| **Duralumin** | Aluminium ($95\%$) + Copper + Magnesium + Manganese | Aircraft bodies, pressure cookers |
| **Amalgam** | Alloy of any metal with **Mercury** (Iron, Platinum, Tungsten do not form amalgam) | Dental fillings ($Ag-Hg$ amalgam) |

## Carbon & Its Allotropes

### Allotropes of Carbon
1. **Diamond:**
   - Giant 3D tetrahedral structure ($sp^3$ hybridized).
   - **Hardest naturally occurring substance known**.
   - Bad conductor of electricity (no free electrons); high refractive index ($2.42$).
2. **Graphite:**
   - 2D hexagonal layers ($sp^2$ hybridized) held by weak van der Waals forces.
   - Soft, slippery; used as solid **lubricant** and in pencil leads.
   - **Good conductor of electricity** (due to free $\pi$-electrons).
3. **Fullerenes ($C_{60}$ / Buckyball):**
   - Soccer-ball shape discovered by Richard Smalley, Robert Curl, and Harold Kroto.
4. **Graphene:**
   - Single atomic layer of graphite; 200 times stronger than steel, transparent, ultra-conductive.

## Environmental Chemistry

- **Acid Rain:** Caused by emissions of **Sulphur Dioxide ($SO_2$)** and **Nitrogen Oxides ($NO_x$)** which form sulphuric acid ($H_2SO_4$) and nitric acid ($HNO_3$). pH of acid rain is $< 5.6$. Causes yellowing/corrosion of marble monuments (**Taj Trapezium Zone / Stone Leprosy**).
- **Greenhouse Effect:** Trapping of heat in atmosphere. Major gases: **Water vapour ($H_2O$), Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$), Methane ($CH_4$), Nitrous Oxide ($N_2O$), Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), Ozone ($O_3$)**.
- **Ozone Layer Depletion:** Ozone ($O_3$) in stratosphere absorbs harmful UV-B radiation. Depleted by **CFCs (Freons)** releasing chlorine free radicals.
  - Regulated internationally by the **Montreal Protocol (1987)**.
  - World Ozone Day: **September 16**.
- **Photochemical Smog:** "Los Angeles smog"; brown haze formed in warm sunny weather involving $NO_x$, hydrocarbons, and ozone (contains PAN: Peroxyacetyl Nitrate).

## Last 10 Years PYQs with Detailed Explanations

### PYQ 1 — SSC CHSL 2024
**Q.** Which of the following is added to LPG to give it a strong smell so that leaks can be detected?
- A) Methyl alcohol
- B) Ethyl mercaptan
- C) Carbon monoxide
- D) Chloroform
- **Answer:** **B) Ethyl mercaptan** ✅
- *Explanation:* LPG (propane and butane) is naturally odourless. Ethyl mercaptan ($C_2H_5SH$) contains sulphur and gives a foul rotten cabbage smell to detect leakages.

### PYQ 2 — SSC CHSL 2023
**Q.** What is the chemical formula of Plaster of Paris?
- A) $CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$
- B) $CaSO_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}H_2O$
- C) $CaSO_4 \cdot H_2O$
- D) $CaCO_3$
- **Answer:** **B) $CaSO_4 \cdot \frac{1}{2}H_2O$** ✅
- *Explanation:* Plaster of Paris is calcium sulphate hemihydrate. Gypsum is $CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$.

### PYQ 3 — SSC CHSL 2023
**Q.** Which of the following metals has the highest electrical conductivity?
- A) Copper
- B) Aluminium
- C) Silver
- D) Gold
- **Answer:** **C) Silver** ✅
- *Explanation:* Silver ($Ag$) is the best conductor of electricity, followed by copper, gold, and aluminium.

### PYQ 4 — SSC CHSL 2022
**Q.** Which acid is present in ant sting?
- A) Tartaric acid
- B) Methanoic acid (Formic acid)
- C) Oxalic acid
- D) Lactic acid
- **Answer:** **B) Methanoic acid** ✅
- *Explanation:* Ant stings contain methanoic acid ($HCOOH$), causing burning pain. Applying mild base like baking soda or calamine ($ZnCO_3$) provides relief.

### PYQ 5 — SSC CHSL 2022
**Q.** In the modern periodic table, elements are arranged in the order of increasing:
- A) Atomic mass
- B) Atomic number
- C) Mass number
- D) Number of neutrons
- **Answer:** **B) Atomic number** ✅
- *Explanation:* Henry Moseley in 1913 stated that properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers.

### PYQ 6 — SSC CHSL 2021
**Q.** Galvanization is the process of depositing a thin protective coating of which metal on iron to prevent rusting?
- A) Copper
- B) Tin
- C) Zinc
- D) Nickel
- **Answer:** **C) Zinc** ✅
- *Explanation:* Zinc sacrifices itself because it is more electropositive than iron, forming a protective zinc oxide/carbonate layer.

### PYQ 7 — SSC CHSL 2020
**Q.** Which of the following is an allotrope of carbon that conducts electricity?
- A) Diamond
- B) Graphite
- C) Fullerene
- D) Coal
- **Answer:** **B) Graphite** ✅
- *Explanation:* In graphite, each carbon is bonded to three other carbons in hexagonal sheets with one free delocalized electron, allowing electrical conduction.

### PYQ 8 — SSC CHSL 2019
**Q.** Bauxite is the principal ore of which of the following metals?
- A) Iron
- B) Aluminium
- C) Zinc
- D) Copper
- **Answer:** **B) Aluminium** ✅
- *Explanation:* Bauxite ($Al_2O_3 \cdot 2H_2O$) is the main ore of aluminium, extracted via Bayer's process and Hall-Héroult electrolysis.

### PYQ 9 — SSC CHSL 2018
**Q.** Aqua Regia is a freshly prepared mixture of concentrated hydrochloric acid and concentrated nitric acid in the ratio of:
- A) $1:3$
- B) $3:1$
- C) $2:1$
- D) $3:2$
- **Answer:** **B) $3:1$** ✅
- *Explanation:* Aqua Regia contains 3 parts conc. $HCl$ and 1 part conc. $HNO_3$ by volume; it can dissolve noble metals like gold and platinum.

### PYQ 10 — SSC CHSL 2017
**Q.** German Silver is an alloy of copper, zinc, and:
- A) Silver
- B) Nickel
- C) Tin
- D) Lead
- **Answer:** **B) Nickel** ✅
- *Explanation:* German Silver consists of copper ($50\%$), zinc ($20\%$), and nickel ($30\%$). Despite its name, it contains **0% silver**!
