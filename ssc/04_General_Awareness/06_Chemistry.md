## 1. Matter, States, Mixtures, Solutions & Colloids

### 1.1 Five States of Matter

Matter is anything that occupies space and has mass. Based on intermolecular forces, kinetic energy, and particle arrangement, matter exists in **five states**:

| State | Intermolecular Force & Space | Shape & Volume | Compressibility & Fluidity | Key Facts & Exam Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Solid** | Maximum force; minimum space; particles vibrate about fixed mean positions | Definite shape & definite volume | Negligible compressibility; rigid (does not flow) | Highest density among classical states (**Exception:** Ice is less dense than liquid water due to cage-like H-bonding). |
| **Liquid** | Intermediate force & space; particles slide past one another | No definite shape; definite volume | Nearly incompressible; fluid (flows) | Rate of diffusion of liquids > solids. Gases like $O_2$ and $CO_2$ diffuse into water to support aquatic life. |
| **Gas** | Minimum force; maximum space; rapid random motion | Neither definite shape nor definite volume | Highly compressible; fluid; exerts pressure equally in all directions | Highest kinetic energy & diffusion rate among classical states (**Graham's Law:** $r \propto 1/\sqrt{M}$). |
| **Plasma** | Super-energetic, super-excited ionized gas (free $e^-$ + positive ions) | No definite shape or volume | Conducts electricity; responds to magnetic & electric fields | **4th state**; most abundant state in the universe (stars, Sun, lightning). Identified by **William Crookes** (1879); named by **Irving Langmuir** (1928). Used in neon sign bulbs & fluorescent tubes. |
| **Bose–Einstein Condensate (BEC)** | Ultra-low-density gas of bosons cooled near **Absolute Zero ($0\text{ K}$ / $-273.15^\circ\text{C}$)** | Atoms lose individual identity and merge into a single quantum state ("super-atom") | Macroscopic quantum behaviour (superfluidity) | **5th state**; predicted by **S.N. Bose & Albert Einstein (1924–25)**; experimentally created in 1995 by **Eric Cornell, Carl Wieman** (Rb-87) & **Wolfgang Ketterle** (Na-23) — 2001 Nobel Prize in Physics. |

### 1.2 Phase Transitions, Latent Heat & Evaporation

```text
          Melting (Fusion)                Vaporization (Boiling / Evaporation)
SOLID  ---------------------->  LIQUID  --------------------------------------->  GAS
       <----------------------          <---------------------------------------
        Freezing (Solidification)                    Condensation

       ================ Sublimation (Solid -> Gas directly) ==================>
       <=============== Deposition / Desublimation (Gas -> Solid) =============
```

- **Sublimation:** Direct conversion of solid to gas without passing through the liquid state upon heating.
  - **Exam-Tested Examples:** **Camphor**, **Dry Ice (solid $CO_2$)**, **Ammonium chloride ($NH_4Cl$)**, **Naphthalene**, **Iodine ($I_2$)**, **Anthracene**, **Benzoic acid**.
- **Deposition (Desublimation):** Direct conversion of gas to solid without passing through the liquid state (e.g., frost formation, soot).
- **Effect of Pressure on Boiling & Melting Point:**
  - **Boiling Point (BP):** Increases with increase in external pressure (principle of **pressure cooker**: water boils at $>100^\circ\text{C}$, cooking food faster); decreases at high altitudes due to lower atmospheric pressure.
  - **Melting Point (MP):** Increases with pressure for most substances (e.g., wax), **except** for substances that contract on melting (**Ice**, Bismuth, Antimony) — increasing pressure **lowers** the melting point of ice (**Regelation**).
- **Latent Heat ("Hidden Heat"):** Heat absorbed or released during a change of state at **constant temperature** ($Q = mL$); used solely to overcome intermolecular forces, not to raise kinetic energy.
  - **Latent Heat of Fusion of Ice:** $3.34 \times 10^5\text{ J/kg}$ ($80\text{ cal/g}$). *Exam fact:* Ice at $0^\circ\text{C}$ produces more cooling than water at $0^\circ\text{C}$ because ice absorbs extra latent heat of fusion from the surroundings.
  - **Latent Heat of Vaporization of Water:** $2.26 \times 10^6\text{ J/kg}$ ($540\text{ cal/g}$). *Exam fact:* Steam at $100^\circ\text{C}$ causes far more severe burns than boiling water at $100^\circ\text{C}$ because steam carries extra latent heat of vaporization.
- **Evaporation vs Boiling:**
  - **Boiling** is a **bulk** phenomenon occurring at a fixed temperature (BP).
  - **Evaporation** is a **surface** phenomenon occurring at **any temperature below BP** and always causes **cooling** (e.g., water cooling in an earthen pot/*matka*, cooling sensation of acetone/petrol on skin, sweating, desert coolers working best on hot dry days).
  - **Factors Increasing Evaporation Rate:**
    1. **Increase in Surface Area** (drying clothes spread out; cooling hot tea in a saucer).
    2. **Increase in Temperature** (more molecules gain sufficient kinetic energy).
    3. **Increase in Wind Speed** (sweeps away surrounding vapour molecules).
    4. **Decrease in Humidity** (evaporation is **inversely proportional** to humidity).

### 1.3 Laws of Chemical Combination & Gas Laws

#### Laws of Chemical Combination

| Law | Scientist (Year) | Exact Statement & Formula | Examples & Exam Exceptions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Law of Conservation of Mass** | **Antoine Lavoisier** (1789) | Mass can neither be created nor destroyed in a chemical reaction: $\sum m_{\text{reactants}} = \sum m_{\text{products}}$. | $12\text{ g C} + 32\text{ g O}_2 \rightarrow 44\text{ g CO}_2$. **Exception:** Does not hold strictly in **nuclear reactions** where mass converts to energy ($E = \Delta mc^2$). |
| **Law of Definite (Constant) Proportions** | **Joseph Proust** (1799) | A pure chemical compound always contains the exact same elements combined in a **fixed ratio by mass**, regardless of its source or method of preparation. | Pure water ($H_2O$) always has $H:O = 1:8$ by mass; $CO_2$ always has $C:O = 3:8$. **Exceptions:** Isotopic variations and non-stoichiometric (Berthollide) compounds like $Fe_{0.95}O$. |
| **Law of Multiple Proportions** | **John Dalton** (1803) | When two elements combine to form two or more compounds, the masses of one element combining with a fixed mass of the other bear a **simple whole-number ratio**. | In $CO$ and $CO_2$, oxygen combining with $12\text{ g}$ of C is $16\text{ g}$ and $32\text{ g}$ ($1:2$). In $H_2O$ & $H_2O_2$, $O$ ratio is $1:2$. In $N_2O, NO, N_2O_3, NO_2, N_2O_5$, $O$ combining with $28\text{ g N}$ is $16:32:48:64:80 = 1:2:3:4:5$. |
| **Law of Reciprocal Proportions** | **Jeremias Richter** (1792) | Ratio of masses of two elements $A$ and $B$ combining separately with a fixed mass of element $C$ is the same (or a simple multiple) as the ratio in which $A$ and $B$ combine with each other. | $C, H, O$ forming $CH_4$ ($12:4$), $CO_2$ ($12:32$), and $H_2O$ ($4:32 = 1:8$). |
| **Gay-Lussac's Law of Gaseous Volumes** | **Joseph Louis Gay-Lussac** (1808) | When gases react, they do so in volumes that bear a **simple whole-number ratio** to one another and to gaseous products at constant $T$ and $P$. | $2H_2(g) + O_2(g) \rightarrow 2H_2O(g)$ (volume ratio $2:1:2$). |
| **Avogadro's Law** | **Amedeo Avogadro** (1811) | Equal volumes of all gases under identical temperature and pressure contain **equal numbers of molecules**: $V \propto n$ (constant $T, P$). | **Avogadro's Number ($N_A$):** $6.022 \times 10^{23}\text{ mol}^{-1}$. **Molar Volume at STP:** $22.4\text{ L/mol}$ (at $0^\circ\text{C}, 1\text{ atm}$); $22.7\text{ L/mol}$ (IUPAC STP: $273.15\text{ K}, 1\text{ bar}$). |

#### Gas Laws & Ideal Gas Equation

| Law | Constant Parameters | Mathematical Relation | Graphical / Process Name & Exam Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Boyle's Law** (1662) | Temperature ($T$), Moles ($n$) | $P \propto \frac{1}{V} \implies P_1V_1 = P_2V_2$ | **Isothermal** process ($P$ vs $V$ is a rectangular hyperbola). Deep-sea divers & syringe action. |
| **Charles' Law** (1787) | Pressure ($P$), Moles ($n$) | $V \propto T(\text{K}) \implies \frac{V_1}{T_1} = \frac{V_2}{T_2}$ | **Isobaric** process. Establishes **Absolute Zero** ($-273.15^\circ\text{C} = 0\text{ K}$), the theoretical temperature where gas volume becomes zero. Hot-air balloons. |
| **Gay-Lussac's (Amontons') Law** | Volume ($V$), Moles ($n$) | $P \propto T(\text{K}) \implies \frac{P_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2}{T_2}$ | **Isochoric** process. Explains why car tyre pressure rises in summer / bursts on overheating. |
| **Combined Gas Law** | Moles ($n$) | $\frac{P_1V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2V_2}{T_2}$ | Relates $P, V, T$ simultaneously for a fixed mass of gas. |
| **Ideal Gas Equation** | — | $PV = nRT$ | Universal Gas Constant $R = 8.314\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1} = 0.0821\text{ L}\cdot\text{atm mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}$. |
| **Dalton's Law of Partial Pressures** | Volume ($V$), Temperature ($T$) | $P_{\text{total}} = P_1 + P_2 + P_3 + \dots$ | Applies only to **non-reacting** gas mixtures (e.g., $N_2 + O_2$, not $NH_3 + HCl$). |

> **Exam Trap — Real vs Ideal Gas:** Real gases behave closest to ideal gases at **High Temperature and Low Pressure** (intermolecular forces and molecular volume become negligible). Real gases follow the **Van der Waals Equation**: $\left(P + \frac{an^2}{V^2}\right)(V - nb) = nRT$.

### 1.4 Elements, Compounds & Mixtures

- **Element:** Pure substance made of only one kind of atom; cannot be broken down further by chemical means (e.g., $Fe, O_2, S_8$).
  - **Metals** (malleable, ductile, lustrous, sonorous, conductors), **Non-metals** (brittle, non-conductors except graphite), **Metalloids** (borderline properties: **B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te, Po**).
- **Compound:** Pure substance formed by chemical combination of two or more elements in a **fixed mass ratio**; properties are completely different from constituent elements; separated only by chemical/electrochemical reactions (e.g., $H_2O, NaCl, CO_2$).
- **Mixture:** Physical combination of two or more substances in **any proportion**; constituents retain their individual properties and can be separated by physical methods.
  - **Homogeneous Mixture:** Uniform composition throughout, no visible boundary (e.g., salt/sugar in water, air, **alloys** like Brass [$Cu+Zn$], Bronze [$Cu+Sn$], Solder [$Pb+Sn$], Stainless Steel [$Fe+Cr+Ni+C$]).
  - **Heterogeneous Mixture:** Non-uniform composition with visible boundaries (e.g., sand in water, oil + water, soil, smoke, milk).

### 1.5 True Solution vs Colloid vs Suspension

| Property | True Solution | Colloid (Colloidal Solution) | Suspension |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature** | Homogeneous | **Heterogeneous** (appears homogeneous) | Heterogeneous |
| **Particle Size** | $< 1\text{ nm}$ ($< 10^{-9}\text{ m}$) | **$1\text{ nm} \text{ to } 1000\text{ nm}$** ($10^{-9}\text{ to } 10^{-6}\text{ m}$) | $> 1000\text{ nm}$ ($> 10^{-6}\text{ m}$) |
| **Visibility** | Invisible even under ultramicroscope | Invisible to naked eye; visible under ultramicroscope | Visible to naked eye |
| **Filtration** | Passes through both ordinary filter paper & parchment/animal membrane | Passes through ordinary filter paper, **retained by parchment/ultrafilter** | Retained by both ordinary filter paper & parchment membrane |
| **Stability / Settling** | Stable; solute never settles | Stable; particles do not settle under gravity | **Unstable**; particles settle down on standing |
| **Tyndall Effect** | Does **NOT** show | **Shows** Tyndall effect | Shows (until particles settle) |
| **Brownian Motion** | Not observable | **Shows** Brownian motion | May show very weakly / settles |
| **Examples** | Salt water, sugar solution, vinegar, tincture of iodine, brass | Milk, blood, ink, fog, smoke, jelly, soap lather | Chalk in water, muddy water, sand in water, milk of magnesia |

#### Types of Colloids (Dispersed Phase & Dispersion Medium)

| Colloid Type | Dispersed Phase | Dispersion Medium | Exam-Tested Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Sol** | Solid | Liquid | Paint, ink, starch sol, mud, gold sol, cell fluid |
| **Solid Sol** | Solid | Solid | Coloured gemstones (ruby glass), milky glass, alloys |
| **Gel** | Liquid | Solid | **Jelly, cheese, butter**, curd, shoe polish *(NCERT classifies cheese & butter as Gel)* |
| **Emulsion** | Liquid | Liquid | **Milk**, face cream, hair cream, cod liver oil, butter (water-in-oil) |
| **Aerosol (Liquid)** | Liquid | Gas | **Fog, mist, clouds**, insecticide spray |
| **Aerosol (Solid)** | Solid | Gas | **Smoke**, automobile exhaust, dust storm |
| **Foam** | Gas | Liquid | Shaving cream, soap lather, froth, whipped cream |
| **Solid Foam** | Gas | Solid | Foam rubber, sponge, pumice stone, bread |

> **Critical Exam Rule:** **Gas + Gas** always forms a **homogeneous mixture (True Solution)**, **never** a colloid!
> - **Tyndall Effect:** Scattering of a beam of light by colloidal particles (making the path of light visible), discovered by **John Tyndall**. Examples: Sunlight entering a dark room through a small hole (dust/smoke particles), sunlight passing through a dense forest canopy (mist droplets).
> - **Brownian Motion:** Continuous, random zig-zag motion of colloidal particles caused by unequal bombardment by molecules of the dispersion medium (discovered by **Robert Brown**, 1827, using pollen grains in water). It prevents colloidal particles from settling under gravity.

### 1.6 Separation Techniques

| Technique | Principle / Basis | Mixtures Separated (Exam Examples) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Filtration** | Difference in particle size using porous barrier | Insoluble solid from liquid (sand + water, chalk + water, tea leaves). |
| **Centrifugation** | Rapid spinning forces denser particles to bottom & lighter to top | Cream/butter from milk, blood cells from plasma, urine tests, drying clothes in washing machine. |
| **Separating Funnel** | Difference in densities of **immiscible liquids** | Oil + water, kerosene + water, extraction of iron from ore (molten slag floats above molten iron). |
| **Sublimation** | One component sublimes on heating while other does not | Camphor + salt, $NH_4Cl$ + $NaCl$, iodine + sand, naphthalene + salt. |
| **Simple Distillation** | Difference in boiling points ($> 25\text{ K}$ gap) of **miscible liquids** (without decomposition) | Acetone + water, pure water from sea water, chloroform + aniline. |
| **Fractional Distillation** | Small difference in boiling points ($< 25\text{ K}$ gap) using a fractionating column | **Crude oil (petroleum)** into petrol/diesel/kerosene/LPG, **gases of air** ($N_2, O_2, Ar$), ethanol + water. |
| **Chromatography** | Difference in solubility/adsorption of solutes moving with a solvent phase | **Dyes in black ink**, natural pigments from plants, **drugs from blood** (forensics). |
| **Crystallization** | Pure solid crystallizes out from saturated solution on cooling | **Salt from sea water**, pure **copper sulphate ($CuSO_4$)**, **alum**. *(Preferred over evaporation because heat-sensitive solids like sugar don't char and soluble impurities remain dissolved).* |
| **Magnetic Separation** | Magnetic vs non-magnetic components | Iron filings + sulphur powder; separating magnetite ore from gangue. |

### 2.2 Atomic Models Timeline

1. **Dalton's Atomic Theory (1808) — John Dalton:**
   - Matter consists of indivisible, indestructible solid spheres ("atoms").
   - All atoms of a given element are identical in mass and properties (later disproved by **isotopes**).
   - Atoms combine in fixed whole-number ratios to form compounds.
2. **Thomson's Plum Pudding / Watermelon Model (1904) — J.J. Thomson:**
   - Atom is a uniformly positively charged sphere (pudding/red pulp) with negatively charged electrons (plums/seeds) embedded inside, making the atom electrically neutral.
   - *Failure:* Could not explain Rutherford's $\alpha$-scattering results.
3. **Rutherford's Nuclear Model (1911) — Ernest Rutherford (with Geiger & Marsden):**
   - **Gold Foil Experiment:** Bombarded ultra-thin gold foil ($\sim 100\text{ nm}$) with high-energy $\alpha$-particles ($^4_2He^{2+}$) detected on a $ZnS$ screen.
   - **Observations & Conclusions:**
     - Most $\alpha$-particles passed straight undeflected $\rightarrow$ **Most of the atom is empty space**.
     - A few deflected at small angles $\rightarrow$ Positive charge occupies a very small space.
     - **1 in ~12,000–20,000** bounced back at $180^\circ$ $\rightarrow$ Discovered the tiny, dense, positively charged **Nucleus**.
   - **Scale:** Atomic radius $\approx 10^{-10}\text{ m}$ ($1\text{ Å}$); Nuclear radius $\approx 10^{-15}\text{ m}$ ($1\text{ Fermi}$).
   - *Failure:* According to Maxwell's electromagnetic theory, an accelerating orbiting electron must radiate energy and spiral into the nucleus — Rutherford could not explain atomic stability or discrete line spectra.
4. **Bohr's Model (1913) — Niels Bohr:**
   - Electrons revolve in fixed, quantized **stationary orbits/shells** ($K, L, M, N$ for $n = 1, 2, 3, 4$) without radiating energy.
   - Angular momentum is quantized: $mvr = \frac{nh}{2\pi}$.
   - Energy is absorbed or emitted only when an electron jumps between shells: $\Delta E = E_2 - E_1 = h\nu$.
   - **Bohr–Bury Scheme ($2n^2$ Rule):**
     - Maximum electrons in shell $n$ $= 2n^2$: $K(n=1)=2$, $L(n=2)=8$, $M(n=3)=18$, $N(n=4)=32$.
     - **Outermost (valence) shell** cannot hold more than **8 electrons** (Octet Rule), and penultimate shell cannot exceed 18.
   - *Failure:* Explained only single-electron species ($H, He^+, Li^{2+}$); failed for multi-electron atoms, Zeeman effect (magnetic field splitting), Stark effect (electric field splitting), and wave-particle duality ($\lambda = h/mv$, de Broglie).
5. **Heisenberg's Uncertainty Principle (1927) — Werner Heisenberg:**
   - It is impossible to determine simultaneously both the exact position ($\Delta x$) and exact momentum ($\Delta p$) of a microscopic particle:
     $$\Delta x \cdot \Delta p \ge \frac{h}{4\pi}$$
   - Led to the **Quantum Mechanical Model (Schrödinger, 1926)** replacing fixed 2D orbits with 3D probability regions called **orbitals** ($\psi^2$).

### 2.3 Four Quantum Numbers & Electronic Configuration Rules

| Quantum Number | Symbol | Possible Values | Physical Meaning |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Principal** | $n$ | $1, 2, 3, 4 \dots$ ($K, L, M, N$) | Main **shell**, determines **size** and **energy** of orbital. |
| **Azimuthal (Subsidiary)** | $l$ | $0$ to $(n-1)$ ($s=0, p=1, d=2, f=3$) | **Subshell** and **shape** of orbital: $s$ = spherical, $p$ = dumbbell, $d$ = double dumbbell, $f$ = complex. |
| **Magnetic** | $m_l$ | $-l$ to $+l$ (total $2l+1$ values) | **Spatial orientation** of orbital ($s$: 1 orbital; $p$: 3 orbitals; $d$: 5 orbitals; $f$: 7 orbitals). |
| **Spin** | $m_s$ | $+\frac{1}{2}$ ($\uparrow$) or $-\frac{1}{2}$ ($\downarrow$) | Direction of **electron spin** (Uhlenbeck & Goudsmit). Max **2 electrons** per orbital. |

- **Aufbau Principle** (*German: building up*): Electrons fill orbitals in order of increasing energy given by the **$(n+l)$ rule**:
  $$1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < 4s < 3d < 4p < 5s < 4d < 5p < 6s < 4f < 5d < 6p \dots$$
  *(If $(n+l)$ is equal for two orbitals, the one with lower $n$ is filled first, e.g., $3d$ [$3+2=5$] fills before $4p$ [$4+1=5$]).*
  - **Exam Exceptions (Half-filled & Fully-filled stability):**
    - **Chromium ($Z=24$):** $[Ar]\,3d^5 4s^1$ *(NOT $3d^4 4s^2$)*
    - **Copper ($Z=29$):** $[Ar]\,3d^{10} 4s^1$ *(NOT $3d^9 4s^2$)*
- **Pauli Exclusion Principle (1925):** No two electrons in the same atom can have the **same set of all four quantum numbers** (an orbital can hold at most 2 electrons, and they must have opposite spins).
- **Hund's Rule of Maximum Multiplicity:** Electron pairing in degenerate orbitals ($p, d, f$ orbitals of equal energy) does not occur until **every orbital in that subshell is singly occupied** with parallel spins.

### 2.5 Radioactivity & $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ Rays

- **Discovery:**
  - **Henri Becquerel (1896):** Accidentally discovered spontaneous radioactivity in uranium salts (potassium uranyl sulphate).
  - **Marie & Pierre Curie (1898):** Coined the term **"Radioactivity"**; isolated **Polonium ($Po$)** and **Radium ($Ra$)** from **pitchblende** ore.
  - **Units:** SI unit = **Becquerel (Bq)** ($1\text{ decay/s}$); **1 Curie (Ci)** $= 3.7 \times 10^{10}\text{ Bq}$; **1 Rutherford (Rd)** $= 10^6\text{ Bq}$.
  - **Rule of Stability:** All elements with **$Z > 82$ (after Lead, $^{208}_{82}Pb$)** are naturally radioactive due to high neutron-to-proton instability.
- **Soddy–Fajans Group Displacement Law (1913):**
  - **$\alpha$-decay ($^4_2He$):** $Z$ decreases by **$2$**, $A$ decreases by **$4$** $\rightarrow$ daughter shifts **2 groups left** in the periodic table.
    $$^A_Z X \longrightarrow ^{A-4}_{Z-2}Y + ^4_2He$$
  - **$\beta^-$-decay ($^0_{-1}e$):** $Z$ increases by **$1$**, $A$ stays **unchanged** $\rightarrow$ daughter shifts **1 group right** (a neutron converts to proton + electron + antineutrino).
    $$^A_Z X \longrightarrow ^A_{Z+1}Y + ^0_{-1}e + \bar{\nu}$$
  - **$\gamma$-decay:** No change in $Z$ or $A$; nucleus simply drops from an excited state to a lower energy state.

#### Comparison of $\alpha, \beta, \gamma$ Rays

| Property | $\alpha$-Rays (Alpha) | $\beta$-Rays (Beta) | $\gamma$-Rays (Gamma) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Nature** | Helium nuclei ($^4_2He^{2+}$: 2 protons + 2 neutrons) | Fast-moving electrons ($^0_{-1}e$) | High-energy **electromagnetic waves** (photons) |
| **Charge** | $+2e$ | $-1e$ | $0$ (Neutral) |
| **Rest Mass** | $4\text{ u}$ (Heaviest) | $\frac{1}{1836}\text{ u}$ (Light) | $0$ (Massless) |
| **Speed** | $\sim 1/10\text{th}$ speed of light ($10^7\text{ m/s}$) | Up to $99\%$ speed of light | Speed of light ($c = 3 \times 10^8\text{ m/s}$) |
| **Ionizing Power** | **Maximum** ($\sim 10,000\times \gamma$) | Moderate ($\sim 100\times \gamma$) | **Minimum** ($1$) |
| **Penetrating Power** | **Minimum** (stopped by paper / skin) | Moderate (stopped by few mm Al foil) | **Maximum** (requires thick Lead/concrete; $\alpha < \beta < \gamma$) |
| **Deflection in E/B Field** | Deflected towards **negative** plate (less curved) | Deflected towards **positive** plate (strongly curved) | **Undeflected** |

## 3. Modern Periodic Table & Trends (Exhaustive)

### 3.1 Historical Evolution

1. **Döbereiner's Triads (1817 / 1829) — Johann Wolfgang Döbereiner:**
   - Grouped elements in threes (**triads**) with similar chemical properties; atomic mass of the middle element $\approx$ arithmetic mean of the other two.
   - **Three Famous Triads:**
     - **(Li, Na, K):** $(7 + 39)/2 = 23$
     - **(Ca, Sr, Ba):** $(40 + 137)/2 = 88.5$ ($\approx 88$)
     - **(Cl, Br, I):** $(35.5 + 127)/2 = 81.25$ ($\approx 80$)
   - *Limitation:* Could classify only a few elements into triads.
2. **Newlands' Law of Octaves (1865) — John Newlands:**
   - Arranged known elements (56) by increasing atomic mass; **every 8th element** repeated the properties of the 1st (analogous to musical octaves).
   - *Limitations:* Valid **only up to Calcium ($Ca$)**; failed for heavier elements; placed Co & Ni in the same slot with halogens (F, Cl, Br); assumed only 56 elements existed in nature.
3. **Mendeleev's Periodic Table (1869) — Dmitri Mendeleev:**
   - **Mendeleev's Periodic Law:** *"Physical and chemical properties of elements are a periodic function of their **atomic masses**."*
   - Classified **63 known elements** into **7 horizontal rows (Periods)** and **8 vertical columns (Groups)**.
   - **Merit (Predictive Power):** Left gaps for undiscovered elements and accurately predicted their properties:
     - **Eka-boron** $\longrightarrow$ **Scandium ($Sc$)**
     - **Eka-aluminium** $\longrightarrow$ **Gallium ($Ga$)**
     - **Eka-silicon** $\longrightarrow$ **Germanium ($Ge$)**
   - *Limitations:* Position of Hydrogen uncertain; no place for isotopes; anomalous atomic mass inversions (**Ar [39.9] before K [39.1]**, **Co [58.9] before Ni [58.7]**, **Te [127.6] before I [126.9]**).
4. **Modern Periodic Table (1913) — Henry Moseley:**
   - Using X-ray spectra ($\sqrt{\nu} \propto Z$), Moseley proved **Atomic Number ($Z$)** is the fundamental property of an element.
   - **Modern Periodic Law:** *"Physical and chemical properties of elements are a periodic function of their **atomic numbers**."*
   - **Structure (Long Form):** **18 Groups** (same valence electron count) & **7 Periods** (same valence shell $n$):
     - Period 1: 2 elements (Shortest; H to He)
     - Period 2 & 3: 8 elements each (Short periods)
     - Period 4 & 5: 18 elements each (Long periods)
     - Period 6 & 7: 32 elements each (Longest periods; include Lanthanides & Actinides; total **118 elements**, ending at **Oganesson, $Z=118$**).
   - **Four Blocks:**
     - **s-block:** Groups 1–2 (+ He). Reactive metals (except H, He).
     - **p-block:** Groups 13–18. Contains metals, **all non-metals**, and **all metalloids**.
     - **d-block:** Groups 3–12 (**Transition elements**). *Exception:* **Zn, Cd, Hg** have fully filled $d^{10}$ subshells in ground & common oxidation states, so they are **not typical transition elements**.
     - **f-block:** **Lanthanides ($Z=58\text{–}71$)** & **Actinides ($Z=90\text{–}103$)** placed separately at the bottom (belong to Group 3, Periods 6 & 7) — **Inner transition elements**.

### 3.2 Important Groups Deep Dive

- **Group 1 — Alkali Metals ($Li, Na, K, Rb, Cs, Fr$):**
  - Valence config: $ns^1$ (Valency $+1$). *(Hydrogen is placed above Group 1 due to $1s^1$ config, but is a non-metal).*
  - Soft, low-density metals (Na, K can be cut with a knife; Li, Na, K float on water); form strongly alkaline hydroxides ($NaOH, KOH$).
  - Stored under **kerosene oil** to prevent reaction with air/moisture (**Exception:** **Lithium** floats on kerosene, so it is stored wrapped in **paraffin wax**).
  - **Strongest reducing agents** ($Li$ is strongest in aqueous solution due to very high hydration energy).
  - **Flame Colours:** Li = Crimson red, Na = Golden yellow, K = Lilac/violet. **Francium ($Fr$)** is radioactive.
- **Group 2 — Alkaline Earth Metals ($Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra$):**
  - Valence config: $ns^2$ (Valency $+2$). Harder and less reactive than Group 1.
  - Oxides & hydroxides are basic (**Exception:** **Beryllium [$BeO, Be(OH)_2$]** is **amphoteric**).
  - **Biological/Applied Roles:** **Magnesium ($Mg$)** is the central metal in **Chlorophyll**; **Calcium ($Ca$)** in bones/teeth; **Radium ($Ra$)** is radioactive; **Strontium ($Sr$)** gives crimson red and **Barium ($Ba$)** gives apple green in **fireworks**.
- **Groups 3–12 — Transition Metals (d-block):**
  - Variable oxidation states ($Mn$ shows $+2$ to $+7$, highest $+7$ in $KMnO_4$).
  - Form **coloured ions/salts** (due to $d\text{–}d$ electronic transitions; e.g., $Cu^{2+}$ blue, $Fe^{2+}$ green, $Fe^{3+}$ yellow/brown, $MnO_4^-$ purple).
  - Act as **industrial catalysts** ($Fe, Ni, Pt, Pd, V_2O_5$), form alloys & complex coordination compounds, exhibit paramagnetism, high MP/BP & density.
  - **Exceptions:** **Mercury ($Hg$)** is liquid at room temperature; **Zn, Cd, Hg** are colourless, diamagnetic, low-melting, and show fixed $+2$ oxidation state ($Hg$ also $+1$).
- **f-Block — Lanthanides & Actinides:**
  - **Lanthanides ($4f$, $Ce\text{ }58 \rightarrow Lu\text{ }71$):** Known as Rare Earth metals; **Promethium ($Pm, 61$)** is the only radioactive lanthanide. Show **Lanthanide Contraction** (poor shielding of $4f$ electrons pulls outer shells inward $\rightarrow$ $4d$ & $5d$ elements like $Zr$ and $Hf$ have nearly identical atomic radii).
  - **Actinides ($5f$, $Th\text{ }90 \rightarrow Lr\text{ }103$):** **All actinides are radioactive**. Elements beyond Uranium ($Z > 92$) are synthetic **Transuranic elements** (e.g., $Np, Pu, Am$).
- **Group 15 — Pnictogens ($N, P, As, Sb, Bi, Mc$):**
  - Valence config: $ns^2 np^3$ (Valency 3 or 5).
  - $N_2$ has a strong triple bond ($N \equiv N$, inert at room temp).
  - **Phosphorus allotropes:** **White P ($P_4$)** is waxy, toxic, spontaneously ignites in air, glows in dark (**chemiluminescence**), stored under **water**; **Red P** is stable and used on **matchbox striking surfaces** (match head uses $KClO_3 + Sb_2S_3$).
- **Group 16 — Chalcogens / Ore-forming ($O, S, Se, Te, Po, Lv$):**
  - Valence config: $ns^2 np^4$ (Valency 2).
  - $O_2$ (paramagnetic) & $O_3$ (Ozone, absorbs UV-B/UV-C); Sulphur ($S_8$ crown/puckered ring; used in **vulcanization of rubber**); **Polonium ($Po$)** is radioactive (first element discovered by Marie Curie).
- **Group 17 — Halogens / Salt-forming ($F, Cl, Br, I, At, Ts$):**
  - Valence config: $ns^2 np^5$ (Valency 1). Most reactive non-metals; exist as **diatomic molecules**:
    - $F_2$: Pale yellow gas (strongest oxidizing agent; **never shows a positive oxidation state**, always $-1$).
    - $Cl_2$: Greenish-yellow gas (bleaching agent by oxidation; water disinfectant).
    - **$Br_2$**: **Reddish-brown liquid** (the **only liquid non-metal** at room temperature).
    - $I_2$: Dark violet/grey lustrous solid (sublimes; deficiency causes **goitre**).
    - **Astatine ($At$):** Radioactive.
- **Group 18 — Noble / Inert Gases / Aerogens ($He, Ne, Ar, Kr, Xe, Rn, Og$):**
  - Valence config: $ns^2 np^6$ (except **$He: 1s^2$**). Zero valency, monoatomic, zero/positive electron gain enthalpy.

| Noble Gas | Key Properties & Exam-Tested Uses |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Helium ($He$)** | 2nd most abundant element in universe; lightest non-flammable gas. Used in **weather balloons**, airships, **deep-sea diving cylinders** ($He + O_2$ prevents nitrogen narcosis/"bends"), and liquid He for **MRI** cooling. |
| **Neon ($Ne$)** | Emits bright reddish-orange glow. Used in **neon advertising signs**, high-voltage indicators, and **airport beacon lights** (penetrates fog). |
| **Argon ($Ar$)** | **Most abundant noble gas in Earth's atmosphere ($\sim 0.93\%$)**. Used to fill **incandescent electric bulbs** (prevents tungsten oxidation) and in **arc welding** of metals. |
| **Krypton ($Kr$)** | Used in high-speed **flash photography** lamps and airport runway lights. |
| **Xenon ($Xe$)** | Called **"Stranger Gas"**. **Only noble gas to readily form stable chemical compounds** ($XeF_2, XeF_4, XeF_6, XeO_3$; first $Xe^+[PtF_6]^-$ by Neil Bartlett, 1962). Used in xenon arc lamps, car headlamps, and ion thrusters. |
| **Radon ($Rn$)** | **Radioactive** gas (absent in free atmosphere; formed from decay of Radium). Used in **cancer radiotherapy**. |

### 3.3 All Periodic Trends & Exceptions

| Property | Across a Period (Left $\rightarrow$ Right) | Down a Group (Top $\rightarrow$ Bottom) | Major Exam Exceptions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Atomic Radius** | **Decreases** (effective nuclear charge $Z_{\text{eff}}$ increases) | **Increases** (new shells added) | Noble gases (Group 18) have larger **van der Waals radii** than Halogens. $Ga \approx Al$ ($d$-shielding); $Hf \approx Zr$ (Lanthanide contraction). |
| **Ionization Enthalpy (IE)** | **Increases** (electrons held tighter) | **Decreases** (larger size, easier removal) | $Be > B$ (filled $2s^2$); $N > O$ (half-filled $2p^3$). **Highest IE:** **Helium ($He$)**; **Lowest (stable):** **Cesium ($Cs$)**. |

Group 18 has zero EA ($+\Delta_{eg}H$). |
| **Electronegativity (EN)** | **Increases** | **Decreases** | **Highest EN:** **Fluorine ($F = 3.98 / 4.0$)** $> O\text{ }(3.44) > Cl\text{ }(3.16) > N\text{ }(3.04)$. **Lowest:** **Cesium / Francium**. |
| **Metallic Character** | **Decreases** | **Increases** | **Most metallic stable metal:** **Cesium ($Cs$)**. |
| **Non-metallic Character** | **Increases** | **Decreases** | **Most reactive non-metal:** **Fluorine ($F$)**. |
| **Valency** | Increases **$1 \rightarrow 4$**, then decreases **$3 \rightarrow 0$** | Remains **Same** (e.g., all Group 1 = 1) | Transition metals show **variable valency**. |
| **Oxide Nature** | **Basic $\rightarrow$ Amphoteric $\rightarrow$ Acidic** | **Basicity Increases** | **Amphoteric oxides** (react with both acids & bases): **$Al_2O_3, ZnO, PbO, SnO, BeO, Cr_2O_3$**. **Neutral oxides:** **$CO, NO, N_2O, H_2O$**. |

## 4. Chemical Bonding, Mole Concept & Chemical Reactions

### 4.1 Types of Chemical Bonds

1. **Ionic / Electrovalent Bond:**
   - Formed by **complete transfer** of valence electron(s) from a metal (low IE) to a non-metal (high EA), creating electrostatic attraction between cation & anion (e.g., $NaCl, MgCl_2, CaO, KBr$).
   - **Properties:** Hard crystalline solids; high MP & BP; soluble in polar solvents (water); **conduct electricity ONLY in molten state or aqueous solution** (not in solid state, as ions are fixed); non-directional.
2. **Covalent Bond (G.N. Lewis):**
   - Formed by **mutual sharing** of electron pairs between non-metals:
     - **Single bond ($1\sigma$):** $H_2, Cl_2, CH_4, H_2O$
     - **Double bond ($1\sigma + 1\pi$):** $O_2, C_2H_4\text{ (ethene)}, CO_2$
     - **Triple bond ($1\sigma + 2\pi$):** $N_2, C_2H_2\text{ (ethyne)}, CO$
   - **Polar vs Non-Polar Covalent:**
     - **Non-polar:** Equal sharing ($\Delta EN = 0$, e.g., $H_2, Cl_2, O_2$) or symmetric geometry cancelling dipoles (net dipole moment $\mu = 0$, e.g., **$CO_2, CH_4, CCl_4, BF_3$**).
     - **Polar:** Unequal sharing ($\Delta EN > 0$, net $\mu \ne 0$, e.g., $HCl, H_2O, NH_3$).
   - **Properties:** Low MP/BP; generally soluble in non-polar solvents; poor conductors (**Exception:** **Graphite** conducts electricity due to delocalized $\pi$-electrons; **Diamond** is a non-conductor); directional.
3. **Coordinate (Dative) Bond:**
   - A special covalent bond where **both shared electrons are contributed by one atom** (donor $\rightarrow$ acceptor), denoted by an arrow ($\rightarrow$).
   - **Examples:** $NH_4^+, H_3O^+, O_3, CO, HNO_3, NH_3 \rightarrow BF_3$.
   - **Classic SSC Question:** **Ammonium chloride ($NH_4Cl$)** contains **all three** types of bonds — **Ionic** (between $NH_4^+$ and $Cl^-$), **Covalent** (N–H), and **Coordinate** ($N \rightarrow H^+$).
4. **Metallic Bond:**
   - Electrostatic attraction between positively charged metal ions (kernels) and a **sea of delocalized mobile valence electrons**. Explains lustre, malleability, ductility, and electrical/thermal conductivity.
5. **Hydrogen Bonding:**
   - Electrostatic attraction between a hydrogen atom covalently bonded to a small, highly electronegative atom (**F, O, or N** — remember **FON**) and another electronegative atom. Weaker than covalent/ionic bonds, stronger than Van der Waals forces.
   - **Intermolecular H-bonding (between different molecules):** Raises BP, MP, and viscosity.
     - **Why Ice Floats on Water:** In ice, each $H_2O$ forms 4 tetrahedral H-bonds creating an open **cage-like structure** with empty spaces; density of ice ($0.917\text{ g/cm}^3$) $<$ liquid water (maximum density **$1.0\text{ g/cm}^3$ at $4^\circ\text{C}$**).
     - **Why $H_2O$ is liquid while $H_2S$ is gas:** Strong intermolecular H-bonding exists in $H_2O$, but is absent in $H_2S$ (larger S atom, lower EN).
     - Order of BP of hydrides: $H_2O > HF > NH_3$ (abnormal high BP due to H-bonding).
   - **Intramolecular H-bonding (within the same molecule):** Prevents intermolecular association, **lowering BP** and increasing steam volatility. Example: ***o*-Nitrophenol** is steam-volatile (intramolecular), whereas ***p*-Nitrophenol** has a high BP (intermolecular).
6. **Van der Waals Forces:**
   - Weak, short-range intermolecular forces present in all atoms/molecules (crucial in non-polar molecules & noble gases; e.g., London dispersion forces). Strength increases with molecular mass and surface area (explaining why $F_2, Cl_2$ are gases, $Br_2$ is liquid, and $I_2$ is solid).

### 4.3 Mole Concept Quick Formulas

- **1 Mole** $= 6.022 \times 10^{23}$ particles ($N_A$, Avogadro's Number).
- **Number of Moles ($n$):**
  $$n = \frac{\text{Given Mass (g)}}{\text{Molar Mass (g/mol)}} = \frac{\text{Number of Particles}}{N_A} = \frac{\text{Volume of Gas at STP (L)}}{22.4\text{ L}}$$
- **Concentration Terms:**
  - **Molarity ($M$):** $\frac{\text{Moles of solute}}{\text{Volume of solution in Litres}}$ ($\text{mol/L}$) — **Temperature-dependent** (volume expands on heating).
  - **Molality ($m$):** $\frac{\text{Moles of solute}}{\text{Mass of solvent in kg}}$ ($\text{mol/kg}$) — **Temperature-independent** (mass does not change with $T$).

### 4.4 Types of Chemical Reactions & Catalysts

#### Classification of Reactions

| Reaction Type | Pattern & Definition | Key Exam Examples |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Combination** | $A + B \rightarrow AB$ | $2H_2 + O_2 \rightarrow 2H_2O$; **$CaO\text{ (Quick lime)} + H_2O \rightarrow Ca(OH)_2\text{ (Slaked lime)} + \text{Heat}$**. |
| **Decomposition** | $AB \rightarrow A + B$ (requires heat, electricity, or light) | **Thermal:** $CaCO_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} CaO + CO_2$. **Electrolytic:** $2H_2O \rightarrow 2H_2 + O_2$. **Photolytic:** $2AgCl \xrightarrow{h\nu} 2Ag + Cl_2$ & $2AgBr \xrightarrow{h\nu} 2Ag + Br_2$ (**Black & white photography**). |
| **Displacement** | $A + BC \rightarrow AC + B$ ($A$ more reactive than $B$) | $Fe + CuSO_4\text{ (blue)} \rightarrow FeSO_4\text{ (pale green)} + Cu\text{ (red-brown)}$. (**Reactivity Series:** $K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > [H] > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au > Pt$). |
| **Double Displacement** | $AB + CD \rightarrow AD + CB$ (ion exchange) | **Precipitation:** $BaCl_2 + Na_2SO_4 \rightarrow BaSO_4\downarrow\text{ (white)} + 2NaCl$. **Neutralization:** $HCl + NaOH \rightarrow NaCl + H_2O$. |
| **Exothermic vs Endothermic** | **Exothermic ($\Delta H < 0$):** Releases heat. **Endothermic ($\Delta H > 0$):** Absorbs heat. | **Exo:** Combustion, respiration, neutralization, slaking of lime, dilution of acid. **Endo:** Photosynthesis, melting/evaporation, $N_2 + O_2 \rightarrow 2NO$, decomposition of $CaCO_3$. |

#### Redox Reactions (Oxidation & Reduction)

- **OIL RIG Rule:** **O**xidation **I**s **L**oss of $e^-$ (or gain of O / loss of H / increase in Oxidation Number); **R**eduction **I**s **G**ain of $e^-$ (or loss of O / gain of H / decrease in Oxidation Number).
- **Oxidation Number (ON) Rules:**
  1. Free/uncombined element $= 0$ ($Na, Cl_2, O_2, S_8$).
  2. Monoatomic ion $=$ its charge ($Na^+ = +1, O^{2-} = -2$).
  3. **Fluorine** is **always $-1$**. Alkali metals $= +1$; Alkaline earth metals $= +2$.
  4. **Hydrogen** $= +1$ (**Exception:** $-1$ in ionic metal hydrides like $NaH, CaH_2$).
  5. **Oxygen** $= -2$ (**Exceptions:** $-1$ in peroxides $H_2O_2, Na_2O_2$; $-\frac{1}{2}$ in superoxides $KO_2$; $+2$ in $OF_2$).
  6. Sum of ON in a neutral molecule $= 0$; in a polyatomic ion $=$ ion charge.
- **Agents & Special Case:**
  - **Oxidizing Agent (Oxidant):** Gets **reduced** itself; oxidizes others (e.g., $KMnO_4, K_2Cr_2O_7, HNO_3, O_3, F_2, Cl_2$).
  - **Reducing Agent (Reductant):** Gets **oxidized** itself; reduces others (e.g., $H_2, C, CO, H_2S, Na, LiAlH_4$).
  - **Both Oxidant & Reductant:** **$H_2O_2, SO_2, HNO_2$**.
  - **Disproportionation:** Same element simultaneously oxidized and reduced (e.g., $2H_2O_2 \rightarrow 2H_2O + O_2$).

#### Catalysts & Bio-Catalysts (Enzymes)

- A **catalyst** alters the rate of a reaction by lowering the **activation energy ($E_a$)** without being consumed; it **does not shift the position of chemical equilibrium** (accelerates forward and reverse reactions equally).
- **Types:**
  - **Positive Catalyst:** Increases rate ($MnO_2$ in thermal decomposition of $KClO_3$).
  - **Negative Catalyst (Inhibitor):** Decreases rate (Glycerol or phosphoric acid slows $H_2O_2$ decomposition; Tetraethyl lead [TEL] as anti-knocking agent).
  - **Promoter:** Enhances catalyst efficiency (**Molybdenum [$Mo$]** promotes $Fe$ in Haber process).
  - **Catalytic Poison:** Destroys catalyst activity ($As_2O_3, CO, H_2S$).
  - **Bio-catalysts (Enzymes):** Globular proteins (with rare RNA ribozymes) catalyzing biochemical reactions (e.g., **Zymase**: glucose $\rightarrow$ ethanol; **Invertase**: sucrose $\rightarrow$ glucose + fructose; **Urease**: urea $\rightarrow NH_3 + CO_2$; **Amylase**: starch $\rightarrow$ maltose).

| Industrial Process | Product | Catalyst (and Promoter) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Haber–Bosch Process** | Ammonia ($NH_3$) | Finely divided **Iron ($Fe$)** + **Molybdenum ($Mo$)** promoter |
| **Contact Process** | Sulphuric acid ($H_2SO_4$) | **Vanadium pentoxide ($V_2O_5$)** (or Platinum) |
| **Ostwald Process** | Nitric acid ($HNO_3$) | **Platinum–Rhodium ($Pt\text{–}Rh$)** gauze |

### 4.5 Electrochemistry, Batteries & Corrosion

- **Electrolytes:** Substances that dissociate into ions in molten state or aqueous solution and conduct electricity.
  - **Strong:** Completely ionized ($HCl, H_2SO_4, NaOH, NaCl$).
  - **Weak:** Partially ionized ($CH_3COOH, NH_4OH, H_2CO_3$).
  - **Non-electrolytes:** Do not ionize (sugar, urea, glucose, pure ethanol).
- **Electrolysis & Electrode Sign Rule:**
  - **OXIDATION always occurs at ANODE (AN OX); REDUCTION always occurs at CATHODE (RED CAT).**
  - **Electrolytic Cell** (electrical $\rightarrow$ chemical energy): **Anode = Positive ($+$)**, **Cathode = Negative ($-$)**.
  - **Galvanic / Voltaic Cell** (chemical $\rightarrow$ electrical energy): **Anode = Negative ($-$)**, **Cathode = Positive ($+$)** (*mnemonic: LOAN — Left Oxidation Anode Negative*).
- **Faraday's Laws of Electrolysis (Michael Faraday, 1833):**
  - **First Law:** Mass of substance deposited/liberated ($m$) is directly proportional to total electric charge passed ($Q = I \cdot t$):
    $$m = Z \cdot I \cdot t = \frac{E \cdot I \cdot t}{F}$$
    where $F \approx 96,500\text{ C/mol}$ ($1\text{ Faraday}$ = charge on $1\text{ mol } e^-$), $E = \frac{\text{Molar mass}}{n\text{-factor}}$ (Equivalent weight).
  - **Second Law:** When the same quantity of electricity passes through different electrolytes connected in series, masses deposited are proportional to their equivalent weights: $\frac{m_1}{m_2} = \frac{E_1}{E_2}$.

#### Corrosion & Rusting of Iron

- **Corrosion:** Slow electrochemical oxidation of metals when exposed to air, moisture, or chemicals.
  - **Silver tarnishing:** Black coating of **Silver sulphide ($Ag_2S$)** (reacts with $H_2S$ in air).
  - **Copper corrosion:** Green coating of **Basic copper carbonate ($CuCO_3 \cdot Cu(OH)_2$)**.
- **Rusting of Iron:**
  - Requires **BOTH Oxygen ($O_2$) AND Moisture/Water ($H_2O$)** (accelerated by salts/electrolytes like seawater and acids).
  - **Chemical Formula of Rust:** **Hydrated ferric oxide ($Fe_2O_3 \cdot xH_2O$)** — reddish-brown, flaky, porous layer.
  - **Classic SSC Fact:** **The weight of iron INCREASES upon rusting** (because oxygen and water combine chemically with iron).
  - **Prevention Methods:** Painting, oiling/greasing, alloying (Stainless steel: $Fe + Cr + Ni + C$), and **Galvanization**.
  - **Galvanization:** Coating iron/steel with a thin layer of **Zinc ($Zn$)**. Even if the zinc layer is scratched, iron does not rust because **zinc is more reactive than iron** and acts as a **sacrificial anode** (corrodes preferentially).

## 5. Acids, Bases, Salts, pH Scale & Indicators

### 5.1 Three Theories of Acids & Bases

| Theory (Year) | Acid Definition | Base Definition | Key Examples | Scope & Limitations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Arrhenius Theory** (1884) | Releases **$H^+$** (or $H_3O^+$) ions in **aqueous** solution | Releases **$OH^-$** ions in **aqueous** solution | Acids: $HCl, HNO_3, H_2SO_4$<br>Bases: $NaOH, KOH$ | Restricted strictly to water; cannot explain basic nature of $NH_3$ or acidic nature of $CO_2, BF_3$. |
| **Brønsted–Lowry Theory** (1923) | **Proton ($H^+$) donor** | **Proton ($H^+$) acceptor** | $HCl + NH_3 \rightleftharpoons NH_4^+ + Cl^-$<br>(Acid₁ + Base₂ $\rightleftharpoons$ Acid₂ + Base₁) | Works in non-aqueous media; introduces **Conjugate Acid–Base pairs** (differ by one $H^+$). Fails for aprotic acids like $BF_3, AlCl_3$. |
| **Lewis Theory** (1923) | **Electron-pair acceptor** (Electrophile) | **Electron-pair donor** (Nucleophile) | Acids: $BF_3, AlCl_3, FeCl_3, H^+, Cu^{2+}$<br>Bases: $NH_3, H_2O, OH^-, Cl^-$ | **Most general theory**; explains reactions without $H^+$ via coordinate covalent bond formation ($NH_3 \rightarrow BF_3$). |

> **Exam-Tested Concepts:**
> - **Amphoteric / Amphiprotic Species** (can act as both Brønsted acid & base): $H_2O$, $HCO_3^-$, $HSO_4^-$, $HS^-$, $NH_3$.
> - **Conjugate Pairs:** Strong acid has a **weak** conjugate base (e.g., $HCl \rightarrow Cl^-$), and a weak acid has a **strong** conjugate base (e.g., $CH_3COOH \rightarrow CH_3COO^-$).
> - **Basicity of Acids** (number of replaceable $H^+$ ions): $HCl = 1$, $H_2SO_4 = 2$, $H_3PO_4 = 3$ (tribasic). **Exceptions:** $H_3PO_3 = 2$ (dibasic) and $H_3PO_2 = 1$ (monobasic), because only $H$ atoms attached to $O$ ($-OH$) are ionizable, not $P-H$ bonds.

### 5.2 Strong vs Weak Acids & Bases, Aqua Regia & Antacids

| Category | Definition | Common Examples | Exam Notes & Exceptions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Strong Acids** | Ionize **completely ($\sim 100\%$)** in water | $HCl$, $HNO_3$, $H_2SO_4$, $HBr$, $HI$, $HClO_4$ | **Perchloric acid ($HClO_4$)** is the strongest common mineral acid. $HI > HBr > HCl \gg HF$. |
| **Weak Acids** | Ionize **partially ($< 5\%$)** in water | $CH_3COOH$, $HCOOH$, $H_2CO_3$, $H_3PO_4$, $HF$, Oxalic acid | **Hydrofluoric acid ($HF$)** is a *weak* acid due to strong $H-F$ bond, yet it is the only common acid that **etches/dissolves glass ($SiO_2$)**! |
| **Strong Bases** | Dissociate **completely** to give $OH^-$ | $NaOH$ (Caustic soda), $KOH$ (Caustic potash), $LiOH$, $Ba(OH)_2$, $Ca(OH)_2$ | Water-soluble bases are called **Alkalis** ("All alkalis are bases, but all bases are not alkalis"). |
| **Weak Bases** | Dissociate **partially** in water | $NH_4OH$, $Mg(OH)_2$, $Al(OH)_3$, $Fe(OH)_3$, $Cu(OH)_2$ | $Cu(OH)_2$ and $Fe(OH)_3$ are insoluble bases (not alkalis). |

- **Aqua Regia ("Royal Water"):**
  - Freshly prepared mixture of **Concentrated Hydrochloric Acid ($HCl$) and Concentrated Nitric Acid ($HNO_3$) in a $3:1$ ratio by volume/moles** ($3\text{ HCl} : 1\text{ HNO}_3$).
  - Capable of dissolving noble metals like **Gold ($Au$)** and **Platinum ($Pt$)** (which resist single mineral acids) by generating nascent chlorine ($[Cl]$) and nitrosyl chloride ($NOCl$), forming soluble chloroauric acid ($H[AuCl_4]$) or chloroplatinic acid ($H_2[PtCl_6]$).
- **Antacids:**
  - Mild, non-corrosive bases used to neutralize excess gastric $HCl$ during hyperacidity/indigestion.
  - Most common: **Milk of Magnesia — $Mg(OH)_2$** (Magnesium hydroxide), **Baking soda ($NaHCO_3$)**, **Aluminium hydroxide ($Al(OH)_3$)**, Magnesium trisilicate.
  - *Note:* Strong bases like $NaOH$ or $KOH$ are **never** used as antacids because they are highly corrosive to stomach tissue.

### 5.5 The pH Scale (Sørensen, 1909)

- **Proposed by:** Danish biochemist **S. P. L. Sørensen (1909)** ("*potenz* of Hydrogen" / power of Hydrogen).
- **Formula:**
  $$\text{pH} = -\log_{10}[H^+] = \log_{10}\left(\frac{1}{[H^+]}\right)$$
  At $25^\circ\text{C}$ ($298\text{ K}$): Ionic product of water $K_w = [H^+][OH^-] = 10^{-14} \implies \mathbf{\text{pH} + \text{pOH} = 14}$.
- **Scale Interpretation ($0$ to $14$):**
  - $\text{pH} < 7 \rightarrow$ **Acidic** (higher $[H^+]$, lower pH)
  - $\text{pH} = 7 \rightarrow$ **Neutral** ($[H^+] = [OH^-] = 10^{-7}\text{ M}$)
  - $\text{pH} > 7 \rightarrow$ **Basic / Alkaline** (higher $[OH^-]$, higher pH)
  - *Note:* Each $1$-unit change in pH represents a **$10$-fold** change in $[H^+]$ concentration (a pH change from $2$ to $5$ reduces acidity by $10^3 = 1000\times$).

| Substance / System | Exact / Standard pH Value | Nature & Exam Significance |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Concentrated $HCl$ ($1\text{ M}$) / Battery Acid** | $\mathbf{0.0\text{–}1.0}$ | Extremely acidic ($H_2SO_4$ in lead-acid battery $\approx 0.5\text{–}1.0$). |
| **Gastric Juice (Stomach)** | **$\mathbf{1.2}$** | Aids protein digestion (pepsin) and kills pathogens. |
| **Lemon Juice** | **$\mathbf{2.2}$** | Rich in citric acid. |
| **Vinegar** | **$\mathbf{2.4\text{–}3.4}$** | Acetic acid ($5\text{–}8\%$). |
| **Soft Drinks / Wine** | **$2.5\text{–}3.5$** | Carbonic / phosphoric / tartaric acids. |
| **Tomato Juice** | **$4.1\text{–}4.4$** | Oxalic & citric acids. |
| **Black Coffee** | **$5.0$** | Mildly acidic. |
| **Tooth Decay Threshold** | **$\mathbf{< 5.5}$** | Mouth bacteria ferment sugars into acid; below pH $5.5$, tooth enamel (**Calcium hydroxyapatite**, $Ca_5(PO_4)_3OH$) corrodes. |
| **Acid Rain** | **$\mathbf{< 5.6}$** | Normal rain has pH $\approx 5.6$ due to dissolved $CO_2$; $<5.6$ is acid rain (due to $SO_2, NO_x$). |

## 6. Metals, Non-Metals, Metallurgy, Ores & Alloys

### 6.1 Reactivity Series of Metals

$$\mathbf{K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > [H] > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au > Pt}$$

*(Mnemonic: **K**edar **Na**th **Ca** **Ma**li **Al**oo **Z**ara **Fe**eke **P**akata **H**ai — **Cu**te **H**arish **Ag**ra **Au**r **Pt**na)*

- **Key Rules:**
  1. **Reaction with Dilute Acids:** Metals **above Hydrogen ($[H]$)** ($K$ to $Pb$) displace $H_2$ gas from dilute $HCl / H_2SO_4$; metals **below $[H]$** ($Cu, Hg, Ag, Au, Pt$) do **not** liberate $H_2$ from dilute acids. *(Exception: Nitric acid $HNO_3$ is a strong oxidizing agent, so it oxidizes $H_2 \rightarrow H_2O$ and emits $NO/NO_2$, except very dilute $HNO_3$ with **$Mg$ and $Mn$** which still liberate $H_2$!)*
  2. **Displacement Reactions:** A more reactive metal displaces a less reactive metal from its salt solution (e.g., $Fe + CuSO_4 \text{ (blue)} \rightarrow FeSO_4 \text{ (pale green)} + Cu\downarrow$).
  3. **Extraction Strategy:**
     - **Top ($K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al$):** Very reactive $\rightarrow$ **Electrolytic reduction** of molten salts/oxides.
     - **Middle ($Zn, Fe, Pb, Cu$):** Moderately reactive $\rightarrow$ **Reduction with Carbon ($C$), $CO$, or Aluminium** (Thermite).
     - **Bottom ($Ag, Au, Pt$):** Least reactive $\rightarrow$ Found in **native (free) state**.

| Property / Category | Normal Trend | Exam-Tested Exception |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Physical State** | Metals are solid; Non-metals are solids/gases | **Mercury ($Hg$)** is the only **liquid metal** at room temp ($25^\circ\text{C}$). **Bromine ($Br_2$)** is the only **liquid non-metal**. |
| **Low Melting Point Metals** | Metals have high melting points | **Gallium ($Ga$, $29.76^\circ\text{C}$)** and **Caesium ($Cs$, $28.5^\circ\text{C}$)** melt on the palm of your hand! |

| **Malleability & Ductility** | Metals are malleable & ductile | **Gold ($Au$)** is the most malleable & ductile ($1\text{ g}$ can be drawn into a $2\text{ km}$ wire), followed by Silver ($Ag$). **Zinc ($Zn$)** is brittle at room temp. |
| **Highest Melting Point** | Variable | **Tungsten ($W$, $3422^\circ\text{C}$)** has the highest MP among metals (used in bulb filaments). **Carbon/Diamond** has the highest MP among all elements ($\sim 3550^\circ\text{C}$). |
| **Density (Heaviest / Lightest)** | Metals have high density | **Osmium ($Os$, $22.59\text{ g/cm}^3$)** is the densest/heaviest naturally occurring element (followed by Iridium $Ir$). **Lithium ($Li$, $0.53\text{ g/cm}^3$)** is the lightest metal (floats on water). |
| **Lustre** | Non-metals are dull | **Iodine ($I_2$)** and **Graphite** are lustrous non-metals. |
| **Hardest Natural Substance** | Metals are generally harder | **Diamond** (non-metal allotrope) is the hardest natural substance; **Chromium ($Cr$)** is the hardest metal. |
| **Phosphorus Allotropes** | — | **White Phosphorus ($P_4$):** Waxy, garlic smell, poisonous, **glows in the dark (chemiluminescence)**, low ignition temp ($\sim 30^\circ\text{C}$) $\rightarrow$ **stored under water**.<br>**Red Phosphorus:** Polymeric, non-poisonous, odourless, stable, used on **matchbox striking surface** *(matchstick head has $Sb_2S_3 + KClO_3$)*. |
| **Amphoteric Oxides** | Metal oxides are basic; Non-metal oxides are acidic | **$Al_2O_3, ZnO, PbO, SnO, BeO$** react with **both acids and bases**. Neutral non-metal oxides: **$CO, NO, N_2O, H_2O$**. |

### 6.3 Metallurgy Steps & Key Processes

1. **Terminology:**
   - **Minerals:** Naturally occurring metallic compounds in Earth's crust.
   - **Ores:** Minerals from which a metal can be extracted **profitably and conveniently** (*"All ores are minerals, but all minerals are not ores"*).
   - **Gangue (Matrix):** Unwanted earthy/rocky impurities ($SiO_2$, sand, clay) mixed with the ore.
   - **Flux + Gangue $\rightarrow$ Slag:** Flux is added to remove infusible gangue as fusible **slag** (e.g., $\text{CaO (basic flux)} + \text{SiO}_2\text{ (acidic gangue)} \rightarrow \text{CaSiO}_3\text{ (slag)}$).

2. **Concentration (Ore Dressing):**
   - **Gravity / Hydraulic Washing:** Based on density difference (used for heavy oxide ores like Haematite, Cassiterite).
   - **Magnetic Separation:** Separates magnetic from non-magnetic particles (e.g., Magnetite $Fe_3O_4$; Chromite; Cassiterite $SnO_2$ separated from magnetic Wolframite $FeWO_4$).
   - **Froth Floatation:** Specifically for **Sulphide ores** ($ZnS, PbS, CuFeS_2$). Ore is preferentially wetted by **pine oil** (frother) + potassium ethyl xanthate (collector), while gangue is wetted by water.
   - **Chemical Leaching (Bayer's Process):** Used to purify **Bauxite ($Al_2O_3 \cdot 2H_2O$)** using hot concentrated **$NaOH$** (dissolves $Al_2O_3$ as sodium aluminate; $Fe_2O_3/SiO_2$ remain insoluble as red mud).

3. **Roasting vs Calcination (Conversion to Metal Oxide):**

| Feature | **Roasting** | **Calcination** |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ore Type** | **Sulphide ores** | **Carbonate** & **hydrated oxide** ores |
| **Air Supply** | Heated strongly in **excess air / $O_2$** | Heated strongly in **limited or absence of air** |
| **Gas Evolved** | **$SO_2$** (Sulphur dioxide) | **$CO_2$** (from carbonates) or **$H_2O$** vapour |
| **Equation** | $2ZnS + 3O_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} 2ZnO + 2SO_2\uparrow$ | $ZnCO_3 \xrightarrow{\Delta} ZnO + CO_2\uparrow$ |

4. **Reduction to Free Metal:**
   - **Smelting:** Reduction of metal oxide using Carbon/Coke ($C$) or $CO$ at high temperature with flux (e.g., Blast Furnace for Iron: $Fe_2O_3 + 3CO \rightarrow 2Fe + 3CO_2$).
   - **Thermite Process (Goldschmidt):** Reduction of oxides like $Fe_2O_3, Cr_2O_3, Mn_3O_4$ using **Aluminium powder** ($Fe_2O_3 + 2Al \rightarrow 2Fe + Al_2O_3 + \text{Heat}$); used for **welding railway tracks** and cracked machine parts.
   - **Hall–Héroult Process (for Aluminium):** Electrolytic reduction of purified alumina ($Al_2O_3$) dissolved in **molten Cryolite ($Na_3AlF_6$)** and **Fluorspar ($CaF_2$)**. Cryolite + Fluorspar **lower the melting point** (from $>2000^\circ\text{C}$ to $\sim 950^\circ\text{C}$) and **increase electrical conductivity**.

5. **Refining (Purification):**
   - **Electrolytic Refining:** Used for $Cu, Ag, Au, Zn, Ni$.
     - **Anode:** Impure metal block; **Cathode:** Thin strip of pure metal; **Electrolyte:** Acidified salt solution of the metal.
     - Precious metal impurities ($Au, Ag, Pt$) settle below the anode as **Anode Mud**.
   - **Other Special Methods:** **Mond's Process** (Nickel via $Ni(CO)_4$); **Van Arkel Method** ($Zr, Ti$ via volatile iodides); **Zone Refining** (ultra-pure semiconductors $Si, Ge, Ga$); **Liquation** (low-MP metals $Sn, Pb, Bi$); **Distillation** (volatile metals $Zn, Hg$).

### 6.6 Hardness of Water (Temporary vs Permanent)

- **Why Hard Water Doesn't Lather:** $Ca^{2+}$ and $Mg^{2+}$ ions react with soluble sodium stearate (soap) to precipitate insoluble white **scum** (calcium/magnesium stearate).

| Type of Hardness | Causing Salts | Removal Methods | Chemical Principle |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Temporary Hardness** | **Bicarbonates** of Calcium & Magnesium:<br>$Ca(HCO_3)_2$, $Mg(HCO_3)_2$ | 1. **Boiling**<br>2. **Clark's Method** (adding calculated slaked lime $Ca(OH)_2$) | Boiling: $Ca(HCO_3)_2 \xrightarrow{\Delta} CaCO_3\downarrow + H_2O + CO_2$<br>Clark's: $Ca(HCO_3)_2 + Ca(OH)_2 \rightarrow 2CaCO_3\downarrow + 2H_2O$ |
| **Permanent Hardness** | **Chlorides & Sulphates** of Calcium & Magnesium:<br>$CaCl_2, MgCl_2, CaSO_4, MgSO_4$ | 1. **Washing Soda ($Na_2CO_3$)**<br>2. **Calgon's Method** ($Na_6P_6O_{18}$)<br>3. **Permutit / Zeolite Method** ($NaAlSiO_4$)<br>4. **Synthetic Ion-Exchange Resins** | Washing soda: $CaCl_2 + Na_2CO_3 \rightarrow CaCO_3\downarrow + 2NaCl$<br>Calgon: Traps $Ca^{2+}/Mg^{2+}$ in soluble complex anions.<br>Zeolite: Exchanges $2Na^+$ for $Ca^{2+}/Mg^{2+}$ (regenerated with brine).<br>Ion-exchange: Gives **demineralized / deionized water**. |

### 7.1 Allotropes & Forms of Carbon

- **Charcoal:**
  - **Wood Charcoal:** Porous, high surface area; used as **adsorbent** in gas masks and water filters.
  - **Bone / Animal Charcoal:** Used to **decolorize raw sugar** (brown $\rightarrow$ white).
  - **Sugar Charcoal:** **Purest form of amorphous carbon** (prepared by dehydrating cane sugar with conc. $H_2SO_4$).
- **Coke:** Residue from destructive distillation of coal; clean fuel and **reducing agent** in blast furnace metallurgy.
- **Lampblack (Carbon Black):** Soot from incomplete combustion of hydrocarbons; printer ink, black shoe polish, **rubber tyre reinforcement**.

- **Ethanol vs Methanol:**
  - **Ethanol ($C_2H_5OH$):** "Grain alcohol"; prepared by fermentation of molasses/sugar using **yeast** (invertase & **zymase** enzymes). Used in alcoholic drinks, hand sanitizers, tincture of iodine, and **Gasohol** ($10\text{–}20\%$ ethanol + petrol). **Denatured alcohol** = Ethanol spiked with toxic **Methanol** + Pyridine / $CuSO_4$ to prevent drinking.

- **Saponification & Soap vs Detergents:**
  - **Saponification:** Alkaline hydrolysis of oils/fats (triglycerides) with $NaOH$ or $KOH \rightarrow$ **Soap + Glycerol** (by-product).
    - **$NaOH$** makes **hard soaps** (washing/bathing); **$KOH$** makes **soft soaps** (shaving creams, liquid soaps).
  - **Soap vs Synthetic Detergents:**
    - **Soaps** ($RCOO^-Na^+$): Biodegradable, work only in soft water, form **scum** in hard water & precipitate in acidic water.
    - **Detergents** (Sodium alkyl benzene sulphonates, $R-SO_3^-Na^+$): Work effectively in **both soft & hard water** (and acidic water) because $Ca^{2+}/Mg^{2+}$ sulphonates are soluble. Branched-chain detergents are non-biodegradable (cause foam pollution in rivers).

| **Coal Gas** | **$H_2 + CH_4 + CO$** | Obtained by destructive distillation of bituminous coal. |

#### B. Fuel Efficiency & Quality Indices
- **Calorific Value:** Heat energy released on complete combustion of $1\text{ kg}$ (or $1\text{ g}$) of fuel ($\text{kJ/kg}$).
  - **Highest calorific value:** **Hydrogen ($H_2$, $\sim 150,000\text{ kJ/kg}$)** (used as rocket fuel — Cryogenic $LH_2 + LOX$).
  - Order: $\text{Hydrogen} > \text{Methane} > \text{LPG} > \text{Petrol/Diesel} > \text{Biogas} > \text{Coal} > \text{Wood} > \text{Cow dung cake}$.
- **Octane Number (Petrol / Spark-Ignition Engines):**
  - Measures **anti-knocking quality** of petrol.
  - Scale: ***n*-heptane** (severe knocking) $= \mathbf{0}$; **Iso-octane** (2,2,4-trimethylpentane, minimal knocking) $= \mathbf{100}$.
  - **Anti-knocking agent:** **TEL — Tetraethyl Lead ($Pb(C_2H_5)_4$)** (added historically; now banned/phased out due to toxic lead pollution, replaced by unleaded petrol, MTBE, and ethanol).
- **Cetane Number (Diesel / Compression-Ignition Engines):**
  - Measures **ignition quality / short ignition delay** of diesel.
  - Scale: **$\alpha$-methylnaphthalene** $= \mathbf{0}$; **Cetane (hexadecane, $C_{16}H_{34}$)** $= \mathbf{100}$.

#### B. Chemistry in Agriculture (Fertilizers & Pesticides)
- **NPK Primary Macronutrients:**
  - **Nitrogen ($N$):** Leafy/vegetative growth & protein synthesis.
  - **Phosphorus ($P$):** Root growth, flowering, seed formation, ATP.
  - **Potassium ($K$):** Stalk strength, water balance, disease resistance.
- **Urea ($NH_2CONH_2$ / Carbamide):**
  - Contains **$46\%$ Nitrogen** (highest N% among solid fertilizers; exact molar mass calc $\approx 46.67\%$).
  - **Landmark Discovery:** **First organic compound synthesized in a laboratory** from an inorganic salt (Ammonium cyanate, $NH_4CNO \xrightarrow{\Delta} NH_2CONH_2$) by **Friedrich Wöhler (1828)**, disproving Berzelius's *Vital Force Theory*.
- **Other Key Fertilizers:**
  - **Ammonium Sulphate** ($(NH_4)_2SO_4$, $\sim 21\%\text{ N}$); **CAN** (Calcium Ammonium Nitrate — *"Kisan Khad"*, neutral fertilizer); **DAP** (Diammonium Phosphate, $18\%\text{ N}, 46\%\text{ }P_2O_5$); **Superphosphate of Lime** ($Ca(H_2PO_4)_2 + 2CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$).
- **Pesticides:** **DDT** (persistent, causes biomagnification & thin eggshells in birds), **BHC / Gammexane / Lindane** ($C_6H_6Cl_6$), **Bordeaux Mixture** ($CuSO_4 + Ca(OH)_2$, fungicide).

#### C. Cement (Building Material)
- **Portland Cement:** Invented by **Joseph Aspdin (1824)** in England (resembled Portland stone).
- **Raw Materials:** Limestone ($CaCO_3$) + Clay (Silica $SiO_2$, Alumina $Al_2O_3$, Iron oxide $Fe_2O_3$).
- **Composition:** **Lime ($CaO$: $60\text{–}65\%$)**, **Silica ($SiO_2$: $20\text{–}25\%$)**, **Alumina ($Al_2O_3$: $5\text{–}10\%$)**, Iron oxide ($Fe_2O_3$: $1\text{–}2\%$), Magnesia ($MgO$: $2\text{–}3\%$).
- **Role of Gypsum ($CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$, $2\text{–}3\%$):** Added after grinding clinker to **retard / slow down the setting time** of cement so it can be worked/moulded properly. Setting of cement is an **exothermic** hydration process (requires curing with water).

### 8.2 Water Pollution, BOD/COD & Toxic Metal Diseases

- **Dissolved Oxygen (DO):** Cold water holds up to $\sim 10\text{ ppm}$ $O_2$. Fish struggle if DO drops below **$4\text{–}6\text{ ppm}$**.
- **BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand):** Amount of $O_2$ consumed by aerobic bacteria to decompose biodegradable organic matter (measured over **5 days at $20^\circ\text{C}$**).
  - **Clean water:** $\text{BOD} < 5\text{ ppm}$; **Highly polluted water:** $\text{BOD} \ge 17\text{ ppm}$.
- **COD (Chemical Oxygen Demand):** Total $O_2$ required to oxidize *both* biodegradable and non-biodegradable matter chemically (using acidified $K_2Cr_2O_7$). Always **$\text{COD} \ge \text{BOD}$**.
- **Eutrophication:** Excess **Nitrates ($NO_3^-$) & Phosphates ($PO_4^{3-}$)** (from fertilizers & detergents) wash into lakes $\rightarrow$ **Algal bloom** $\rightarrow$ blocks sunlight & bacteria consume DO decomposing dead algae $\rightarrow$ fish/aquatic life suffocate.

| Water Pollutant | Permissible / Threshold Limit | Disease / Health Effect (Highly Exam-Tested) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Mercury ($Hg$)** | $< 0.001\text{ ppm}$ | **Minamata disease** (neurological disorder from methylmercury-contaminated fish, Japan). |
| **Cadmium ($Cd$)** | $< 0.003\text{ ppm}$ | **Itai-Itai ("Ouch-Ouch") disease** (bone softening/osteomalacia & kidney damage). |
| **Nitrate ($NO_3^-$)** | $> 50\text{ ppm}$ | **Methemoglobinemia / Blue Baby Syndrome** (reduces $O_2$-carrying capacity in infants). |
| **Fluoride ($F^-$)** | $> 1.5\text{ ppm}$ | **Fluorosis** (brown mottling of teeth & bone/skeletal deformities). *(Note: $\sim 1\text{ ppm}$ prevents tooth decay.)* |
| **Arsenic ($As$)** | $> 0.01\text{ ppm}$ | **Blackfoot disease**, skin hyperpigmentation, cancer (groundwater in West Bengal/Bihar). |
| **Lead ($Pb$)** | $> 0.01\text{–}0.05\text{ ppm}$ | **Plumbism** — damages kidneys, liver, nervous system & lowers IQ in children. |

- **Explanation:** **Bromine ($Br_2$)** is the only liquid non-metal at room temperature. **Mercury ($Hg$)** is the liquid metal.

**Q21. Which allotrope of phosphorus is stored under water and glows in the dark?**
(a) Red phosphorus
(b) Black phosphorus
(c) White phosphorus
(d) Scarlet phosphorus
- **Answer:** **(c)**
- **Explanation:** **White phosphorus ($P_4$)** has a very low ignition temperature ($\sim 30^\circ\text{C}$) and shows **chemiluminescence** in moist air, so it is stored under water.

**Q22. Which variety of coal contains the highest percentage of carbon ($90\%+$)?**
(a) Peat
(b) Lignite
(c) Bituminous
(d) Anthracite
- **Answer:** **(d)**
- **Explanation:** **Anthracite** is the highest-rank coal ($90\%+$ carbon), burning with high heat and minimal smoke.

**Q23. Non-stick cooking utensils are coated with which polymer?**
(a) Bakelite
(b) PVC
(c) Teflon (PTFE)
(d) Melamine
- **Answer:** **(c)**
- **Explanation:** **Teflon (Polytetrafluoroethylene, PTFE)** has an extremely low coefficient of friction and high thermal/chemical resistance.

**Q24. Which was the first fully synthetic thermosetting plastic, commonly used for electrical switches?**
(a) Nylon-6,6
(b) Bakelite

(d) Rayon
- **Answer:** **(b)**

**Q25. Which foul-smelling substance is added to LPG cylinders to detect gas leakage?**
(a) Methanol

- **Answer:** **(b)**
- **Explanation:** LPG (Butane + Propane) is odourless; **Ethyl mercaptan ($C_2H_5SH$)** is added for immediate smell detection of leaks.

**Q26. "Water gas" is an industrial fuel mixture of:**
(a) $CO + N_2$
(b) $CO + H_2$
(c) $CO_2 + H_2$
(d) $CH_4 + H_2$
- **Answer:** **(b)**
- **Explanation:** **Water gas** is **$CO + H_2$** (steam over red-hot coke). **Producer gas** is **$CO + N_2$** (air over red-hot coke).

**Q27. Which compound was historically added to petrol as an anti-knocking agent to boost its Octane Number?**
(a) Calcium carbide
(b) Tetraethyl lead (TEL)
(c) Sodium benzoate
(d) Ethylene glycol
- **Answer:** **(b)**

**Q28. Ingestion of which alcohol ("wood spirit") causes permanent blindness and death?**
(a) Ethanol
(b) Propanol
(c) Methanol
(d) Glycerol
- **Answer:** **(c)**
- **Explanation:** **Methanol ($CH_3OH$)** is metabolized in the liver to formaldehyde and formic acid, damaging the **optic nerve** and causing blindness.

**Q29. Which gas is known as "Laughing Gas"?**
(a) Nitric oxide ($NO$)
(b) Nitrous oxide ($N_2O$)
(c) Nitrogen dioxide ($NO_2$)
(d) Ammonia ($NH_3$)
- **Answer:** **(b)**
- **Explanation:** **Nitrous oxide ($N_2O$)** induces mild euphoria and analgesia when inhaled, earning the name laughing gas.

**Q30. Why is Gypsum ($CaSO_4 \cdot 2H_2O$) added during the manufacture of Portland cement?**
(a) To increase the strength of cement immediately
(b) To make the cement white
(c) To slow down (retard) the setting time of cement
(d) To act as a binding flux
- **Answer:** **(c)**
- **Explanation:** Without $2\text{–}3\%$ **Gypsum**, cement clinker sets almost instantly ("flash set") upon adding water. Gypsum retards setting so concrete can be mixed and poured.

**Q31. Which acid is used to remove rust and ink stains from clothes, and is naturally found in tomatoes?**
(a) Citric acid
(b) Malic acid
(c) Oxalic acid
(d) Lactic acid
- **Answer:** **(c)**
- **Explanation:** **Oxalic acid** reduces insoluble ferric oxide (rust) into soluble ferrous oxalate complex, removing stains cleanly.

**Q32. Which type of glass is used to make laboratory glassware and ovenware due to its low thermal expansion?**
(a) Flint glass
(b) Soda-lime glass
(c) Borosilicate (Pyrex) glass
(d) Crookes glass
- **Answer:** **(c)**
- **Explanation:** **Borosilicate / Pyrex glass** contains boric oxide ($B_2O_3$), giving it high thermal shock resistance.

**Q33. Which toxic gas leaked during the 1984 Bhopal Gas Tragedy?**
(a) Phosgene ($COCl_2$)
(b) Methyl Isocyanate ($CH_3NCO$)
(c) Mustard gas
(d) Sulphur dioxide ($SO_2$)
- **Answer:** **(b)**

**Q34. Which international treaty (1987) was signed to phase out Ozone-Depleting Substances (ODS) like CFCs?**
(a) Kyoto Protocol
(b) Montreal Protocol
(c) Paris Agreement
(d) Stockholm Convention
- **Answer:** **(b)**
- **Explanation:** The **Montreal Protocol (1987)** targeted CFCs, halons, and ODS to protect the stratospheric ozone layer. *(Kyoto Protocol deals with greenhouse gases.)*

**Q35. Photochemical smog (Los Angeles smog) is formed in warm, sunny climates and primarily contains:**
(a) $SO_2$, smoke, and fog
(b) $CO$, $CO_2$, and methane
(c) Ozone ($O_3$), PAN (Peroxyacetyl nitrate), and $NO_x$
(d) $H_2S$ and ammonia
- **Answer:** **(c)**
- **Explanation:** Sunlight triggers reactions between nitrogen oxides ($NO_x$) and volatile organic hydrocarbons to form oxidizing pollutants: **Ozone ($O_3$), PAN, and aldehydes**.
