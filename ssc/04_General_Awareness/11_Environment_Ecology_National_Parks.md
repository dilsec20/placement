## Basic Concepts of Ecology & Ecosystems

### Fundamental Terms
- **Ecology:** Term coined by **Ernst Haeckel** (1866); study of interactions between living organisms and their environment.
- **Ecosystem:** Term coined by **A.G. Tansley** (1935); dynamic structural and functional unit consisting of biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living) components.
- **Trophic Levels & 10% Law of Energy:**
  - Formulated by **Raymond Lindeman** (1942).
  - Only **$10\%$ of energy** is transferred from one trophic level to the next higher level; $90\%$ is lost as metabolic heat.
  - Primary Producers (Green plants) $\rightarrow$ Primary Consumers (Herbivores) $\rightarrow$ Secondary Consumers (Carnivores) $\rightarrow$ Tertiary Consumers (Top Predators).
- **Ecological Pyramids (Charles Elton, 1927):**
  - **Pyramid of Energy:** **Always upright** (energy diminishes progressively at higher levels).
  - **Pyramid of Biomass:** Usually upright on land; **inverted in aquatic / pond ecosystems** (phytoplankton biomass < zooplankton < fish).
  - **Pyramid of Numbers:** Usually upright; inverted in a tree ecosystem (one tree supports many birds and parasites).
- **Bioaccumulation:** Gradual accumulation of a toxic chemical (e.g., DDT, Mercury) in an individual organism over its lifetime.
- **Biomagnification:** Progressive increase in concentration of non-biodegradable toxins along successively higher trophic levels of a food chain (causes thinning of eggshells in raptor birds due to DDT).
- **Eutrophication:** Excessive nutrient enrichment (Nitrates and Phosphates from fertilizers/sewage) in water bodies causing algal blooms, oxygen depletion (high BOD - Biochemical Oxygen Demand), and fish suffocation.

## National Parks of India (State-Wise Complete List)

India has **106 National Parks** (as of 2024). Protected under the **Wildlife (Protection) Act, 1972**. Human activities (grazing, timber harvesting, private landholding) are strictly prohibited.

### Record-Holding National Parks
- **First National Park in India:** **Jim Corbett National Park** (established in 1936 as Hailey National Park, Uttarakhand; now Ramganga NP).
- **Largest National Park in India:** **Hemis National Park** (Ladakh, $\approx 4,400\text{ km}^2$; famous for Snow Leopards).
- **Smallest National Park in India:** **South Button Island National Park** (Andaman & Nicobar Islands, $0.03\text{ km}^2$).
- **Only Floating National Park in the World:** **Keibul Lamjao National Park** (Loktak Lake, Manipur; home to the endangered Brow-antlered deer / Sangai / "Dancing Deer" on floating vegetative biomass called *phumdis*).
- **State with the Highest Number of National Parks:** **Madhya Pradesh** (11 National Parks; also called the "Tiger State").

## Tiger Reserves of India & Project Tiger

- **Project Tiger Launched:** **April 1, 1973** at Jim Corbett National Park under Prime Minister Indira Gandhi (First Director: Kailash Sankhala — "Tiger Man of India").
- **Statutory Body:** **National Tiger Conservation Authority (NTCA)**, constituted under 2006 amendment to Wildlife Protection Act.
- **Tiger Census:** Conducted every **4 years** by NTCA and Wildlife Institute of India (WII).
  - India hosts **over $75\%$ of the world's wild tiger population** ($3,682+$ tigers).
  - State with most tigers: **Madhya Pradesh (785)** $\rightarrow$ Karnataka (563) $\rightarrow$ Uttarakhand (560).
- **International Tiger Day:** **July 29** (established at St. Petersburg Tiger Summit, 2010; TX2 goal = double wild tigers).
- **Notable Tiger Reserves:**
  - 53rd: Guru Ghasidas-Tamor Pingla (Chhattisgarh) / Ranipur (UP)
  - 54th: Veerangana Durgavati (MP)
  - 55th: Dholpur-Karauli (Rajasthan)

## Biosphere Reserves of India (MAB Programme)

- Biosphere Reserves are divided into 3 zones:
  1. **Core Zone:** Strictly protected, no human activity.
  2. **Buffer Zone:** Research, education, eco-tourism permitted.
  3. **Transition Zone:** Sustainable human settlements, agriculture, forestry.
- Total in India: **18 Biosphere Reserves**.
- **12 Biosphere Reserves** are part of **UNESCO's World Network of Biosphere Reserves (WNBR)** under the Man and the Biosphere (MAB) Programme:
  1. **Nilgiri** (Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Karnataka — First in India, established 1986; UNESCO 2000)
  2. **Gulf of Mannar** (Tamil Nadu)
  3. **Sundarbans** (West Bengal)
  4. **Nanda Devi** (Uttarakhand)
  5. **Nokrek** (Meghalaya)
  6. **Pachmarhi** (Madhya Pradesh)
  7. **Simlipal** (Odisha)
  8. **Achanakmar-Amarkantak** (Madhya Pradesh / Chhattisgarh)
  9. **Great Nicobar** (Andaman & Nicobar)
  10. **Agasthyamala** (Kerala / Tamil Nadu)
  11. **Khangchendzonga** (Sikkim)
  12. **Panna** (Madhya Pradesh)

## Ramsar Wetland Sites in India

- **Ramsar Convention:** Intergovernmental treaty signed on **February 2, 1971** in Ramsar, Iran for conservation of wetlands. (World Wetlands Day: **February 2**).
- India became a party on **February 1, 1982**.
- India has **85 Ramsar Sites** (as of 2024; second highest in Asia after China; highest state: **Tamil Nadu** with 16 sites, followed by Uttar Pradesh with 10).
- **First Ramsar Sites of India (1981):** **Chilika Lake (Odisha)** and **Keoladeo Ghana National Park (Rajasthan)**.
- **Largest Ramsar Site in India:** **Sundarbans Wetland** (West Bengal, $4,230\text{ km}^2$).
- **Smallest Ramsar Site in India:** **Renuka Wetland** (Himachal Pradesh, $0.2\text{ km}^2$ / 20 hectares).
- **Montreux Record:** Register of Ramsar wetland sites where ecological changes have occurred/are occurring due to human interference.
  - Currently 2 Indian sites are in Montreux Record: **Keoladeo National Park (Rajasthan)** and **Loktak Lake (Manipur)**. (Chilika Lake was removed after successful restoration).

## Major Environmental Conventions & Protocols

| Convention / Treaty | Year | Focus / Mandate |
|---------------------|------|-----------------|
| **Ramsar Convention** | 1971 | Conservation and wise use of wetlands |
| **Stockholm Conference** | 1972 | UN Conference on Human Environment; led to creation of **UNEP** (HQ: Nairobi, Kenya) |
| **CITES** | 1973 | Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora |
| **Bonn Convention (CMS)** | 1979 | Conservation of Migratory Species of Wild Animals |
| **Vienna Convention** | 1985 | Framework for protection of Stratospheric Ozone Layer |
| **Montreal Protocol** | **1987** | Legally binding treaty phasing out Ozone Depleting Substances (**ODSs / CFCs**); most successful environmental treaty |
| **Basel Convention** | 1989 | Control of transboundary movements of hazardous wastes and their disposal |
| **Rio Earth Summit (UNCED)** | **1992** | Landmark summit in Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. Produced: <br>1. **UNFCCC** (Climate Change) <br>2. **CBD** (Biological Diversity) <br>3. **UNCCD** (Desertification) <br>4. **Agenda 21** |
| **Kyoto Protocol** | **1997** | Legally binding emission reduction targets for Annex-I countries (6 Greenhouse Gases); entered into force in 2005 |
| **Stockholm Convention** | 2001 | Elimination/restriction of Persistent Organic Pollutants (**POPs** / "Dirty Dozen") |
| **Paris Agreement** | **2015** | Limit global average temperature rise to **well below $2^\circ\text{C}$** above pre-industrial levels and pursue efforts to limit to **$1.5^\circ\text{C}$** |
| **Minamata Convention** | 2013 | Protecting human health from toxic emissions of **Mercury ($Hg$)** |

## Important Indian Environmental Legislation

| Act / Law | Year | Key Objective / Institution Established |
|-----------|------|-----------------------------------------|
| **Wildlife (Protection) Act** | **1972** | Protection of wild animals, birds, plants; created 6 schedules (rationalized to 4 in 2022); established National Parks, Wildlife Sanctuaries, NTCA |
| **Water (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act** | 1974 | Established CPCB (Central Pollution Control Board) and SPCBs |
| **Forest (Conservation) Act** | 1980 | Restricts de-reservation of reserved forests and diversion of forest land for non-forest purposes |
| **Air (Prevention & Control of Pollution) Act** | 1981 | Control of industrial and vehicular air emissions; amended in 1987 to include **noise** as an air pollutant |
| **Environment (Protection) Act (EPA)** | **1986** | **"Umbrella Act"** passed under Article 253 in the wake of the 1984 **Bhopal Gas Tragedy** (leak of toxic **Methyl Isocyanate - MIC**) |
| **Biological Diversity Act** | 2002 | Access and benefit-sharing; National Biodiversity Authority (NBA, Chennai) |
| **National Green Tribunal (NGT) Act** | **2010** | Established NGT for speedy disposal of environmental disputes (Principal bench: New Delhi; mandate: dispose within 6 months) |

## Environmental Movements in India

- **Bishnoi Movement (1730):** Led by **Amrita Devi Bishnoi** in Khejarli, Rajasthan; 363 Bishnois sacrificed lives hugging Khejri trees to prevent them from being felled by soldiers of Maharaja Abhai Singh.
- **Chipko Movement (1973):** Originated in Reni village, Chamoli district, Uttarakhand; villagers hugged trees to stop commercial logging. Prominent leaders: **Sunderlal Bahuguna** (slogan: *"Ecology is permanent economy"*), **Chandi Prasad Bhatt**, Gaura Devi.
- **Appiko Movement (1983):** Southern counterpart of Chipko; launched in Uttara Kannada district of Karnataka led by **Pandurang Hegde**.
- **Narmada Bachao Andolan (NBA - 1985):** Protest against large dams on Narmada River (Sardar Sarovar Dam) and displacement of tribal people. Led by **Medha Patkar** and Baba Amte.
- **Silent Valley Movement (1978):** Kerala; saved tropical evergreen rainforests of Silent Valley from a proposed hydroelectric dam on the Kunthipuzha River.

## Last 10 Years PYQs with Detailed Explanations

### PYQ 1 — SSC CHSL 2024
**Q.** Which is the only floating national park in the world?
- A) Keoladeo National Park
- B) Keibul Lamjao National Park
- C) Kaziranga National Park
- D) Silent Valley National Park
- **Answer:** **B) Keibul Lamjao National Park** ✅
- *Explanation:* Located on Loktak Lake in Bishnupur district, Manipur. It is famous for floating biomass called *phumdis* and the endangered Eld's deer (Sangai).

### PYQ 2 — SSC CHSL 2023
**Q.** In which year was "Project Tiger" launched in India?
- A) 1970
- B) 1972
- C) 1973
- D) 1980
- **Answer:** **C) 1973** ✅
- *Explanation:* Project Tiger was launched on April 1, 1973 at Corbett National Park to protect the Royal Bengal tiger from extinction.

### PYQ 3 — SSC CHSL 2023
**Q.** The Montreal Protocol is an international treaty designed to protect:
- A) Marine biodiversity
- B) Ozone layer
- C) Wetlands of international importance
- D) Endangered bird species
- **Answer:** **B) Ozone layer** ✅
- *Explanation:* Agreed on September 16, 1987, the Montreal Protocol phased out the production of substances responsible for stratospheric ozone depletion (CFCs, halons).

### PYQ 4 — SSC CHSL 2022
**Q.** Gir National Park in Gujarat is globally renowned as the only natural habitat of:
- A) Royal Bengal Tiger
- B) One-Horned Rhinoceros
- C) Asiatic Lion
- D) Snow Leopard
- **Answer:** **C) Asiatic Lion** ✅
- *Explanation:* Gir Forest National Park and Wildlife Sanctuary is the sole sanctuary in the world where the Asiatic lion (*Panthera leo persica*) survives in the wild.

### PYQ 5 — SSC CHSL 2022
**Q.** Which was the first Biosphere Reserve established in India?
- A) Sundarbans
- B) Nanda Devi
- C) Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve
- D) Gulf of Mannar
- **Answer:** **C) Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve** ✅
- *Explanation:* Established in 1986 across Tamil Nadu, Kerala, and Karnataka, covering Mudumalai, Mukurthi, Wayanad, Bandipur, and Silent Valley.

### PYQ 6 — SSC CHSL 2021
**Q.** According to the 10% law of energy transfer in an ecosystem, who formulated this principle?
- A) Ernst Haeckel
- B) Raymond Lindeman
- C) Arthur Tansley
- D) Charles Elton
- **Answer:** **B) Raymond Lindeman** ✅
- *Explanation:* Raymond Lindeman in 1942 proposed the Ten Percent Law, stating that only $10\%$ of energy is transferred to each successive trophic level.

### PYQ 7 — SSC CHSL 2020
**Q.** Dachigam National Park is famous for the conservation of which endangered mammal?
- A) Hangul (Kashmir Stag)
- B) Sangai Deer
- C) Nilgiri Tahr
- D) Blackbuck
- **Answer:** **A) Hangul (Kashmir Stag)** ✅
- *Explanation:* Located near Srinagar, Dachigam is the last viable refuge of the Hangul (*Cervus hanglu hanglu*).

### PYQ 8 — SSC CHSL 2019
**Q.** Which toxic gas was released during the infamous Bhopal Gas Tragedy on December 2–3, 1984?
- A) Methyl Isocyanate
- B) Phosgene
- C) Potassium cyanide
- D) Carbon monoxide
- **Answer:** **A) Methyl Isocyanate (MIC)** ✅
- *Explanation:* MIC leaked from the Union Carbide pesticide plant in Bhopal, leading directly to the enactment of the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986.

### PYQ 9 — SSC CHSL 2018
**Q.** The Chipko Movement, a non-violent tree-hugging movement, originated in which modern Indian state?
- A) Himachal Pradesh
- B) Uttarakhand
- C) Madhya Pradesh
- D) Jharkhand
- **Answer:** **B) Uttarakhand** ✅
- *Explanation:* Started in 1973 in Chamoli district (then Uttar Pradesh, now Uttarakhand), led by Sunderlal Bahuguna, Chandi Prasad Bhatt, and Gaura Devi.

### PYQ 10 — SSC CHSL 2017
**Q.** Which of the following ecological pyramids is ALWAYS upright and can never be inverted?
- A) Pyramid of Numbers
- B) Pyramid of Biomass
- C) Pyramid of Energy
- D) None of the above
- **Answer:** **C) Pyramid of Energy** ✅
- *Explanation:* Since energy is continually lost as heat at each trophic transfer (Second Law of Thermodynamics), the energy pyramid is always upright.
