## 1. Ecology, Ecosystems & Ecological Pyramids

### 1.1 Key Pioneers & Core Definitions

| Term / Concept | Coined / Propounded By | Year | Exam-Tested Definition & Key Fact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ecology (*Oekologie*)** | **Ernst Haeckel** | **1866** | Scientific study of the reciprocal relationship between living organisms and their environment (*Oikos* = home, *Logos* = study). |
| **Ecosystem** | **A.G. Tansley** | **1935** | Structural and functional unit of nature where biotic (living) communities interact with abiotic (non-living) surroundings. |
| **Biocoenosis** | **Karl Möbius** | **1877** | Early term describing a community of interacting organisms living together in a habitat. |
| **Ecological Niche** | **Joseph Grinnell** | **1917** | The unique functional role and position of a species in an ecosystem (no two species can occupy the exact same niche indefinitely — **Gause's Competitive Exclusion Principle**). |
| **Father of Modern Ecology** | **Eugene P. Odum** | — | Authored *Fundamentals of Ecology* (1953); pioneered ecosystem energetics. |
| **Father of Indian Ecology** | **Prof. Ramdeo Misra** | — | Established ecological teaching and research in India at **Banaras Hindu University (BHU)**, Varanasi; key role in establishing NCEPC (1972). |
| **Deep Ecology** | **Arne Naess** | **1973** | Environmental philosophy emphasizing the inherent worth of all living beings regardless of human utility. |

### 1.2 Levels of Ecological Organization

$$\text{Organism (Individual)} \rightarrow \text{Population} \rightarrow \text{Biological Community} \rightarrow \text{Ecosystem} \rightarrow \text{Biome} \rightarrow \text{Biosphere}$$

- **Organism:** Basic unit of ecological study; an individual living entity.
- **Population:** Group of individuals of the **same species** living in a specific area at a given time.
- **Community:** Assemblage of **all populations (different species)** interacting in a given area.
- **Ecosystem:** Community + abiotic environment interacting via energy flow and nutrient cycling. *(Smallest: a drop of pond water; Largest: Biosphere/Oceans; Largest man-made ecosystem: Agro-ecosystem / Aquarium).*
- **Biome:** Large regional terrestrial/aquatic unit characterized by distinct climate, flora, and fauna (e.g., Tundra, Taiga, Desert).
- **Biosphere:** Narrow zone of Earth where lithosphere, hydrosphere, and atmosphere overlap to support life (~11 km below sea level to ~9 km above).
- **Ecotone:** Transition zone between two adjacent biomes/ecosystems (e.g., Mangroves, Estuaries, Grasslands). Often shows **Edge Effect** (higher species diversity and density at the boundary than in either adjacent ecosystem; species mainly found here are **Edge Species**).

### 1.3 Ecosystem Components, Food Chains & Energy Flow

| Component | Category | Examples & Functional Role |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Abiotic** | Physical / Inorganic / Organic | Sunlight, temperature, water, soil (edaphic factors), pH, $N, P, K$, humus, proteins, lipids. |
| **Biotic — Producers** | Autotrophs (Photo/Chemo) | Green plants, algae, cyanobacteria (*Nostoc, Anabaena*), chemosynthetic bacteria; convert solar energy into chemical energy (**GPP** = Gross Primary Productivity; **NPP = GPP − Respiration**). |
| **Biotic — Consumers** | Heterotrophs | **Primary** (Herbivores: deer, grasshopper, zooplankton), **Secondary** (Primary carnivores: frog, fox), **Tertiary/Apex** (Top carnivores: tiger, eagle, shark). |
| **Biotic — Decomposers** | Saprotrophs / Osmotrophs | Fungi and bacteria; secrete digestive enzymes onto dead organic matter (**mineralization & humification**) to recycle nutrients. *(Detritivores like earthworms ingest detritus directly).* |

- **Food Chain vs. Food Web:**
  - **Food Chain:** Linear transfer of food energy ($T_1 \rightarrow T_2 \rightarrow T_3 \rightarrow T_4$).
    - **Grazing Food Chain (GFC):** Starts from living green plants (major conduit of energy in **aquatic** ecosystems).
    - **Detritus Food Chain (DFC):** Starts from dead organic matter (major conduit of energy in **terrestrial** forest ecosystems).
  - **Food Web:** Interconnected network of multiple food chains; increases **ecosystem stability** by offering alternative food pathways.
- **Energy Flow is ALWAYS Unidirectional** (Sun $\rightarrow$ Producers $\rightarrow$ Consumers $\rightarrow$ Lost as heat), whereas **Nutrient Cycling (Biogeochemical Cycles)** is **Cyclic**.
- **Lindeman's 10% Law (Raymond Lindeman, 1942):** Only about **10%** of the energy at one trophic level is assimilated and transferred to the next higher trophic level; the remaining **~90%** is lost as metabolic heat, respiration, and unassimilated waste. This limits most food chains to **4–5 trophic levels**. *(Additionally, plants capture only **1–5%** of incident solar radiation, or **2–10%** of **PAR** [Photosynthetically Active Radiation, 400–700 nm]).*

### 1.4 The 3 Ecological Pyramids (Eltonian Pyramids — Charles Elton, 1927)

| Pyramid Type | Shape in Terrestrial (Grassland / Forest) | Shape in Aquatic (Pond / Ocean) | Special / Inverted Exceptions (SSC Favorites) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pyramid of Numbers** | **Upright** (Grassland: Grasses > Grasshoppers > Frogs > Snakes) | **Upright** (Pond: Phytoplankton > Zooplankton > Small Fish > Large Fish) | **Inverted:** Parasitic food chain on a single tree ($1 \text{ Tree} \rightarrow \text{Birds} \rightarrow \text{Lice/Fleas} \rightarrow \text{Protozoa}$). **Spindle-shaped:** Forest tree $\rightarrow$ herbivorous birds $\rightarrow$ few hawks. |
| **Pyramid of Biomass** | **Upright** (Trees/Grass > Herbivores > Carnivores) | **INVERTED** (Small standing crop of rapidly reproducing phytoplankton supports much larger biomass of zooplankton & fishes) | **Classic SSC Trap:** Biomass pyramid in a **Sea/Pond** is **Inverted**; in a **Grassland/Forest** it is **Upright**. |
| **Pyramid of Energy** | **ALWAYS Upright** | **ALWAYS Upright** | **NEVER Inverted** in any natural ecosystem (governed by **2nd Law of Thermodynamics** and **Lindeman's 10% Law**). |

> **Limitations of Ecological Pyramids:** They do not accommodate **food webs**, exclude **saprophytes/decomposers**, and assume a species belongs to a single trophic level (ignoring omnivores like humans or sparrows).

### 1.5 Ecological Interactions Table

| Interaction | Species A | Species B | Definition | Classic SSC Exam Examples |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **Mutualism** | $+$ | $+$ | Obligatory or mutually beneficial relationship for both species. | **Lichen** (Algae/Cyanobacteria + Fungi), **Mycorrhiza** (Fungi + Roots of higher plants), **Rhizobium** in legume root nodules, **Coral polyps + Zooxanthellae**, **Fig & Fig Wasp**, **Pollination** (Bees + Flowers). |
| **Commensalism** | $+$ | $0$ | One species benefits; the other is neither harmed nor benefited. | **Barnacles on a Whale**, **Clownfish & Sea Anemone**, **Cattle Egret & Grazing Cattle**, **Epiphytes (Orchids) on Mango tree**, **Remora (suckerfish) & Shark**. |
| **Parasitism** | $+$ | $-$ | Parasite derives nutrition/shelter at the expense of the host. | **Cuscuta (*Amarbel*)** on hedge plants (total stem parasite), **Plasmodium** in humans, **Ticks/Lice** on dogs, **Tapeworm** in intestine, **Brood parasitism** (**Cuckoo/Koel** laying eggs in **Crow's** nest). |
| **Predation** | $+$ | $-$ | Predator kills and consumes prey; regulates prey population. | **Tiger & Deer**, **Snake & Frog**, **Pitcher plant (*Nepenthes*) / Venus flytrap** (insectivorous plants eating insects for **Nitrogen**). |
| **Competition** | $-$ | $-$ | Both species suffer due to shared limited resources. | **Flamingos & Resident Fishes** competing for zooplankton in South American lakes; **Abingdon Tortoise vs. Goats** in Galápagos. |
| **Amensalism** | $-$ | $0$ | One species is inhibited/destroyed while the other remains unaffected. | ***Penicillium* mould** secreting **penicillin** that kills *Staphylococcus* bacteria (**Antibiosis**); **Black Walnut (*Juglans nigra*)** secreting **juglone** suppressing nearby plants. |
| **Proto-cooperation** | $+$ | $+$ | Non-obligatory mutual benefit (can survive separately). | **Oxpecker bird & Rhinoceros**, **Hermit crab & Sea anemone**. |
| **Neutralism** | $0$ | $0$ | Neither species affects the other directly. | **Cactus & Tarantula** sharing desert space without direct trophic interaction. |

### 1.6 Ecological Succession & World Biomes

- **Ecological Succession (Clements, 1916):** Orderly, directional change in species composition of a community over time until a stable **Climax Community** is established.
  - **Primary Succession:** Occurs on previously lifeless, bare substrates without soil (bare rock, newly cooled lava, sand dunes). **Very slow** (hundreds/thousands of years). **Pioneer species:** **Lichens** (on bare rock — secrete acids to weather rock into soil) and **Phytoplankton** (in water).
  - **Secondary Succession:** Occurs in areas where a previous community was destroyed by fire, flood, deforestation, or abandoned farmland. **Much faster** because soil and propagules/seeds are already present.
  - **Hydrarch Succession:** Begins in wet/aquatic areas (phytoplankton $\rightarrow$ submerged $\rightarrow$ floating $\rightarrow$ reed-swamp $\rightarrow$ marsh-meadow $\rightarrow$ scrub $\rightarrow$ **mesic forest**).
  - **Xerarch Succession:** Begins in dry areas/bare rock (crustose lichens $\rightarrow$ foliose lichens $\rightarrow$ mosses $\rightarrow$ herbs $\rightarrow$ shrubs $\rightarrow$ **mesic forest**). *Both converge toward a mesic (medium-water) climax forest!*
  - **Sere:** The entire sequence of communities replacing one another; individual intermediate stages are **Seral communities**.

| Biome | Climate & Soil | Dominant Vegetation | Characteristic Fauna & Exam Facts |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Tundra** (Arctic & Alpine) | Extremely cold, low precipitation, **Permafrost** (permanently frozen subsoil) | Mosses, **Lichens (Reindeer moss)**, sedges, dwarf shrubs; **treeless** | Reindeer/Caribou, Arctic Fox, Polar Bear, Musk Ox. |
| **Taiga / Boreal Forest** | Long severe winters, cool short summers | Evergreen **Coniferous trees** (Pine, Spruce, Fir, Larch) with needle leaves | **Largest terrestrial biome** by area; Moose, Lynx, Siberian Tiger, Mink. |

### 1.7 Bioaccumulation, Biomagnification & Eutrophication

- **Bioaccumulation:** Gradual build-up of a persistent, non-biodegradable toxin (e.g., DDT, Mercury, PCBs) in the tissues of a **single organism** over its lifetime (absorption rate $>$ excretion rate).
- **Biomagnification (Biological Magnification):** Progressive **increase in concentration** of a persistent toxicant at **each successive trophic level** of a food chain.
  - *Classic SSC Example:* **DDT** in aquatic food chain: Water ($0.003\text{ ppb}$) $\rightarrow$ Zooplankton ($0.04\text{ ppm}$) $\rightarrow$ Small fish ($0.5\text{ ppm}$) $\rightarrow$ Large fish ($2\text{ ppm}$) $\rightarrow$ **Fish-eating birds ($25\text{ ppm}$)**. High DDT disrupts calcium metabolism in birds, causing **thinning of eggshells** and premature breaking (highlighted in Rachel Carson's *Silent Spring*, 1962).
- **Eutrophication:** Nutrient enrichment (mainly **Nitrates $NO_3^-$** and **Phosphates $PO_4^{3-}$** from agricultural fertilizer runoff, sewage, and detergents) of a water body.
  - **Mechanism:** Excess nutrients $\rightarrow$ Explosive **Algal Bloom** $\rightarrow$ Blocks sunlight to submerged plants $\rightarrow$ Algae die and decompose $\rightarrow$ Aerobic bacteria consume dissolved oxygen $\rightarrow$ **Hypoxia / Anoxia** $\rightarrow$ Mass fish mortality.
  - **Cultural (Accelerated) Eutrophication:** Human-induced speeding up of lake aging.
  - **BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand):** Amount of dissolved oxygen ($O_2$) required by aerobic microorganisms to break down **biodegradable** organic matter in water (at $20^\circ\text{C}$ over 5 days). **Higher BOD = Higher water pollution** (Clean river water has $\text{BOD} < 5\text{ ppm}$; heavily polluted sewage has $\text{BOD} > 17\text{ ppm}$).
  - **COD (Chemical Oxygen Demand):** Total oxygen required to chemically oxidize **both biodegradable and non-biodegradable** organic matter using a strong oxidant ($K_2Cr_2O_7$). **Always $\text{COD} \ge \text{BOD}$**.
  - **Terror of Bengal:** **Water Hyacinth (*Eichhornia crassipes*)** — invasive aquatic weed introduced for its purple flowers; grows rapidly in eutrophic waters, draining dissolved oxygen and killing fish.

## 2. Biodiversity, Hotspots, IUCN & Endemic Species

### 2.1 Biodiversity Concepts & Measurement

- **Term:** Coined by **Walter G. Rosen (1985)** as "biological diversity" and popularized by **Edward O. Wilson (1988)** ("Father of Biodiversity").
- **Three Levels of Biodiversity:**
  1. **Genetic Diversity:** Variation of genes within a single species (e.g., $>50,000$ rice strains in India; *Rauwolfia vomitoria* varying in reserpine potency).
  2. **Species Diversity:** Variety of species within a region (e.g., Western Ghats has higher amphibian species diversity than Eastern Ghats).
  3. **Ecological / Ecosystem Diversity:** Variety of ecosystems in a geographical area (deserts, rainforests, mangroves, coral reefs).
- **Measurement of Biodiversity (R.H. Whittaker, 1960/1972):**
  - **Alpha ($\alpha$) Diversity:** Species richness **within** a particular habitat/ecosystem.
  - **Beta ($\beta$) Diversity:** Rate of **species turnover / change** in composition **between** two adjacent ecosystems.
  - **Gamma ($\gamma$) Diversity:** Total overall species diversity across a large **landscape or geographical region**.
  - **Quantitative Indices:** **Shannon-Wiener Index ($H'$)** (measures both species richness & evenness) and **Simpson's Diversity Index ($D$)**.
- **Latitudinal Gradient:** Biodiversity **increases from poles toward the equator** (tropics hold maximum biodiversity due to stable climate, high solar energy, and longer evolutionary time).

### 2.2 Global & Indian Biodiversity Hotspots

- **Concept:** Proposed by **Norman Myers (1988)**. Currently **36 Biodiversity Hotspots** globally (occupy ~2.5% of Earth's land surface but hold $>50\%$ of endemic plant species).
- **Two Strict Criteria for a Hotspot:**
  1. **Species Endemism:** Must contain at least **1,500 species of endemic vascular plants** ($>0.5\%$ of the world's total).
  2. **Degree of Threat:** Must have lost at least **70% of its original primary vegetation** ($\le 30\%$ remaining).

### 2.3 In-situ vs. Ex-situ Conservation Comparison Table

| Feature | In-situ Conservation ("On-site") | Ex-situ Conservation ("Off-site") |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Meaning** | Conserving species inside their **natural habitats** and ecosystems. | Conserving threatened species **outside** their natural habitats under human care. |
| **Examples** | **National Parks**, **Wildlife Sanctuaries**, **Biosphere Reserves**, **Conservation & Community Reserves**, **Sacred Groves**, **Ramsar Wetlands**. | **Zoological Parks (Zoos)**, **Botanical Gardens**, **Seed Banks**, **Gene Banks**, **Aquaria**, **Wildlife Safari Parks**, **Cryopreservation**, **Tissue Culture**. |
| **Scope** | Protects the entire ecosystem, food web, and evolutionary processes. | Focuses on individual endangered species, gametes, embryos, or seeds. |
| **Key Indian Facts** | **Sacred Groves** (forest patches protected by indigenous religious beliefs): *Devrai* (Maharashtra), *Orans* (Rajasthan), *Kavu* (Kerala), *Law Kyntang / Mawphlang* (Meghalaya), *Sarna* (Jharkhand). | **Cryopreservation:** Preserving gametes, seeds, or embryos in **liquid nitrogen at $-196^\circ\text{C}$**; **National Bureau of Plant Genetic Resources (NBPGR)**, New Delhi. |

### 2.4 IUCN Red List Categories

- **IUCN:** International Union for Conservation of Nature — founded **1948**; HQ in **Gland, Switzerland**; publishes the **Red Data Book** (since **1964**; pink pages = Critically Endangered, green pages = formerly endangered species recovered).
- **9 Official Categories (from highest to lowest risk):**
  1. **Extinct (EX):** No reasonable doubt the last individual has died (e.g., Dodo, Passenger Pigeon, Indian Cheetah [extinct in India 1952]).
  2. **Extinct in the Wild (EW):** Survives only in captivity/cultivation (e.g., Spix's Macaw, Hawaiian Crow).
  3. **Critically Endangered (CR):** Extremely high risk of extinction in the wild ($\ge 80\%$ decline in 10 yrs / 3 generations).
  4. **Endangered (EN):** Very high risk of extinction ($\ge 50\%$ decline).
  5. **Vulnerable (VU):** High risk of extinction ($\ge 30\%$ decline).
  *(Note: **CR + EN + VU** together constitute **"Threatened"** species.)*
  6. **Near Threatened (NT):** Close to qualifying for threatened soon.
  7. **Least Concern (LC):** Widespread and abundant.
  8. **Data Deficient (DD)** & 9. **Not Evaluated (NE)**.

## 3. Protected Areas of India (Updated 2024–2026 Quick Reference)

### 3.1 National Parks of India (106 WII Baseline → 107–108 Post-2025 Notifications)

- **Legal Basis:** Declared by State/Central Government under **Section 35 of the Wild Life (Protection) Act, 1972**. Grazing and private land rights are **strictly prohibited** (unlike Wildlife Sanctuaries where limited regulated rights/grazing may be permitted).
- **Latest Tally (2025–2026):**
  - **WII Official Database Baseline:** **106 National Parks** (covering ~1.35% of India's geographical area).
  - **2025 Notifications:**
    - **Sikhna Jwhwlao National Park (Assam):** Notified on **March 5, 2025** ($316.29\text{ km}^2$ in Kokrajhar & Chirang districts, Bodoland Territorial Region) — **Assam's 8th National Park** (after Raimona & Dehing Patkai in 2021).
    - **Similipal National Park (Odisha):** Final notification under Section 35 issued on **April 24, 2025** ($845.70\text{ km}^2$, home to the world's only wild pseudo-melanistic/black tigers) — **Odisha's 2nd National Park**. *(Note: Since Similipal was already included in some 106-park compilations as a proposed/partly-notified NP, the effective national tally is **107–108** depending on the baseline used.)*

### 3.3 Biosphere Reserves of India (All 18 Reserves — 13 in UNESCO MAB)

- **Structure of a Biosphere Reserve (3 Concentric Zones):**
  1. **Core Zone:** Strictly protected, legally inviolate area (often a National Park/WLS); no human activity.
  2. **Buffer Zone:** Surrounds core; permits limited eco-tourism, scientific research, monitoring, and environmental education.
  3. **Transition Zone:** Outermost flexible zone; sustainable agriculture, forestry, and human settlements coexist with conservation.
- **UNESCO MAB Programme:** Launched in **1971** (Man and the Biosphere). With **Cold Desert (Himachal Pradesh)** added in **September 2025** (37th MAB-ICC session, Hangzhou), **13 out of 18** Indian Biosphere Reserves are in UNESCO's World Network of Biosphere Reserves (WNBR).

## 4. Pollution, Toxic Diseases & Climate Change

### 4.1 Air Pollution, National AQI & Smog

- **Primary Pollutants:** Emitted directly from sources — $CO$ (Carbon Monoxide — binds to hemoglobin forming **carboxyhemoglobin**, 200–250× higher affinity than $O_2$), $SO_2$, $NO_x$, Particulate Matter ($PM_{10}, PM_{2.5}$), Lead ($Pb$), VOCs.
- **Secondary Pollutants:** Formed in the atmosphere by chemical reactions between primary pollutants — **Tropospheric Ozone ($O_3$)**, **PAN (Peroxyacetyl Nitrate)**, Sulfuric acid ($H_2SO_4$), Nitric acid ($HNO_3$), Photochemical Smog.
- **National Air Quality Index (NAQI):** Launched on **October 17, 2014** ("One Number – One Colour – One Description") by MoEFCC / CPCB.
  - **6 Categories:** Good (0–50, Dark Green), Satisfactory (51–100, Light Green), Moderately Polluted (101–200, Yellow), Poor (201–300, Orange), Very Poor (301–400, Red), Severe (401–500, Maroon).
  - **8 Monitored Pollutants (24-hr / 8-hr averages):**
    1. **$PM_{10}$** (Particulate Matter $\le 10\ \mu\text{m}$)
    2. **$PM_{2.5}$** (Fine Particulate Matter $\le 2.5\ \mu\text{m}$)
    3. **$NO_2$** (Nitrogen Dioxide)
    4. **$SO_2$** (Sulphur Dioxide)
    5. **$CO$** (Carbon Monoxide)
    6. **$O_3$** (Ozone)
    7. **$NH_3$** (Ammonia)
    8. **$Pb$** (Lead)
  - **CRITICAL SSC TRAP:** **Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$) and Methane ($CH_4$) are NOT included in India's AQI!**
- **Types of Smog (Smoke + Fog):**

| Feature | London Smog (Classical / Sulfurous) | Los Angeles Smog (Photochemical) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Main Components** | $SO_2$ + Coal smoke + Fog | $NO_x$ + Hydrocarbons (VOCs) + **Sunlight** $\rightarrow$ **$O_3$ + PAN** |
| **Chemical Nature** | **Reducing** smog | **Oxidizing** smog (brownish haze) |
| **Peak Timing** | Cold, humid **winter mornings** (e.g., Great London Smog, **1952**) | Hot, dry, sunny **summer afternoons** |
| **Health Effect** | Bronchitis, lung irritation | Severe **eye irritation** (PAN), respiratory distress |

- **Bio-indicators of Pollution:**
  - **Lichens:** Natural bio-indicators of **$SO_2$ (air pollution)** — die out in $SO_2$-polluted industrial cities.
  - ***Escherichia coli (E. coli)*:** Indicator of **fecal contamination** in drinking water.
  - ***Tubifex* (Sludge worm / Bloodworm):** Indicator of **heavily polluted, low-oxygen organic water**.
- **Bharat Stage Emission Standards (BS-VI):** Enforced nationwide from **April 1, 2020** (India **skipped BS-V** directly from BS-IV). Cuts $NO_x$ by ~70% (diesel) / ~25% (petrol), cuts Particulate Matter by ~80%, and reduces fuel **sulphur content from 50 ppm to 10 ppm**.

### 4.3 Climate Change, Greenhouse Effect, Acid Rain & Ozone Depletion

- **Greenhouse Effect:** Natural warming process (discovered by **Joseph Fourier, 1824**; quantified by **John Tyndall** & **Svante Arrhenius**) trapping outgoing **long-wave infrared (terrestrial) radiation** while allowing incoming short-wave solar radiation. Without it, Earth's average temperature would be **$-18^\circ\text{C}$** instead of **$+15^\circ\text{C}$**.
- **6 Kyoto Protocol Greenhouse Gases & Relative Contributions / GWP:**

| Greenhouse Gas | Anthropogenic Share (~Global Warming) | 100-Year GWP ($CO_2 = 1$) | Major Sources |
| :--- | :---: | :---: | :--- |
| **Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$)** | **~60–76%** *(Largest volume)* | **1** | Fossil fuel combustion, cement, deforestation. |
| **Methane ($CH_4$)** | **~16–20%** | **~28–30** | **Paddy/rice fields**, wetlands, **ruminant cattle** (enteric fermentation), landfills, natural gas leaks. |
| **Nitrous Oxide ($N_2O$)** | **~6%** | **~273** | Nitrogenous fertilizers, nylon production, biomass burning. |
| **Hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs)** | $<2\%$ | **100s – 14,800** | Replaced CFCs in AC/refrigerators (now phased down under **Kigali Amendment, 2016**). |
| **Perfluorocarbons (PFCs)** | $<1\%$ | **6,500 – 9,200** | Aluminium smelting, semiconductor manufacturing. |
| **Sulphur Hexafluoride ($SF_6$)** | $<1\%$ | **~24,300** *(Highest GWP)* | Electrical switchgear/insulators. |

*(Note: **Water Vapour ($H_2O$)** is the most abundant **natural** GHG; **Tropospheric $O_3$** and **Black Carbon** are short-lived climate forcers. **Carbon Monoxide ($CO$)** is NOT a direct GHG.)*

- **Acid Rain:**
  - Term coined by **Robert Angus Smith (1852/1872)** ("Father of Acid Rain").
  - **Threshold:** Rainwater with **$\text{pH} < 5.6$** (normal unpolluted rain is slightly acidic at **pH ~5.6** due to dissolved $CO_2$ forming weak carbonic acid $H_2CO_3$).
  - **Causes:** **$SO_2$ (~60–70%)** forming Sulfuric Acid ($H_2SO_4$) and **$NO_x$ (~30–40%)** forming Nitric Acid ($HNO_3$).
  - **Stone Leprosy / Marble Cancer:** Acid rain reacts with marble/limestone ($CaCO_3 + H_2SO_4 \rightarrow CaSO_4 + H_2O + CO_2$), corroding monuments like the **Taj Mahal** (Mathura Oil Refinery $SO_2$ emissions).
- **Ozone Depletion:**
  - **Good Ozone vs. Bad Ozone:** **Stratospheric Ozone** (15–35 km altitude) absorbs harmful **UV-B** radiation; **Tropospheric Ozone** (ground level) is a toxic secondary pollutant and GHG.
  - **Measurement:** **Dobson Units (DU)** ($1\text{ DU} = 0.01\text{ mm}$ thickness of pure ozone at STP; normal column ~300 DU; **$<220\text{ DU}$** = **Ozone Hole**). Measured via **Dobson Spectrophotometer**.
  - **Antarctic Ozone Hole:** Discovered in **1985** by **Joe Farman, Brian Gardiner, and Jonathan Shanklin** (British Antarctic Survey).
  - **Ozone-Depleting Substances (ODS):** **CFCs (Freons)**, **Halons** (fire extinguishers), **Carbon Tetrachloride ($CCl_4$)**, **Methyl Bromide ($CH_3Br$)**, HCFCs. One Chlorine ($Cl^\bullet$) radical destroys **~100,000 ozone molecules** in a catalytic chain reaction.
  - **Polar Stratospheric Clouds (PSCs / Nacreous Clouds):** Form in extreme cold ($<-78^\circ\text{C}$) during Antarctic winter/spring, providing ice surfaces that accelerate chlorine release and ozone destruction.
  - **UV-B Effects:** Skin cancer (melanoma), cataracts, **snow blindness** (inflammation of cornea), immune suppression, phytoplankton death.

| **CMS (Bonn Convention)** | **1979** / 1983 | Bonn, Germany | Conservation of **Migratory Species** of wild animals; COP13 held in **Gandhinagar, India (2020)**. |
| **Vienna Convention** | **1985** / 1988 | Vienna, Austria | Framework for protection of the **Ozone Layer** (no binding targets — paved way for Montreal). |
| **Montreal Protocol** | **Sept 16, 1987** / 1989 | Montreal, Canada | Binding phase-out of **ODS (CFCs, Halons)**; **World Ozone Day = Sept 16**; most successful UN treaty. |
| **Basel Convention** | **1989** / 1992 | Basel, Switzerland | Controls **transboundary movement of hazardous wastes** & their disposal (excludes radioactive waste). |
| **Rio Earth Summit (UNCED)** | **June 1992** | Rio de Janeiro, Brazil | Produced **Agenda 21** (sustainable development), **Rio Declaration**, Forest Principles, and opened **3 Rio Conventions**: **UNFCCC**, **CBD**, **UNCCD**. |
| **UNCCD** | **1994** / 1996 | Paris | Combats **Desertification** & drought; **COP14** held in **New Delhi (2019)**; **Land Degradation Neutrality by 2030**. |
| **Kyoto Protocol** | **Dec 1997** / **Feb 2005** | Kyoto, Japan | **1st legally binding** GHG reduction targets for **Annex I (developed) countries** under **CBDR**; introduced CDM, Joint Implementation, Emissions Trading. |
| **Rotterdam Convention** | **1998** / 2004 | Rotterdam, Netherlands | **Prior Informed Consent (PIC)** procedure for trade in hazardous chemicals & pesticides. |
| **Cartagena Protocol** | **2000** / 2003 | Montreal (adopted) | Biosafety under CBD; regulates safe handling/transport of **Living Modified Organisms (LMOs / GMOs)**. |
| **Stockholm Convention (POPs)** | **2001** / 2004 | Stockholm, Sweden | Eliminates/restricts **Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs)** — initially **"Dirty Dozen"** (DDT, Aldrin, Dieldrin, PCBs, Dioxins, Furans, etc.). |
| **Nagoya Protocol** | **2010** / 2014 | Nagoya, Japan | Access & Benefit-Sharing (**ABS**) of genetic resources under CBD; adopted **Aichi Biodiversity Targets (2011–2020)**. |
| **Minamata Convention** | **2013** / 2017 | Kumamoto, Japan | Global treaty to protect health & environment from anthropogenic **Mercury ($Hg$)** emissions/releases. |
| **Paris Agreement (COP21)** | **Dec 2015** / Nov 2016 | Paris, France | Limit global warming to **well below $2^\circ\text{C}$**, pursue **$1.5^\circ\text{C}$** via voluntary **NDCs**; **ISA** launched here. |
| **Kigali Amendment** | **Oct 2016** / 2019 | Kigali, Rwanda | Amendment to **Montreal Protocol** to phase down **HFCs** (non-ODS potent GHGs) by 80–85% by 2047. |
| **Kunming–Montreal GBF** | **Dec 2022** | Montreal (CBD COP15) | Replaced Aichi Targets; **4 Goals for 2050 & 23 Targets for 2030**, including **"30×30" Target** (protect 30% of land & oceans by 2030). |

## 6. Last 10 Years SSC CHSL / CGL Environment PYQs (35 Solved Questions)

1. **Q (SSC CGL):** Who coined the term 'Ecosystem' in 1935?  
   **Ans:** **A.G. Tansley** *(Ernst Haeckel coined 'Ecology' in 1866; Ramdeo Misra is the Father of Indian Ecology).*

2. **Q (SSC CHSL):** According to Lindeman's 10% law, if producers have $10,000\text{ J}$ of energy, how much energy will reach the tertiary consumer (4th trophic level)?  
   **Ans:** **$10\text{ J}$** *(Producers $10,000 \rightarrow$ Primary $1,000 \rightarrow$ Secondary $100 \rightarrow$ Tertiary $10\text{ J}$).*

3. **Q (SSC CGL):** Which ecological pyramid is ALWAYS upright in all natural ecosystems?  
   **Ans:** **Pyramid of Energy** *(Pyramid of Biomass is inverted in aquatic/pond/sea ecosystems).*

4. **Q (SSC CHSL):** Lichens are a classic symbiotic (mutualistic) association between which two organisms, and they serve as bio-indicators of which pollutant?  
   **Ans:** **Algae (or Cyanobacteria) and Fungi**; bio-indicators of **Sulphur Dioxide ($SO_2$)** air pollution.

5. **Q (SSC CGL):** Which interaction describes barnacles growing on the back of a whale, or an orchid growing as an epiphyte on a mango branch?  
   **Ans:** **Commensalism ($+/0$)** *(One benefits, the other is unaffected).*

6. **Q (SSC GD / MTS):** What is the sequence of ecological succession starting in a newly formed pond or aquatic body called?  
   **Ans:** **Hydrarch succession** *(Starts with **Phytoplankton** as pioneer species and ends in a mesic forest climax community).*

7. **Q (SSC CGL):** Which gas is NOT included among the 8 pollutants monitored under India's National Air Quality Index (NAQI)?  
   **Ans:** **Carbon Dioxide ($CO_2$)** *(The 8 pollutants are $PM_{10}, PM_{2.5}, NO_2, SO_2, CO, O_3, NH_3, Pb$).*

8. **Q (SSC CGL):** Minamata disease, first reported in Japan in 1956, is caused by poisoning from which heavy metal?  
   **Ans:** **Mercury / Methylmercury ($Hg$)**.

9. **Q (SSC CHSL):** Itai-Itai (Ouch-Ouch) disease is caused by chronic poisoning of which element?  
   **Ans:** **Cadmium ($Cd$)**.

10. **Q (SSC CGL):** Blue Baby Syndrome (Methemoglobinemia) in infants is caused by excessive contamination of drinking water by:  
    **Ans:** **Nitrates ($NO_3^-$)**.

11. **Q (SSC CHSL):** Blackfoot disease is associated with prolonged intake of groundwater contaminated with:  
    **Ans:** **Arsenic ($As$)**.

12. **Q (SSC GD):** Which toxic gas leaked during the Bhopal Gas Tragedy on the night of December 2–3, 1984?  
    **Ans:** **Methyl Isocyanate (MIC, $CH_3NCO$)** from the Union Carbide pesticide plant.

13. **Q (SSC CGL):** Acid rain is characterized by rainwater having a pH below what value, and is primarily caused by which gases?  
    **Ans:** **$\text{pH} < 5.6$**; caused mainly by **$SO_2$ and $NO_x$** forming $H_2SO_4$ and $HNO_3$.

14. **Q (SSC CHSL):** What unit is used to measure the thickness of the atmospheric ozone layer?  
    **Ans:** **Dobson Unit (DU)** *(Ozone Hole corresponds to $<220\text{ DU}$).*

15. **Q (SSC CGL):** Which international treaty signed on September 16, 1987 aims to phase out ozone-depleting substances like CFCs?  
    **Ans:** **Montreal Protocol** *(World Ozone Day is celebrated on **September 16**).*

16. **Q (SSC CHSL):** The Kigali Amendment (2016) to the Montreal Protocol specifically targets the phase-down of which group of chemicals?  
    **Ans:** **Hydrofluorocarbons (HFCs)** *(Potent greenhouse gases).*

17. **Q (SSC CGL):** Which is the ONLY floating National Park in the world, famous for the Sangai deer and floating 'phumdis'?  
    **Ans:** **Keibul Lamjao National Park** *(Located on **Loktak Lake**, Manipur).*

18. **Q (SSC CHSL):** Which state in India has the highest number of National Parks?  
    **Ans:** **Madhya Pradesh (11 National Parks)** *(Among UTs, Andaman & Nicobar Islands has 9; Punjab has 0).*

19. **Q (SSC CGL):** Which is India's first National Park (1936), largest National Park, and smallest National Park respectively?  
    **Ans:** **Jim Corbett NP** (Uttarakhand, 1936, formerly Hailey NP); **Hemis NP** (Ladakh — largest); **South Button Island NP** (A&N Islands — smallest).

20. **Q (SSC CHSL):** Project Tiger was launched in India on April 1, 1973 from which National Park, and who is known as the 'Tiger Man of India'?  
    **Ans:** **Jim Corbett National Park**; **Kailash Sankhala**.

21. **Q (SSC CGL):** According to the 2022 All-India Tiger Estimation (released 2023), which state holds the highest tiger population in India?  
    **Ans:** **Madhya Pradesh (785 tigers)** out of **3,682** total wild tigers, followed by **Karnataka (563)** and **Uttarakhand (560)**.

22. **Q (SSC CGL):** Which is the largest Tiger Reserve in India by area?  
    **Ans:** **Nagarjunasagar–Srisailam Tiger Reserve** *(Andhra Pradesh / Telangana).*

23. **Q (SSC CHSL):** Which was the FIRST Biosphere Reserve established in India (1986), and which is the largest by area?  
    **Ans:** **Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve** (1986, spanning TN, Kerala & Karnataka); **Great Rann of Kutch** (Gujarat, $12,454\text{ km}^2$) is the largest; **Dibru-Saikhowa** (Assam) is the smallest.

24. **Q (SSC CGL / Current Affairs):** Which Indian Biosphere Reserve became the 13th Indian site to be included in UNESCO's World Network of Biosphere Reserves (MAB) in September 2025?  
    **Ans:** **Cold Desert Biosphere Reserve** *(Himachal Pradesh).*

25. **Q (SSC CHSL):** Which were the FIRST two wetlands in India designated under the Ramsar Convention in 1981?  
    **Ans:** **Chilika Lake (Odisha)** and **Keoladeo National Park (Rajasthan)**.

26. **Q (SSC CGL):** Which two Indian Ramsar sites are currently listed under the Montreux Record?  
    **Ans:** **Keoladeo National Park (Rajasthan)** and **Loktak Lake (Manipur)** *(Chilika Lake was removed in 2002).*

27. **Q (SSC CHSL):** Which state in India has the highest number of Ramsar Wetlands?  
    **Ans:** **Tamil Nadu (20 sites)**, followed by **Uttar Pradesh (14 sites)** *(out of 103 Indian Ramsar sites as of Oct 2026).*

28. **Q (SSC CGL):** How many Biodiversity Hotspots exist in India, and which one covers the Nicobar Islands?  
    **Ans:** **4 Hotspots** (Himalaya, Western Ghats, Indo-Burma, Sundaland); **Sundaland** covers the Nicobar Islands *(Andaman Islands fall under Indo-Burma).*

29. **Q (SSC CHSL):** The drastic decline (>95%) of vultures in India was caused by livestock treated with which veterinary painkiller?  
    **Ans:** **Diclofenac** *(Banned for veterinary use in 2006; replaced by **Meloxicam**).*

30. **Q (SSC CGL):** Under Project Cheetah (September 17, 2022), cheetahs from Namibia and South Africa were translocated to which National Park?  
    **Ans:** **Kuno National Park** *(Sheopur district, Madhya Pradesh).*

31. **Q (SSC GD):** Which Sanctuary in Bihar is dedicated to India's National Aquatic Animal (Gangetic River Dolphin)?  
    **Ans:** **Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Sanctuary** *(Bhagalpur, Bihar).*

32. **Q (SSC CGL):** Which Indian environmental act is known as the 'Umbrella Act' and was enacted in 1986 after the Bhopal Gas Tragedy?  
    **Ans:** **Environment (Protection) Act, 1986**.

33. **Q (SSC CHSL):** Under the Wildlife (Protection) Amendment Act, 2022, the original 6 schedules were reduced to how many schedules?  
    **Ans:** **4 Schedules** *(Schedule I & II for animals, Schedule III for plants, Schedule IV for CITES-listed specimens; old Schedule V for vermin was omitted).*

34. **Q (SSC CGL):** Who led the 1973 Chipko Movement in Chamoli (Uttarakhand) and the 1983 Appiko Movement in Karnataka respectively?  
    **Ans:** **Sunderlal Bahuguna, Chandi Prasad Bhatt & Gaura Devi** (Chipko); **Pandurang Hegde** (Appiko).

35. **Q (SSC CGL / Current Affairs):** What net-zero target year and 'Panchamrit' pledges did India announce at UNFCCC COP26 (Glasgow), and where were COP29 (2024) & COP30 (2025) held?  
    **Ans:** India pledged **Net Zero by 2070** (and 500 GW non-fossil capacity by 2030) at COP26; **COP29 (2024)** was held in **Baku, Azerbaijan** (USD 300B/yr NCQG) and **COP30 (2025)** was held in **Belém, Brazil** (Baku–Belém USD 1.3T Roadmap).
