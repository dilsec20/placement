## Introduction

Sentence Improvement and Sentence Rearrangement are **two distinct but equally important** question types in SSC CHSL English. Together, they account for 3-5 questions in Tier 1.

**Sentence Improvement:** A sentence with an underlined/bold part is given. You must choose the best replacement for that part, or "No improvement."

**Sentence Rearrangement (Para Jumbles):** Jumbled sentences are given. You must arrange them in the correct logical/chronological order to form a meaningful paragraph.

## Sentence Improvement

### What is Tested?
1. **Grammar correctness** — tense, voice, articles, prepositions
2. **Better word choice** — more precise or appropriate word
3. **Conciseness** — removing wordiness
4. **Parallelism** — maintaining consistent structure
5. **Idiomatic expressions** — using correct idioms

### Common Error Categories for Improvement

#### Category 1: Tense Errors
```
❌ He has went to the market.
✅ He has gone to the market. (Present Perfect: has + V3)

❌ She is working here since 2010.
✅ She has been working here since 2010. (Present Perfect Continuous for duration)

❌ If I will come, I will help you.
✅ If I come, I will help you. (Conditional Type 1: Present + will)

❌ He told that he is coming.
✅ He told that he was coming. (Indirect speech: is → was)
```

#### Category 2: Subject-Verb Agreement
```
❌ The team are playing well.
✅ The team is playing well. (collective noun acting as unit)

❌ Neither of the boys have come.
✅ Neither of the boys has come. (Neither → singular)

❌ One of my friend is a doctor.
✅ One of my friends is a doctor. (One of the + plural noun)
```

#### Category 3: Article Corrections
```
❌ He is a honest man.
✅ He is an honest man. (silent h → vowel sound)

❌ She is an university student.
✅ She is a university student. (u = /juː/ → consonant sound)

❌ The gold is a precious metal.
✅ Gold is a precious metal. (no "the" for general statements)
```

#### Category 4: Preposition Corrections
```
❌ He is married with her.
✅ He is married to her.

❌ I prefer tea than coffee.
✅ I prefer tea to coffee.

❌ He is good in mathematics.
✅ He is good at mathematics.

❌ She is angry on me.
✅ She is angry with me.
```

#### Category 5: Word Choice Improvement
```
❌ The criminal was hung. 
✅ The criminal was hanged. (hanged = executed; hung = suspended)

❌ He gave me an advice.
✅ He gave me advice. (advice = uncountable; no "an")

❌ Please return back the book.
✅ Please return the book. (return already means "give back")

❌ She literally died of embarrassment.
✅ She almost died of embarrassment. (literally = actually happened)
```

#### Category 6: Parallelism Improvement
```
❌ He likes reading, writing, and to play.
✅ He likes reading, writing, and playing.

❌ She is intelligent, hardworking, and has beauty.
✅ She is intelligent, hardworking, and beautiful.

❌ The teacher told students to read carefully, write neatly, and that they should submit on time.
✅ The teacher told students to read carefully, write neatly, and submit on time.
```

#### Category 7: Redundancy Removal
```
❌ He returned back home.
✅ He returned home.

❌ She repeated it again.
✅ She repeated it.

❌ We cooperated together.
✅ We cooperated.

❌ It was his past experience.
✅ It was his experience.

❌ The end result was positive.
✅ The result was positive.

❌ Free gift with every purchase.
✅ Gift with every purchase.
```

#### Category 8: Voice Improvement
```
❌ A letter was being written by her.
✅ She was writing a letter. (Active voice is preferred when possible)

❌ The work has been completed by us.
✅ We have completed the work.
```

#### Category 9: Connectors and Transitions
```
❌ He is poor but he is honest. (acceptable but can be improved)
✅ Although he is poor, he is honest. (more formal)

❌ He was tired, he continued working.
✅ Although he was tired, he continued working.
✅ He was tired; however, he continued working.
✅ He was tired, but he continued working.
```

### Master List: Common Sentence Improvement Patterns

| Incorrect Pattern | Correct Pattern |
|-------------------|----------------|

| More + -er comparative | Just -er OR just more |
| Prefer...than | Prefer...to |

## Sentence Rearrangement (Para Jumbles)

### Types of Rearrangement Questions

#### Type 1: PQRS Format
A fixed opening and/or closing sentence is given, with 4 jumbled sentences (P, Q, R, S) to arrange.
```
S1: India is a diverse country.
P: It has many languages and cultures.
Q: Hindi is the most spoken language.
R: English is used for official purposes.
S: Unity in diversity is India's strength.
S6: This makes India unique.

Correct order: S1-P-Q-R-S-S6
```

#### Type 2: Complete Rearrangement
All sentences are jumbled with no fixed opening/closing.

#### Type 3: Sentence Position Questions
"Which sentence should come FIRST/SECOND/LAST?"

### Step-by-Step Method to Solve Para Jumbles

#### Step 1: Find the Opening Sentence
Look for:
- **Introduction of a topic/person/concept** — "There was a king..." or "India is..."
- **General statement** — broad statements come first
- **No backward reference** — no "he," "she," "they," "this," "that," "such," "these" (unless context is clear)

#### Step 2: Find the Closing Sentence
Look for:
- **Conclusion words** — therefore, thus, hence, finally, in conclusion
- **Summary statement** — wraps up all ideas
- **Result/consequence** — the outcome of the narrative

#### Step 3: Find Mandatory Pairs
Look for:
- **Pronoun links** — "He" must follow a sentence that mentions the person
- **This/That/These/Such** — refers to something just mentioned
- **Cause-Effect** — "Because of this..." must follow the cause
- **Chronological links** — time-based sequences
- **Contrast links** — "However," "But," "On the other hand"
- **Additional information** — "Moreover," "Furthermore," "Also"
- **Example links** — "For example," "For instance," "Such as"

#### Step 4: Use Transition Words to Connect

| Word/Phrase | Signal |
|------------|--------|
| However, But, Yet | Contrast — opposing idea follows |
| Moreover, Furthermore, Also | Addition — similar/supporting idea follows |
| Therefore, Hence, Thus | Conclusion — result follows |
| For example, For instance | Example — illustration follows |
| First, Second, Finally | Sequence — ordering |
| In addition, Besides | Extra point |
| On the other hand | Opposite viewpoint |
| In contrast, Conversely | Comparison of opposites |
| As a result, Consequently | Cause-effect |
| Meanwhile, Simultaneously | Events happening at the same time |

#### Step 5: Verify the Arrangement
Read the entire paragraph to check:
- Does it flow logically?
- Is there a clear beginning, middle, and end?
- Do pronouns match their references?
- Is the chronological order correct?

### Common Linking Patterns

#### Pattern 1: General → Specific
```
General statement about a topic
↓
Specific details/examples
↓
Conclusion
```

#### Pattern 2: Problem → Cause → Effect → Solution
```
Statement of the problem
↓
What caused it
↓
What happened because of it
↓
How to fix it
```

#### Pattern 3: Chronological (Time-based)
```
First event (earliest)
↓
Second event
↓
Third event
↓
Last event / Current situation
```

#### Pattern 4: Introduction → Elaboration → Contrast → Conclusion
```
Introduce the topic
↓
Explain in detail
↓
Present the opposite view
↓
Conclude with final thought
```

## Tips and Tricks

### ⚡ For Sentence Improvement
1. **Read the sentence completely** before looking at the underlined part
2. **Identify what type of error** it is (tense, preposition, article, etc.)
3. **Check if "No improvement" is valid** — don't change correct sentences
4. **Compare all options** — choose the most grammatically correct AND concise one
5. **Trust the rules** — not what "sounds right" (spoken English ≠ written English)

### ⚡ For Sentence Rearrangement
1. **Find the independent sentence** (no pronouns, no backward reference) — it's likely first
2. **Look for proper nouns** — they usually come before pronouns
3. **Match pronouns to their antecedents** — create mandatory pairs
4. **Use transition words** as connectors
5. **If stuck, try elimination** — use the answer options to narrow down
6. **Read the formed paragraph aloud** — it should sound natural

### 🎯 Most Tested Sentence Improvement Patterns
1. Tense correction (especially since/for, if clauses)
2. Preposition correction (interested in, prefer to, senior to)
3. Redundancy removal (return back, repeat again)
4. Article correction (a/an based on sound)
5. Subject-verb agreement
6. Parallelism errors in lists

## Last 10 Years PYQs

### Sentence Improvement PYQs

### PYQ 1 — SSC CHSL 2024
**Q.** Improve the underlined part: "He has been working here **since five years**."
- (a) for five years ✅ (b) from five years (c) since five year (d) No improvement
- **Explanation:** "five years" is a period → use "for"

### PYQ 2 — SSC CHSL 2024
**Q.** "She **prefers milk than** juice."
- (a) prefers milk to ✅ (b) prefers milk over (c) prefer milk than (d) No improvement
- **Explanation:** Prefer A to B (not "than")

### PYQ 3 — SSC CHSL 2023
**Q.** "The meeting **was comprised of** ten members."
- (a) comprised ✅ (b) was comprising of (c) comprised with (d) No improvement
- **Explanation:** "Comprise" doesn't take "of" — "The meeting comprised ten members"

### PYQ 4 — SSC CHSL 2023
**Q.** "He asked me **that where did I** live."
- (a) where I lived ✅ (b) that where I live (c) where do I live (d) No improvement
- **Explanation:** Indirect speech: no "that" with wh-words; no did; past tense

### PYQ 5 — SSC CHSL 2022
**Q.** "She is **more senior than** me in this office."
- (a) senior to ✅ (b) more senior to (c) senior from (d) No improvement
- **Explanation:** "Senior to" not "senior than"

### PYQ 6 — SSC CHSL 2022
**Q.** "Unless you **do not** study hard, you will fail."
- (a) study ✅ (b) will not study (c) don't study (d) No improvement
- **Explanation:** Unless = if not; adding "not" makes double negative

### PYQ 7 — SSC CHSL 2021
**Q.** "He **discussed about** the problem with his friends."
- (a) discussed ✅ (b) discussed on (c) discussed upon (d) No improvement
- **Explanation:** "Discuss" is transitive — no preposition needed

### PYQ 8 — SSC CHSL 2021
**Q.** "She **could be able to** complete the task."
- (a) could ✅ (b) can be able to (c) could able to (d) No improvement
- **Explanation:** "Could" already means "was able to" — don't combine

### PYQ 9 — SSC CHSL 2020
**Q.** "No sooner did he arrive **when** the show started."
- (a) than ✅ (b) then (c) that (d) No improvement
- **Explanation:** "No sooner...than" is the correct pair

### PYQ 10 — SSC CHSL 2020
**Q.** "The criminal was **hung** till death."
- (a) hanged ✅ (b) hunged (c) hanging (d) No improvement
- **Explanation:** hanged = executed; hung = suspended an object

### Sentence Rearrangement PYQs

### PYQ 11 — SSC CHSL 2024
**Q.** Arrange in correct order:
P: However, its effects on the environment are concerning.
Q: Technology has brought many benefits to mankind.
R: We must find a balance between progress and preservation.
S: Pollution, deforestation, and climate change are some examples.

- (a) Q-P-S-R ✅ (b) P-Q-R-S (c) S-Q-P-R (d) R-S-Q-P
- **Explanation:** Q introduces topic → P shows contrast → S gives examples → R concludes

### PYQ 12 — SSC CHSL 2023
**Q.** Arrange in correct order:
P: He decided to work harder.
Q: Raman failed in his first attempt.
R: Finally, he cleared the exam with flying colors.
S: He enrolled in a coaching class.

- (a) Q-P-S-R ✅ (b) P-Q-R-S (c) Q-S-P-R (d) S-Q-R-P
- **Explanation:** Q (event) → P (decision) → S (action) → R (result)

### PYQ 13 — SSC CHSL 2022
**Q.** Arrange in correct order:
P: The Mughal Empire lasted for over 300 years.
Q: Babur founded the Mughal Empire in 1526.
R: Akbar was one of its greatest rulers.
S: The last Mughal emperor was Bahadur Shah Zafar.

- (a) Q-P-R-S ✅ (b) P-Q-S-R (c) R-Q-P-S (d) S-R-Q-P
- **Explanation:** Q (origin) → P (duration) → R (highlight) → S (end)

### PYQ 14 — SSC CHSL 2021
**Q.** Arrange in correct order:
P: This led to widespread destruction.
Q: A powerful earthquake struck Nepal in 2015.
R: International aid poured in from many countries.
S: Thousands of people lost their homes.

- (a) Q-P-S-R ✅ (b) P-Q-R-S (c) Q-S-P-R (d) R-S-Q-P
- **Explanation:** Q (event) → P (immediate effect) → S (specific impact) → R (response)

### PYQ 15 — SSC CHSL 2020
**Q.** Arrange in correct order:
P: It is the largest democracy in the world.
Q: India became independent on August 15, 1947.
R: The constitution came into effect on January 26, 1950.
S: Since then, India has made tremendous progress.

- (a) Q-R-P-S ✅ (b) P-Q-R-S (c) Q-P-R-S (d) R-Q-S-P
- **Explanation:** Q (independence) → R (constitution) → P (fact about India) → S (conclusion)

### PYQ 16 — SSC CHSL 2019
**Q.** Arrange in correct order:
P: She studied medicine at AIIMS.
Q: After graduation, she joined a rural hospital.
R: Priya was born in a small village.
S: She wanted to serve the underprivileged.

- (a) R-P-Q-S ✅ (b) P-R-Q-S (c) R-S-P-Q (d) S-R-P-Q
- **Explanation:** R (birth) → P (education) → Q (career) → S (motivation/goal)

### PYQ 17 — SSC CHSL 2018
**Q.** Improve: "He **entered into** the classroom."
- (a) entered ✅ (b) entered in (c) entered to (d) No improvement
- **Explanation:** "Enter" is transitive — no preposition needed

### PYQ 18 — SSC CHSL 2017
**Q.** Improve: "She **availed the opportunity** to learn."
- (a) availed herself of the opportunity ✅ (b) availed of the opportunity (c) availed for the opportunity (d) No improvement
- **Explanation:** Correct usage: "avail oneself of"

### PYQ 19 — SSC CHSL 2016
**Q.** Improve: "I **reached at** the station on time."
- (a) reached ✅ (b) reached to (c) reached in (d) No improvement
- **Explanation:** "Reach" is transitive — no preposition needed

### PYQ 20 — SSC CHSL 2015
**Q.** Improve: "He **emphasized on** the importance of discipline."
- (a) emphasized ✅ (b) emphasized upon (c) emphasized about (d) No improvement
- **Explanation:** "Emphasize" is transitive — no preposition needed
