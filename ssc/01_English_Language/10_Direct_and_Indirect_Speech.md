## Introduction

Direct and Indirect Speech (also called Reported Speech) is a **must-prepare** topic for SSC CHSL. Questions ask you to convert sentences from direct to indirect speech or vice versa.

**Weightage in SSC CHSL Tier 1:**
- Questions: 2-3 out of 25
- Marks: 2 marks each
- Negative marking: 0.50 marks

---

## What is Direct and Indirect Speech?

### Direct Speech
The exact words spoken by a person, enclosed in quotation marks.
```
He said, "I am going to the market."
         ↑ Reporting verb    ↑ Reported speech (exact words)
```

### Indirect Speech
The meaning of what was said, without quotation marks.
```
He said that he was going to the market.
```

### Key Terms
- **Reporting Verb:** said, told, asked, ordered, requested, etc.
- **Reported Speech:** The actual words spoken
- **Conjunction:** that, if, whether (used to connect)

---

## Rules for Conversion

### Step 1: Remove quotation marks
### Step 2: Add appropriate conjunction (that, if, whether, to)
### Step 3: Change pronouns (SON Rule)
### Step 4: Change tense (one step back)
### Step 5: Change time/place expressions

## Tense Changes (Backshift Rule)

### When Reporting Verb is in PAST tense, change tenses:

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech |
|--------------|----------------|
| Simple Present → | Simple Past |
| Present Continuous → | Past Continuous |
| Present Perfect → | Past Perfect |
| Present Perfect Continuous → | Past Perfect Continuous |
| Simple Past → | Past Perfect |
| Past Continuous → | Past Perfect Continuous |
| Past Perfect → | Past Perfect (No change) |
| will → | would |
| shall → | should/would |
| can → | could |
| may → | might |
| must → | must / had to |

### Detailed Examples

#### Simple Present → Simple Past
```
Direct:   He said, "I go to school daily."
Indirect: He said that he went to school daily.

Direct:   She said, "I like mangoes."
Indirect: She said that she liked mangoes.
```

#### Present Continuous → Past Continuous
```
Direct:   He said, "I am reading a book."
Indirect: He said that he was reading a book.

Direct:   She said, "I am cooking food."
Indirect: She said that she was cooking food.
```

#### Present Perfect → Past Perfect
```
Direct:   He said, "I have finished my work."
Indirect: He said that he had finished his work.

Direct:   She said, "I have seen this movie."
Indirect: She said that she had seen that movie.
```

#### Present Perfect Continuous → Past Perfect Continuous
```
Direct:   He said, "I have been waiting for two hours."
Indirect: He said that he had been waiting for two hours.
```

#### Simple Past → Past Perfect
```
Direct:   He said, "I went to Delhi."
Indirect: He said that he had gone to Delhi.

Direct:   She said, "I wrote a letter."
Indirect: She said that she had written a letter.
```

#### Past Continuous → Past Perfect Continuous
```
Direct:   He said, "I was watching TV."
Indirect: He said that he had been watching TV.
```

#### Past Perfect → Past Perfect (No Change)
```
Direct:   He said, "I had already left."
Indirect: He said that he had already left. (No change)
```

#### Modal Changes
```
Direct:   He said, "I will come tomorrow."
Indirect: He said that he would come the next day.

Direct:   She said, "I can solve this."
Indirect: She said that she could solve that.

Direct:   He said, "I may go to Delhi."
Indirect: He said that he might go to Delhi.

Direct:   She said, "I shall help you."
Indirect: She said that she would help me.

Direct:   He said, "I must leave now."
Indirect: He said that he had to leave then.
```

### When Tense Does NOT Change
Tenses remain unchanged when:

1. **Reporting verb is in present/future tense:**
```
Direct:   He says, "I am happy."
Indirect: He says that he is happy. (No change)

Direct:   She will say, "I love reading."
Indirect: She will say that she loves reading.
```

2. **Universal truths / Habitual facts:**
```
Direct:   The teacher said, "The Earth revolves around the Sun."
Indirect: The teacher said that the Earth revolves around the Sun. (No change)

Direct:   She said, "Water boils at 100°C."
Indirect: She said that water boils at 100°C. (No change)

Direct:   He said, "Honesty is the best policy."
Indirect: He said that honesty is the best policy. (No change)
```

3. **Hypothetical conditions (If clauses Type 2 and 3):**
```
Direct:   He said, "If I were a bird, I would fly."
Indirect: He said that if he were a bird, he would fly. (No change)
```

4. **Could, would, should, might, ought to (already past):**
```
Direct:   She said, "I could swim."
Indirect: She said that she could swim. (No change)

Direct:   He said, "I would help you."
Indirect: He said that he would help me. (No change)
```

## Pronoun Changes (SON Rule)

### SON Rule
- **S** = Subject of reporting verb changes 1st person pronouns
- **O** = Object of reporting verb changes 2nd person pronouns
- **N** = No change for 3rd person pronouns

### First Person Pronouns (I, me, my, mine, we, us, our, ours)
Change according to the **Subject** of the reporting verb.
```
Direct:   He said, "I am happy."
Indirect: He said that he was happy.
(I → he, because subject is "He")

Direct:   She said, "I love my mother."
Indirect: She said that she loved her mother.
(I → she, my → her)

Direct:   They said, "We are students."
Indirect: They said that they were students.
(We → they)
```

### Second Person Pronouns (you, your, yours)
Change according to the **Object** of the reporting verb.
```
Direct:   He said to me, "You are intelligent."
Indirect: He told me that I was intelligent.
(You → I, because object is "me")

Direct:   She said to him, "You should study."
Indirect: She told him that he should study.
(You → he, because object is "him")

Direct:   He said to them, "You are late."
Indirect: He told them that they were late.
(You → they)
```

### Third Person Pronouns (he, she, it, they, him, her, them)
**No change.**
```
Direct:   He said, "She is a doctor."
Indirect: He said that she was a doctor. (No change in "she")
```

## Time and Place Changes

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech |
|--------------|----------------|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tonight | that night |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| yesterday | the previous day / the day before |
| last night | the previous night / the night before |
| last week | the previous week / the week before |
| last month | the previous month |
| last year | the previous year |
| next week | the following week |
| next month | the following month |
| next year | the following year |
| ago | before / earlier |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| these | those |
| thus | so |
| hither | thither |
| hence | thence |
| come | go |

```
Direct:   He said, "I will come here tomorrow."
Indirect: He said that he would go there the next day.

Direct:   She said, "I bought this book yesterday."
Indirect: She said that she had bought that book the previous day.

Direct:   He said, "I am leaving now."
Indirect: He said that he was leaving then.
```

## Types of Sentences in Indirect Speech

### 1. Assertive/Declarative Sentences (Statements)

#### Reporting Verbs: said, told, informed, stated, mentioned, remarked, replied, explained

```
Rule: Subject + reporting verb + that + changed sentence

Direct:   He said, "I am a doctor."
Indirect: He said that he was a doctor.

Direct:   She said to me, "I will help you."
Indirect: She told me that she would help me.
(Note: "said to" → "told")

Direct:   They said, "We have finished the work."
Indirect: They said that they had finished the work.
```

**Important:** "said to" changes to "told" (told MUST have an object)
```
✅ He told me that...
❌ He told that... (wrong — "told" needs an object)
✅ He said that... (correct — "said" doesn't need object)
```

### 2. Interrogative Sentences (Questions)

#### Yes/No Questions
```
Reporting Verbs: asked, inquired, wanted to know
Conjunction: if / whether (NOT "that")
Order: Change to statement order (no question mark)

Direct:   He said to me, "Are you a student?"
Indirect: He asked me if/whether I was a student.

Direct:   She said, "Do you like coffee?"
Indirect: She asked if/whether I liked coffee.

Direct:   He said, "Will you come tomorrow?"
Indirect: He asked if/whether I would come the next day.

Direct:   She said, "Can you help me?"
Indirect: She asked if/whether I could help her.

Direct:   He said, "Have you finished your work?"
Indirect: He asked if/whether I had finished my work.
```

#### Wh-Questions
```
Conjunction: Use the wh-word itself (who, what, when, where, why, how)
NO "that", NO "if/whether"
Order: Change to statement order

Direct:   He said, "Where do you live?"
Indirect: He asked where I lived. (NOT "where did I live")

Direct:   She said, "What is your name?"
Indirect: She asked what my name was.

Direct:   He said, "When will you come?"
Indirect: He asked when I would come.

Direct:   She said, "Why are you crying?"
Indirect: She asked why I was crying.

Direct:   He said, "How did you solve this?"
Indirect: He asked how I had solved that.

Direct:   She said, "Who taught you English?"
Indirect: She asked who had taught me English.
```

**Critical Rule:** In indirect questions, use **statement order** (subject before verb):
```
❌ He asked where did I live.
✅ He asked where I lived.

❌ She asked what was my name.
✅ She asked what my name was.
```

### 3. Imperative Sentences (Commands, Requests, Advice)

```
Reporting Verbs: ordered, commanded, requested, advised, urged, 
                 warned, begged, forbade, suggested

Use "to + V1" for positive; "not to + V1" for negative
NO "that" conjunction

Commands/Orders:
Direct:   He said to me, "Go away."
Indirect: He ordered me to go away.

Direct:   The teacher said, "Sit down."
Indirect: The teacher ordered the students to sit down.

Negative Commands:
Direct:   She said, "Don't make noise."
Indirect: She ordered them not to make noise.

Direct:   He said, "Don't touch it."
Indirect: He forbade me to touch it. / He ordered me not to touch it.

Requests:
Direct:   He said, "Please help me."
Indirect: He requested me to help him.

Direct:   She said, "Kindly lend me your book."
Indirect: She requested me to lend her my book.

Advice:
Direct:   The doctor said, "Take this medicine regularly."
Indirect: The doctor advised me to take that medicine regularly.

Direct:   He said, "You should work hard."
Indirect: He advised me to work hard.

Warnings:
Direct:   The teacher said, "Don't be late again."
Indirect: The teacher warned the students not to be late again.

Suggestions:
Direct:   He said, "Let us go for a walk."
Indirect: He suggested that they should go for a walk.
```

### 4. Exclamatory Sentences

```
Reporting Verbs: exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise/wonder/disgust

Remove exclamation mark; remove interjections (Alas!, Hurrah!, etc.)
Use "that" as conjunction

Joy/Happiness:
Direct:   She said, "Hurrah! We have won the match."
Indirect: She exclaimed with joy that they had won the match.

Direct:   He said, "What a beautiful painting!"
Indirect: He exclaimed that it was a very beautiful painting.

Sorrow/Grief:
Direct:   He said, "Alas! I have lost my wallet."
Indirect: He exclaimed with sorrow that he had lost his wallet.

Surprise/Wonder:
Direct:   She said, "What a surprise!"
Indirect: She exclaimed with surprise.

Direct:   He said, "How beautiful the sunset is!"
Indirect: He exclaimed that the sunset was very beautiful.

Wish/Prayer:
Direct:   He said, "May God bless you!"
Indirect: He prayed that God might bless me.

Direct:   She said, "May you live long!"
Indirect: She wished/prayed that I might live long.
```

### 5. Optative Sentences (Wishes)
```
Direct:   He said, "May you succeed!"
Indirect: He wished that I might succeed.

Direct:   She said, "Would that I were rich!"
Indirect: She wished that she were rich.
```

## Special Cases

### 1. "Said" vs "Told"
```
said → doesn't need an object
told → MUST have an object

✅ He said that he was happy.
✅ He told me that he was happy.
❌ He told that he was happy. (WRONG — needs object)
❌ He said me that he was happy. (WRONG — "said" doesn't take object directly)
```

### 2. Reporting Verb Selection
| Sentence Type | Reporting Verb Options |
|--------------|----------------------|
| Statement | said, told, informed, stated, mentioned, remarked |
| Question | asked, inquired, wanted to know, questioned |
| Command | ordered, commanded, directed |
| Request | requested, begged, implored, entreated |
| Advice | advised, suggested, recommended |
| Warning | warned, cautioned |
| Exclamation | exclaimed (with joy/sorrow/surprise) |
| Wish | wished, prayed, blessed |
| Threat | threatened |
| Promise | promised, assured |

### 3. "Let" in Different Contexts
```
Suggestion:
Direct:   He said, "Let us play."
Indirect: He suggested that they should play.

Permission:
Direct:   He said, "Let him go."
Indirect: He suggested/ordered that he should be allowed to go.

Request:
Direct:   He said, "Let me do it."
Indirect: He requested to be allowed to do it.
```

### 4. "Needn't" and "Mustn't"
```
Direct:   He said, "You needn't come."
Indirect: He said that I needn't come. (No change)

Direct:   She said, "You mustn't lie."
Indirect: She said that I mustn't lie. / She forbade me to lie.
```

## Tips and Tricks

### ⚡ Quick Solving Strategy
1. **Identify the sentence type** — statement, question, command, exclamation
2. **Change the reporting verb** — said/told/asked/ordered
3. **Apply SON rule** for pronouns
4. **Apply backshift** for tenses
5. **Change time/place words**
6. **Check conjunction** — that / if / whether / to

### 🎯 Most Common Exam Traps
1. **"said to" → "told"** (not "said to him that...")
2. **No "that" with if/whether** (questions)
3. **Statement order in indirect questions** (not "where did I live")
4. **No "did/do/does" in indirect questions** 
5. **Universal truths — no tense change**
6. **"Let us" → "suggested"** (not "ordered")
7. **"Please" → "requested"** (not "said")

### 🧠 Memory Tricks
```
SON Rule for pronouns:
S = Subject changes 1st person
O = Object changes 2nd person  
N = No change for 3rd person

Conjunction Guide:
Statements → that
Yes/No Questions → if / whether
Wh-Questions → the wh-word itself
Commands/Requests → to / not to
```
