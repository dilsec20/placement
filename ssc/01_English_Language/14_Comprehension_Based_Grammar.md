## All Grammar Topics — Quick Reference

### Parts of Speech (8 types)

| Part of Speech | Function | Examples |
|---------------|----------|---------|
| **Noun** | Names a person, place, thing, idea | Ram, Delhi, book, happiness |
| **Pronoun** | Replaces a noun | I, he, she, it, they, who |
| **Verb** | Shows action or state | run, eat, is, have, seem |
| **Adjective** | Describes a noun | big, beautiful, clever, red |
| **Adverb** | Modifies verb/adjective/adverb | quickly, very, well, often |
| **Preposition** | Shows relationship | in, on, at, by, with, for |
| **Conjunction** | Connects words/clauses | and, but, or, because, although |
| **Interjection** | Expresses emotion | Alas!, Hurrah!, Oh!, Wow! |

### Types of Nouns
```
Common Noun: boy, city, country
Proper Noun: Ram, Delhi, India (capitalized)
Collective Noun: team, flock, committee
Abstract Noun: happiness, courage, love
Material Noun: gold, water, iron
Countable: book, pen, chair (can be counted)
Uncountable: information, advice, furniture (cannot be counted)
```

### Types of Pronouns
```
Personal: I, you, he, she, it, we, they
Possessive: my, your, his, her, its, our, their, mine, yours
Reflexive: myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, themselves
Demonstrative: this, that, these, those
Interrogative: who, whom, whose, which, what
Relative: who, whom, whose, which, that
Indefinite: someone, anyone, nobody, everyone, each, every
```

## 12 Tenses — Complete Summary

### Tense Structure Table

| Tense | Structure | Signal Words | Example |
|-------|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Simple Present** | V1 / V1+s(es) | daily, always, usually, often, sometimes, every | He **goes** to school. |
| **Present Continuous** | is/am/are + V-ing | now, at present, currently, right now, look!, listen! | She **is reading** now. |
| **Present Perfect** | has/have + V3 | already, just, yet, ever, never, recently, so far, since, for | I **have finished** it. |
| **Present Perfect Continuous** | has/have + been + V-ing | since, for, all day, all morning | He **has been working** since 9 am. |
| **Simple Past** | V2 | yesterday, last week, ago, in 2020, when | She **bought** a car. |
| **Past Continuous** | was/were + V-ing | while, when, at that time | I **was sleeping** when he called. |
| **Past Perfect** | had + V3 | before, after, by the time, already, until | He **had left** before I came. |
| **Past Perfect Continuous** | had + been + V-ing | since, for (before a past event) | She **had been waiting** for 2 hours. |
| **Simple Future** | will/shall + V1 | tomorrow, next week, soon | I **will come** tomorrow. |
| **Future Continuous** | will + be + V-ing | at this time tomorrow, this time next week | He **will be playing** at 5 pm. |
| **Future Perfect** | will + have + V3 | by tomorrow, by next year, by then | She **will have finished** by 6 pm. |
| **Future Perfect Continuous** | will + have + been + V-ing | by, for (in future context) | I **will have been working** for 5 hours by then. |

## Sentence Types

### By Structure
```
1. Simple Sentence: One independent clause
   → He plays cricket.

2. Compound Sentence: Two independent clauses joined by FANBOYS
   → He plays cricket, and she plays tennis.

3. Complex Sentence: One independent + one dependent clause
   → Although he was tired, he continued working.

4. Compound-Complex: Two independent + one or more dependent clauses
   → Although it rained, he went out, and she stayed home.
```

### By Function
```
1. Declarative (Statement): India is a democratic country.
2. Interrogative (Question): Where do you live?
3. Imperative (Command/Request): Open the door. / Please sit down.
4. Exclamatory (Emotion): What a beautiful day! / How wonderful!
5. Optative (Wish): May God bless you! / Long live the king!
```

## Transformation of Sentences

### Affirmative ↔ Negative
```
Affirmative: He is honest.
Negative: He is not dishonest.

Affirmative: Everyone was present.
Negative: No one was absent.

Affirmative: I will always remember you.
Negative: I will never forget you.

Affirmative: He is the tallest boy in the class.
Negative: No other boy in the class is as tall as he is.
```

### Assertive ↔ Interrogative
```
Assertive: Nobody can deny this truth.
Interrogative: Who can deny this truth? (rhetorical)

Assertive: It is a wonderful day.
Interrogative: Isn't it a wonderful day?

Assertive: Everyone loves flowers.
Interrogative: Who doesn't love flowers?
```

### Assertive ↔ Exclamatory
```
Assertive: It is a very beautiful flower.
Exclamatory: What a beautiful flower it is!

Assertive: He is very brave.
Exclamatory: How brave he is!

Assertive: India is a great country.
Exclamatory: What a great country India is!
```

### Simple ↔ Compound ↔ Complex
```
Simple: Being tired, he went to sleep.
Compound: He was tired, so he went to sleep.
Complex: As he was tired, he went to sleep.

Simple: In spite of his hard work, he failed.
Compound: He worked hard, but he failed.
Complex: Although he worked hard, he failed.

Simple: On seeing the police, the thief ran away.
Compound: The thief saw the police and ran away.
Complex: As soon as the thief saw the police, he ran away.
```

### Degree Changes (Positive ↔ Comparative ↔ Superlative)
```
Positive: No other city in India is as big as Mumbai.
Comparative: Mumbai is bigger than any other city in India.
Superlative: Mumbai is the biggest city in India.

Positive: Very few students are as intelligent as Ram.
Comparative: Ram is more intelligent than most other students.
Superlative: Ram is one of the most intelligent students.

Positive: No other metal is as useful as iron.
Comparative: Iron is more useful than any other metal.
Superlative: Iron is the most useful metal.
```

## Subject-Verb Agreement — Master Summary

| Rule | Example |
|------|---------|
| Singular subject → Singular verb | He **goes** to school. |
| Plural subject → Plural verb | They **go** to school. |
| Each/Every/Either/Neither → Singular | Each boy **has** a pen. |
| One of the + Plural Noun → Singular Verb | One of the boys **is** absent. |
| A number of → Plural | A number of students **are** present. |
| The number of → Singular | The number of students **is** 50. |
| Uncountable nouns → Singular | The news **is** true. |
| Either...or / Neither...nor → Nearest subject | Neither he nor they **are** wrong. |
| Both...and → Plural | Both Ram and Shyam **are** here. |
| Not only...but also → Nearest subject | Not only he but also they **are** guilty. |
| Subjects joined by "and" → Plural | Tea and coffee **are** available. |
| Same person/thing with "and" → Singular | Bread and butter **is** his breakfast. |
| Titles/Books → Singular | "Wings of Fire" **is** a good book. |
| Distances/Amounts → Singular | Five kilometers **is** a long walk. |
| Collective noun (unit) → Singular | The team **is** playing well. |
| Collective noun (individuals) → Plural | The jury **are** divided. |

## Common Grammar Formulas

### Quick-Reference Formula Sheet

```
1.  Since + Point of time | For + Period of time
2.  Prefer A to B (not than)
3.  Superior/Senior/Junior to (not than)
4.  Unless = If not (never add "not")
5.  Despite / In spite of (never "despite of")
6.  Hardly...when | Scarcely...when
7.  No sooner...than
8.  Not only...but also
9.  Either...or | Neither...nor
10. Both...and
11. The more...the more
12. Too + adj + to + V1 (excess, negative meaning)
13. Adj + enough + to + V1 (sufficient)
14. So + adj + that (result)
15. Such + noun + that (result)
16. Lest...should
17. Would rather + V1
18. Had better + V1
19. It's time / It's high time + Past Tense
20. As if / As though + Past (present unreal)
21. Wish + Past (present unreal) / Past Perfect (past unreal)
22. Make/Let/Help + Object + V1 (bare infinitive)
23. Suggest/Recommend + that + Subject + V1 (subjunctive)
24. Need not / Dare not + V1
25. Passive: Object + be + V3 + by + Subject
```

### Verbs NOT Commonly Used in Continuous Tense
```
Stative Verbs (usually NOT in -ing form):
Know, believe, understand, remember, forget, realize
Like, love, hate, want, need, prefer, wish
See, hear, smell, taste, feel (perception)
Seem, appear, look, sound
Have (possession), own, belong, possess
Think (opinion), suppose, assume, consider
Agree, disagree, deny, promise, mean

❌ I am knowing the answer.
✅ I know the answer.

❌ She is belonging to Delhi.
✅ She belongs to Delhi.

BUT some stative verbs CAN be continuous with changed meaning:
✅ I am having lunch. (eating — action)
✅ I am thinking about it. (considering — action)
✅ She is seeing the doctor. (visiting — action)
```

## Tips and Tricks

### ⚡ Exam Day Grammar Checklist
1. ☑️ Subject-Verb Agreement
2. ☑️ Tense Consistency
3. ☑️ Correct Preposition
4. ☑️ Correct Article (a/an/the)
5. ☑️ Pronoun Agreement
6. ☑️ Correct Degree of Comparison
7. ☑️ Parallelism in Lists
8. ☑️ No Redundancy
9. ☑️ Correct Conjunction
10. ☑️ Correct Voice/Narration

### 🎯 Most Common Tricks in SSC CHSL
1. **"Each" trap** — Each takes singular verb
2. **"Since/For" trap** — Point vs Period of time
3. **"Unless" trap** — Don't add "not"
4. **"Despite of" trap** — No "of" after "despite"
5. **"Prefer...than" trap** — Use "to" not "than"
6. **"More taller" trap** — No double comparative
7. **"Return back" trap** — Redundancy
8. **"An university" trap** — "A university" (consonant sound)
